## 🔑 Central Theme

Faith is not a religious feeling we manufacture by intensity, a rational conclusion we reach by sufficient evidence, or a technique we master by repetition. It is the God-initiated trust by which we *receive* what God is already offering — relationship, salvation, fellowship — and through which we *act* on what cannot yet be seen. The quarter's theme of *Growing in a Relationship With God* converges here: every prior lesson (knowing God, humility, the Bible, study, prayer) presupposes faith, and every later lesson (sin and the gospel, repentance, setbacks, witness, eternity) flows from it.

The lesson navigates four core tensions:

- **Faith vs. sight** — God refuses signs to the sign-demanders ([Mark 8:11–12](#)) while patiently meeting honest doubt ([Mark 9:24](#)). Why this asymmetry?
- **Faith as gift vs. faith as response** — "By grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God" ([Eph 2:8–9](#)). If faith is gift, can it be commanded? If response, how is it not works?
- **Faith vs. feeling** — Believers oscillate between exuberance and despondency, yet biblical faith persists across both. What is the substrate beneath the emotion?
- **Our faith vs. the faith of Jesus** — [Rev 14:12](#) describes God's end-time people as keeping the commandments and *having the faith of Jesus*. Is this faith *in* Jesus, or faith *like* His own?

The Sabbath introduction's image — "faith is like Wi-Fi: invisible, but it connects you to what you need" — points to faith's relational nature. It is the medium of contact, not the content. The aim of this lesson is not to *strengthen our faith* by effort, but to *fix our gaze* on the One who is faithful, so that faith — His gift to us — does what it was designed to do.

## 📚 Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### [Hebrews 11:1](#) (Memory Text)

- **Context:** Hebrews was written to Jewish Christians tempted to abandon Christ and return to Mosaic ritual under persecution. Chapter 11 functions as the writer's "hall of faith" — a litany of saints who endured because they trusted a God they could not see.
- **"Substance" or "assurance":** The word literally means "what stands under" — the solid foundation beneath what cannot yet be seen. In the legal world of the day it referred to a title deed, the document that proves ownership before the property is in hand. Faith is not wishful thinking; it is the title deed to what God has promised.
- **"Evidence" or "conviction":** A courtroom word — the conclusive proof that wins the case. Faith is the inner verdict that things unseen are nonetheless real.
- **"Things hoped for" and "things not seen":** Not vague optimism. Hope in Hebrews is tied to specific promises — resurrection, the heavenly Jerusalem, a better country. The unseen is not unreal; it is awaiting unveiling.
- **NLT vs. NKJV:** The NLT renders the verse with admirable freshness ("faith shows the reality... it is the evidence"), but the older language ("substance... evidence") preserves the legal and ontological weight. Faith is both *what we possess now* (title deed) and *what proves the case* (courtroom evidence) for things yet unseen.

### [Mark 8:11–12](#) (Sunday — Sign-Seeking)

- **Context:** The Pharisees demand a sign from heaven *after* Jesus has fed four thousand. They have already seen miracles; they are not asking for evidence but for proof on their terms.
- **"Sighed deeply in His spirit":** This is a rare, intense word used only here in the New Testament. Jesus' grief is bodily and audible — the same root appears for the Spirit's "groanings" in [Rom 8:26](#). This is not impatience; it is sorrow.
- **"Why does this generation seek a sign?":** The *genea* (generation) language echoes Moses' indictment of "this perverse and crooked generation" ([Deut 32:5](#)). Sign-demanding is not the action of seekers but of a generation hardened against what is already revealed.
- **"No sign shall be given":** Compare [Matt 12:39](#) where Jesus adds "except the sign of Jonah" — His death and resurrection. The cross *is* the sign, but it is precisely the sign that sign-seekers cannot accept because it does not bend to their terms.

### [Matthew 15:21–28](#) (Monday — The Canaanite Woman)

- **Context:** Jesus has withdrawn to Tyre and Sidon, Gentile territory. A Canaanite (Mark calls her Syrophoenician) woman approaches.
- **"Have mercy on me, O Lord, Son of David!":** Astonishing for a Gentile woman — she addresses Jesus with a *Jewish messianic title*. Her theology is more advanced than the Pharisees'.
- **Jesus' silence and the disciples' rebuff:** Jesus *answered her not a word*. This is uncharacteristic and pedagogically deliberate. He is allowing her faith to be displayed for the disciples' instruction.
- **"It is not good to take the children's bread and throw it to the little dogs":** The word for "dogs" here is the diminutive — household pets, not street curs — but the saying is still scandalous on its face. Jesus is voicing the conventional Jewish view *aloud* so the woman can answer it.
- **"Yes, Lord, yet even the little dogs eat the crumbs":** She does not deny the order of salvation (Israel first) but claims the overflow of grace. This is theological precision under duress.
- **"O woman, great is your faith!":** Jesus uses the same superlative ("great faith") for only two people in the Gospels — this woman and a Roman centurion ([Matt 8:10](#)). Both are Gentiles. Both demonstrate that faith is not ethnic.

### [Luke 7:1–10](#) (Monday — The Centurion)

- **Context:** A Roman officer — a man of authority in the occupying army — asks Jesus to heal his servant. He sends Jewish elders first, then a second delegation: "I am not worthy that You should enter under my roof."
- **"Only speak a word, and my servant will be healed" (v. 7):** The centurion grasps something the religious establishment misses: Jesus' *word* carries divine authority. He reasons by analogy from his own military command — "I say to one, 'Go,' and he goes" — to Jesus' command over disease and death.
- **"I have not found such great faith, not even in Israel!" (v. 9):** Jesus *marveled* (*ethaumasen*) — one of only two times the Gospels record Him marveling (the other is at unbelief in Nazareth, [Mark 6:6](#)). The grammar of divine wonder is striking: Jesus is genuinely impressed.
- **Theological pattern:** Both the centurion and the Canaanite woman are outsiders. Both demonstrate that great faith does not require great theology so much as great trust in the One whose authority extends to what we cannot reach.

### [Mark 9:24](#) (Monday — Honest Unbelief)

- **Context:** A father whose son is demon-possessed brings the boy to the disciples, who fail. He then says to Jesus, "*If* You can do anything, have compassion." Jesus replies, "*If* you can believe, all things are possible."
- **"Lord, I believe; help my unbelief!":** One verb of action ("I believe") and one verb of plea ("help my unbelief") — spoken in the same breath. He does not pretend to faith he does not have. He confesses what he has *and* what he lacks at the same time.
- **Theological significance:** Faith does not require the absence of doubt; it requires the willingness to bring doubt to the One who can answer it. The father is healed precisely because he tells the truth about his condition.
- **Pastoral implication:** The opposite of faith is not doubt but pretense. The father's faith is "small," but it is *honest*, and honest faith is enough because faith's power is in its object, not its intensity.

### [Ephesians 2:8–9](#) (Tuesday — Faith as Gift)

- **"By grace you have been saved through faith":** The verb tense here is a completed state — "you are in a saved condition, and have been so." Salvation is a finished reality, not a process to achieve.
- **"And that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God":** The pronoun "that" in the original is neuter, while both "grace" and "faith" are feminine — so "that" refers to the whole package of salvation-by-grace-through-faith, not to faith alone. The entire transaction, including the faith that receives it, is gift.
- **"Not of works, lest anyone should boast":** Paul cuts off the possibility of spiritual self-congratulation. The architecture of grace makes boasting impossible (cf. [Rom 3:27](#)).

### [Romans 12:3](#) (Tuesday — A Measure of Faith)

- **"A measure of faith":** Some read this as "the *standard* of faith" — the gospel norm by which we measure ourselves — but the simpler reading is that God distributes faith as a gift, in varying portion, like spiritual gifts later in the chapter.
- **Implication:** No one can plead, "I don't have any faith because God hasn't given me any." The text guarantees that faith has been *dealt out* to each one. The question is what we do with what has been given.

### [Hebrews 11](#) (Wednesday — The Hall of Faith)

- **The repeated formula "By faith...":** Eighteen times in the chapter. The grammar emphasizes *the means by which* — these saints did what they did *by* faith, not *because* they had unusually strong faith.
- **Categories of faith's accomplishment:** Faith enables (1) understanding what cannot be observed (v. 3, creation), (2) acceptable worship (Abel), (3) translation without death (Enoch), (4) salvation through judgment (Noah), (5) pilgrim obedience (Abraham), (6) bearing the impossible (Sarah), (7) endurance under suffering (Moses, prophets, martyrs).
- **The "not having received" tension (v. 13, 39–40):** All these died *without* receiving what was promised. Faith is not always vindicated in this life. The chapter refuses to romanticize: some "had trial of mocking and scourging... were stoned, were sawn in two, were tempted, were slain with the sword." Faith does not guarantee deliverance from suffering; it guarantees fellowship with the God who suffers alongside.
- **"God having provided something better for us, that they should not be made perfect apart from us" (v. 40):** The chapter ends with the church-now and the saints-then bound together. Our faith is part of theirs; we complete what they could not see.

### [Revelation 14:12](#) (Thursday — The Faith of Jesus)

- **Context:** The third angel's message concludes with the description of the end-time saints: "Here is the perseverance of the saints, those who keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus."
- **"The faith of Jesus":** The original allows two readings — Jesus' own faith/faithfulness (*subjective*), or faith *in* Jesus (*objective*). Both are theologically defensible, and the ambiguity may be intentional. End-time saints both *trust in* Jesus and *exhibit the trust that He embodied*.
- **The subjective reading:** Jesus *had* faith — He trusted the Father unto death (cf. [Heb 12:2](#): "the author and finisher of *our* faith"; or "the founder and perfecter of faith"). His faith is the model and source of ours.
- **The objective reading:** End-time saints are characterized by faith *in* Jesus, not in works, in performance, or in their own perseverance. Even their commandment-keeping is *of faith*, not against it.
- **Adventist trajectory:** Until the 1888 General Conference (Minneapolis), much Adventist preaching was heavy on commandment-keeping and light on the faith of Jesus. The 1888 message of righteousness by faith — and its slow reception over the following decades — was, by Ellen White's own assessment, the recovery of "the third angel's message in verity" (*Selected Messages*, vol. 1, p. 372).

### [Matthew 26:36–42](#) (Thursday — Gethsemane)

- **"My soul is exceedingly sorrowful, even to death":** The word for "exceedingly sorrowful" means grief that surrounds and encloses — coming from every direction at once. Jesus' anguish is total.
- **"O My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as You will":** The verbal mood matters. "Let pass" (*parelthatō*) is an aorist imperative — a real request. "Not as I will, but as You will" is the substance of His faith. Faith is not the absence of the request to be spared; it is the willingness to release the request to the Father's wisdom.
- **The cup (*potērion*):** Old Testament imagery for the wrath of God against sin ([Isa 51:17](#); [Jer 25:15](#)). Jesus is not asking to escape death generally; He is asking, if possible, to escape *this* — bearing the sin of the world.
- **Faith as obedience under terror:** This is the faith that Revelation 14 commends. Not absence of fear, not absence of sweat-like-blood, but the choice to entrust the soul to the Father even when the body is breaking.

## 💡 Deep Discussion Questions

### On Sign-Seeking and the Hidden God

1. **Jesus sighed deeply and refused the Pharisees' sign ([Mark 8:11–12](#)) but later told doubting Thomas, "Reach your finger here, and look at My hands" ([John 20:27](#)). What is the difference between honest doubt that Jesus accommodates and sign-demanding that Jesus refuses?** Have you ever asked God for a sign? Looking back, were you seeking confirmation of something He had already revealed, or seeking permission to keep waiting? How do you tell the difference in real time?

2. **The lesson observes that we have "6,000 years of biblical history" available and yet still ask for signs. Is there a kind of skepticism that no amount of evidence can satisfy?** What is it actually protecting? When you've been most resistant to faith, was the obstacle intellectual, emotional, or volitional — and how did you know?

3. **The Sabbath intro likens faith to Wi-Fi — invisible but essential for connection. What does this analogy capture, and where does it break down?** Is faith more like a connection medium, a posture, a gift, a relationship, or a muscle? What does each metaphor get right and miss?

4. **God hid Himself from Israel for "their good" ([Deut 8:2–3](#)) so they would learn to depend on His word. Have you experienced a season where God seemed silent and you later understood it as formation rather than abandonment?** What sustained you when the silence was present? What do you wish you had known?

### On Faith That Jesus Recognizes

5. **The Canaanite woman ([Matt 15:21–28](#)) and the centurion ([Luke 7:1–10](#)) — both Gentiles, both outsiders — receive Jesus' praise of "great faith." Why are the two whom Jesus singles out for great faith *not* in the covenant community?** What does this say about where God finds faith? About where we look for it (and miss it)?

6. **The father in [Mark 9:24](#) prays, "Lord, I believe; help my unbelief!" — confessing faith and lack of faith in the same breath. When you pray, are you more likely to overstate your faith or understate it?** Which is more honest before God? Is performative faith a form of unbelief?

7. **Jesus "did not commit Himself" to those who believed because of signs ([John 2:23–25](#)), "for He knew what was in man." What kind of belief was that, and why was it insufficient?** Have you ever believed in Jesus for what He could *do for you* rather than for who He *is*? What's the difference, and how does it show?

8. **The centurion reasoned from his own military authority ("I say to one, 'Go,' and he goes") to Jesus' authority over disease. He had no Jewish theological training. What does this suggest about how faith is formed — through theology, through analogy, through encounter?** What in your own life or work has functioned as the "analogy" by which you understood God's authority?

### On Faith and Feeling

9. **The lesson insists "faith is not a feeling." But Scripture is full of feeling — Hannah's bitterness, David's despair, Jeremiah's tears, Jesus' Gethsemane grief. What is the right relationship between faith and feeling?** Is feeling the *enemy* of faith, the *expression* of faith, the *test* of faith, or something else? When does feeling mislead, and when does it tell the truth?

10. **The "measure of faith" in [Romans 12:3](#) suggests faith is distributed — some have more, some less, but everyone receives some. Does this trouble you?** If God distributes faith in varying measure, what does that imply about those who seem to have little, or none? What is *our* responsibility within God's distribution?

11. **The mustard seed parable ([Matt 17:20](#)) says even tiny faith can move mountains. Yet in your experience, has small faith felt powerful, or has the smallness been the main feature you noticed?** What "mountains" has God moved through faith you barely had? What mountains have not moved, and what does that mean?

12. **Faith is described as a gift ([Eph 2:8](#)), but also as something we are responsible to exercise, grow, and not lose ([1 Tim 4:1](#)). How do you hold "faith is gift" and "faith is your responsibility" together without collapsing one into the other?** When you pray for "more faith," what exactly are you asking for?

### On the Faith of Jesus

13. **[Rev 14:12](#): end-time saints have "the faith of Jesus." Is this faith *in* Jesus or faith *like* Jesus' own? Does the answer change anything practical about how you live?** If Jesus *had* faith (Gethsemane), what did He trust *for*, and what does His trust teach us about ours?

14. **In Gethsemane, Jesus said, "Not as I will, but as You will." Is this submission, or is this faith?** Is there a difference? When you've prayed similar prayers, what shifted in you between the asking and the surrendering?

15. **The early Adventist understanding of [Rev 14:12](#) emphasized "the commandments of God" heavily and "the faith of Jesus" only later (after 1888). What changed when "the faith of Jesus" was given its proper weight?** What does our current preaching of commandment-keeping look like in practice — does it carry the faith of Jesus, or has it sometimes drifted back?

16. **Hebrews 11 ends with saints who "died, not having received the promises" (v. 13, 39). Faith was not always rewarded with the thing promised in this life. How does that reshape your understanding of what faith is *for*?** Are you willing to die having not received? What promise are you most attached to receiving in this life?

### On Faith and Practice

17. **Romans 10:17: "Faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God." If faith is generated through Scripture, what does that mean for someone whose Bible reading has gone dry?** Is the problem the practice, the heart, the method — or is dryness itself sometimes a season faith must move through?

18. **Hebrews 12:1–2 says we "lay aside every weight, and the sin which so easily ensnares us... looking unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith." What weights — not necessarily sins, but encumbrances — are presently slowing your faith?** What would "laying aside" actually require for you this week?

19. **Galatians 2:20: "It is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me; and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God." How does life *by faith* differ from life *informed by faith*?** Where in your own life is faith the operating system, and where is it the wallpaper?

20. **Looking at the breadth of Hebrews 11 — Abel's worship, Noah's ark, Abraham's pilgrimage, Sarah's pregnancy, Moses' refusal of Egypt, Rahab's hospitality, the martyrs' endurance — which biblical figure's faith looks most like the kind of faith God is currently asking of you?** What does that tell you about the next step?

## ✍️ Ellen White Integration Points

*Used sparingly, as amplification of Scripture rather than substitute.*

### 1. On signs and spiritual renovation

> *"No mere external evidence could benefit them. What they needed was not intellectual enlightenment, but spiritual renovation."*
> — *The Desire of Ages*, p. 406

**Discussion:** Connect this to [Mark 8:11–12](#). The Pharisees' problem was not insufficient evidence but unrenovated hearts. When have you needed *renovation* more than *information*? How do you tell the difference in your own seeking? Is there a question you have been pursuing intellectually that is really a question of the will?

### 2. On the necessity of cherished or rejected unbelief

> *"The unbelief which is cherished in the soul has a bewitching power... The soul must have the precious plants of faith and love put in the soil of the heart."*
> — *Faith and Works*, p. 78

**Discussion:** Unbelief is here described as *cherished* — tended, watered, kept alive. Faith is described as a plant *put in* the soil by God but tended by us. What unbelief have you, perhaps, been tending without realizing? What would replanting look like? Where does the planting come from — Scripture, prayer, community, suffering, all of these?

### 3. On the faith of Jesus

> *"The commandments of God have been proclaimed, but the faith of Jesus Christ has not been proclaimed by Seventh-day Adventists as of equal importance, the law and the gospel going hand in hand."*
> — *Selected Messages*, vol. 3, p. 184

**Discussion:** Ellen White herself names a historic imbalance — and corrects it. The third angel's message in [Rev 14:12](#) holds both commandment-keeping *and* the faith of Jesus together. When the two come apart in practice, what does that look like in a congregation? In your own walk? How would you describe the present balance in your spiritual community?

## 🌐 Practical Application for Modern Context

**Individual:**

- **The "I believe; help my unbelief" prayer:** Take the father's prayer in [Mark 9:24](#) and make it your daily prayer this week. Each morning, name one specific thing you believe about God, and one specific area where you struggle to believe. Say both aloud. Notice what shifts over a week of honest, two-handed prayer.

- **Sign-audit:** Examine your prayer life over the past month. Have you been asking God for *signs* (visible confirmations on your terms) or for *grace* (the capacity to trust without confirmation)? There is nothing wrong with asking God to lead clearly, but sign-demanding and faith-seeking are different postures. Which has been dominant?

- **Faith biography:** Write a one-page faith biography — not what you believe, but how you came to believe it. Trace the people, books, experiences, and seasons through which faith was given. This is an exercise in noticing grace. Hebrews 11 is, after all, a list of names.

**In Sabbath School:**

- **Two-Gentile study:** Take 20 minutes to study the Canaanite woman ([Matt 15:21–28](#)) and the centurion ([Luke 7:1–10](#)) side by side. What do they have in common? What is different? Why does Jesus specifically commend their faith? Discuss what it means that Jesus' twin examples of "great faith" are both outsiders.

- **Faith and feeling round-robin:** Invite each member to complete two sentences: "My faith feels strongest when ___" and "My faith feels weakest when ___." Notice the patterns — are the answers about external circumstances, internal states, spiritual disciplines, community? What does this reveal about the architecture of your communal faith life?

- **The faith of Jesus, in concrete terms:** Discuss what it would look like, in your local context, to "keep the commandments of God *and* have the faith of Jesus" with equal weight. Are there practices in your church that emphasize one over the other? How could the balance be restored — without abandoning either?

**Intellectually:**

- **The genitive question:** "The faith of Jesus" ([Rev 14:12](#)) is grammatically ambiguous — Jesus' own faith, or faith in Jesus. For a deep study, work through the parallel construction in [Gal 2:16, 20](#); [Rom 3:22](#); [Phil 3:9](#). The "faith of Christ" question is one of the most consequential debates in contemporary New Testament scholarship. What is at stake theologically in choosing one reading over the other? Why might Scripture preserve the ambiguity?

- **Hebrews 11's quiet realism:** Note v. 35–38 — saints who were tortured, mocked, sawn in two. Faith does not always end in deliverance. How does this affect prosperity-gospel readings of faith? How does it shape pastoral care for those whose faith has not produced the outcome they prayed for?

## 🧵 Theological Threads to Follow

- **Faith and grace:** [Eph 2:8](#) puts grace *prior* to faith — we are saved *by grace* (the ground) *through faith* (the means). Faith does not earn grace; faith receives it. The architecture matters: collapse one and you get either legalism (faith without grace) or presumption (grace without faith).

- **Faith and the Spirit:** [Rom 8:26](#) describes the Spirit's intercession when "we do not know what we should pray for as we ought." Faith is not a solo performance. The Spirit groans within us; Christ intercedes above us; the Father receives. Faith is participation in Trinitarian life.

- **Faith and works:** James 2 holds faith and works together — "faith without works is dead." Paul holds them *apart* — "to him who does not work but believes... his faith is accounted for righteousness" ([Rom 4:5](#)). The harmony is in the order: works flow from faith, not toward it. Faith is the root; works are the fruit.

- **Faith and the Great Controversy:** Satan's accusation against Job ("Does Job fear God for nothing?" [Job 1:9](#)) is precisely an attack on the possibility of faith-without-reward. The Great Controversy turns on whether creatures will trust God *for who He is*, not for what He gives. Every act of faith vindicates God against the accuser.

- **Faith and Adventist identity:** [Rev 14:12](#) defines the remnant by both commandment-keeping and the faith of Jesus. Adventism's distinct contribution to Christian witness is not the Sabbath alone but the integration — law and gospel, obedience and trust, commandment and faith — held in undivided unity.

## 📝 Lesson Summary for Quick Reference

**Core assertion:** Faith is God's gift, given in measure to every person, by which we receive the relationship He is already offering and act on what cannot yet be seen. It is neither sentiment nor technique. It is trust in a faithful God, and its power is in its object, not its intensity.

**Key tensions the lesson navigates:**

1. **Faith vs. sight** — Jesus refuses signs to those who demand them but meets honest doubt with patient grace. The opposite of faith is not doubt but pretense.
2. **Faith as gift vs. faith as response** — God gives faith ([Rom 12:3](#); [Eph 2:8](#)), yet we are responsible to exercise it ([Heb 12:1–2](#)). The two are not in conflict; the gift is what makes the response possible.
3. **Faith vs. feeling** — Feelings rise and fall; faith persists across both. But faith is not unemotional — it is the trust that survives emotion, sometimes alongside it, sometimes against it.
4. **Our faith vs. the faith of Jesus** — [Rev 14:12](#) likely means both: Jesus' own faith is the model and source, *and* faith in Jesus is the substance of end-time saints' identity.

**The bottom line:** You cannot manufacture faith by effort. You can only ask for it, exercise what has been given, and fix your eyes on the One who is faithful. Faith's power is not in its intensity but in its Object. The Canaanite woman, the centurion, the father of the demonized boy, the saints of Hebrews 11 — they did not have superior faith. They had faith in a superior God, and they brought what they had, however small, to Him.
