## 🔑 Central Theme

This is the quarter's capstone — and its resolution. For thirteen weeks the theme has been *Growing in a Relationship With God*. Every lesson — humility, Bible study, prayer, faith, repentance, setbacks, witness — has been oriented toward a single end: being with Him. Lesson 13 names that end explicitly. The relationship begun in time will be completed in eternity. The restlessness of Ecclesiastes 3:11 ("He has set eternity in their hearts") is answered not by philosophy but by a Person: "we shall see Him as He is" (1 John 3:2).

The lesson holds two movements together. The first is *endurance* — Matthew 24's signs of the end, Psalm 80's plea for restoration, James 4's reminder that life is a vapor. The second is *anticipation* — Revelation 21's New Jerusalem, Isaiah 25's death swallowed up, the final "Come!" of Revelation 22. The Christian is asked to live with full awareness of both: this world is ending, and *that* world is coming. Together they produce the New Testament posture: *watchful expectation*.

## 📚 Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### 1 John 3:2 (Memory Text)
- **"We shall be like Him" (*homoioi autō*):** Not identical — *homoios* means similar, resembling. The transformation is real but does not erase personhood. We will not be absorbed into God; we will finally be what we were designed to be — bearers of His image fully restored (cf. Gen 1:26–27).
- **"For we shall see Him as He is":** The mechanism of transformation is *vision* — direct beholding of God. Paul: "We all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image" (2 Cor 3:18). The beatific vision is not passive; it *makes* us what we see.
- **"It has not yet been revealed what we shall be":** We know *that* we will be like Him; we cannot yet know *what* that fully means. Appropriate eschatological humility.

### Ecclesiastes 3:11 (Monday)
- **"He has set eternity (*ha-olam*) in their heart":** God has placed within human consciousness a longing that no temporal thing can satisfy — a built-in awareness that this is not all there is.
- **"Yet so that man cannot find out the work that God has done":** The eternity-hunger is real but cannot be self-satisfied. We ache for it; we cannot reach it unaided. This is why "Come!" (Rev 22:17) is not an offer but a rescue.

### 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17 (Monday — the Second Coming)
- **"Ever be with the Lord" (*pantote syn kyriō*):** The climax is not the location but the relationship: *with the Lord*, always. The destination of eschatology is Person, not place.
- **"Caught up" (*harpazō*):** Seized, taken by force — an active divine intervention. The word used for Paul being "caught up" to the third heaven (2 Cor 12:2). This is not a human achievement.

### Revelation 21:2, 9–11 (Tuesday — The Bride/New Jerusalem)
- **The double identity:** In Revelation 21, the New Jerusalem is simultaneously a *city* (architectural, measured) and a *bride* (personal, beloved). The people of God and their eternal home are one.
- **"Coming down out of heaven from God" (v.2):** The direction matters. Heaven does not take us up and away; God brings the new creation *down*. Eternity is embodied, physical, material existence on a restored earth — the Adventist emphasis.
- **"Having the glory of God" (v.11):** The presence hidden in the cloud at Sinai, veiled in the tabernacle, incarnated in Bethlehem — is now the ambient reality of the whole city.

### Matthew 25:1–13 (Tuesday — Ten Virgins)
- **The crisis:** All ten had lamps; all ten slept. The difference was *preparation* — whether the oil was there before the midnight cry. The moment of arrival is not the time to acquire what you don't have.
- **Oil as the Spirit:** What cannot be borrowed at the last moment is the *inner life of relationship* cultivated over time. You cannot improvise thirteen weeks of growing in relationship with God at the last hour.
- **"I know you not" (v.12):** The Greek *oida* — relational knowing, not intellectual acquaintance. The five foolish virgins are excluded not for wickedness but for *unfamiliarity*. The goal of the quarter: become so known by God that this verdict becomes impossible.

### Revelation 22:17, 20 (Thursday)
- **"Come!" (v.17):** The Spirit says Come. The Bride says Come. The hearer says Come. The invitation of Matthew 11:28 ("Come to me") reaches its cosmic form here — simultaneously divine initiative and human response.
- **"Come, Lord Jesus" (*Maranatha*, v.20):** The Greek *erchou kyrie Iēsou* translates the Aramaic *maranatha* — one of the earliest Christian prayers (1 Cor 16:22). The last prayer of Scripture is not a request for blessing; it is the cry for the Person Himself.
- **The last word of Scripture (v.21):** After everything — seals, trumpets, judgment — the Bible's final word is *grace*. This is where the whole story lands.

### Philippians 1:6 (Friday)
- **"He who began a good work in you will perfect it (*epiteleō*)":** *Epiteleō* — to bring to full completion. The work God began at conversion — and continued through every lesson of this quarter — He guarantees to complete.
- **The quarter's answer to every discouragement:** Felt inadequate in prayer? Failed in faith? Experienced setbacks? He will complete it. The relationship is God's project, not just yours — and He finishes what He starts.

## 💡 Deep Discussion Questions

### On Eternity and Human Longing
1. **Ecclesiastes 3:11 says God has set eternity in the human heart. C.S. Lewis called this "the inconsolable longing" (*Sehnsucht*) — a desire nothing earthly satisfies. How do you experience this longing? And how does the Christian gospel address it differently from how philosophy, art, or achievement attempt to?**

2. **1 John 3:2 says "it has not yet been revealed what we shall be." What are the limits of our current ability to imagine eternity — and what are the dangers of either under-imagining it (cloud-sitting) or over-imagining it (projecting current earthly preferences)? How do we hold appropriate expectation without presumption?**

3. **The Adventist emphasis on a physical, material new earth (Rev 21:2 — New Jerusalem coming *down*) stands against popular conceptions of heaven as disembodied bliss. What difference does embodied, material eternity make practically — for how we treat our bodies now, how we view creation, how we understand resurrection?**

### On Endurance and Readiness
4. **Matthew 24:6–11 lists wars, famines, earthquakes, and false prophets as signs of the end — "but the end is not yet." These have been present throughout history. How do we maintain appropriate end-times alertness without either alarmism (every headline is the final sign) or numbness (we've heard it before)?**

5. **James 4:13–14 — "What is your life? For ye are a vapor." How does the Christian hope of eternity transform life's transience from existential threat to spiritual invitation? Where is the line between healthy urgency and anxiety?**

6. **The ten virgins were all virgins, all had lamps, all waiting for the same bridegroom. The difference was invisible until midnight. What are the markers of genuine relational depth with God that cannot be borrowed, performed, or acquired at the last moment?**

7. **Psalm 80 is a communal lament — "Turn us again, O LORD." What does it mean to live in the *now* from the posture of Psalm 80 — with grief over the present and longing for restoration — rather than either denial or despair?**

### On the New Jerusalem and the Second Coming
8. **Revelation 21:22 — "I saw no temple in it." The entire OT sanctuary system pointed forward to the Presence it represented; in the New Jerusalem, mediation is no longer needed. What does "no temple" mean for Adventist sanctuary theology? What is completed, and what is fulfilled?**

9. **1 Thessalonians 4:17 says we will "ever be with the Lord" — the climax is relationship, not location. What does it mean to say eternity is about *Person* more than *place*? How should this reframe how we talk about heaven to people who fear it will be boring or alien?**

10. **Revelation 22:4 — "They shall see His face." Looking back over Q2 2026: how has growing in relationship with God this quarter changed (even slightly) what it means to *want* to see His face?**

### On "Come!" and Invitation
11. **Matthew 11:28–30 — "Come unto me, all ye that labor and are heavy laden." How is this the *same* "Come" as Revelation 22:17 — both extending the same offer across two millennia? What does it mean that the first word of Jesus' invitation and the last word of Scripture are the same?**

12. **Revelation 22:17 — the Spirit, the Bride, and the hearer all say "Come." How does this trinitarian/communal structure of invitation shape how we extend the gospel — not as individuals with a message, but as participants in a chorus already in progress?**

13. **"Come, Lord Jesus" (*Maranatha*, Rev 22:20) is the oldest known Christian prayer. It has been prayed for two thousand years. How do we pray it today with genuine longing rather than liturgical habit? What would it mean for this prayer to cost you something?**

### On the Quarter's Resolution
14. **Philippians 1:6 — "He who began a good work in you will perfect it." Looking back over thirteen weeks, what is the "good work" you are most aware God has been doing in you? And what does it mean to trust Him to complete it — without knowing what "complete" will look like?**

15. **The quarter theme was "Growing in a Relationship With God." After thirteen lessons — how has your understanding of what "relationship with God" actually means changed? Is it different from what you thought at Lesson 1?**

16. **Revelation 14:4 — "These are they that follow the Lamb wherever He goes." In eternity, the redeemed still *follow* — still in a posture of discipleship. Does heaven complete the journey, or begin a new one?**

### Harder/Edge Questions
17. **1 John 3:2 says we shall be like Him when we see Him. But 2 Corinthians 3:18 says we are already being transformed by beholding Him now. What is the relationship between present transformation through spiritual beholding and the eschatological transformation at the Parousia? Is there continuity — or is the final transformation categorically different?**

18. **Revelation 21:4 — "Death shall be no more; neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain." What is lost as well as gained in the elimination of all pain? Or is nothing lost — and if so, what happened to what suffering built in us?**

19. **Matthew 25:12 — "I know you not." In context, this is not a verdict on wickedness but on *unfamiliarity*. How does "I know you not" reframe the judgment as fundamentally relational? What does it mean to be *known by God* — and is that something you can be sure of?**

20. **The last word of the Bible (Rev 22:21) is *grace*. After everything — seals, trumpets, bowls, judgment — the final word is not victory, not justice, not holiness, but *grace*. What does it tell us about the character of the God this whole quarter has been about?**

## ✍️ Ellen White Integration Points

Use sparingly — as amplification of scriptural truth, not replacement for it.

**1. On the inadequacy of imagination:**
> *"Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither have entered into the heart of man the things which God hath prepared for them that love Him. Only through His word can we gain some knowledge of these things."*
> — *The Great Controversy*, p. 675

*Discussion use:* Grounds 1 John 3:2's "it has not yet been revealed." Our best imaginings of eternity are underestimates.

**2. On eternity as ongoing growth:**
> *"The work of the Holy Spirit is immeasurably vast. Throughout ceaseless ages we shall never reach the end of it. We shall always be finding more and more of its glories."*
> — *Testimonies*, vol. 8, p. 288

*Discussion use:* Grounds question #16 — eternity is not static arrival but ongoing discovery. The relationship that grew through thirteen weeks continues forever.

**3. On God's completing work:**
> *"Those who accept Christ as a personal Saviour are not left as orphans, to bear the trials of life as best they can. He receives the heritage of woes that has fallen to the human race, that He might make it possible for all who believe to become the children of God."*
> — *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 84

*Discussion use:* Grounds Philippians 1:6. The "good work" God began is an adoption — He took on the heritage of our woes so we could take on the heritage of His glory.

## 🌐 Practical Application for Modern Context

**Individual:**
- **The quarter's inventory:** Before Sabbath, recall the arc of Q2 2026 (Lessons 1–13). Where did God most clearly meet you? What one thing do you want to carry into Q3?
- **Psalm 80 as a personal prayer:** Read through Psalm 80 and personalize its pleas. Let it become your honest cry for restoration. Then hold it alongside 1 John 3:2: the same God who hears this lament has promised what you will become.
- **"Come, Lord Jesus" as a daily prayer:** For the remaining weeks, close each morning prayer with this sentence — not as liturgy but as genuine expression. What would you need to let go of to mean it fully?

**In Sabbath School:**
- Close the quarter with this question: *Looking back at Lesson 1 ("Reality Check"), what is one thing about your relationship with God that is different today?* Not more information — a difference in the *relationship itself*. Share one sentence each.
- Discuss Revelation 22:4 — "They shall see His face." What does it mean to you personally that the goal of all redemption history is a moment of direct, unmediated seeing?

**Intellectually:**
- The New Jerusalem (Rev 21) is measured in multiples of 12 — the number of God's people. What does it mean that the eternal city is *measured in the image of the church*? What does this say about what God most values?

## 🧵 Theological Threads to Follow

- **The *Imago Dei* restored:** Genesis 1:26–27 (image of God) → Genesis 3 (image marred) → 2 Corinthians 3:18 (image being restored) → 1 John 3:2 (image fully restored). This is the arc of redemption that the entire quarter traces.
- **The New Earth (not just new heaven):** Revelation 21:1 — "a new earth." Adventism insists on the physicality of eternity: resurrection bodies, material renewed creation, the New Jerusalem touching down. This has profound implications for ecology, embodiment, and creation theology.
- **The Lamb at the center:** From John 1:29 ("Behold, the Lamb") to Revelation 22:3 ("the throne of God and of the Lamb"), the Lamb is the central figure of both Testaments' redemptive narrative. The Lamb's presence is what makes the New Jerusalem what it is.
- **Maranatha and the Church's longing:** The prayer "Come, Lord Jesus" has been the church's deepest expression for two millennia. Adventism holds it with particular intensity — it is the eschatological identity of a people who have staked everything on the promise that He is coming.

## 📝 Lesson Summary for Quick Reference

**Core assertion:** The relationship with God cultivated across thirteen weeks reaches its ultimate destination: face-to-face, "we shall see Him as He is." The New Jerusalem is not a destination we travel to — it is the completion of a relationship that has been growing all along.

**Key tensions the lesson navigates:**
1. Present endurance (Matt 24 signs) ↔ Future anticipation (Rev 21 vision)
2. "It has not yet been revealed" (humility) ↔ "We shall be like Him" (confident hope)
3. The city as architectural reality ↔ The city as relational community (the Bride)
4. Human response ("Come!") ↔ Divine initiative (He who began will complete it)

**The bottom line:** He who began a good work in you will perfect it. The last word is grace. And the last prayer is: Come.
