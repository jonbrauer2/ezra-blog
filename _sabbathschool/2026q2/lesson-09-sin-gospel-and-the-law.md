## 🔑 Central Theme

This lesson sits at the structural center of the quarter. Every preceding lesson has built toward this moment: relationship requires that we honestly face what separates us from God (sin), receive what God has done to bridge that separation (the gospel), and walk in the path He has marked out for life with Him (the law). Sever any one of these three from the others and the architecture collapses — sin without gospel becomes despair, gospel without law becomes presumption, law without gospel becomes legalism.

The lesson's controlling case study is the tragedy of Samson ([Judges 14, 16](#)) — a Spirit-gifted man whose talents flourished while his character disintegrated. Samson is the inverse of Joseph: same temptations, opposite outcomes. He is the warning we need precisely because *gifting is not sanctification*. The Spirit's power can rest on a man whose heart is far from God, but only for a season, and only at terrible cost.

Four tensions structure the week:

- **Sin as act vs. sin as condition** — Is sin what we *do* (transgression of law, [1 John 3:4](#)) or what we *are* (a fallen nature, [Ps 51:5](#); [Jer 17:9](#))? The biblical answer is both — and the distinction matters pastorally.
- **The law as accusation vs. the law as gift** — Paul: "By the law is the knowledge of sin" ([Rom 3:20](#)). The psalmist: "By them You have given me life" ([Ps 119:93](#)). The same law functions differently depending on whether we stand under it as judge or live within it as covenant.
- **Justified by faith vs. faith expressed in obedience** — "A man is justified by faith apart from the deeds of the law" ([Rom 3:28](#)). "If you love Me, keep My commandments" ([John 14:15](#)). The harmony is in the order, not the cancellation.
- **Hearing vs. doing** — Jesus ends the Sermon on the Mount with the two builders ([Matt 7:24–29](#)). The difference is not in knowledge but in obedience. To *know* without *doing* is to build on sand.

The week's progression is internal-to-external: distractions that capture the heart (Sunday), strongholds in the mind (Monday), the law's diagnosis (Tuesday), the gospel's remedy (Wednesday), and the integrated life of knowing-and-doing (Thursday). The aim is not legal correctness but covenantal life with God.

## 📚 Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### [Psalm 119:93–94](#) (Memory Text)

- **Context:** The longest chapter in the Bible — 176 verses, an acrostic on the Hebrew alphabet, every verse extolling God's word (described under eight near-synonyms: *torah*, *mitzvah*, *piqqudim*, *huqqim*, *mishpatim*, *edut*, *davar*, *imrah*).
- **"Your precepts":** The Hebrew word means "to oversee, attend to" — God's instructions are not arbitrary impositions but specific care for the details of our lives. A shepherd's *attention*, not a tyrant's *demand*.
- **"By them You have given me life":** The verb means to live, revive, preserve alive. Not "they showed me the path of life" but "by them You revived me." The law is described as life-giving, contradicting any reading that makes it inherently death-dealing.
- **"I am Yours, save me":** The order is theological. Belonging precedes salvation. The psalmist does not say, "Save me so that I might be Yours," but "I am Yours; therefore, save me." Identity grounds the appeal.

### [Judges 14, 16](#) (Sunday — Samson)

- **Context:** The book of Judges is structured as a *downward spiral* — each judge worse than the last, until "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" ([Judg 21:25](#)). Samson is the *last* judge before the monarchy, the culmination of moral collapse even within God's leadership.
- **The Nazirite vow ([Num 6](#); [Judg 13:5](#)):** Samson was set apart from the womb — no wine, no contact with the dead, uncut hair. The vow signified consecration. By the end, Samson has broken all three: he handles the lion's carcass (death contact), participates in a wine-feast (the *mishteh*, [Judg 14:10](#)), and surrenders his hair to Delilah.
- **"She is right in my eyes" ([Judg 14:3, 7](#)):** A Hebrew idiom for visual desire driving choice — "right in my eyes" means "I want it." Samson's pattern of seeing-then-taking ([Judg 14:1, 16:1](#)) prefigures the lustful gaze Jesus addresses in [Matt 5:28](#).
- **"His father and mother did not know that it was of the LORD" ([Judg 14:4](#)):** This is one of the most theologically vexing verses in Scripture. God *uses* Samson's compromised desires for redemptive purpose — without endorsing them. Divine sovereignty does not sanitize human choice; it weaves both into history.
- **The descent narrative — Timnah, Gaza, Sorek:** Each location is *lower* than the last (geographically and morally). Samson goes *down* to Timnah (14:1), *down* to Gaza (16:1), into the valley of Sorek (16:4). The geography is moral commentary.
- **"He told her all his heart" ([Judg 16:17](#)):** Three times Samson lied to Delilah; the fourth, he told the truth. Sin's gradualism — each lie inched closer to the secret. By the time he gave it up, the betrayal felt like just another concession.
- **"He did not know that the LORD had departed from him" ([Judg 16:20](#)):** The most chilling sentence in the chapter. Samson rose to fight as before, *not knowing* the Spirit was gone. Gifting outlasted intimacy long enough that he could not tell when intimacy had ended.

### [1 John 3:4](#) (Tuesday — Sin Defined)

- **"Sin is lawlessness":** The word *anomia* (lawlessness) is more than "breaking rules" — it is a posture of rebellion against the law itself. Sin is not merely missing the mark; it is rejecting the standard.
- **The definitional power of the verse:** When asked "What is sin?", Christian theology has rightly used this verse as the answer. But the equation runs both ways: sin = lawlessness, *and* lawlessness = sin. Where the law is annulled (in practice or in doctrine), sin is redefined out of existence.
- **Theological implication:** A church that softens the law inevitably softens its diagnosis of sin, which inevitably weakens its preaching of the gospel. The cross only makes sense against the moral weight of what it remedies.

### [Romans 3:20, 28](#) (Tuesday/Wednesday — Law and Justification)

- **"By the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified in His sight, for by the law is the knowledge of sin" (v. 20):** Paul makes two assertions, often confused. (1) The law does not *justify* — it cannot produce righteousness. (2) The law *diagnoses* — it makes sin visible. To remove the law is to remove the diagnosis, which is to leave the patient untreated.
- **"A man is justified by faith apart from the deeds of the law" (v. 28):** Accounting language — a verdict is rendered on the basis of faith, not performance. "Apart from the works of the law" does not mean "without obedience" but "without obedience as the *ground* of acceptance."
- **Luther's "alone":** Luther famously added *allein* ("alone") to his German translation of v. 28 — "justified by faith *alone* apart from the deeds of the law." The Greek does not contain the word, but Luther argued the sense requires it. Whether or not one accepts the translation, the theological point is biblical: justification rests on faith, not on works added to faith.

### [Matthew 5:17–18](#) (Wednesday — Christ and the Law)

- **"Do not think that I came to destroy the Law or the Prophets":** The *nomos kai prophētai* is the Jewish term for the whole Hebrew Bible. Jesus' negation is sweeping — He did not come to dismantle any of it.
- **"I did not come to destroy but to fulfill":** "Fulfill" means "to fill up, to bring to completion." Christ does not abolish the law; He brings it to its intended fullness. He fulfills the *predictive* law (prophecy, type, ceremony) by becoming what it pointed to; He fulfills the *moral* law by living it perfectly and writing it on the hearts of His people ([Jer 31:33](#); [Heb 8:10](#)).
- **"Till heaven and earth pass away, one jot or one tittle will by no means pass from the law":** *Iota* (the smallest Greek letter) and *keraia* (the tiny pen-stroke that distinguishes Hebrew letters). The smallest mark of the law endures until the cosmos itself ends. Anyone claiming that the law has been "abolished" must reckon with this sentence.
- **The Adventist hermeneutical pattern:** Distinguish (1) the *moral* law (Decalogue) — eternal, perpetual; (2) the *ceremonial* law (sacrifices, festivals) — fulfilled in Christ and ended at the cross ([Col 2:14–17](#)); (3) the *civil* law (Israel's theocratic statutes) — applicable to that specific polity. All three are *fulfilled* in Christ in distinct ways.

### [Matthew 7:24–29](#) (Sabbath/Thursday — The Two Builders)

- **Context:** The conclusion of the Sermon on the Mount. After three chapters of teaching on the kingdom — heart-level righteousness, prayer, money, judging others — Jesus ends with two builders.
- **"Whoever hears these sayings of Mine, and does them" (v. 24):** The participial structure links *hearing* and *doing* inseparably. The wise builder is not described as the one who *understood* the sermon, the one who *agreed* with it, or the one who *taught* it — but the one who *did* it.
- **"The rains descended, the floods came, the winds blew" (v. 25):** The storm is identical for both houses. Discipleship does not exempt from storms; it determines what the storm reveals.
- **"The people were astonished at His teaching, for He taught them as one having authority" (v. 28–29):** The scribes derived their teaching from rabbinic precedent — "Rabbi so-and-so said..." Jesus spoke as the source itself. The hearers recognized the difference immediately.

### [Galatians 2:16, 20](#); [Galatians 3:13](#) (Wednesday)

- **"A man is not justified by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ" ([Gal 2:16](#)):** The "faith of/in Jesus Christ" phrase recurs — the same one debated in [Rev 14:12](#). Whether read as "faith *in* Jesus" or "the faithfulness *of* Jesus," the conclusion is identical: justification is by faith, not by performance.
- **"I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me" ([Gal 2:20](#)):** The verb tense points to a completed event whose effects continue — "I am in a state of having-been-crucified." Christian identity is anchored in that finished reality.
- **"Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law" ([Gal 3:13](#)):** Redemption — bought out of bondage. The freedom is not from the law itself but from its *curse* — the death sentence it pronounces on the lawbreaker. The law remains; the curse is borne by Christ.

### [Hosea 4:1, 6](#) (Thursday — Knowledge of God)

- **"There is no truth or mercy or knowledge of God in the land":** In Hebrew, "to know" (*yada*) is relational, not merely cognitive — it is the same word used for the deepest human intimacy. To "know" God is to be in covenantal communion with Him, not merely to know facts about Him.
- **"My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge" (v. 6):** Not lack of information — Israel had Torah, priests, prophets. They had data without relationship, content without communion. *Hosea*'s indictment is hauntingly modern: a people who own Bibles, attend services, and recite creeds while *not knowing* God in the biblical sense.

## 💡 Deep Discussion Questions

### On Samson and the Anatomy of Distraction

1. **Samson was Spirit-gifted from the womb ([Judg 13:5](#)) — and yet he ended in tragedy. What does Samson's story tell us about the relationship between spiritual *gifting* and spiritual *character*?** Have you encountered (in yourself or others) the gap between gifting and discipleship? How do you guard against assuming that visible blessing equals invisible health?

2. **Three times Samson lied to Delilah; the fourth, he told the truth. The pattern is a slow erosion, not a single fall. Where in your own life have you noticed gradualism — small concessions, each justifying the next — that you didn't recognize until you were far from where you intended?** What would have interrupted the pattern earlier?

3. **The most haunting verse in the Samson narrative is [Judg 16:20](#): "He did not know that the Lord had departed from him." Is it possible to lose intimacy with God without realizing you've lost it?** What would be the signs that intimacy is leaving, even while the outward forms remain? How do you check for that?

4. **The Samson story is told *as commentary on Israel*, not just one man — a Spirit-empowered nation whose obvious gifting masked moral collapse. Read corporately, what is the warning to communities that have been blessed, gifted, and visible?** Where might that warning land for the people of God now?

5. **Samson's parents objected to his Philistine bride: "Is there no woman among the daughters of your brethren, or among all my people?" ([Judg 14:3](#)). Their objection was right, but the text says God was working through Samson's choice anyway. How do you read God's sovereignty *through* sin without baptizing the sin?** What's the difference between God using a fallen choice and God endorsing it?

### On Sin, Lawlessness, and the Mirror of the Law

6. **[1 John 3:4](#) defines sin as *anomia* — lawlessness. If sin is defined *by the law*, what happens when the law is removed (in doctrine or in practice)?** Does sin still exist, or does it dissolve into "harm" or "preference violation"? How is the church's diagnosis of sin connected to its preaching of the law?

7. **Paul: "By the law is the knowledge of sin" ([Rom 3:20](#)). David: "By Your precepts You have given me life" ([Ps 119:93](#)). Same law, different functions. When has the law functioned for you as *diagnosis*, and when as *delight*?** What changes the experience of the law from burden to gift?

8. **Jesus' Sermon on the Mount intensifies, rather than relaxes, the law — anger is murder, lust is adultery, casual oaths are unfaithfulness ([Matt 5:21–48](#)). Is Jesus making the law impossible, or is He revealing what the law has always demanded?** What does this say about how comfortable we should be with our current law-keeping?

9. **Tuesday's lesson distinguishes sin as *act* (transgression) from sin as *condition* ([Ps 51:5](#); [Jer 17:9](#)). Pastorally, when is each emphasis appropriate, and when does each become distorted?** If we only preach sin-as-act, what do we miss? If we only preach sin-as-condition, what do we miss?

### On Law and Gospel

10. **"A man is justified by faith apart from the deeds of the law" ([Rom 3:28](#)) and "If you love Me, keep My commandments" ([John 14:15](#)). How do you hold both without collapsing one into the other?** What is the *order* between them, and why does the order matter for the gospel?

11. **[Matthew 5:17](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Matt5.17) — "Do not think that I came to destroy the Law... but to fulfill." What does *fulfill* mean? Does it mean "complete and set aside," "perfectly obey," "bring to full meaning," or something else?** How do the three uses of "fulfill" (predictive, exemplary, transformative) function together?

12. **Luther added *alone* to [Rom 3:28](#) — "justified by faith *alone*." Was he adding to Scripture or surfacing its sense?** Where does Adventist preaching land on the "alone" — is faith alone the *sole instrument* of justification, even while works are the *necessary fruit*? What does that look like in your local context?

13. **[Galatians 3:13](#) — "Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law." The law's curse is borne by Christ. But Paul does not say He redeemed us from the law itself. What is the practical difference between freedom *from the curse* of the law and freedom *from the law*?** How does this shape your daily relationship with God's commands?

14. **The lesson contrasts *legalism* (law without grace) and *cheap grace* (grace without law). Which error is your own community more prone to?** Which is the more common temptation in your own heart? What guards against drifting toward either?

### On Knowing and Doing

15. **[Matthew 7:24–27](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Matt7.24) — the two builders. Both *heard* Jesus' words. The difference was that one *did* them. What does it cost to be a hearer-only?** What would it look like in your life this week to move one specific teaching from "heard" to "done"?

16. **[Hosea 4:6](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Hos4.6) — "My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge." But Israel had the Torah. What kind of "knowledge" was missing — and how is that missing for the church today?** Is biblical literacy enough, or is knowledge of God something deeper than information?

17. **[Matthew 7:21](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Matt7.21) — "Not everyone who says to Me, 'Lord, Lord,' shall enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of My Father." Some who called Jesus "Lord" were turned away. What separates confession from obedience?** Where is the boundary between honest struggle (the father of [Mark 9:24](#)) and confessing-without-doing?

18. **The lesson connects "strongholds" ([2 Cor 10:4–5](#) implicit) with internal thoughts — judgment, contempt, lust, anger. Modern psychology calls these "cognitive distortions." Is the biblical category of stronghold the same thing, or something more?** What is the relationship between thinking patterns and spiritual bondage? What is the path out?

19. **[Revelation 3:14–22](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev3.14) — Laodicea is neither hot nor cold but lukewarm. Lukewarmness is *not* immorality; it is *indifference*. Why does Jesus describe lukewarmness as more nauseating than open rebellion?** What does lukewarm faith look like in the context of this lesson on sin, gospel, and law?

20. **The week ends with the call to *integrate* — to know and to do, to love and to keep, to be saved by grace and to walk by law. If you had to name one place where the integration is currently weakest in your own walk, what would it be?** What single step, this week, would strengthen the integration?

## ✍️ Ellen White Integration Points

*Used sparingly, as amplification of Scripture rather than substitute.*

### 1. On the law as mirror

> *"The law of God is the mirror in which the sinner sees his moral defects. The law brings him face to face with his sins; but the gospel offers him a remedy."*
> — *Selected Messages*, vol. 1, p. 199 (paraphrase of common formulation)

**Discussion:** A mirror does not clean the face; it shows the face. The law does not save; it diagnoses. Yet without the diagnosis, we will not seek the cure. Where in your spiritual life do you avoid looking in the mirror? How do you keep the mirror useful — for diagnosis — without making it the source of cleansing?

### 2. On Samson and squandered gifting

> *"The Lord chose Samson as one through whom He could work to deliver Israel; but the wayward youth, accustomed to indulge his unholy desires, deprived himself of the protecting hand of God."*
> — *Patriarchs and Prophets*, p. 562

**Discussion:** Note the agency: Samson *deprived himself*. God did not withdraw arbitrarily; Samson, by his choices, severed the connection. Is there a way in which we, by accumulated choices, deprive ourselves of God's protection without recognizing it? How does Samson's tragedy differ from cases where God seems silent through no fault of the sufferer? How do you distinguish?

### 3. On the integration of law and gospel

> *"As the sinner looks into the great moral looking-glass, he sees his defects of character. He sees himself just as he is, spotted, defiled, and condemned. But he knows that the law cannot in any way remove the guilt or pardon the transgressor. He must go farther than this. The law is but the schoolmaster to bring him to Christ. He must look to his sin-bearing Saviour."*
> — *Christ's Object Lessons*, p. 158

**Discussion:** Ellen White names the trajectory: law → schoolmaster → Christ. The law sends us to the Savior; the Savior sends us back to walk in the law's path, no longer under its curse. Where in your community is this trajectory preached well? Where does it falter — does the message stop at the law without reaching Christ, or skip the law altogether?

## 🌐 Practical Application for Modern Context

**Individual:**

- **The Samson audit:** Spend twenty minutes this week reading [Judges 13–16](#) slowly. As you read, ask one question of yourself: *Where is my hair growing back, or being cut?* Samson's hair was the *visible sign* of an invisible vow. What is your visible sign — a discipline, a relationship, a habit of prayer, an integrity — that signals the consecration underneath? Is it intact, or is someone (perhaps yourself) busy cutting it?

- **The mirror practice:** Take a single commandment from [Exodus 20](#) this week. Don't pick the one that feels safest; pick the one that has gone unexamined. Hold it up like a mirror for the week. What does the mirror show? What do you bring to the Savior because of what you see?

- **Hearing-to-doing inventory:** Make a list of three things you *know* God is asking of you that you have not yet *done*. Be specific. Don't add new things; just write what is already on the table. Pick the smallest one and act on it within 72 hours. The two builders text is brutal precisely because the difference is so concrete.

**In Sabbath School:**

- **Samson as case study:** Spend the bulk of the class on [Judges 14, 16](#). Read the descent geography (Timnah → Gaza → Sorek). Trace the broken Nazirite vow (death contact, wine, hair). Discuss what spiritual erosion looks like *in this congregation* — not abstract, but the specific shapes it takes locally. The aim is not voyeurism into Samson's sin but vigilance about our own gradualism.

- **Law and gospel role-play:** Assign one half of the class the position that "we are saved by grace alone, apart from any obedience"; the other half, "obedience is the necessary expression of saving faith." Have each side defend their position from Scripture. Then ask: what would be lost if either side were not represented? The aim is to feel the tension biblically, not to resolve it cheaply.

- **The Laodicean question:** Spend ten minutes on [Rev 3:14–22](#). Ask each member to honestly identify *one area* where lukewarmness, rather than open sin or living devotion, characterizes their walk. (Some may not want to share aloud — that's fine; the honesty itself does the work.)

**Intellectually:**

- **The "faith of Christ" question:** Continue last week's study of "the faith of Jesus" by reading [Gal 2:16, 20](#); [Rom 3:22](#); [Phil 3:9](#). Compare how older translations (KJV: "the faith *of* Christ") render it versus newer ones (NIV: "faith *in* Christ"). What does each rendering preserve, and what does it lose?

- **The "works of the law" debate:** New Testament scholars (E. P. Sanders, James Dunn, N. T. Wright) have argued that "works of the law" in Paul refers narrowly to *boundary markers* of Jewish identity (circumcision, Sabbath, food laws), not law-keeping in general. Traditional Reformed and Adventist readings hold that it refers to law-keeping as a means of justification more broadly. What evidence weighs each way? Why does the answer matter for the gospel?

- **The Sabbath in the moral law:** Adventist hermeneutics places the Sabbath in the moral (perpetual) law, not the ceremonial (fulfilled). For deep study, work through the arguments — the Sabbath's origin at creation ([Gen 2:2–3](#), pre-Sinai), its placement in the Decalogue, Jesus' Sabbath teaching ([Mark 2:27–28](#)), the eschatological Sabbath ([Heb 4:9](#)), and [Rev 14:12](#)'s "commandments of God" in end-time context. What is the cumulative weight of evidence?

## 🧵 Theological Threads to Follow

- **Sin and original sin:** Adventism has historically held a nuanced view — humans inherit a *fallen nature* (sinful tendencies) but are not held *guilty* for Adam's sin until they ratify it with their own choices. This differs from classical Reformed federalism (imputed guilt) and from Pelagian or Wesleyan-Arminian alternatives. Study how this shapes how we preach the gospel to children, to seekers, to the dying.

- **Law and covenant:** The Decalogue is given *to a delivered people* ([Exod 20:2](#): "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt"). Salvation precedes the law. The law is the *shape* of the covenant relationship, not its *entry condition*. Trace this pattern throughout Scripture — law comes *after* deliverance, not before.

- **Law and the Spirit:** [Jer 31:33](#); [Ezek 36:26–27](#); [Heb 8:10](#) — the new covenant is law-written-on-the-heart by the Spirit. The law is not abolished but internalized. Sanctification is the Spirit's work of conforming the inner person to the law's shape, so that obedience flows from love rather than fear.

- **Sin and atonement:** If sin is *lawlessness* (rebellion against God's character), then atonement must address both the *guilt* (forensic — penal substitution) and the *power* (transformative — sanctifying grace). The Adventist sanctuary doctrine integrates both: Christ's death deals with guilt; Christ's high priestly ministry deals with the ongoing reality of remaining sin in the believer.

- **The third use of the law:** Reformed theology speaks of three uses — civil restraint, theological mirror, and *guide for the redeemed*. Adventism's view of the law fits most naturally with the third use — the law as the path of life for those already saved by grace. The psalmist's "Your precepts have given me life" ([Ps 119:93](#)) presupposes this use.

## 📝 Lesson Summary for Quick Reference

**Core assertion:** Relationship with God requires honest reckoning with what separates us from Him (sin), grateful receiving of what He has done to bridge the gap (the gospel), and willing walking in the path He has marked out for life with Him (the law). The three are inseparable. To preach any without the others is to distort all.

**Key tensions the lesson navigates:**

1. **Sin as act vs. sin as condition** — Both true ([1 John 3:4](#); [Ps 51:5](#)). Pastorally, the question is which the person in front of you most needs to hear in this moment.
2. **Law as accusation vs. law as gift** — Same law, different functions depending on where the hearer stands. The law that *condemns* the unredeemed is the law that *delights* the redeemed.
3. **Justification by faith vs. faith expressed in obedience** — The order is decisive. Faith justifies; obedience demonstrates the justification it cannot produce.
4. **Hearing vs. doing** — The Sermon on the Mount ends with this distinction because everything before depends on it. Faith that does not move into action is not faith.

**The bottom line:** Samson is the warning; the two builders are the choice. You can be Spirit-gifted, theologically literate, communally respected — and still be Samson if the inner consecration has been cut without your noticing. The lesson's call is integration: take the law seriously as God's standard, take sin seriously as the gap, take the gospel seriously as the bridge, and take obedience seriously as the walk. Not one without the others. Not one *over* the others. All four, held together, become a life.
