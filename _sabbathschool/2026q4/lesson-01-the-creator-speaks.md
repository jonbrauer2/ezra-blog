## 🔑 Central Theme

This is the opening lesson of a quarter on the gift of prophecy, and it begins where every doctrine of revelation must: not with the prophets, but with the *Creator who speaks*. The lesson asks a deceptively simple question — *why* does God communicate at all, and what is He communicating *toward*?

The answer is relational, not merely informational. God's first act after sin entered Eden was not a sentence — it was a *question*: "Where are you?" ([Gen. 3:9](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.9)). That question is the seed of the entire prophetic enterprise. God did not stop talking when humanity stopped listening; He changed the medium. He kept seeking.

The lesson navigates four threads:

- **Communication as the substance of relationship.** Eden was not paradise because of its trees but because God walked there "in the cool of the day" and spoke with His children. When sin breaks communion, it breaks *conversation* first.
- **The asymmetry of hiding and seeking.** Adam hides; God calls. Adam blames; God promises. From [Genesis 3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3) forward, the initiative is always God's. The prophets are not human innovations to reach upward — they are God's innovation to reach down.
- **Multiple voices, one Voice.** [Heb. 1:1–2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Heb1.1) catalogues the methods (creation, prophets, Scripture, Christ, Spirit) but insists on the integration. God has not used many *voices*; He has used many *means* to speak with one Voice — Christ.
- **The first gospel and the long road.** [Gen. 3:15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.15) ("the protoevangelium") is the moment prophecy becomes redemptive. From that point on, every prophet's word, every Scripture's line, every visible miracle is a footnote to one announcement: a Redeemer is coming who will undo what Eden lost.

The aim of this opening lesson is to set the theological foundation: prophecy is *God seeking us*, not us reaching God. Everything in the next twelve weeks — call narratives, inspiration, testing the prophets, end-time revelation — presupposes this seeking love.

## 📚 Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### [Hebrews 1:1–2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Heb1.1) (Memory Text — the quarter's thesis verse)

- **Context:** Hebrews opens not with greetings but with a thunder-clap of Christology. The author addresses Jewish believers tempted to retreat from Christ back to Mosaic mediation, and immediately reframes the prior covenant: it was God speaking, but partially.
- **"In various times and in various ways" (*polymerōs kai polytropōs*):** Two adverbs piled at the front of the sentence — literally "many-parted and many-fashioned." God spoke through visions, dreams, theophanies, audible voices, symbolic acts, oracles, written instruction. The variety itself is part of the message: God adapts the *form* of His speaking to the receiver, but never compromises the *content*.
- **"In these last days":** The author writes during the messianic age — what the prophets called "the latter days." Christ's coming inaugurates the eschatological era, not merely a religious one.
- **"Spoken to us by His Son":** The Greek is striking — *en huiō*, "in a Son," without a definite article. The structure compares "by the prophets" (one mode) with "in a Son" (a categorically different mode). The prophets *received* the word; the Son *is* the Word.
- **"Heir of all things... through whom He made the worlds":** Cosmological bookends. Christ is both the agent of creation (looking back) and the heir of consummation (looking forward). The Creator who spoke at the dawn of time is the same Christ who now speaks in the latter days.

### [Genesis 1:27–28](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen1.27) (Sunday — Image of God)

- **"In the image of God He created him":** The phrase *tselem ʾelōhîm* ("image of God") is repeated three times in two verses — the Hebrew narrator wants the point pressed. To bear God's image is, among other things, to be addressable — able to receive divine speech.
- **"Male and female He created them":** The image is borne by humanity as a relational whole. Communication is built into the design — first horizontally (with one another), and the horizontal images the vertical (with God).
- **"God blessed them, and God said to them":** Note the order. Blessing precedes commandment. God's first words to humans are not "do" but a gift, then a calling.

### [Genesis 3:1–8](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.1) (Monday — The Architecture of Hiding)

- **The serpent's first move is verbal:** "Has God indeed said...?" (v. 1). Before sin enters as an act, it enters as a *question about what God said*. The first attack is on revelation itself.
- **The triple lure of v. 6:** "Good for food, pleasant to the eyes, desirable to make one wise." John echoes this triplet exactly in [1 John 2:16](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn2.16) — "lust of the flesh, lust of the eyes, pride of life." The Fall's grammar repeats itself in every generation.
- **"The eyes of both of them were opened":** A bitter inversion. They expected revelation; they received shame. The first thing they "see" with their new "wisdom" is their own nakedness.
- **"They hid themselves":** Sin's first sociological effect is not violence but *concealment*. Communication breaks before community does.

### [Genesis 3:9](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.9) (Tuesday — "Where are you?")

- **"Then the LORD God called (*qārāʾ*) to Adam":** The verb *qārāʾ* is the standard Hebrew verb for prophetic summons — God *calls* Moses, Samuel, Isaiah, the disciples. The first prophetic call in Scripture is God's call *back to Adam*. The entire prophetic enterprise begins here.
- **"Where are you?":** Not a request for information — God knows. The question is a *pastoral* device. It invites the hidden one to come out, to name his location, to face his condition. Throughout Scripture, God's questions are diagnostic, not interrogative.
- **The literary parallel to [Luke 15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Luke15):** The father in the prodigal parable runs to meet the son who is still "a great way off." Same God, same posture. God's seeking precedes our turning.

### [Genesis 3:14–24](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.14) (Wednesday — Judgment and Promise)

- **The order of speaking:** God speaks first to the serpent, then to the woman, then to the man — *reverse* of the order of disobedience. The grammar of judgment retraces the steps of fall in reverse, undoing what sin established.
- **[Gen. 3:15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.15) — the protoevangelium:** "He shall bruise (*shûp*) your head, and you shall bruise his heel." The same verb is used for both bruisings, but the locations differ — head wound (mortal) vs. heel wound (painful but not fatal). Within the judgment on the serpent is the promise of Christ.
- **"The LORD God made tunics of skin, and clothed them" (v. 21):** The first death recorded in Scripture is an animal sacrificed to clothe sinners. Eden ends with a substitutionary covering — the entire sacrificial system, and the cross, in seed form.
- **"He drove out the man":** The verb is forceful (*gārash*), the same word used for Hagar's expulsion and for casting out demons. Yet the same God who drives out also clothes. Mercy and judgment are not opposites here; they are integrated.

### [Luke 15:11–32](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Luke15.11) (Tuesday — The Father Who Runs)

- **"When he was still a great way off, his father saw him":** The waiting father has been *watching* — the verb implies long-standing expectation. The father's seeing precedes the son's arriving.
- **"He ran":** In Middle Eastern culture, an elderly patriarch did not run. Running required pulling up one's robe, exposing the legs — undignified, even shameful. The father takes the son's shame *upon himself* to spare the son the village's contempt.
- **The unanswered question:** The parable ends with the elder brother still outside, the father still pleading. Jesus tells this parable to Pharisees who, like the elder brother, resent the Father's seeking love. The question is left open: will *you* come in?

### [Romans 7:15–25](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rom7.15) (Tuesday — The Universal Witness to Brokenness)

- **"What I am doing, I do not understand":** Paul's confession is universal, not autobiographical alone. The split between desire and deed names what every honest person knows.
- **"O wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death?":** The cry is not despair — it is *positioned* to call forth the answer. Paul does not stop at the diagnosis; he names the Deliverer.
- **Connection to Eden:** Paul's split self is the long shadow of [Genesis 3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3). The brokenness of the will is the most enduring legacy of the Fall, and only the Last Adam can undo it.

### [John 14:7–9](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jn14.7) (Thursday — The Final Speaking)

- **"He who has seen Me has seen the Father":** Jesus' claim is not that He resembles the Father, nor that He represents Him — He says *seeing* Him *is* seeing the Father. The fullness of revelation is not propositional but personal.
- **The trajectory of [Hebrews 1](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Heb1):** Many ways → one Way. The Son is not one prophet among many; He is the *content* the prophets were always pointing toward.

## 💡 Deep Discussion Questions

### On Communication and the Image of God

1. **The lesson opens by noting that even with modern technology, "how much better to meet in person." Why do we still long for face-to-face presence — and what does that longing reveal about how we were made?** When in your life have you most clearly felt the difference between hearing about someone and *being with* them? How does that experience shape your understanding of what Eden was?

2. **[Genesis 1:27](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen1.27) says humans were made "in the image of God" — singular among creation. Of all the things "image-bearing" might mean, the lesson highlights *addressability* — being able to receive divine speech. What other aspects of bearing God's image have shaped your understanding of yourself? Where does the "image" feel most marred in your daily life — and where does it still feel intact?**

3. **Eden's defining feature was not its garden but its *communion* — God walking with His children "in the cool of the day." If communion was the substance of paradise, what does that suggest about what restoration looks like? Are you primarily hoping for restored circumstances, or restored conversation?**

### On Hiding and Being Found

4. **[Genesis 3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3) describes sin's first sociological effect as concealment, not violence. Why does shame produce hiding before it produces anything else? Where in your own life have you noticed that the first response to sin was the impulse to *cover* — from God, from family, from yourself?**

5. **God's first words after the Fall are not condemnation but a question: "Where are you?" If God's pastoral instinct is to invite the hidden one to come out and name his location, how have you experienced that question in your own seasons of distance? What did it sound like — through Scripture, through a friend, through silence, through circumstance?**

6. **The Hebrew verb in [Gen. 3:9](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.9) (*qārāʾ*, "call") is the same word used later when God *calls* Moses, Samuel, and Isaiah to prophetic ministry. The first prophetic call in Scripture is God's call to a hiding sinner. What does it mean for the doctrine of prophecy that it begins not with chosen heroes but with hiding sinners?**

7. **Adam blamed Eve, Eve blamed the serpent, and Adam — through Eve — indirectly blamed God ("the woman *whom You gave me*"). What patterns of blame-shifting do you recognize in yourself when you've been caught? What would honest naming look like instead?**

### On the Long Reach of Sin and the Long Reach of God

8. **[Isaiah 59:2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa59.2) describes iniquity as something that has "separated you from your God." Yet the entire prophetic story insists that the separation has not stopped God's pursuit. How do you hold the *reality* of separation and the *fact* of pursuit together? Have you ever felt both at once?**

9. **Paul's confession in [Romans 7:15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rom7.15) — "what I will to do, that I do not practice; but what I hate, that I do" — is one of the most candid lines in Scripture. How does the universality of this brokenness affect how you think about your own struggles with sin? Does it make them feel less isolating, or more discouraging?**

10. **[Genesis 3:15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.15) has been called "the first gospel promise" — a flash of mercy inside a sentence of judgment. Why might God have spoken the promise *before* expelling them from the Garden, rather than after? What does that order suggest about how God leads sinners through consequence?**

### On How God Speaks Today

11. **[Hebrews 1:1–2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Heb1.1) says God has spoken "in various times and in various ways" — visions, dreams, audible voices, written Scripture, the Son, the Spirit. Of these modes, which has been most formative for *you*? Which have you been most resistant to or least practiced in? What does that resistance suggest?**

12. **The lesson notes that nature reveals God but also bears the marks of the Fall — beauty alongside death. How do you discern God's voice in the natural world without sentimentalizing it? When has creation taught you something true about God you could not have learned otherwise?**

13. **[Heb. 1:2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Heb1.2) says God has spoken "in [a] Son" — not just *through* but *in*. Jesus does not merely deliver God's message; He *is* the message. How does this shape what we mean when we say someone "has heard from God"? Does the test of true hearing always trace back to the character of Christ?**

14. **The "Friday — Ellen G. White" sections this quarter are biographical rather than thematic. Why might the lesson writers (the White Estate) have chosen to introduce Ellen White through her *story* — her injury, her shame, her gradual discovery of God's love — rather than her doctrine? What does that say about how prophets are formed?**

### For Personal Reflection

15. **The lesson asks: "What has been your own experience with shame and seeking to hide from God after you have sinned?" Without rushing to resolution, sit with the question. Is there a specific incident you can name? What kept you from coming out of hiding? What eventually brought you back?**

16. **"How much comfort do you get from knowing that Jesus, His love, His character, and His self-sacrifice, reveal to us what God the Father is really like?" Many believers carry an inherited image of God the Father that is harsher than Jesus. If your image of the Father were entirely shaped by Jesus ([John 14:9](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jn14.9)), what would change?**

17. **The lesson notes that God's "garment of light and glory" was lost in the Fall, and that He clothed Adam and Eve with skins from a slain animal. The first death in Scripture is a sacrifice to cover shame. When you think about Christ's death, do you tend to emphasize what it *accomplishes* (substitution, atonement) or what it *reveals* about God's character (the lengths He will go to clothe His children)? Both are true — but which has been more healing for you, and why?**

18. **If communication is the substance of relationship, what is the present state of your communication with God? Where has it grown? Where has it gone silent? What single change might begin to restore the conversation?**

## ✍️ Ellen White Integration

### 1. On God's relentless seeking

> *"God's love has been expressed in the greatest gift heaven could bestow.... It was not a glance into a hopeless future that revealed the depth of the love of Jesus. He saw all that men were doing, and all that they would do.... He saw the cross on which He must be lifted up; He felt the anguish which would rend His heart. Yet for the joy that was set before Him He endured the cross, despising the shame."*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *The Desire of Ages*, p. 753

**Context:** Ellen White writes in the closing chapters of *Desire of Ages* of the cost of redemption. The seeking love that called "Where are you?" in Eden ultimately bore the full weight of the question.

**Discussion prompt:** Connect this to [Gen. 3:9](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.9) and [Luke 15:20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Luke15.20). If God's seeking love already cost Him everything to find you, what does that imply about how He responds when you return today — even slowly, even partially?

### 2. On Eden and the restoration of communion

> *"The whole earth was bright and beautiful. Then it was that the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy. And the Lord looked with delight upon the work of His hands, pronouncing it 'very good.' Heaven and earth were filled with joy and praise, and 'everlasting joy was upon their heads.' "*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *Patriarchs and Prophets*, p. 47

**Context:** EGW's account of Eden emphasizes the *communion* of the unfallen creation — heaven, earth, angels, humanity all in one conversation.

**Discussion prompt:** Eden was the original "conversation" of creation. What does it mean that the consummation in [Revelation 21](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev21)–22 is described as restored speaking — "they shall see His face," and God dwelling with His people? If salvation is restored conversation, how does that reframe what you are praying for?

### 3. On Ellen White's own discovery of a speaking God

> *"My views of the Father were changed. I now looked upon Him as a kind and tender parent, rather than a stern tyrant compelling men to a blind obedience. My heart went out toward Him in a deep and fervent love."*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *Life Sketches of Ellen G. White*, p. 39

**Context:** Ellen White recounts her own conversion under Methodist preacher Levi Stockman, who showed her that God's character was nothing like she had feared.

**Discussion prompt:** Many believers carry a fearful image of God inherited from upbringing, culture, or past wounds. What might it look like for *your* "view of the Father" to change in the way Ellen White describes? What old image needs to be replaced? Who or what has been (or could be) your Levi Stockman?

## 🛠️ Practical Application

**Individual:**

1. **The "Where are you?" prayer.** Each evening this week, sit quietly for two minutes and hear God ask, "Where are you?" — not as accusation but as invitation. Name your honest location: distracted, exhausted, ashamed, hopeful, doubting. Resist the urge to dress up the answer. The conversation begins where you actually are.

2. **Eden inventory.** Write down two or three moments in your past when you experienced something like *Eden communion* — unhurried, unguarded presence with God or with another person who reflected Him. What conditions enabled those moments? What in your current rhythms is making them less likely, and what one change might recover them?

3. **One-page revelation history.** Sketch how God has spoken to *you* across your life — through Scripture, through people, through circumstance, through the Spirit's inner witness. The exercise is not to manufacture certainty but to notice the pattern of a seeking God.

**In Sabbath School:**

1. **Read [Genesis 3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3) aloud together, slowly.** Pause at v. 9. Ask the class: "Whose voice does God's 'Where are you?' sound like in your life right now — judge, parent, friend, stranger, silence?" Let people answer honestly without correction.

2. **Catalogue [Hebrews 1:1–2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Heb1.1).** Make a list together of the "many ways" God has spoken in Scripture (dreams, visions, audible voice, written word, prophets, Christ, Spirit). Which of these does your local church most emphasize? Which does it tend to neglect? What might rebalancing look like?

3. **The protoevangelium discussion.** Why does God speak the promise of the Redeemer ([Gen. 3:15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.15)) *before* announcing the consequences (vv. 16–19)? What does that ordering teach about how to give hard truth?

**Intellectually:**

1. **Study the verb *qārāʾ* (call).** Trace its use in [Gen. 3:9](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.9); [Exod. 3:4](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Ex3.4); [1 Sam. 3:4](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Sam3.4); [Isa. 6:8](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa6.8); [Jer. 1:5](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jer1.5); [Acts 9:4](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Acts9.4). Notice that God's "call" always *names* the one called and *invites* response. What pattern emerges? How does it shape your understanding of prophetic vocation?

2. **The "image of God" debate.** Theologians have located the *imago Dei* variously in rationality, relationality, dominion, moral capacity, addressability. Read [Genesis 1:26–28](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen1.26) and 9:6 together. Which interpretation does the immediate context most support? What is at stake in each reading?

## 🧵 Theological Threads

- **Communication and creation.** God *speaks* the worlds into being ([Gen. 1](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen1); [Heb. 1:2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Heb1.2); [John 1:1](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jn1.1)). Reality itself is a spoken thing. Prophecy is not God adding speech to a silent universe; it is God continuing the speech by which the universe began.

- **The protoevangelium as the spine of prophecy.** Every later prophetic word is exposition of [Gen. 3:15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.15). Isaiah's Servant, Daniel's Son of Man, Zechariah's pierced One, John's Lamb — all are unfoldings of the seed that crushes the serpent's head.

- **Hiding and revelation.** The Fall introduces hiddenness; salvation reverses it. [Revelation 22:4](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev22.4) — "they shall see His face" — is the deliberate undoing of [Genesis 3:8](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.8). The whole biblical narrative is a story of God dragging hidden things back into the light, beginning with hidden sinners.

- **The Son as the final speaking.** [Hebrews 1:2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Heb1.2) does not say God's speaking *stopped* with the Son; it says it *culminated*. All prior revelation pointed forward; all subsequent revelation points back. Christ is the interpretive key to every prophetic word.

- **The Spirit's continued voice.** [John 16:13](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jn16.13) — the Spirit will guide into all truth — does not introduce new content but applies Christ's revelation to new contexts. The Adventist understanding of the prophetic gift ([Rev. 12:17, 19](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev12.17):10) belongs in this stream: not a new word, but the Spirit's continuing application of the Word.
