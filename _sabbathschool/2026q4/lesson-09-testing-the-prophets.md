## 🔑 Central Theme

If the previous lesson taught readers *how to interpret* prophetic writings, this lesson teaches them *how to recognize* the prophets in the first place. The memory text is unambiguous: "do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits." Biblical Christianity is not credulous. It is discerning.

The lesson uses the diamond analogy. A diamond looks beautiful but so do some counterfeits. Multiple tests, applied together, distinguish the real from the fake. No single test is sufficient. So with the prophetic gift — supernatural phenomena alone do not prove a true prophet; biblical harmony alone does not prove a true prophet; fulfilled predictions alone do not prove a true prophet. *All* the tests must be present.

The week catalogues the classical biblical tests of a prophet:

- **Supernatural manifestation.** Real prophets often experience physical phenomena — visions, dreams, the eyes-open trance state, loss of breath, supernatural strength ([Dan. 10:7–19](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Dan10.7)). But [Deut. 13:1–5](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Deut13.1) is blunt: *even if signs and wonders occur, if the prophet leads you to other gods, do not listen.* Supernatural is necessary but not sufficient.

- **Harmony with previous Scripture.** [Isaiah 8:20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa8.20): "To the law and to the testimony! If they do not speak according to this word, it is because there is no light in them." A true prophet's message will be consistent with — though not necessarily identical to — what God has already revealed. New light cannot contradict old light.

- **Confession of the incarnate Christ.** [1 John 4:1–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn4.1) supplies the Christological test: any spirit that does not confess "Jesus Christ has come in the flesh" is not of God. This is not merely a creedal nod to Jesus' historical existence; it is the affirmation of His full humanity, sinless life, atoning death, resurrection, and high-priestly ministry.

- **Positive fruit.** [Matt. 7:15–20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Matt7.15). The prophet's *life* and the *effects* of his ministry. The lesson is careful here: prophets are not sinless. Jonah ran. Peter compromised on Gentile inclusion. Paul and Barnabas had a sharp disagreement. The test is not perfection but *trajectory* — does the life trend toward Christ, and does the ministry produce genuine spiritual fruit?

- **Fulfilled predictions.** [Deut. 18:22](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Deut18.22): "When a prophet speaks in the name of the LORD, if the thing does not happen, that is the thing which the LORD has not spoken." The test must be applied with care, however, because some prophecies are *conditional*. Jonah said Nineveh would be overthrown in 40 days; it was not, because Nineveh repented. [Jer. 18:7–10](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jer18.7) makes the principle explicit. Conditional prophecy depends on response.

Friday closes with Ellen White and the Adventist tradition of applying these tests to her. Three arguments are historically central — Scripture's promise of last-days prophecy ([Joel 2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Joel2); [Rev. 12:17](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev12.17)), the positive fruit of her ministry, and the supernatural elements of her experience. But the lesson lands on a more personal note: "Taste and see." The strongest argument for the validity of any prophetic ministry is engagement with its actual content. Read her books. Notice what they do in your life.

The aim of this lesson is discernment without cynicism — a faith disciplined enough to test and humble enough to receive when the tests pass.

## 📚 Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### [1 John 4:1](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn4.1) (Memory Text — Test the Spirits)

- **"Do not believe (*pisteuete*) every spirit":** A present imperative — stop believing every spirit. The verb is the same one used for saving faith; John is asking believers to *discipline* their faith.
- **"Test (*dokimazete*) the spirits":** From the verb used for assaying precious metal. The metaphor is metallurgical: heat applied to determine genuineness.
- **"Many false prophets have gone out":** Plural and present-tense. The danger is not hypothetical or historical. It is current and contemporaneous to every generation of the church.

### [Numbers 12:6](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Num12.6) (Sunday — How God Speaks to Prophets)

- **"I, the LORD, make Myself known to him in a vision; I speak to him in a dream":** Vision (*ḥāzôn*) and dream (*ḥālôm*) are the two primary modalities. The verse is part of God's defense of Moses against the criticism of Aaron and Miriam — God establishes Moses as exceptional ("Not so with My servant Moses... I speak with him face to face").

### [Daniel 10:7–19](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Dan10.7) (Sunday — Physical Phenomena)

- **"I alone saw the vision, for the men who were with me did not see the vision; but a great terror fell upon them":** The vision is private; the *atmosphere* is corporate. Witnesses can sense something is happening without seeing what.
- **"My vigor was turned to frailty in me, and I retained no strength":** The physical cost is dramatic. Real visionary experience is bodily, not abstract.
- **"He touched me again and strengthened me":** Angelic touch is the recurring restorative act. Daniel cannot bear the vision in his own strength.

### [Deuteronomy 13:1–5](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Deut13.1) (Sunday — The Sign-Wonder Test)

- **The shocking scenario:** A prophet arises, gives a sign or wonder that actually comes to pass, *and yet* says, "let us go after other gods." The text is clear that signs alone do not authenticate.
- **"For the LORD your God is testing you":** The trial is *Yahweh's*. Permitted apparent supernatural confirmation of a false message is part of how God tests covenant loyalty.
- **The verdict:** "That prophet shall be put to death." OT covenant penalties for false prophecy were severe — reflecting the gravity of leading God's people astray.

### [Isaiah 8:20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa8.20) (Monday — The Bible as Standard)

- **"To the law and to the testimony":** Two terms — *tôrâ* (instruction, the Pentateuch) and *tě'ûdâ* (testimony, the witness of God's revealed will). Together they form the canon-standard against which all later voices are measured.
- **"If they do not speak according to this word, it is because there is no light in them":** Light is the criterion. Speech contradicting prior revelation is darkness, however bright it may appear.

### [1 John 4:1–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn4.1) (Tuesday — The Christological Test)

- **"Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God":** The confession is specific. Not "Jesus is a great teacher" or "Jesus existed." The required confession is the incarnation — divine and human in one Person, truly *come in flesh*.
- **The Docetic background:** John addresses Christians being tempted by proto-Gnostic teachers who denied Christ's full humanity. The Christological test is sharp enough to expose this error.
- **"And every spirit that does not confess... is not of God":** John does not allow neutrality. Silence on the incarnation is itself a failure of the test.

### [Matthew 7:15–20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Matt7.15) (Wednesday — Fruit Inspection)

- **"By their fruits you will know them":** *Epignōsesthe* — you will *recognize* them. Recognition is gradual; fruit takes time to ripen.
- **The wolf-in-sheep's-clothing:** The disguise is outward; the inward reality is predatory. Discernment requires looking past wrapping.
- **The fruit metaphor:** Trees produce after their nature. A bad tree cannot consistently produce good fruit, no matter how long it tries. The character of the source determines the character of the output.

### [Deuteronomy 18:22](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Deut18.22) (Thursday — The Fulfilment Test)

- **"If the thing does not happen or come to pass":** The test is empirical. Time vindicates or exposes.
- **"Presumptuously":** The Hebrew *zādôn* — willful, arrogant speech. False prophets speak from their own heart but in the name of the LORD.
- **"You shall not be afraid of him":** The implicit reverence due a true prophet is withdrawn. The audience is liberated from fear of his judgments.

### [Jeremiah 18:7–10](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jer18.7) (Thursday — Conditional Prophecy)

- **"If that nation against whom I have spoken turns from its evil... I will relent":** God's announced judgments are not deterministic. They are *announcements of trajectory* offered with the hope of intervention by repentance.
- **The Jonah-Nineveh case:** "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown" — but Nineveh repented and was spared. Jonah's prophecy *did its work* by producing the response that averted its fulfilment.
- **Implication for testing prophets:** A conditional prophecy that does not occur because conditions changed has not failed the [Deut. 18:22](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Deut18.22) test. The interpreter must consider whether response could have altered outcome.

## 💡 Deep Discussion Questions

### On Why Testing Is Necessary

1. **The memory text commands believers to "test the spirits." Christianity is not credulous; it is discerning. Yet "discernment" in some Christian circles has hardened into reflexive suspicion. What is the difference between *biblical testing* and *modern cynicism*? Where do you find yourself on the spectrum?**

2. **The diamond analogy: multiple tests applied together distinguish real from fake. Have you ever applied only *one* test to a teacher or movement and accepted it on that basis? What did you miss? What would have changed if you had applied the full set?**

3. **[Deut. 13:1–5](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Deut13.1) is a sobering passage: even if signs and wonders happen, if the prophet leads you to other gods, *do not follow*. What does this say about Christians who treat charisma, miracles, or apparent supernatural confirmation as sufficient validation? Where in contemporary religious culture is this most dangerous?**

### On the Supernatural Test

4. **The lesson notes that "supernatural manifestations are not a test of the true prophetic gift." Yet many believers find supernatural experience *most* persuasive. Why is that? What is the proper place of supernatural manifestation in the assessment of a prophetic claim?**

5. **[Daniel 10](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Dan10)'s physical phenomena (loss of strength, mute lips, terrified companions) are striking. But Satan can also produce supernatural effects ([Exod. 7:11](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Ex7.11); [2 Thess. 2:9](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.2Thess2.9); [Rev. 13:13, 14](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev13.13)). How do we hold the *legitimacy* of supernatural phenomena in some prophetic ministry alongside the *insufficiency* of it as a stand-alone test?**

6. **The lesson observes: "In some cultures, supernatural occurrences are more common than in others." How do you think about prophetic claims that arise from cultures where the supernatural is normalized? Does cultural setting change the urgency of the tests?**

### On Harmony With Scripture

7. **[Isaiah 8:20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa8.20) — "to the law and to the testimony." The Bible is the standard against which all later voices are measured. How do you read EGW *under* Scripture rather than *alongside* it? Where in Adventist practice has the order been reversed?**

8. **"New light cannot contradict old light." The lesson uses this principle to anchor prophetic development. Yet *progressive revelation* is real — the NT clarifies what the OT only foreshadowed. How do you distinguish legitimate *unfolding* from illegitimate *contradiction*? What is the test?**

9. **Many false prophets *quote Scripture*. The test is not whether they cite the Bible but whether they *agree with the Bible's central themes*. Where in contemporary religious teaching have you seen heavy biblical citation accompanied by departure from the Bible's central message?**

### On the Christological Test

10. **[1 John 4:1–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn4.1) makes confession of "Jesus Christ come in the flesh" the test. The lesson is careful to say this is "more than an intellectual agreement that Jesus lived on earth." What does *substantive* confession of the incarnation entail — what claims about Jesus' deity, humanity, atonement, and resurrection are part of the package?**

11. **The lesson says: "Jesus must be the center of a prophet's teachings and not just something added on." How can you tell whether Jesus is *central* to a teacher's message or *appended* to it? What are the markers of each?**

12. **A true prophet "will not try to create their own following or promote themselves." Where in contemporary religious leadership have you noticed self-promotion masquerading as prophetic ministry? What is the difference between healthy ambition and self-aggrandizement in spiritual leadership?**

### On Fruit and Conditional Prophecy

13. **The lesson is candid: "Being a prophet does not make a person sinless or personally infallible." Jonah ran. Peter compromised. Paul and Barnabas split sharply. How do you hold the dual reality — true prophets carry true messages *and* remain flawed people — without using either truth to cancel the other?**

14. **"The trend of the life must clearly be toward God." Trajectory matters more than perfection. Where in your evaluation of spiritual leaders have you mistaken a *snapshot* (a moment of failure or success) for the *trajectory* (the long pattern)? What changes when you read trajectory instead?**

15. **Conditional prophecy (Jonah, [Jer. 18:7–10](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jer18.7)) means a prediction that does not occur is not necessarily a failed prediction. How do you apply this principle without making it an escape hatch for every failed prediction? What guards the distinction between *conditional* and *false*?**

### On Ellen White Specifically

16. **The lesson lists three classical defenses of EGW's prophetic gift: Scripture predicts last-days prophecy, her ministry has borne good fruit, and her experience included supernatural phenomena. Which of these arguments has been most persuasive to you personally — and which has been least? Why?**

17. **The lesson concludes: "A powerful 'proof' of Ellen G. White's gift is a personal experience with her writings. As the psalmist David urged: 'Taste and see.'" When was the last time you actually *read* a chapter of EGW with an open heart, not for argument but for formation? What did it do?**

18. **Steps to Christ is recommended as a starting place. What is your honest relationship to EGW's writings — devoted reader, occasional consultant, polite avoider, anxious critic? What might mature engagement look like over the next 90 days?**

## ✍️ Ellen White Integration

### 1. On the perpetuity of the prophetic gift

> *"The remnant church will be brought into great trial and distress. Those who keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus will feel the ire of the dragon and his hosts.... To stand in defense of truth and righteousness when the majority forsake us, to fight the battles of the Lord when champions are few — this will be our test."*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *Testimonies for the Church*, vol. 5, p. 136

**Context:** EGW writes of the end-time church under pressure, including the need for the prophetic gift to sustain its witness.

**Discussion prompt:** [Joel 2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Joel2) and [Rev. 12:17](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev12.17) predict last-days prophecy. EGW understood herself in that stream. Where does the contemporary church most need the testing-and-receiving discipline this lesson teaches — to be both alert against false prophets and open to the genuine gift's continuation?

### 2. On comparing prophets to Scripture

> *"In our time there is a wide departure from their doctrines and precepts, and there is need of a return to the great Protestant principle — the Bible, and the Bible only, as the rule of faith and duty."*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *The Great Controversy*, p. 204–205

**Context:** EGW affirms the Protestant principle that Scripture is the supreme rule for testing every teaching, including her own.

**Discussion prompt:** EGW herself anchored authority in *Scripture alone*. She did not see her ministry as adding a new source of authority but as directing the church back to Scripture. How is this self-understanding sometimes obscured in contemporary Adventist practice? What would re-centering on the Bible-only principle look like?

### 3. On personal experience as confirmation

> *"O taste and see that the Lord is good. Blessed is the man that trusteth in him."*
> — **[Psalm 34:8](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Ps34.8)**, cited in EGW's writings as a model of personal verification

**Context:** EGW frequently appealed to personal engagement with truth as the deepest confirmation of its reality.

**Discussion prompt:** *Tasting and seeing* is experiential verification. It is not blind credulity — it is engagement that produces evidence. What does this kind of testing look like in practice — for Scripture, for EGW, for any spiritual claim? How is it different from both naive acceptance and cynical rejection?

## 🛠️ Practical Application

**Individual:**

1. **The five-test exercise.** Pick a contemporary teacher whose ministry has shaped you (positively or negatively). Apply the five tests: supernatural confirmation, harmony with Scripture, Christological orthodoxy, life-fruit, fulfilled prophecy (where applicable). What does the cumulative assessment reveal?

2. **The [1 John 4](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn4) test sentence.** Memorize [1 John 4:1–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn4.1). Make it your reflex when you encounter a new teacher: "Does this voice confess Jesus Christ as having come in the flesh — in His full divinity, humanity, atonement, and resurrection?" If yes, continue evaluating. If no, walk away.

3. **The "taste and see" experiment.** Read one chapter of *Steps to Christ* prayerfully every day for two weeks. Notice what happens — not in argument but in formation. Keep a brief journal. At the end, ask: what has this reading actually *done*?

**In Sabbath School:**

1. **The diamond-test discussion.** Read the lesson's opening diamond analogy. Discuss: in our community, which test do we apply *most* (supernatural, Scripture-harmony, Christology, fruit, prediction)? Which do we apply *least*? What does the imbalance suggest about how we are likely to err — too credulous, or too suspicious?

2. **The conditional-prophecy study.** Read [Jeremiah 18:7–10](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jer18.7) alongside [Jonah 3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jonah3). Discuss: how does conditional prophecy *enhance* rather than weaken the [Deut. 18:22](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Deut18.22) test? Where in contemporary applications of prophecy is the conditional element ignored?

3. **The Christological-confession workshop.** Write together a brief paragraph affirming "Jesus Christ has come in the flesh" with content sufficient to fail false christologies (denials of deity, denials of humanity, denials of atonement, denials of resurrection). What is the minimum content of orthodox confession? Why does it matter for testing prophetic claims?

**Intellectually:**

1. **The history of false prophecy study.** Survey notable historic claims to prophetic gift outside Adventism (Joseph Smith, Ellen Harmon's contemporaries who fell away, modern televangelist "prophets"). For each, apply the five tests. What patterns emerge? What does the comparison teach about the necessity of *all* five?

2. **The Jonah-and-conditional-prophecy study.** Read Jonah alongside [Jer. 18:7–10](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jer18.7) and [Ezek. 18:21–32](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Ezek18.21). What is the theology of conditional prophecy — and what does it say about God's character? About human agency?

## 🧵 Theological Threads

- **Testing is biblical.** "Do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits" ([1 John 4:1](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn4.1)) is not skepticism; it is faithfulness. The Bible itself commands the testing of prophetic claims. Believers who refuse to test are not more spiritual; they are more vulnerable.

- **No single test suffices.** The diamond analogy. Supernatural manifestation alone, Scripture-citation alone, Christological orthodoxy alone, observed fruit alone, fulfilled prediction alone — none is sufficient by itself. Discernment is *cumulative*.

- **Scripture is the supreme test.** [Isaiah 8:20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa8.20). All subsequent revelation is measured against the canon. This includes EGW. The "Bible-only" principle is not in tension with the prophetic gift; it is the framework that makes the prophetic gift safe.

- **The incarnation is the Christological hinge.** [1 John 4:2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn4.2) makes "Jesus Christ come in the flesh" the test. Every major false christology in history — from Docetism to Arianism to modernism — fails at this point. The fully divine, fully human, atoning, risen Christ is the test of every prophetic word.

- **Conditional prophecy preserves God's character.** [Jer. 18:7–10](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jer18.7). God's announced judgments are not deterministic; they are *relational*. When Nineveh repents, the judgment is averted. The conditional element is not weakness in prophecy; it is the moral structure of a God who relents.

- **The Adventist self-test.** Adventists have historically applied the five tests to Ellen White and concluded her ministry passes. The five tests are not partisan to her; they apply to *every* claim. A church that applies the tests honestly is a church that will receive genuine gifts and reject counterfeits.
