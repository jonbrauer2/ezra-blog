## 🔑 Central Theme

Faith is the bridge between the visible and the invisible, the temporary and the eternal. This lesson presents faith not as intellectual assent to propositions but as *trust* in a Person — a living, breathing confidence in God's character that sustains us when circumstances contradict His promises. The lesson navigates three critical tensions:

1. **Faith vs. Signs:** Why Jesus refuses to give proof on demand (Sabbath/Sunday)
2. **Fear vs. Faith:** How faith grows not in safety but in crisis (Monday/Tuesday)
3. **Human Faith vs. Divine Faith:** The distinction between our faith *in* Jesus and *the faith of Jesus* (Wednesday-Thursday)

The underlying question of the lesson: *What kind of faith actually sustains a relationship with God — and how do we get it?*

## 📚 Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### Hebrews 11:1 (Memory Text)
- **Context:** Opening of the "Faith Hall of Fame" — not a definition of faith as cognitive agreement but a description of what faith *does* and *is*.
- **"Faith is the assurance of things hoped for" (*hypostasis tōn elpizomenōn*):** *Hypostasis* means "substance," "reality," "what stands under." Faith is not wishful thinking (*elpis*, hope) but the *substance* that makes hope real. It's the title deed to unseen promises.
- **"The conviction of things not seen" (*elegchos tōn blepomenōn*):** *Elegchos* is proof, conviction, reproof. Faith provides internal evidence for external realities. The paradox: faith is evidence for what cannot be seen — it is the eyes of the soul.
- **Theological implication:** Faith is not opposed to reason; it's another mode of knowing. Reason operates in the visible; faith operates in the invisible. Both are necessary, neither sufficient alone.

### Mark 8:11-12 (Sabbath — Faith vs. Signs)
- **Context:** Pharisees demand "a sign from heaven" — something spectacular, undeniable, sky-shattering.
- **"He sighed deeply in his spirit" (*anastenaxas tō pneumati autou*):** This is the only place Mark records Jesus sighing. It's not frustration but grief — grief over the condition of a heart that would demand proof from the God who has already revealed Himself.
- **"Why does this generation seek a sign?"** The problem isn't asking for signs — God gives signs throughout Scripture. The problem is the *generation's* disposition: skeptical, transactional, treating God like a performer who must audition.

### Matthew 15:21-28 (The Canaanite Woman)
- **Context:** A Gentile woman, outside the covenant, begs for her demon-possessed daughter. Jesus appears to ignore, then refuse, then insult her ("dogs").
- **"Lord, help me" (v.25):** Three words. Theology compressed to its essence. She doesn't explain, justify, or debate — she just holds on.
- **"Even the dogs eat the crumbs" (v.27):** She accepts Jesus' own metaphor and turns it back on Him with brilliance and humility. She's not arguing *against* His statement — she's arguing *within* it. She accepts her position and still trusts His grace.
- **"Great is your faith!" (v.28):** The only person Jesus explicitly praises for *great* faith. A Gentile woman. A social and religious outsider. The faith that amazes Jesus is not the impressive, polished kind — it's the kind that holds on when everything seems to say "no."

### Luke 7:1-10 (The Centurion)
- **Context:** A Roman military officer (a Gentile occupier) asks Jesus to heal his slave. He understands authority — he commands men and follows orders. He applies this understanding to Jesus.
- **"Just say the word" (v.7):** The centurion grasps what most of Israel missed: Jesus' authority is not constrained by proximity. Word and reality are aligned in Jesus — He speaks, and it is.
- **"Not even in Israel have I found such faith" (v.9):** Again, the highest-commended faith comes from an outsider. Pattern: Jesus praises faith that is humble, specific, and confident in His *character* rather than demanding proof of His *power*.

### Mark 9:24 (The Desperate Father)
- **"I believe; help my unbelief!"** The most honest prayer in the Gospels. Not a formula, not a confession of strong faith — a confession of *mixed* faith, brought to Jesus. This is the template for authentic faith: not performing certainty, but honestly presenting the contradiction to the One who can resolve it.

### Revelation 14:12 (The Faith of Jesus)
- **Greek:** *pistis Iēsou* — genitive construction, ambiguous: "faith in Jesus" (objective genitive) or "faith of Jesus" (subjective genitive).
- **Adventist interpretation:** The "faith of Jesus" as the *faithfulness Jesus himself exercised* — His trust in the Father, His obedience to the covenant, His endurance to the cross. End-time saints participate in this faith, not merely as belief *about* Jesus but as sharing in the very faithfulness He embodied.
- **Theological weight:** This shifts the center of gravity. Salvation is not anchored in the quality of *my* faith but in the faithfulness of Jesus. I am held not by how firmly I grip, but by the grip of the One I cling to.

## 🗣️ Deep Discussion Questions

### On Faith vs. Signs (Sabbath/Sunday)
1. **Mark 8:11-12: Jesus sighs deeply when asked for a sign. What does this reveal about the relationship between faith and demand? Is it ever appropriate to ask God for a sign — and if so, how is that different from what the Pharisees were doing?**

2. **Deuteronomy 4:7-10 says Israel's God is uniquely near — this itself was evidence of God. Yet they still doubted. Why do we keep wanting new evidence even when we already have substantial evidence? What is it about human psychology that drives sign-seeking?**

3. **John 20:29: Jesus says, "Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed." In our scientific, evidence-driven culture, this sounds like anti-intellectualism. How do you respond to that? Is faith unreasonable, or is it a different kind of reason?**

4. **John 20:30-31 says the miracles were written "so that you may believe." Signs *can* produce faith — they did for many in the Gospels. What's the difference between signs that produce genuine faith and the sign-seeking Jesus condemned?**

### On Fear and Faith (Monday)
5. **Mark 4:40: Jesus asks, "Why are you so afraid? Do you still have no faith?" Fear and faith are presented as opposites. But is that psychologically accurate? Can you be genuinely afraid and still have genuine faith? What does that look like?**

6. **The Canaanite woman (Matt 15:21-28) faced three obstacles: silence, refusal, and insult. Most people would have quit at the first. What kept her going? What does her persistence tell us about the nature of mature faith?**

7. **John 2:23-25: Jesus "did not entrust himself to them" even though "many believed in his name when they saw the signs." Why would Jesus distrust people who believed in Him? What kind of faith was He looking for that He wasn't seeing?**

### On Growing Faith (Tuesday/Wednesday)
8. **Matthew 17:20: Faith as small as a mustard seed can move mountains. Jesus says it's not the *size* of faith but the *object*. What does this mean practically? Is there such a thing as faith that's "too small" to work, or is any genuine faith sufficient?**

9. **Romans 12:3 says God has given each person "a measure of faith." Does this mean faith is a gift, not an achievement? If so, how does "grow your faith" square with "faith is given"? Where does responsibility and grace begin?**

10. **Matthew 17:20: "Faith as a grain of mustard seed." What does seed-faith look like in practice? Can you identify a time when your faith was "mustard-sized" but God honored it? What happened?**

11. **Romans 10:17: "Faith comes from hearing." In the digital age, we're bombarded with words but starved for the Word. What does it mean to "hear" Christ's word today, and how do we distinguish it from all the other voices?**

12. **Luke 17:5: The apostles ask, "Increase our faith." Jesus doesn't say "I will" or "pray more" or "believe harder" — He talks about a slave's duty (v.7-10). What's He teaching here about the nature of faith?**

### On The Faith of Jesus (Thursday/Friday)
13. **Revelation 14:12 mentions "the faith of Jesus" — ambiguous whether it's faith *in* Jesus or faith *of* Jesus. How would the Christian life look different if we understood it as Jesus' faith in the Father sustaining us, not just our faith in Jesus?**

14. **Matthew 26:36-42 (Gethsemane): Jesus prays "not as I will, but as you will" — three times. This is the ultimate expression of faith. How does Jesus' struggle in Gethsemane change our understanding of faith? Is faith about *not* struggling, or struggling and still trusting?**

15. **Galatians 2:20: "The life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me." Paul says he lives by faith *in* the Son *who loved him*. What's the connection between knowing you're loved and having faith?**

16. **Hebrews 12:2: Jesus is "the founder and perfecter of our faith." Does this mean faith is something He starts and He finishes? What role do we have then — or is it all Him?**

### On Faith and the Christian Life
17. **Hebrews 11:1: Faith as "evidence of things not seen." How do you explain this to someone who says "faith is believing what you know isn't true"? What's the difference between blind faith and reasonable faith?**

18. **The lesson distinguishes between faith as intellectual assent and faith as trust. Can you have one without the other? What does a Sabbath School class look like that focuses on building trust rather than increasing knowledge?**

19. **2 Corinthians 5:7: "We walk by faith, not by sight." What does "walking by faith" look like in 2026 — not in crises (cancer diagnosis, job loss) but in daily life (commuting, conversations, decisions)?**

20. **The quarter's theme is "Growing in a Relationship With God." This lesson focuses on faith. Is faith *the* relationship, or *part* of the relationship? What else is needed, and how does faith connect to love, obedience, prayer, and the other elements of relationship with God?**

## ✍️ Ellen White Integration Points

Use sparingly — as amplification of scriptural truth, not replacement for it.

**1. On faith as sight:**
> *"Faith is the reason for all that is done by the children of God, because faith is the evidence of things not seen. Those who live by faith see the invisible, and realize the reality of the promises of God. They have an assurance that He who has promised is faithful, and will perform His word."*
> — *The Youth's Instructor*, July 24, 1902

*Discussion use:* Connect this to Hebrews 11:1 and the concept of "seeing the invisible." How does faith function as spiritual sight? What are we supposed to "see" that natural vision cannot?

**2. On mustard seed faith:**
> *"It is not the amount of talent possessed, or the greatness of the ability, that makes the work acceptable. It is the spirit in which the work is done. A small talent, rightly used, will accomplish much. If you have faith as a grain of mustard seed, you may say to the mountain, Remove hence to yonder place, and it shall remove."*
> — *The Review and Herald*, May 25, 1886

*Discussion use:* Connect to Matthew 17:20 and question #10. If the amount of faith doesn't matter, what does? What is the "spirit" in which work is done that makes it acceptable to God?

**3. On Jesus as the source of faith:**
> *"Looking unto Jesus, the Author and Finisher of our faith, we may rise above the level of our low, selfish, earthly attainments. We may ascend the mount of God's holy hill, and there behold the glories of His kingdom, and receive strength to walk in the pathway cast up for the ransomed of the Lord."*
> — *The Review and Herald*, July 22, 1884

*Discussion use:* Connect to Hebrews 12:2 and the concept of "the faith of Jesus" (Rev 14:12). If Jesus is both the source and the completion of our faith, what happens when we stop looking at Him and look at our faith instead?

## 🌐 Practical Application for Modern Context

**Individual:**
- **The "help my unbelief" prayer:** This week, every time you notice doubt, fear, or skepticism in your heart, pray Mark 9:24: "I believe; help my unbelief." Don't try to talk yourself out of doubt; bring it to Jesus honestly. Track what happens after 7 days.
- **Faith anchor meditation:** Pick one promise from Scripture (e.g., Jer 31:3, "I have loved you with an everlasting love"). Read it aloud 3 times each morning, then ask: "What would today look like if this were actually true?" Live as if it is — and notice where this creates tension or comfort.
- **Mountain identification:** Identify one "mountain" in your life — a circumstance that feels immovable (chronic issue, strained relationship, persistent doubt). Don't ask God to remove it this week. Instead, ask Him to show you what mustard-seed faith would look like *with* the mountain still there.

**In Sabbath School:**
- **Faith testimonies:** The lesson highlights the Canaanite woman (silence + persistence), the centurion (understanding authority), and the desperate father (honest doubt). Invite 3 people to share: (1) a time silence preceded an answer, (2) a moment when recognizing God's authority changed everything, (3) a prayer of honest struggle. Frame these as "faith in the wild," not "victory stories."
- **Small group exercise:** Break into groups of 3-4. Each person identifies a current "sign-seeking" area — something they want God to prove. The group discusses: (a) What does this desire reveal about how we view God? (b) What would it mean to trust Him without the sign? (c) What Scripture speaks to this need? Close with prayer: "Lord, help our unbelief."

**Intellectually:**
- **Faith and epistemology:** The lesson addresses the modern crisis of confidence in truth. Hebrews 11:1 defines faith as "evidence" — not evidence for others (apologetics) but evidence *to* the believer. Discuss: How do we know faith is genuine if it's not based on proof? What's the role of experience, community, and Scripture in forming faith?
- **The nature of belief:** Modern neuroscience suggests belief isn't a choice but a cognitive response to evidence. The Bible presents faith as something we're commanded to have (Mark 11:22). How do we reconcile these? Is faith a decision, a gift, or both — and does it matter?
- **The faith of Jesus problem:** If faith is trusting God's character, and Jesus had perfect faith, why did He cry "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Matt 27:46)? Did Jesus lose faith, or is there something we're missing about the nature of faith?

## 🧵 Theological Threads to Follow

- **Faith and justification:** Eph 2:8-9 and Rom 5:1 present faith as the instrument of justification. This is the Reformation sola fide — faith alone. But how does this relate to faith as ongoing trust, not just a one-time decision? Are there two kinds of faith (saving vs. daily), or one faith with two aspects?
- **Faith and works:** James 2:14-26 and Romans 4:1-25 seem to contradict. James says faith without works is dead; Paul says faith is credited as righteousness apart from works. The lesson suggests the resolution: saving faith produces works, but works don't produce saving faith. How does this play out practically in a church culture that often evaluates spirituality by activity?
- **Faith and perseverance:** Hebrews 11 describes heroes who "died in faith, not having received the promises" (v.13). Endurance is part of faith. But is perseverance a requirement for final salvation (the "once saved, always saved" debate), or evidence of genuine faith? How do Adventists navigate this without falling into perfectionism or antinomianism?
- **Faith and the Trinity:** We have faith *in* God the Father (Rom 4:20-22), through Jesus Christ (John 14:6), by the Holy Spirit (1 Cor 12:9). Each Person plays a role in faith. Understanding this Trinitarian dimension can prevent a flat, transactional view of faith.
- **Faith in the great controversy:** Satan's first temptation (Gen 3) was "Did God really say?" — an attack on faith in God's word. The end-time crisis (Rev 14) will be resolved by those who have "the faith of Jesus" — trust in God's character regardless of circumstances. Faith is front-and-center in the cosmic conflict, not a peripheral spiritual exercise.

## 📝 Lesson Summary for Quick Reference

**Core assertion:** Faith is trust in God's character that persists when circumstances contradict His promises. It is not intellectual agreement, emotional certainty, or bargaining for miracles — it is the eyes of the soul that see what is invisible to natural vision.

**Four biblical models this week:**
1. **The Pharisees:** Faith as sign-seeking — demanding proof before trusting. Jesus rejects this approach.
2. **The Canaanite woman & Centurion:** Faith as humble persistence — trusting Jesus even when He seems silent or distant.
3. **The desperate father:** Faith as honest struggle — believing and not believing simultaneously, bringing the whole mess to Jesus.
4. **Jesus in Gethsemane:** Faith as surrendered obedience — "not my will, but yours" despite the terror of what that meant.

**Key tensions the lesson navigates:**
1. Signs vs. Trust — faith that demands proof vs. faith that trusts God's character
2. Doubt vs. Belief — is mixed belief legitimate, or does real faith exclude doubt?
3. My Faith vs. Jesus' Faith — faith in Jesus vs. the faith of Jesus sustaining me
4. Instant vs. Grown — faith as decision vs. faith as something developed over time

**The bottom line:** You cannot have a relationship with God without faith, but you also cannot have faith without growing it. Faith is not a one-time decision but a daily orientation — a way of seeing reality, a habit of trust, a choice to believe God's character more than your circumstances. The Canaanite woman, the centurion, the desperate father — they didn't have perfect faith, but they had the right object: Jesus. And that's enough.
