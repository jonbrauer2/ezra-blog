## 🔑 Central Theme

Faith is not a religious sentiment, a psychological technique, or an emotional state we generate by intensity of feeling. It is a God-initiated relational response — God draws us (*Jeremiah 31:3*), the Spirit prompts us, and we respond with trust. The quarter's theme of *Growing in a Relationship With God* reaches a crucial turning point here: relationship with an invisible God is only possible through faith, and yet that faith itself is God's gift (*Ephesians 2:8–9*).

The lesson explores the anatomy of faith from multiple angles: faith as response to evidence (not blind leaping), faith as perseverance in the face of silence or delay, faith as gift that can grow (mustard-seed principle), faith as distinct from feelings (you can believe in darkness), and ultimately, the faith *of Jesus* — not just *in* Jesus but the pattern of total surrender that Jesus demonstrated in Gethsemane as the model for mature faith.

The great faith chapter of Hebrews 11 is the week's centrepiece, showing that throughout salvation history, living by faith meant holding on to promises not yet received in full — and that God honored both those who received earthly deliverance *and* those who did not, because faith is not vindicated by outcomes but by the resurrection horizon of God's "better thing" (*Heb 11:39–40*).

## 📚 Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### Hebrews 11:1 (Memory Text)

- **Context:** The author of Hebrews is calling readers who are facing persecution and wavering in commitment to hold fast to faith. Chapter 11 is a rhetorical masterpiece — a cloud of witnesses called to strengthen *your* faith today.
- **"*Pistis*" (faith):** The Greek carries the sense of *trust, fidelity, faithfulness*. It is more than intellectual assent — it is covenantal loyalty to what has been revealed. Paul's "faith of Abraham" (*pistis Abraam*) models faith as ongoing obedience, not a moment-in-time belief statement.
- **"Assurance of things hoped for" (*hypostasis*):** The word *hypostasis* literally means "sub-stance" or "that which stands under." It is used in philosophy as the *underlying reality* of a thing. Faith is not wishful thinking — it is the *present, solid basis* on which future realities rest. What we hope for is already real in God; faith is our connection to that reality.
- **"Evidence of things not seen" (*elenchos*):** *Elenchos* is the Greek word for *proof, demonstration, conviction* — the very term used in legal/rhetorical argumentation. Faith is not the *absence* of evidence but the conviction that divine evidence of the unseen is reliable. The universe itself (*Heb 11:3*) was made from what is unseen — faith operates in the same domain as creation.
- **NLT vs ASV contrast:** The ASV reads "assurance of things hoped for, a conviction of things not seen" — where "conviction" (*elenchos*) carries a firmer, more active sense than the NLT's "evidence." Faith is not passive reception of evidence but the active inner conviction that what God has said is real.

### Mark 8:11–12 (Sunday — Demanding Signs)

- **Context:** The Pharisees approach Jesus seeking a sign *from heaven* (*ek tou ouranou*) — specifically a cosmic sign to prove his divine authority, having just refused to attribute his miracles to God's power.
- **"He sighed deeply in his spirit" (*anastenaxas tō pneumati autou*):** The Greek *anastenazō* is a compound of *ana* (up) and *stenazō* (to groan) — a *deep groan rising from within*. This is one of the most humanly intimate moments in the Gospels. Jesus is not angry or contemptuous — he is *grieved*.
- **"There shall no sign be given unto this generation" (v.12):** Ironic in context — they already had sign after sign (healings, exorcisms, feeding of five thousand), and yet demanded cosmic theater. Jesus refuses to feed the hunger for spectacle that refuses to respond to grace.
- **Contrast with John the Baptist (Matt 11:2–6):** John's disciples asked essentially the same question from prison — but from honest doubt, not hostile testing. Jesus responded to John with *evidence* (the blind see, lame walk, dead raised). The difference is not the question but the heart posture behind it.

### Matthew 15:21–28 (Monday — Canaanite Woman's Faith)

- **Context:** Jesus withdraws to Gentile territory (Tyre and Sidon) — outside Israel. A Canaanite woman pursues him despite every social, ethnic, and religious barrier.
- **"Have mercy on me, O Lord, thou son of David":** Remarkable confession from a Gentile — *Kyrie eleison* (*Lord, have mercy*) is the cry of the liturgy, and *Huios Dauid* (Son of David) is the Jewish Messianic title. She recognizes Jesus' identity before most Israelites do.
- **Jesus's silence (v.23) and apparent rebuff (v.24, 26):** Three stages of apparent rejection — silence, indirect refusal, and the "dogs" metaphor. Jesus is not being cruel; he is testing and revealing the depth of her faith, which can only be seen *under pressure*.
- **"Yea, Lord: for even the dogs eat of the crumbs" (v.27):** Her response is breathtaking — she accepts the metaphor while finding within it grounds for hope. If children's bread falls to dogs, and she is a dog, then *crumbs from the Messianic table still have power*. This is faith's logic: not demanding equal standing but trusting the overflow of divine grace.
- **"O woman, great is thy faith" (*megalē sou hē pistis*):** The only time Jesus uses *megalē* (great) for anyone's faith. The disciples get "*little faith*" (*oligopistia*); this outsider gets "great faith." It is faith that perseveres, argues creatively, refuses to be discouraged, and anchors itself in the character of God rather than immediate response.

### Luke 7:1–10 (Monday — The Centurion's Faith)

- **Context:** A Roman military officer, representing the occupying power, approaches Jesus through Jewish intermediaries — a profound act of humility given the social hierarchy.
- **"Say the word, and my servant shall be healed" (v.7):** The centurion understands *authority structures*. He has soldiers who obey him; he recognizes Jesus operates under divine authority (*exousia*) greater than any human chain of command. His faith is not emotional — it is *rational*, based on his understanding of how authority works.
- **"I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel" (v.9):** Jesus marvels — *ethaumasan* (was astonished). Those inside the covenant community with millennia of revelation have less faith than this Gentile military man. Faith is not proportional to religious inheritance or information; it grows from honest encounter with who God is.

### Hebrews 11:6 (Thursday)

- **"Without faith it is impossible to please him" (*chōris de pisteōs adunaton euarestēsai*):** The adjective *adunaton* is absolute — not "very difficult" but *impossible*. This is not moralism but relational ontology: relationship with an infinite personal God necessarily requires faith because we cannot perceive Him with physical senses.
- **"He that cometh to God must believe that he is" (*pisteuein dei*):** The present active infinitive *pisteuin* — ongoing, continuous believing. Not a one-time decision but a sustained posture of trust.
- **"He is a rewarder of them that seek after him" (*misthapodotēs*):** *Misthapodotēs* literally means "wage-payer" — one who gives what is owed or promised. God is *faithful* to those who seek Him.

### Revelation 14:12 (Thursday — "The Faith of Jesus")

- **"The faith of Jesus" (*tēn pistin Iēsou*):** The Greek genitive is grammatically ambiguous — it can be *faith in Jesus* (objective genitive) or *the faith that Jesus himself had* (subjective genitive). The Adventist understanding emphasizes the *subjective* genitive: the saints in the last days will exhibit the same quality of faith Jesus demonstrated — particularly in Gethsemane ("not my will but thine").
- **"Keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus":** Two identifying marks of God's end-time people — moral obedience (commandments) and Christlike relational trust (faith of Jesus). Neither law-keeping without faith nor faith without obedience defines the remnant.

### Ephesians 2:8–9 (Faith as Gift)

- **"By grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God":** The pronoun *touto* (that, this) in "that not of yourselves" is neuter in Greek — referring to the whole *salvation-by-grace-through-faith* package. The gift is the entire salvation arrangement. No component of our salvation is self-generated.
- **"Not of works, that no man should glory" (*ou ex ergōn*):** Salvation by grace through faith is the only arrangement that reserves all the glory for God.

## 💡 Deep Discussion Questions

### On Faith and Doubt

1. **Jesus marveled at the centurion's faith and was grieved by the Pharisees' demand for signs — yet both groups were asking for evidence of God's reality. What is the fundamental difference between the centurion's approach and the Pharisees'?** What does this tell us about the relationship between evidence, faith, and the posture of the heart?

2. **The teaching outline notes a friend whose great faith seemed to evaporate in the face of cancer and personal devastation.** Hebrews 11 holds up heroes of faith many of whom "received not the promise" and died without vindication. What does this mean for our theology of prayer and expectation? Is it possible to have genuine faith and still not receive what we ask?

3. **Mark 9:24 — "I believe; help thou mine unbelief" — is one of the most honest prayers in Scripture.** How do faith and doubt coexist in healthy Christian experience? Is doubt the opposite of faith, or merely the presence of unanswered questions? How does naming doubt differ from surrendering to unbelief?

4. **The lesson distinguishes between "signs for faith" (John the Baptist's disciples) and "signs as tests for God" (Pharisees).** If you were honest with yourself, which mode describes your own prayer life most of the time? What does that tell you about the maturity of your faith?

5. **"These signs were not what the Jews needed. No mere external evidence could benefit them. What they needed was not intellectual enlightenment, but spiritual renovation" (Ellen White, DA 406).** How does this apply to contemporary faith conversations — apologetics, creation-evolution debates, fulfilled prophecy? When does the search for intellectual certainty become a substitute for spiritual renovation?

### On the Nature of Faith

6. **Hebrews 11:1 defines faith as "assurance" (*hypostasis*) and "evidence" (*elenchos*).** *Hypostasis* means "the underlying substance" and *elenchos* means "legal proof." How does this language change the way you think about faith? Is faith more like "hoping for the best" or more like "acting on the most reliable evidence available"?

7. **Romans 12:3 says "God hath dealt to each man a measure of faith."** If faith is apportioned by God, what is the human responsibility in its exercise? How do you account for the vast differences in faith between different believers?

8. **The lesson emphasizes that "faith is not a feeling."** Ellen White writes: "Feeling is not faith.... Faith is ours to exercise, but joyful feeling and the blessing are God's to give" (*Early Writings*, p. 72). Describe a time when you felt spiritually empty but acted in faith anyway. What was the result?

9. **The mustard-seed principle (Matt 17:20) suggests that even tiny faith is sufficient.** Does this mean quality matters more than quantity? What "grows" a mustard seed of faith into a tree? What has most grown your faith historically?

10. **Faith is described as a "gift" in Ephesians 2:8 and yet a "responsibility" throughout Scripture (fight the good fight of faith, 1 Tim 6:12).** How do you hold together the givenness and the exertion of faith without falling into performance religion?

### On the Great Cloud of Witnesses (Hebrews 11)

11. **Hebrews 11:32–34 lists those who received miraculous deliverance — and verses 35–38 list those who were tortured, killed, and saw nothing change.** Both groups are commended equally. What does this tell us about how God evaluates faith? Does God value the faith of the apparently "unsuccessful" believer just as much as the one who sees the miracle?

12. **Many of Hebrews 11's "heroes" had profound moral failures** — Abraham lied about Sarah, Jacob was a deceiver, Samson's story is a moral disaster. What does their inclusion in the "faith chapter" say about the nature of saving faith and the character of God?

13. **Verse 40 says "God having provided some better thing for us, that they without us should not be made perfect."** How does this eschatological completion — that Old Testament saints await *with us* the resurrection — change how you understand your own faith journey?

### On the Canaanite Woman

14. **Jesus initially ignored, then apparently rebuffed, then insulted the Canaanite woman before praising her faith.** She experienced three escalating barriers to her request. What does this narrative reveal about how God sometimes works with us? Is silence from God a sign of absence, indifference, or something else?

15. **The woman's response — "even the dogs eat of the crumbs" — turned Jesus's metaphor into grounds for hope.** Can you think of a time when a hard biblical text or difficult providence became, on reflection, grounds for deeper faith rather than less?

### On the "Faith of Jesus" and Gethsemane

16. **Revelation 14:12 speaks of "the faith of Jesus" as an identifying mark of the end-time saints.** If this is the *subjective* genitive (Jesus's own quality of faith), then the saints must demonstrate the same surrender Jesus did in Gethsemane. What does "not my will but thine" look like in the life of an ordinary 21st-century believer?

17. **In Gethsemane, Jesus asked three times for the cup to pass.** Did he receive what he asked for? Yet we call Gethsemane the supreme act of faith. What does this say about the relationship between "answered prayer" and "successful faith"?

18. **The centurion's faith is built on his understanding of authority. The Canaanite woman's faith is built on persistent hope in mercy. Gethsemane faith is built on surrender to divine wisdom.** Are these different stages of faith, or different dimensions of the same faith? Which dimension do you find hardest?

### Synthesis and Application

19. **John the Baptist — the greatest prophet — doubted from prison whether Jesus was the Messiah.** If John faltered, how should we handle our own periods of faith crisis? What pastoral response does Jesus's answer to John's disciples (Matt 11:4–6) model for us?

20. **How would you describe faith to a genuinely post-Christian person who finds the concept either intellectually contemptible or emotionally irrelevant?** What would you draw from this week's lesson that goes beyond "just trust more"?

## ✍️ Ellen White Integration

### 1. Signs and Spiritual Renovation

> *"These signs were not what the Jews needed. No mere external evidence could benefit them. What they needed was not intellectual enlightenment, but spiritual renovation."*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *The Desire of Ages*, p. 406

**Discussion prompt:** Is your current spiritual hunger primarily for *more information* or for *spiritual renovation*? What would it mean to ask God for renovation rather than signs?

### 2. Faith vs. Feeling

> *"Many do not exercise that faith which it is their privilege and duty to exercise, often waiting for that feeling which faith alone can bring. Feeling is not faith.... Faith is ours to exercise, but joyful feeling and the blessing are God's to give."*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *Early Writings*, p. 72

**Discussion prompt:** Are there areas of your life where you are "waiting to feel like it" before exercising faith — in prayer, obedience, service? How would today be different if you acted on conviction rather than emotion?

---

### 3. Doubts and the Soil of Faith

> *"The unbelief which is cherished in the soul has a bewitching power. The seeds of doubt that they have been sowing will produce their harvest but they must continue to dig up every root of unbelief. When these poisonous plants are pulled up, they cease to grow for want of nourishment in word and action. The soul must have the precious plants of faith and love put in the soil of the heart and enthroned there."*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *Faith and Works*, p. 17

**Discussion prompt:** What "seeds of doubt" have you been unconsciously watering? What specific *plants of faith and love* could you intentionally cultivate this week?

## 🛠️ Practical Application

### Individual

- **Hebrews 11 exercise:** Read the chapter aloud in one sitting. After reading, write three "acts of faith" you are currently exercising — things you are trusting God for that you cannot yet see. Frame them as *hypostasis* — "the substance of what I hope for."
- **Faith journal:** For two weeks, note where you experienced God in ways you wouldn't have predicted — small providences, answered prayers you forgot you prayed, unexpected grace.
- **Find your "Mark 9:24" moment:** Write an honest prayer in the mode of "Lord, I believe; help my unbelief" about one specific area of doubt.

### Sabbath School Class

- **Two-column exercise:** On one side, list "faith based on evidence" (fulfilled prophecy, creation, historical resurrection, personal testimony). On the other, list "areas where faith is still required despite absence of felt evidence." Discuss: does one list strengthen the other?
- **Characters in Hebrews 11:** Assign class members different figures (those who received miracles vs. those who did not) and have them describe their faith from within the story.
- **"Faith of Jesus" reflection:** Read Gethsemane (Matt 26:36–42) together. Ask: What was Jesus trusting the Father for? How does "not my will but thine" redefine "strong faith"?

### Intellectual Challenge

- **The epistemology of faith:** Hebrews 11:1 describes faith as *elenchos* — "proof" or "evidence." How is this compatible with scientific epistemology? Where does the "evidence for the unseen" come from? Is Christian faith fundamentally different in kind from the trust a scientist extends to accepted theory, or different only in its object?

## 🧵 Theological Threads

- **Faith and the covenant:** Throughout Hebrews 11, faith is the mode of living inside God's covenant promises. Every figure lived "in the gap" between promise and fulfillment. Faith is participation in the ongoing story of God's redemptive project.
- **Faith and grace:** Ephesians 2:8–9 establishes that salvation is "by grace, through faith" — faith being the *channel*, grace being the *substance*. Faith cannot become a new form of merit or self-reliance.
- **Faith and obedience:** Revelation 14:12 connects commandment-keeping and the faith of Jesus as two marks of the same people. Genuine faith is always expressed in obedience; obedience without faith is legalism.
- **Faith and eschatology:** Hebrews 11 explicitly frames faith as eschatological: the patriarchs "died in faith, not having received the promises" (v.13). Faith is the *present* expression of *future* reality.
- **The faith of Jesus vs. faith in Jesus:** Both dimensions are essential. We are saved by *faith in* Jesus (his completed work); and we are transformed into people who exhibit the *faith of* Jesus (his relational posture). Growing in a relationship with God means both.
