## 🔑 Central Theme

Twelve weeks of apocalyptic prophecy converge here. Every symbol, every prophecy, every timeline, every crisis has been aimed at this moment: *"Yes, I am coming soon"* ([Rev. 22:20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev22.20)). The whole book of Daniel and the whole Apocalypse are, at their core, ordered toward one climactic event — the personal, visible, global, glorious, physical return of Jesus Christ.

The lesson insists on the *concreteness* of the promise. Not a spiritual, allegorical, or subjective coming — but *this same Jesus*, *in like manner*, *literally* returning. "This same Jesus" ([Acts 1:11](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Acts1.11)) is one of the most important phrases in Christian eschatology. Not another Jesus. Not a different Jesus. Not a redefined Jesus. The same One who ate with sinners, wept at graves, was crucified, rose bodily, and ascended visibly — *He Himself* returns.

The Second Coming has three dimensions in this lesson. First, *for His people*: resurrection of the dead in Christ, transformation of the living, gathering by the angels, everlasting fellowship with the King. Second, *against His enemies*: the beast, the false prophet, the kings of the earth, and finally death itself are all destroyed by "the brightness of His coming" ([2 Thess. 2:8](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.2Thess2.8)). Third, *the ultimate goal*: [Revelation 21](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev21) — a new heaven and new earth, God dwelling with humanity, every tear wiped away, no more death, mourning, crying, or pain.

The millennium sits between the Second Coming and the New Earth. Adventist theology reads the thousand years as an *interlude* — the righteous in heaven with Christ ([Rev. 20:4](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev20.4)), participating in the executive judgment of the wicked ([1 Cor. 6:2–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Cor6.2)); Satan bound on a desolated earth ([Rev. 20:1–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev20.1)); the wicked dead sleeping. Only after the millennium does the final resurrection of the wicked, their final judgment, and their final destruction take place ([Rev. 20:7–15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev20.7)) — and only then does the New Jerusalem descend and the new creation begin.

This is Christianity's *hope*. Not merely that our sins are forgiven (they are). Not merely that we go to heaven when we die (a partial truth — the dead sleep until resurrection). But that *Christ is returning, personally, gloriously, to gather His people, destroy sin, and restore creation*. This is the blessed hope. Every doctrine studied this quarter finds its meaning here.

## 📚 Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### [Acts 1:10, 11](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Acts1.10) (Sunday — *This Same Jesus*)

- **"This same Jesus" (Greek *houtos ho Iēsous*):** Emphatic. Not another. The very Jesus the disciples watched ascend is the very Jesus who will return. Not a symbol, not a spiritual presence — *He Himself*.
- **"In like manner" (*hon tropon*):** The manner of return corresponds to the manner of departure. Visible ([Acts 1:9–10](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Acts1.9)), bodily ([Luke 24:39](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Luke24.39)), literal ([John 14:2–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jn14.2)), with angels present ([Acts 1:10–11](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Acts1.10)), on a cloud ([Acts 1:9](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Acts1.9); [Matt. 24:30](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Matt24.30); [Rev. 1:7](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev1.7)).
- **What this rules out:** Any teaching that spiritualizes the Second Coming into a subjective experience, a historical event already past (e.g., the destruction of Jerusalem in A.D. 70), or a "secret rapture" invisible to most. The parousia is public.

### [1 Thessalonians 4:15–17](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Thess4.15) (Sunday — The Descent)

- **"The Lord Himself will descend from heaven":** Greek *autos ho kyrios katabēsetai ap' ouranou*. The Lord *personally*, not delegated. Descending — coming down, not manifesting internally.
- **"With a shout, with the voice of an archangel, and with the trumpet of God":** Three auditory phenomena that make the return unmistakable. This is not a whisper heard by the elect; it is a public announcement heard by earth and heaven.
- **"The dead in Christ will rise first":** The order matters. Adventist reading: the righteous dead sleep unconsciously in the grave until this moment. They are not currently in heaven; they are *awaiting* resurrection. At the Second Coming, they are raised first.
- **"Then we who are alive... shall be caught up together with them":** The living righteous, transformed ([1 Cor. 15:51–52](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Cor15.51)), are gathered with the resurrected righteous. Together they meet the Lord in the air. This is the "rapture" — but not secret, not seven years before a tribulation, not partial. It is the *simultaneous* gathering of all the redeemed at Christ's visible return.

### [Matthew 24:30](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Matt24.30) / [Revelation 1:7](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev1.7) (Sunday — Visible Return)

- **"Every eye shall see Him" ([Rev. 1:7](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev1.7)):** Greek *opsetai auton pas ophthalmos*. Universal visibility. No one is excluded from seeing.
- **"They also which pierced Him":** Even those long dead who participated in His crucifixion — a special resurrection of specific groups ([Dan. 12:2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Dan12.2) — some to everlasting life, some to shame) — will witness His return.
- **"All the tribes of the earth will mourn":** *kopsontai* — beat their breast, wail. Universal involuntary lament of those who refused Him. The visibility is inescapable; the response is unavoidable.
- **Ruling out the secret rapture:** The pretribulational-secret-rapture teaching, popular in dispensational Christianity, is exegetically incompatible with these texts. Every eye. All the tribes. In like manner as He went (visibly). The Christian hope has never been a secret exit.

### [Titus 2:13](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Titus2.13) (Monday — The Blessed Hope)

- **"The blessed hope, and the glorious appearing":** Greek *tēn makarian elpida kai epiphaneian tēs doxēs*. The word *epiphaneia* — from which we get "epiphany" — means *manifestation*, *appearing*. This is the technical NT word for Christ's public revelation.
- **"Of the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ":** The Granville Sharp construction in Greek indicates that "great God" and "Savior" both refer to *Jesus Christ*. This is one of the New Testament's clearest affirmations of Christ's deity — He is *our great God*, whose appearance we await.

### [1 Corinthians 15:51–54](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Cor15.51) (Tuesday — The Transformation)

- **"We shall all be changed":** The Greek verb *allagēsometha* — undergo transformation. The mortal body of the living saint will not merely be preserved but *transformed* into a resurrection body.
- **"In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye":** Greek *en atomō, en rhipē ophthalmou*. Instantaneous. No process, no gradual glorification during a rapture-tribulation era. One moment — from mortal to immortal.
- **"Death is swallowed up in victory":** Quotation of [Isaiah 25:8](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa25.8). Not merely defeated; swallowed up. The last enemy ([1 Cor. 15:26](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Cor15.26)) is not put on parole; it is destroyed.

### [Revelation 19:11–21](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev19.11) (Wednesday — Christ the Warrior)

- **The White Horse Rider:** Greek *hippos leukos*. The image is of Christ as conquering King, in stark contrast to [Rev. 6:2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev6.2)'s counterfeit white horse. Both are white horses; only one is Christ.
- **"His name is called The Word of God" (v. 13):** The pre-existent Logos ([John 1:1](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jn1.1)) — the same Word that was in the beginning with God is now the returning Warrior.
- **"Out of His mouth goes a sharp sword" (v. 15):** The word *rhomphaia* — a long sword. In [Rev. 1:16](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev1.16) it is the word of Christ. In [2 Thess. 2:8](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.2Thess2.8) it is "the breath of His mouth" that destroys the lawless one. The weapon is *speech* — the Word spoken *is* the weapon.
- **The wicked destroyed by His presence:** Not by military combat. By *appearance*. The wicked cannot survive proximity to the glorified Christ. His holiness is their annihilation. Grace could have made it their salvation.

### [Revelation 20:1–15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev20.1) (Wednesday — The Millennium)

- **The 1,000 years:** Greek *chilia etē*. The word "millennium" comes from Latin *mille* (thousand) + *annus* (year). Six times in [Revelation 20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev20) the thousand years appears. The number is significant.
- **The Adventist reading** — a *heavenly* millennium, not an earthly golden age:
  1. **At the Second Coming:** The righteous are raised (first resurrection, v. 4) or transformed and taken to heaven. The wicked are destroyed by the brightness of Christ's coming.
  2. **Satan is bound (vv. 1–3):** "Bound" with the *chain* of circumstance — no one alive on earth to deceive. Confined to the desolated planet ("bottomless pit" = *abyssos*, the chaos of an uninhabited world).
  3. **The saints reign with Christ in heaven (v. 4):** Not on earth; the earth is desolate. In heaven, participating in the *executive* judgment of the wicked ([1 Cor. 6:2–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Cor6.2)).
  4. **After 1,000 years:** The wicked dead are raised (second resurrection, vv. 5, 13). The New Jerusalem descends to earth ([Rev. 21:2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev21.2)). Satan gathers the wicked for one final assault (vv. 7–9).
  5. **The final judgment (vv. 11–15):** Great white throne. Books opened. Every knee bows. Every tongue confesses ([Phil. 2:10–11](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Phil2.10)). Then fire from heaven — *the second death* — destroys death, hell, and all who refused Christ.
- **Not a literal Christian golden age on earth:** Dispensational premillennialism reads the millennium as an earthly reign of Christ with resurrected saints during the tribulation. Adventist premillennialism reads it as *the interlude between the two resurrections*. The earth is desolate; the saints are in heaven; only Satan and the wicked dead are on earth (Satan alive; wicked dead until year 1,000).

### [Revelation 21:1–7](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev21.1) (Thursday — The New Creation)

- **"A new heaven and a new earth":** Greek *ouranon kainon kai gēn kainēn*. The word *kainos* is important — it means "new in quality," not merely "recent." Not a *different* earth but a *renewed* earth — the same creation, cleansed and restored.
- **"There was no more sea":** In Revelation's symbolism, the sea has represented turmoil, chaos, the peoples in tumult ([Rev. 13:1](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev13.1); [Rev. 17:15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev17.15)). The absence of the sea signifies the end of chaos.
- **"The tabernacle of God is with men, and He will dwell with them":** [Exod. 25:8](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Ex25.8) fulfilled — "let them make Me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them." The whole biblical drama has been aimed at *God dwelling with His creatures*. The sanctuary was a scale model; the new earth is the reality.
- **"God shall wipe away all tears... no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, nor pain":** Not merely the *absence* of these; the *positive* wiping away by God Himself. The consolation is personal.
- **"Behold, I make all things new":** Not "I make new things" but "I make *all things* new." The creation itself is renewed. Every atom of matter, every ecosystem, every relationship — all restored.

### [Isaiah 35](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa35) (Thursday — The Highway Home)

- **"The desert shall rejoice and blossom as the rose":** The creation itself is portrayed as *rejoicing*. Nature under the curse ([Rom. 8:19–22](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rom8.19)) is finally released.
- **"Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped... the lame man shall leap as a hart":** Physical restoration accompanies spiritual. Sin's marks on the body are removed.
- **"And a highway shall be there... called the way of holiness":** The redeemed have a *road home*. Not lost, not wandering — the road is prepared for them.
- **"Sorrow and sighing shall flee away":** The final line of the chapter. What has haunted human existence — the sighs of grief, the tears of loss — is not merely suppressed; it *flees*.

## 💡 Deep Discussion Questions

### On "This Same Jesus"

1. **[Acts 1:11](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Acts1.11) — "this same Jesus." Why does this phrase matter? What theological error is it protecting against? Have you ever encountered teaching about the Second Coming that subtly departs from this?**

2. **The One returning is the One who wept at Lazarus's tomb, ate with sinners, was crucified, rose bodily. Sit with the tenderness of that. The King is the Carpenter. What does that do to your fear of the coming?**

3. **[John 14:2–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jn14.2): "If I go... I will come again, and receive you unto Myself." The purpose of Christ's coming is not primarily to destroy the wicked — it is to *receive His people to Himself*. Have you thought of the Second Coming primarily as *rescue and reunion*, or primarily as *judgment*?**

### On the Visible, Global Return

4. **The pretribulational secret rapture is popular in modern American Christianity. It is exegetically incompatible with [Rev. 1:7](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev1.7) and [Matt. 24:30](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Matt24.30) — "every eye shall see Him." How would you explain the biblical case for a *visible, universal* return to a friend raised on Left Behind theology?**

5. **The lesson observes that the wicked will "flee, crying out for the mountains to fall on them" ([Rev. 6:15–17](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev6.15)). What subtle patterns of fleeing from God do you notice *now*, in yourself or in the culture? The pattern of the final crisis is being rehearsed daily.**

6. **The Second Coming is impossible to control, plan, or manipulate. It descends from heaven; we do not summon it. What does this teach about the limits of human religious project?** What does it teach about the *centrality of trust*?

### On the Resurrection and the Sleep of Death

7. **Adventist theology holds that the dead sleep unconsciously until the resurrection. They are not currently in heaven; they are awaiting Christ's call. How does this doctrine change your grief for those who have died in the Lord?**

8. **[1 Thess. 4:16–17](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Thess4.16): "the dead in Christ shall rise first." Then the living are gathered. Why does the *order* matter to Paul? What was he answering — what concern in the Thessalonian church?**

9. **The transformation is "in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye" ([1 Cor. 15:52](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Cor15.52)). Instantaneous. What is the significance that *no gradual glorification* precedes the change?**

### On the Millennium

10. **The Adventist reading places the millennium in *heaven*, with the earth desolate and Satan bound alone on it. This differs from both amillennialism (millennium is symbolic of the current church age) and dispensational premillennialism (millennium is a golden age on earth). What is the strongest exegetical evidence for the Adventist reading?**

11. **During the millennium, the saints participate in the *executive judgment* of the wicked ([1 Cor. 6:2–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Cor6.2)). What does that involvement teach — is it participation in condemnation, or is it *understanding* God's judgments so no accusation can ever be sustained against Him?**

12. **Adventism's millennium is not primarily about the saints reigning; it is about *God's justice being publicly verified*. The wicked are judged transparently, with the saints as witnesses. What does that tell you about how God cares about *the vindication of His character*?**

### On the Final Judgment and the New Earth

13. **After the millennium, the wicked are raised, gather with Satan for a final assault on the New Jerusalem, and are destroyed by fire from heaven ([Rev. 20:9–15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev20.9)). Every knee bows, every tongue confesses ([Phil. 2:10–11](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Phil2.10)) — including the lost, who *acknowledge God's justice* before their end. What is the pastoral weight of *even the lost confessing God's righteousness*?**

14. **The pastoral hard question: Sadie's parents died apparently unsaved. What can be said to a Christian grieving lost loved ones? The lesson gives no easy answer. What do you say, and what do you not say?**

15. **[Revelation 21](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev21) — the new earth — is not "heaven." It is *earth*, renewed. Adventist theology emphasizes this. What is the significance of God's plan being *this creation restored* rather than *a different creation elsewhere*?**

### On the Blessed Hope

16. **[Titus 2:13](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Titus2.13) calls it "the blessed hope." Is this the reigning hope in your Christian imagination, or has it faded to a doctrinal footnote?** What has replaced it?

17. **[Isaiah 35](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa35) describes a world in which "sorrow and sighing shall flee away." Sit with what that means — every sigh of grief, every weight of shame, every tear. What is the *first* thing you would say to Christ when the sorrow flees?**

18. **The Memory Text — "Come, Lord Jesus" — is the last prayer of Scripture. Is it a prayer you actually pray? What would change in your daily life if this were the prayer *behind* every other prayer?**

## ✍️ Ellen White Integration

### 1. On the anticipation of the new world

> *"As your senses delight in the attractive loveliness of the earth, think of the world that is to come, that shall never know the blight of sin and death; where the face of nature will no more wear the shadow of the curse. Let your imagination picture the home of the saved, and remember that it will be more glorious than your brightest imagination can portray."*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *Steps to Christ*, p. 86

**Context:** Ellen White urges believers to *imagine* the coming world. This is not idle daydreaming; it is spiritual formation. The imagination that has been shaped by the promise sustains faith through the present's difficulties. She insists the reality will exceed even the most vivid imagination.

**Discussion prompt:** How much of your imagination is *taken up* with the new earth? Do you regularly picture it, meditate on it, let its promise shape your endurance now? What would change if you did?

### 2. On eternity as ongoing learning

> *"There, immortal minds will contemplate with never-failing delight the wonders of creative power, the mysteries of redeeming love... There the grandest enterprises may be carried forward, the loftiest aspirations reached, the highest ambitions realized; and still there will arise new heights to surmount, new wonders to admire, new truths to comprehend, fresh objects to call forth the powers of mind and soul and body."*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *The Great Controversy*, p. 677

**Context:** Ellen White refuses a static image of heaven. Eternity is not a golden endless rest; it is *growth without weariness*. Every faculty developed. Every capacity increased. New heights, new wonders, new truths — forever.

**Discussion prompt:** What would you *do* in a life that never ends? Have you thought concretely about the projects, questions, and explorations you would pursue? Ellen White's vision of heaven is *active*, not passive. What does that reveal about God's design for you?

### 3. On the second coming as the deliverance

> *"Amid the shaking of the earth, the flash of lightning, and the roar of thunder, the voice of the Son of God calls forth the sleeping saints... The dying words of the man of God are, 'It is well with my soul.'"*
> — **Ellen G. White**, *The Great Controversy*, p. 645

**Context:** Ellen White describes the moment of resurrection with particular tenderness. The voice that once wept at Lazarus's tomb now calls forth every saint from the grave. The moment is one of joy so acute that even the dying words of the faithful — spoken through tears — become a completed testimony: *it is well*.

**Discussion prompt:** If your final words in this life were "It is well with my soul," what would that require of you *now*? What is God currently forming in you toward the readiness to speak those words?

## 🛠️ Practical Application

**Individual:**

1. **The imagination discipline.** For fifteen minutes each day this week, deliberately imagine the new earth. Use [Isaiah 35](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa35), [Revelation 21](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev21), and [Isaiah 65:17–25](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Isa65.17) as scaffolding. Notice what your imagination produces, and where it fails. Ellen White insists the reality will *exceed* even the most vivid imagining.

2. **"Come, Lord Jesus" as daily prayer.** End every prayer this week with the Memory Text — "Come, Lord Jesus" ([Rev. 22:20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev22.20)). Not as afterthought. As the *prayer behind* every other prayer. Notice how this reframes your requests.

3. **The reunion list.** Write down the names of loved ones who died in Christ. Not to grieve; to *anticipate*. These are the ones you will see again. This is not sentimental; it is *biblical*. The resurrection is family reunion.

**In Sabbath School:**

1. **The "in like manner" walk-through.** Read [Acts 1:9–11](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Acts1.9) slowly. Chart every detail of Christ's ascension. Then read [Matt. 24:30](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Matt24.30) and [Rev. 1:7](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev1.7) — every detail of His return. Notice the parallels. This is the doctrine of the visible parousia in one exercise.

2. **The millennium timeline.** Draw the Adventist millennium timeline on the board: Second Coming → first resurrection → 1,000 years in heaven → Satan bound on desolate earth → second resurrection → final judgment → fire from heaven → new earth. Discuss where each element is grounded in [Revelation 20](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Rev20).

3. **The consolation exercise.** Discuss how to speak comfort to a Christian who has lost a loved one who was not, apparently, a believer. What does the doctrine of conditional immortality allow us to say? What is beyond us to say?

**Intellectually:**

1. **The three millennial views.** Study amillennialism, postmillennialism, and premillennialism (both dispensational and historic). Understand why each view exists and what texts each reads primarily. Then be able to articulate the Adventist premillennial-heavenly-millennium reading and its strongest arguments.

2. **Conditional immortality and the sleep of the dead.** Read [Eccles. 9:5–6, 10](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Eccl9.5); [Ps. 6:5](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Ps6.5); [Ps. 115:17](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Ps115.17); [Ps. 146:4](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Ps146.4); [Job 14:12](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Job14.12); [Dan. 12:2](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Dan12.2); [John 5:28–29](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Jn5.28); [1 Thess. 4:13–17](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Thess4.13). Trace the coherent biblical doctrine of the unconscious sleep of the dead until resurrection. This is one of Adventism's most exegetically strong distinctive doctrines.

3. **Read *The Great Controversy* chapters 40–42.** Ellen White's treatment of "God's People Delivered," "Desolation of the Earth," and "The Controversy Ended" is the classic Adventist eschatological finale. Read prayerfully; do not race.

## 🧵 Theological Threads

- **"This same Jesus" as anchor:** The identity of the returning Christ is the ground of Christian hope. Not a symbol, not a redefined figure, not a spiritualized presence. The One who died for us, rose for us, ascended for us, is the One who returns for us.

- **The public visibility of the return:** Every eye. All the tribes. In like manner. The parousia is not an inside secret; it is a public accession. Any teaching that hides Christ's return misrepresents the Christian hope.

- **The sleep of the dead:** The saints who have died are not currently in heaven singing. They are *asleep in Christ*, awaiting the trumpet. This doctrine is a gospel doctrine — it protects assurance (we do not owe our loved ones' salvation), guards against spiritualism (no communications from the dead are possible), and dignifies the resurrection (Christ's own act, not a soul's own achievement).

- **The millennium as vindication:** Adventist millennium is not primarily about the saints reigning; it is about God's justice being *transparently verified* before the redeemed. The books are opened. Every case is examined. No accusation against God will finally stand.

- **The new earth as the culmination of [Genesis 3:15](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Gen3.15):** From the *protoevangelion* through Daniel's kingdoms and Revelation's beasts, the biblical narrative aims at *this*: the restored creation, God dwelling with His people, sin extinguished, death defeated. All prophecy is ordered here.

- **The blessed hope as present power:** The hope of Christ's return is not merely a future comfort; it is a *present* moral and spiritual power ([Titus 2:12–13](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.Titus2.12); [1 John 3:2–3](https://ref.ly/logosref/Bible.1Jn3.2)). Those who wait for Him *purify themselves*. Eschatology is ethics.

- **"Come, Lord Jesus" as the last word:** The Bible ends with a prayer. This is significant. The final canonical utterance is not a doctrine or a benediction but a *petition* — the church longing for her Lord. The Christian life culminates in longing that is answered.
