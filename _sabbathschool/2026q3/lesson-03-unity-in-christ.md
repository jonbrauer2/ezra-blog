## Central Theme

The problem of factionalism in Corinth was not primarily organizational — it was Christological. When a church organizes itself around human personalities ("I am of Paul," "I am of Apollos"), it displaces Christ from the center. Paul's appeal in 1 Corinthians 1–4 is not "can't we all just get along?" It is a theological argument: **the Cross makes cliques impossible, because the Cross is the great leveler of status, the destroyer of competitive pride, the source of a unity that no human leader can generate.**

The lesson moves from diagnosis (cliques as spiritual immaturity — *sarkinos*, fleshly, rather than *pneumatikos*, spiritual) to remedy (the mind of Christ, the servanthood of the Cross) to model (Paul's own suffering as the template for cruciform leadership). Unity in Christ is not achieved through management techniques or better communication — it is the fruit of a community genuinely centered on a crucified Lord.

## Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### 1 Corinthians 1:10 — *Katartizō* (United/Restored)
- Paul's appeal uses the verb *katartizō* — translated "be united" (ESV) or "be perfectly joined together" (NKJV). The word means to restore something to its proper condition (it is used for mending fishing nets in Matt. 4:21). Unity is not a new construction; it is the *restoration* of what the church was designed to be.
- **"Same mind (*nous*) and same judgment (*gnōmē*)":** Not intellectual uniformity — *nous* refers to the orientation of the will toward God; *gnōmē* is considered purpose. Paul is asking for alignment of fundamental commitments, not identical views on every matter.

### 1 Corinthians 1:12–13 — "Is Christ Divided?"
- The four parties — Paul, Apollos, Cephas, Christ — show that even claiming "Christ" as your exclusive party was a form of factionalism.
- **"Is Christ divided? (*memeristai*)":** The perfect tense asks whether Christ has been *permanently* split. The Corinthians' behavior suggested they were acting as if He had been.
- The two defining events of Christian identity — crucifixion and baptism — are both anchored in Christ alone. To pledge allegiance to a human leader is to misunderstand what you were saved by.

### 1 Corinthians 3:1–4 — *Sarkinos* vs. *Pneumatikos*
- **"Carnal (*sarkinos*)":** Made of flesh — raw material, not yet transformed. By 3:3, *sarkikos* — actively dominated by fleshly impulses.
- **"Milk ... solid food":** Mature Christians can handle the full weight of the Cross's implications — servanthood, suffering, death of self-promotion. The Corinthians' factionalism reveals they are still on milk.

### Philippians 2:5–8 — The Mind of Christ
- **"He emptied himself (*eauton ekenōsen*)":** The *kenōsis* — Christ's voluntary relinquishment of divine prerogatives. This is the paradigm for leadership: cruciform leaders pour themselves out.
- The descent is deliberate: from equality with God → human form → servanthood → death → the specific death of the cross. The opposite of every human social aspiration. This is the "mind" Christians are to have.

## Deep Discussion Questions

### On the Nature of Factionalism
1. **Paul identifies four parties, including one that says "I am of Christ." How can even a Christ-loyalty claim become a form of factionalism? What does it look like when people use devotion to Jesus as positioning above other believers?**

2. **Paul places *eris* (strife) in vice lists alongside murder and malice (Rom. 1:29). Does Adventist church culture take factionalism as seriously as Paul did? Where do we minimize it?**

3. **Contemporary equivalents of "I am of Paul": theologians, conference leaders, YouTube pastors, popular authors. What are the specific Adventist versions of this problem today?**

4. **Paul says the root of division is pride (1 Cor. 4:6). And pride is precisely what the Cross is designed to destroy. Where do people manage to hold the Cross intellectually while maintaining pride practically?**

### On Unity and Uniformity
5. ***Katartizō* implies unity is restoration, not new construction. What does this imply about the church's original design? What force disrupts it, and what restores it?**

6. **"Same mind and judgment" cannot mean identical opinions — Paul sharply disagreed with Peter at Antioch (Gal. 2:11). What is the difference between the "same mind" Paul calls for and intellectual conformity?**

7. **What is the theological marker distinguishing a healthy small group from a clique? When does community become faction?**

### On Spiritual Maturity and Leadership
8. **The Corinthians had abundant spiritual gifts (1 Cor. 1:5–7) but were spiritually immature. How is it possible to have remarkable gifts while remaining dangerously immature? What does this imply about the relationship between gifts and character?**

9. **Is there a form of Adventist theological sophistication — knowing doctrine deeply — that is actually a refined version of immaturity, resistant to Cross-shaped living?**

10. **Paul defines leadership as stewardship (*oikonomos* — manager of someone else's property). Leaders do not own the church. How does this reshape authority and expectations?**

### On Christlike Servanthood and Suffering
11. **In your local church context, what would it look like for a board of elders or worship team to have "the mind of Christ" (Phil. 2:5) in their decision-making?**

12. **Paul's credential for apostolic authenticity is his suffering (2 Cor. 11:23–28). Contemporary ministry often uses opposite credentials — attendance growth, social media following. How do we evaluate this tension?**

13. **"Imitate me" (1 Cor. 4:16) — how can a leader legitimately say this without becoming a cult of personality? What distinguishes modeling from idolizing?**

### On Unity's Witness
14. **EGW: unity "carries powerful conviction to the world." If true, what does Adventist disunity communicate? How seriously should we take denominational division as a missional problem?**

15. **EGW says the enemy is "determined that unity shall not exist." How do you recognize the specific tactics of disunity in a local congregation — theological, relational, or structural?**

16. **"Leaders who display a lifestyle that reflects the Cross are worthy of being heard and followed." By that standard, how do we evaluate leaders with correct theology but self-serving power?**

### Harder Questions
17. **Colossians 1:24 says Paul "fills up in his flesh what is lacking in Christ's afflictions." This is one of the most controversial Pauline verses. What does he mean? Is Christ's atoning work incomplete? How does this relate to the church as Christ's body continuing His ministry?**

18. **Paul's credential list in 2 Corinthians 11 inverts every cultural marker of leadership success. Is there a cost-free version of cruciform ministry, or does genuine Cross-shaped leadership always produce some form of social cost?**

## Practical Application

**Individual:** Identify one relationship in your church community that has fractured around a personality conflict or leadership dispute. What would it mean to be a *katartizō* agent — a restorer of what should have been?

**In Sabbath School:** Before class, name someone in the broader church with whom your group might be in tension. Pray for them by name. This is a small act of *katartizō*.

**Intellectually:** Read Philippians 2:1–11 as a unity text — how does the Incarnation and Cross generate humility? Does doctrine actually produce virtue, or does it just provide language for virtues formed elsewhere?

## Theological Threads

- **Unity as Christological:** The church's unity is grounded in the singular Lordship of Christ — making human faction-formation a theological category error.
- **Kenosis as leadership model:** Phil. 2:5–8 provides the template for all Christian leadership — downward mobility, self-emptying, suffering as credential.
- **Gifts vs. character:** Corinth demonstrates that spiritual gifts and spiritual maturity are not the same thing.
- **Suffering as apostolic identity:** 2 Corinthians 11 frames Paul's suffering as participation in Christ's afflictions — not punishment but identification.
- **Unity's eschatological dimension:** EGW frames unity as part of the remnant's platform before the close of probation. Division has end-time dimensions.

## Lesson Summary

**Core assertion:** Unity in the church is the fruit of communities genuinely centered on a crucified Christ. Wherever human leaders displace Christ from the center, factionalism is inevitable; wherever the Cross reorders all relationships, unity becomes possible.

**Key tensions:**
1. Unity ↔ Uniformity
2. Spiritual gifts ↔ Spiritual maturity
3. Following leaders ↔ Idolizing leaders
4. Unity as pastoral ideal ↔ Unity as missional necessity

**The bottom line:** "I am of Paul" and "I am of Apollos" are ancient versions of every contemporary denominational and congregational tribalism. Paul's answer is not a better organizational structure — it is a deeper encounter with the Christ who cannot be divided, whose Cross makes boasting impossible, and whose Spirit produces the only unity the world finds credible.
