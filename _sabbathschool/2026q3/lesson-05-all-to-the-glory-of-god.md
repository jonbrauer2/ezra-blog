## Central Theme

Idolatry and sexual immorality are repeatedly linked in the New Testament because they share the same root: the displacement of God from the center of one's allegiance. This lesson's governing question: **What does it mean to do "all to the glory of God," and how does that comprehensive orientation protect against every form of idolatry?**

Paul's approach to the idol-food controversy is not primarily about diet — it is about the two great commandments. Love for God above all eliminates idolatry; love for others as oneself prevents the misuse of knowledge and freedom. The synthesis is 1 Corinthians 10:31: *everything* — eating, drinking, every decision about lawful but not-always-expedient actions — is either an act of worship toward God or a form of idolatry. There is no neutral territory.

## Key Passages — Exegetical Notes

### 1 Corinthians 8:1–3 — Knowledge That "Puffs Up" vs. Love That "Builds Up"
- **"Knowledge puffs up (*physioi*), but love builds up (*oikodomeō*)":** *Physioi* — same root as the factionalists of chapter 4 and the scandal-tolerators of chapter 5. Inflated theological pride creates the same disorder as every other form of pride.
- **"If anyone loves God, he is known by God":** The passive is striking — genuine knowledge involves being *known* by God, not merely knowing things about God. The primary move is God's knowing of us.
- **Connection to Deuteronomy 6:4–5:** Paul grounds monotheism and the first great commandment in the same passage. Knowledge of God's unity must produce love for God. Intellectual acknowledgment of monotheism without love is not enough.

### 1 Corinthians 10:1–13 — Israel as Warning
- **"All were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea" (v. 2):** Typological parallels: cloud and sea = baptism; manna and water = Lord's Supper; the Rock = Christ. Same sacramental realities, same spiritual vulnerability.
- **"These things took place as examples (*typoi*) for us" (v. 6):** *Typos* — a pattern. Israel's wilderness failure is typologically prescriptive for the church. Spiritual privilege does not guarantee spiritual perseverance.
- **"Let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall" (v. 12):** Warning directed at the theologically confident "strong." Overconfidence in theological sophistication is itself a form of pride that precedes a fall.

### 1 Corinthians 10:14–22 — The Communion Logic of Idolatry
- **"A participation (*koinōnia*) in the blood of Christ" (v. 16):** The Lord's Supper as *koinōnia* — fellowship, participation, communion with Christ's body and blood. The most explicit Pauline statement about the Supper's meaning.
- **"You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons" (v. 21):** Participatory acts create bonds of allegiance. Eating at a pagan temple feast is not merely dietary — it is spiritual participation in an alternative worship system.

### 1 Corinthians 10:23–31 — "All to the Glory of God"
- **"All things are lawful, but not all things are helpful; not all things build up" (v. 23):** The criterion shifts from "is this permitted?" to "does this glorify God and build up others?"
- **"Do all to the glory of God" (v. 31):** The ultimate ethical criterion — broader than any specific rule. Everything is either an act of worship directed toward God's glory or a displacement of that glory (cf. Isa. 42:8).

## Deep Discussion Questions

### On Knowledge and Love
1. **The "strong" in Corinth had correct theology — idols aren't real gods — but used it in a way that destroyed weaker brothers. Can you be theologically right in a way that damages the church? What does this say about the relationship between correct doctrine and practical love?**

2. **"If anyone thinks he knows something, he does not yet know as he ought to know" (8:2). Awareness of ignorance as a mark of genuine knowledge. How does this apply to theological confidence in a Sabbath School class?**

3. **"If anyone loves God, he is known by God" (8:3). The primary relationship is God's knowledge of us. How does this reframe how we talk about assurance? What is the difference between "I know God" and "I am known by God"?**

4. **Paul connects monotheism (Deut. 6:4) directly to the first great commandment (Deut. 6:5). Where do you see Adventists holding correct monotheistic theology without it producing the love for God that Deuteronomy commands?**

### On Self-Denial and Gospel Contextualization
5. **Paul relinquishes three legitimate rights for the sake of the gospel's advance. What legitimate rights might contemporary Adventist ministers or missionaries need to relinquish for effective ministry? Examples from your community?**

6. **"I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some" (9:22). What is the distinction between adapting *form* for the gospel and compromising *content*? Where is the line?**

7. **Paul's self-discipline (9:27 — "lest after preaching to others I myself should be disqualified") suggests even apostles can be disqualified. What does disqualification look like, and what does this say about assurance and perseverance?**

### On Israel as Warning
8. **Israel had the same sacramental privileges Christians have — yet "God was not pleased with most of them" (10:5). How should the church today read Israel's wilderness failures? What are the contemporary parallels to the golden calf or Baal of Peor?**

9. **"Let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall" (10:12) is directed at the theologically confident. In what ways does theological sophistication create false security against moral failure?**

10. **"God will not let you be tempted beyond your ability, but with the temptation will also provide the way of escape" (10:13). In context this is a call to vigilance. What is the "way of escape" — is it automatic, or does it require something from us?**

### On Idolatry and Worship
11. **Paul says pagan idol-feasts involve "participation with demons" (10:20) even though "an idol has no real existence" (8:4). How do you hold both statements together? Is there real spiritual danger in participating in forms of worship that are theologically empty?**

12. **EGW says idolatry includes "self-serving, love of ease, the gratification of appetite and passion." If idolatry is this broad — anything that displaces God from the center — how do you know if something you care about has become an idol? What are the diagnostic signs?**

13. **The rich young ruler is Paul's example of an idolater: he loved possessions more than God or neighbor. What is the specific idol you are most at risk of?**

### On "All to the Glory of God"
14. **"Do all to the glory of God" (10:31). Is this an intention you attach to actions already decided, or does it shape which actions you choose? How does it actually work in practice?**

15. **Paul's "all lawful, not all expedient" principle means Christians live not at "what is permitted" but "what glorifies God and builds up others." How does this change how a Sabbath School class approaches borderline ethical questions?**

16. **"Give no offense to Jews or to Greeks or to the church of God" (10:32). Three different constituencies. What do you do when avoiding offense to one group causes offense to another?**

### Harder Question
17. **Paul's typological reading of Israel's wilderness experience maps the cloud, sea, manna, and water from the Rock onto Christian sacraments. What are the hermeneutical rules for typological interpretation? When does typology illuminate, and when does it impose meaning?**

## Practical Application

**Individual:** Identify one thing in your life that is lawful but may not be helpful — to your own communion with God or your witness to others. Apply the test: "Does doing this glorify God?" If not, what would relinquishing it look like?

**In Sabbath School:** Paul's principle: what builds up others takes precedence over what I am entitled to. Discuss one thing your class could give up — not because it is wrong, but because it is not expedient for the spiritual health of someone present.

**Intellectually:** Read 1 Corinthians 10:1–13 as a typological argument. Paul maps Israel's wilderness experience onto the Corinthian church. What does this typological method reveal about how Paul reads the Old Testament?

## Theological Threads

- **The two great commandments as the antidote to idolatry:** Love for God (Deut. 6:4–5) prevents idolatry; love for neighbor (Lev. 19:18) prevents the misuse of freedom.
- **Knowledge without love as pride:** The "strong" had correct theology but used it to build themselves up. Theological sophistication as a status marker is its own form of factionalism.
- **The Lord's Supper as defining communion:** 1 Cor. 10:16 defines the Supper as *koinōnia* — exclusive allegiance to Christ that makes simultaneous communion with competing spiritual powers impossible.
- **Typology as hermeneutic:** Paul's reading of Israel's wilderness as *typos* establishes that Old Testament narrative is prescriptive, not merely descriptive, for the church.
- **The glory principle as comprehensive ethics:** "Do all to the glory of God" is the supreme criterion that shapes which decisions are made, not just how they are carried out.

---

## Lesson Summary

**Core assertion:** Idolatry is anything that displaces God from the center of one's allegiance and worship. The antidote is the double love commandment, synthesized in 1 Corinthians 10:31: "Do all to the glory of God."

**Key tensions:**
1. Knowledge ↔ Love
2. Christian freedom ↔ Responsibility to the weak
3. Spiritual privilege ↔ Spiritual vulnerability
4. Lawful ↔ Expedient

**The bottom line:** Eating, drinking, how you use your legitimate rights, and how you treat those weaker in faith are all acts of worship or acts of idolatry. There is no neutral territory. Every decision is a statement about whom you serve.
