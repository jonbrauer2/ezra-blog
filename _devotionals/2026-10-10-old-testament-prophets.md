Nobody sets out on an unfamiliar journey without guidance anymore. A GPS does three things for the traveler: it tells you where you actually are, where you are going, and it warns you of hazards on the road ahead. Take away any one of those three, and even the best route becomes a guess.

Spiritual travelers need the same three mercies — and this week a new lesson opens on how God supplied them. Long before satellites, the Lord ran a heavenly positioning system for His people: the Old Testament prophets. In their narratives, counsels, prophecies, poetry, and songs, God told His people where they really stood, where they were headed, and what dangers lay ahead if they kept driving the way they were going. *"Believe in the Lord your God, and you shall be established; believe His prophets, and you shall prosper"* (2 Chronicles 20:20, NKJV). King Jehoshaphat spoke those words with a vast army marching toward him — and he had only just learned how nearly fatal it is to trust four hundred flattering court prophets over one Micaiah (1 Kings 22, this week's Monday reading).

Here is what makes the system worth trusting: the prophets were not celestial super-beings. *"Elijah was a man subject to like passions as we are"* (James 5:17, NKJV) — fresh from fire on Carmel one day, hiding under a juniper tree asking to die the next. This week we will meet them all: the discouraged Elijah, the imprisoned Micaiah, Miriam and Deborah and Huldah, Jeremiah watching a nation choose pleasant lies over hard truth. Frail, frightened, faithful people — through whom heaven guided travelers all the way home.

Ellen White saw the pattern clearly: *"Little heed is given to the Bible, and the Lord has given a lesser light to lead men and women to the greater light."*—Ellen G. White, *Selected Messages*, book 1, p. 78. That is the mark of every true prophetic voice, ancient or modern: it never points at itself. Like the silver pointer resting on the scroll, the prophetic gift exists to guide the reader's eye to the Word — and through the Word to Christ, the greater Light.

A positioning system, of course, only helps the traveler who turns it on. God's voice through the prophets still marks where we are, where we are going, and what lies ahead. Somewhere in your life this week, heaven's guidance is crossing your preferences — a warning you'd rather ignore, a redirect you'd rather resist. Will you believe His prophets and prosper, or keep driving and recalculate?
