There are sins Paul tells us to resist, and there are sins Paul tells us to flee. The difference matters. Some temptations you can face down. Others you have to get out of the room.

*"Flee sexual immorality."* (1 Corinthians 6:18, NKJV) The Greek is *pheugete* — the same verb used for a person running from a fire, an army, a wild animal. Paul is not asking for a managed response. He is asking for active, physical distance. Do not stand there and reason with it. Do not try to outnegotiate it. Move.

The paradigm case is Joseph. Potiphar's wife has cornered him — day after day, and finally in an empty house. And the text is precise about what Joseph does. *"He left his garment in her hand, and fled and went outside."* (Genesis 39:12, NKJV) He did not lecture her. He did not calmly explain his convictions. He got out — so quickly that he left his coat behind. Losing the coat was a small price. Staying would have cost far more.

To the Thessalonians Paul makes the theological ground plain. *"For this is the will of God, your sanctification: that you should abstain from sexual immorality; that each of you should know how to possess his own vessel in sanctification and honor, not in passion of lust, like the Gentiles who do not know God."* (1 Thessalonians 4:3-5, NKJV) There is a "will of God" question people spend years agonizing over. On this specific point, Paul says the will of God is not mystery. God's will is your sanctification, and this is what that includes.

Notice the phrase: *possess his own vessel.* Self-possession, in Paul's framing, is a gift of the Spirit. It is not natural willpower. It is something you receive by grace and exercise by practice — the ability to be at home in your own body without being ruled by its appetites. Proverbs 5:8 gives the tactical version: *"Remove your way far from her, and do not go near the door of her house."* (NKJV) Do not walk down that street. Do not test the boundary.

Fleeing is not cowardice. It is wisdom that knows the enemy's strength and refuses to fight on his terms. The apostle who told slaves to stand fast under injustice, the apostle who told Christians to face wild beasts in the arena, is the same apostle telling us to run from this. He is not making a psychological point. He is describing what the wise do when they have understood the terrain.

*Ellen White* writes: *"It is a law of the mind that it gradually adapts itself to the subjects upon which it is trained to dwell."* (Great Controversy, p. 555) The mind that lingers where it should have fled is being reshaped by the lingering.

What have you been trying to manage that you actually need to flee — physically, digitally, relationally? Fleeing is not failure. It is faith in disguise, wearing running shoes.

—

*Ezra*
