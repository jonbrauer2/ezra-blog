The trouble at Hazeroth began as a family complaint. Miriam and Aaron had watched their brother carry a nation on his shoulders, and it started to grate: "Has the LORD indeed spoken only through Moses? Has He not spoken through us also?" (Numbers 12:2, NKJV). God's answer came swiftly, and it was less a verdict on their status than a lesson in method: "If there is a prophet among you, I, the LORD, make Myself known to him in a vision; I speak to him in a dream" (Numbers 12:6, NKJV). Notice what God explains. Not only *that* He speaks — *how*: visions, dreams, channels of grace reaching real people.

And the channels run wider than Miriam imagined. Sabbath's devotional glimpsed the variety in passing — Isaiah's poetry, Luke's research, John's eyewitness fire. Monday's lesson slows down on the point. Isaiah was wide awake for his commissioning, undone in a throne room (Isaiah 6:1, 8). John wrote from what he had seen, heard, and handled of the Word of life (1 John 1:1–3). Luke arrived by a road few prophets ever took — careful, Spirit-guided research: "having had perfect understanding of all things from the very first... to write to you an orderly account" (Luke 1:1–3, NKJV). There is no single template for how heaven speaks.

But there is a single Breath. "All Scripture is given by inspiration of God" (2 Timothy 3:16, NKJV). Paul's word there is a coinage found nowhere else in the New Testament — *theopneustos*: God-breathed. Scripture is not humanity's reach toward God; it is God's exhalation through human instruments. And the lesson presses the process one detail further: the Spirit's assistance covers not only receiving the message but communicating it clearly. The messages were objective, not subjective — not to be tampered with or distorted, even when they came out dressed in the writer's own vocabulary, style, and personality. One Breath, many instruments, one music.

The Introduction to The Great Controversy — where Sabbath's devotional found its words about human hands and divine authorship — carries the process a step further: *"God has been pleased to communicate His truth to the world by human agencies, and He Himself, by His Holy Spirit, qualified men and enabled them to do this work."*—Ellen G. White, *The Great Controversy*, Introduction. The Spirit did not merely hand off a message; He qualified the messenger. And He still does.

Monday's lesson is honest about the mystery: many questions remain about how inspiration actually works. So why trust the Book? For the same reason you trust your lungs. You may not understand respiration, but you breathe — and live. The Word asks the same confidence: breathed out by God for doctrine, reproof, correction, instruction, "that the man of God may be complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work" (2 Timothy 3:17, NKJV).

So open the Book today expecting more than information. This is breath on paper — the same Breath that hovered over creation's waters and filled the upper room. Read it until it reads you. And when someone asks how words that old can still be a living voice, you hold Paul's one-word answer: God-breathed.
