The Greek word Paul uses for sincerity — *eilikrineia* — is worth pausing over. It means, literally, tested by sunlight. Something that has nothing to hide when the light hits it directly. In the ancient world, potters would sometimes fill hairline cracks in their vessels with wax to disguise the flaw. When the pot went out into direct sun, the wax melted and the crack showed. Sincere goods were the ones without wax. What you saw in the light was what you got.

Paul's boast, when he defends himself against his critics, is not that he was gifted or persuasive. It is this. *"For our boasting is this: the testimony of our conscience that we conducted ourselves in the world in simplicity and godly sincerity, not with fleshly wisdom but by the grace of God."* (2 Corinthians 1:12, NKJV)

Two words, both about transparency. *Simplicity* — an undivided intent, no hidden agenda, one thing going on inside the person you meet. *Sincerity* — nothing that would melt if the sun hit it. Paul is saying: I am the same man in private that I am in the pulpit, in front of my critics as behind their backs.

He puts the contrast bluntly in the next chapter. *"For we are not, as so many, peddling the word of God."* (2 Corinthians 2:17, NKJV) Peddling — the language of a salesman working the crowd, adjusting the pitch to whichever pocket is closest. The temptation in ministry, and in every Christian conversation, is to package the message for approval. To soften the parts that would cost us listeners. To perform the version of ourselves the room would applaud.

Paul says his standard is different. *"By manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God."* (2 Corinthians 4:2, NKJV) The audience is not the room. The audience is God, and behind God, the conscience of the person listening. That is a much more demanding — and much freer — place to speak from.

Because here is what happens when you stop performing for the room: you stop needing the room's verdict. You can say the true thing without checking how it lands. You can lose an argument without losing yourself. You can walk into a hard conversation and come out on the other side with your soul intact, whether or not you were understood.

Ellen White writes: *"There must be no deception, no false coloring. Christ demands from His followers a truthful representation of His character."*

Is there anywhere in your walk with Christ where you are performing rather than living? Some part of your life that is filled with wax where there ought to be honest clay? What would sunlight-tested sincerity look like there this week?
