Someone in Corinth was asking a hard question. What kind of body do the dead come back in? The imagination stalls. Paul does not brush the question aside. He answers it with a farmer's illustration.

*"Foolish one, what you sow is not made alive unless it dies."* (1 Corinthians 15:36, NKJV) You know this. You have seen it every spring. The grain the farmer plants and the plant that rises are the same organism and yet nothing alike. The kernel goes down small and hard and closed. The plant comes up green and open, moving in the wind. Same life, radically transfigured shape.

That, Paul says, is the pattern of resurrection. Same person, radically transfigured shape. He builds it out in four contrasts. *"It is sown in corruption, it is raised in incorruption; it is sown in dishonor, it is raised in glory; it is sown in weakness, it is raised in power; it is sown a natural body, it is raised a spiritual body."* (1 Corinthians 15:42-44, NKJV)

Notice what a "spiritual body" is not. It is not immaterial. The disciples touched the risen Jesus. He ate broiled fish in front of them. He carried the marks in His hands. A spiritual body, in Paul's vocabulary, is a body animated and shaped by the Spirit — not a ghost. Real, tangible, personal — but freed from the corruption that undoes everything real in this age.

Paul gives us the pattern by name a chapter later: *"Who will transform our lowly body that it may be conformed to His glorious body, according to the working by which He is able even to subdue all things to Himself."* (Philippians 3:21, NKJV) The pattern is not our imagination of what we would like the future to be. The pattern is Him.

Which changes how you view the body you inhabit right now. It is a seed. It is not a shell to escape. It is not an idol to preserve. It is not something to be ashamed of. It is a kernel that will go down and come up again — the same you, but freed and full and finished.

Ellen White writes: *"The same power that raised Christ from the dead will raise His church, and glorify it with Christ, as His bride, above all principalities, above all powers, above every name that is named, not only in this world, but also in the heavenly courts, the world above."*

If your body is a seed of what will be raised, you can afford to neither despise it nor idolize it. You can care for it as a farmer cares for planted grain — attentively, hopefully, and without confusion about what it is going to become.

What would change today if you carried your body with resurrection hope?

*Ezra*
