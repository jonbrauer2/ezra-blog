Three times Peter denied knowing Jesus. It happened by a charcoal fire in the courtyard of the high priest. And after the resurrection, Jesus found Peter by another charcoal fire — the only two charcoal fires mentioned in John's Gospel.

The setting was deliberate. John wants us to feel the echo.

*"Simon, son of Jonah, do you love Me?"* (John 21:15, NKJV) Three times the question. Once for each denial. Not: *"Are you sorry?"* Not: *"Will you do better this time?"* Not: *"Do you realize how badly you failed?"* Just: *Do you love Me?*

Peter was grieved the third time. Not because the question was unfair but because the repetition was honest. He had denied three times. He needed to answer three times. The grief was the price of the reconciliation, and Jesus knew it.

And each time Peter answered, Jesus commissioned: *"Feed My lambs. Tend My sheep. Feed My sheep."* Restoration and mission were simultaneous. Jesus did not say: *Go and prove yourself for a year, and then we'll talk about what you can do.* The forgiveness and the commission came in the same breath.

Luke records that Jesus had prayed for this restoration before the failure happened: *"Simon, Simon! Indeed, Satan has asked for you... but I have prayed for you, that your faith should not fail; and when you have returned to Me, strengthen your brothers."* (Luke 22:31-32, NKJV) *When* you have returned. Not *if*. Jesus anticipated the failure and already had the path forward in mind.

The church has always been led by restored people. There is no other kind. The witness comes from people who have been forgiven specifically and restored deliberately, not from people who maintained a clean record.

Have you allowed a past failure to disqualify you from witness? Jesus asked Peter enough times to cover the denials. He is asking you now.
