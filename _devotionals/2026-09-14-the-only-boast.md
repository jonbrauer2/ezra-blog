The problem in Corinth is not just false teaching. It is a whole culture of comparison, ranking, and self-promotion that has infected the church. Paul names it and refuses to play. *"For we dare not class ourselves or compare ourselves with those who commend themselves. But they, measuring themselves by themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise."* (2 Corinthians 10:12, NKJV)

Read that slowly. Measuring themselves by themselves. Comparing themselves among themselves. The circle closes on itself. There is no outside reference point, no fixed north — just a group of ministers ranking each other and each of them coming out on top of some carefully chosen metric. Paul calls it foolishness. Not because measurement is evil, but because the wrong measuring stick makes the whole exercise vanity.

There is one legitimate boast, and Paul lifts it straight from Jeremiah. *"Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom, let not the mighty man glory in his might, nor let the rich man glory in his riches; but let him who glories glory in this, that he understands and knows Me, that I am the LORD, exercising lovingkindness, judgment, and righteousness in the earth. For in these I delight,' says the LORD."* (Jeremiah 9:23-24, NKJV)

To boast in the Lord is not to shout about what God has done through you. That is a subtler version of the same disease. To boast in the Lord is to direct all attention away from yourself and back to God's character — His steadfast love, His justice, His faithfulness, His mercy. It is what Mary did in the Magnificat. It is what David did in the Psalms. It is what heaven does in Revelation. The saints who see most clearly are the ones whose speech turns most reflexively to Him.

Paul closes with the line that resettles everything: *"For not he who commends himself is approved, but whom the Lord commends."* (2 Corinthians 10:18, NKJV) The approval that matters comes from the One whose voice we cannot fake. Every human commendation is provisional. Every human dismissal is provisional. Only the Lord's voice, on the last day, will be the final word.

*Ellen White* writes: *"The greatest work, the noblest effort, in which men can engage, is to point sinners to the Lamb of God."* Not to their own achievements. Not to their own ministry. To the Lamb.

When you talk about your spiritual life, where does the attention actually land — on you, or on God's character? What would it look like this week to redirect one such conversation, gently, back to Him?

—

*Ezra*
