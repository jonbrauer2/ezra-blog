The same message. Two entirely different receptions. To one hearer, foolishness — nothing to see, nothing to hold. To another, the power of God, and everything begins to reorganize.

*"But to us who are being saved it is the power of God."* (1 Corinthians 1:18, NKJV) The verb tense matters. Not "who have been saved," as though it were a finished transaction and now we are simply waiting. *Being saved.* A rescue in progress. The Cross is not only the moment of pardon — it is the ongoing pressure that keeps working on us long after we first believed.

Paul widens the frame even further in Colossians. Through the blood of His cross God reconciled *"all things, whether things on earth or things in heaven."* (Colossians 1:20, NKJV) All things. The Cross is not a private religious symbol; it is a cosmic event. Something happened at Calvary that reaches into corners of creation you and I have never seen.

Peter, writing later, picks up the shame of the tree and reframes it: *"who Himself bore our sins in His own body on the tree."* (1 Peter 2:24, NKJV) The word he chooses for tree is *xylon* — the same word the Greek Old Testament used for the accursed hanging in Deuteronomy 21. Peter knows what he is doing. He is deliberately taking the place of curse and showing that it has become the place of blessing. The instrument of judgment has become the instrument of liberation. The tree of shame has become the tree of sanctification.

That is what makes Paul unashamed. *"I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, for it is the power of God to salvation for everyone who believes."* (Romans 1:16, NKJV) Power, not persuasion. Reality, not rhetoric. The Cross does something. It does not merely propose something.

And the doing is not confined to forgiveness. The Cross does not stop when it clears the record. It keeps going — into the temper, the appetites, the ambitions, the relationships. It reconciles. It reorders. It disassembles the parts of you that resisted, and rebuilds on a different foundation.

Where in you is the reconciling still happening? What is the blood of the Cross touching this week that you had almost given up on?
