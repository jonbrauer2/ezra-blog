On the last night before the Cross, Jesus prayed. Not for Himself alone. Not only for the disciples in the room. He prayed for the ones who would believe later, through the disciples' word — which includes you.

And the specific thing He prayed for is worth reading slowly.

*"That they all may be one, as You, Father, are in Me, and I in You; that they also may be one in Us, that the world may believe that You sent Me."* (John 17:21, NKJV)

Notice the standard. Not that we would get along. Not that our congregational meetings would be pleasant. Not that our theological differences would be polite. Jesus prayed that we would be one *as* the Father is in Him and He is in the Father — the ontological bond between the Persons of the Godhead as the pattern for the love between believers. That is a staggering standard. And then He gave the reason: *that the world may believe.*

Unity is the world's evidence. Not eloquence, not miracles, not marketing — unity. The one thing the outside is watching for, and the one thing that will tell them Jesus really was sent by the Father, is whether the people who claim His name love each other in a way that cannot be explained by anything but Him.

Paul repeats the practical version. *"Endeavoring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace."* (Ephesians 4:3, NKJV) The verb is *spoudazontes* — active, urgent effort. Unity does not just happen. It has to be tended. The tear in the net does not repair itself.

To the Colossians he adds the wardrobe metaphor. *"But above all these things put on love, which is the bond of perfection."* (Colossians 3:14, NKJV) Love is the outer garment that holds every other virtue together. Kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering — the whole set stays coordinated only when love is worn on top.

Where the Cross is truly received, competitive boasting collapses. That is the engine of unity. Not better conflict-management technique — a truer view of Calvary. The person who has actually seen the Cross has less to defend, less to prove, less to boast about. And that is precisely the person able to be joined to the person next to them without friction.

*Ellen White* writes: *"When the members of the church are united in this work, then, and not until then, will they be filled with the Spirit of Christ."* (Testimonies, vol. 7, p. 30) The filling and the uniting are the same event, seen from different angles.

If someone watching your community came to a conclusion about Jesus based only on the way you love one another, what would they conclude? Where can you take a small step of *katartizō* — restoring what has been torn — this weekend? One small repair is a small proclamation of a Father who sent His Son.

—

*Ezra*
