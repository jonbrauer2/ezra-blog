When Moses came down from Sinai, his face shone. He had been that close to God, and the glory rubbed off on him. When he spoke with the people, he covered his face with a veil.

There is a detail Paul notices that most of us miss. Moses veiled the glory not because it was too bright but because it was fading. He did not want Israel to see the diminishing. The moment ended, and the light with it, and the veil hid the fact.

Paul makes that ancient veil a picture of something more permanent. *"Their minds were blinded. For until this day the same veil remains unlifted in the reading of the Old Testament, because the veil is taken away in Christ."* (2 Corinthians 3:14, NKJV) The veil is not on the text. The veil is on the heart. Something between the reader and the light of God. *"Nevertheless when one turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away."* (2 Corinthians 3:16, NKJV)

Then the sentence that pulls the whole chapter forward. *"But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord."* (2 Corinthians 3:18, NKJV)

Notice the mechanism. The transformation is not manufactured. It happens by beholding. Sustained, unveiled looking at the Lord. And what you look at, you become like. That is the operating principle of the whole spiritual life. Not white-knuckle effort. Not the anxious rearranging of behavior. Turning the face toward the Lord and letting the light do its work.

Which is why it matters what your attention actually rests on. Not what your stated priorities say. What does your gaze return to when nobody is watching? What runs in the background of your mind while you drive, while you scroll, while you fall asleep? That is what is forming you.

The screens we look at are forming us. The people we admire are forming us. The futures we rehearse are forming us. Nothing you gaze at leaves you unchanged. The only question is whether the changes are into an image you would want to become.

Paul's answer is that the Lord is the one image safe to gaze at without limit. Look at Him, and you become like Him. Look at Him for years, and the change is slow enough that you cannot see it, but it is happening. Glory to glory. Not from your effort but from His light.

Ellen White writes: *"By beholding we become changed. As the mind dwells upon the character of Christ, the man himself is transformed into the same likeness."*

What are you looking at most in your ordinary life? Not what you think about — what does your attention actually rest on? That is what is forming you. What would it look like to turn the face today?
