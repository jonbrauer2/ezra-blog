Isaiah describes the source of a witnessing tongue in terms that are easy to miss: *"The Lord GOD has given me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him who is weary."* (Isaiah 50:4, NKJV)

Pause on the next line: *"He awakens me morning by morning; He awakens my ear to hear as the learned."*

The tongue comes from the ear. The ear comes from the morning. The readiness to speak a word in season is the accumulated result of mornings spent listening. You cannot give what you have not received. You cannot speak to the weary with words that have depth if the depth was never cultivated.

Peter says something similar: *"Always be ready to give a defense to everyone who asks you a reason for the hope that is in you, with meekness and fear."* (1 Peter 3:15, NKJV) The word *defense* is *apologia* — not a courtroom argument but an honest explanation. Someone has looked at your life and seen something they can't account for by ordinary means. They want to know what it is. You need to be able to say.

But notice the pre-conditions Peter sets for this readiness. Immediately before, in the same passage, he describes a way of living: blameless, peaceful, turning from evil, doing good, seeking peace. The life is the context that makes the word credible. A defense of the hope is only as compelling as the observable hope behind it.

And the word *always* is the demanding part. Not ready when convenient. Not ready when the conversation moves to spiritual topics. Always. Which means the readiness is a posture maintained by daily cultivation, not an emergency state activated when the moment arrives.

Is your witness currently outrunning your cultivation? Is there something you are speaking about that you have stopped spending mornings listening to?

The tongue comes from the ear, and the ear from the morning. Start there.
