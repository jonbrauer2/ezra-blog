Jesus saved His most solemn warning not for skeptics but for the faithful. *"For false christs and false prophets will rise and show great signs and wonders to deceive, if possible, even the elect"* (Matthew 24:24, NKJV). Read it slowly. The deception He warns about is not cheap — it will be supernatural, miracles and signs and wonders, persuasive enough that "if possible" even the elect could be swept away.

That single phrase rewrites an assumption most of us quietly carry: that power proves God. It does not. Supernatural events, in and of themselves, are not evidence of God's working — Moses faced magicians who could duplicate plagues, and Jesus pressed the warning twice more: "many false prophets will rise up and deceive many" (Matthew 24:11, NKJV; see also Mark 13:22). The enemy's oldest strategy has never been to deny the supernatural. It is to counterfeit it.

A week ago we sat with the tests of a true message — harmony with God's Word, fruit inspected on the tree, Bereans searching the Scriptures daily. Today's lesson tells us why those tests matter so much: *"For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this age, against spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly places"* (Ephesians 6:12, NKJV). Behind false prophecy stands an intelligent, ancient enemy — one who quotes Scripture fluently, because he tried it on Jesus in the wilderness, and who works through impressive messengers.

Which is why discernment is armor, not anxiety. Truth buckled around the waist, righteousness over the heart, faith as a shield, the Word taken up as a sword, "praying always" as the breath of the whole suit (Ephesians 6:10–18). John compresses it to a standing order: *"Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world"* (1 John 4:1, NKJV). And Jesus attached a countercultural red flag: "Woe to you when all men speak well of you" (Luke 6:26, NKJV) — popularity has never been proof.

The escalation has an end point. Ellen White writes, *"As the crowning act in the great drama of deception, Satan himself will attempt to personate Christ"* —Ellen G. White, *The Great Controversy*, p. 624. The people who will stand in that hour are the people who learned to test now.

So take the most impressive religious claim that crossed your feed this week — the healing video, the viral prophecy, the "God told me." Before you share it, open the Word. The elect are not those who feel safest; they are those who stay armed.
