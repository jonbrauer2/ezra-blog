The first question God ever asked a sinner was never a search for information. He knew exactly where Adam was — behind which tree, under which leaves. *"Then the LORD God called to Adam and said to him, 'Where are you?'"* (Genesis 3:9, NKJV). The question was never about location. It was about relationship.

Genesis uses a small Hebrew word with a large meaning here: *qara'* — "called." It is the word Scripture keeps reaching for when God takes the initiative to re-establish communication with His people. Sin had cut the line between Eden's evening walks and the couple crouching in the brush, and God's first concern was not the verdict. It was the connection. Not "What have you done?" but "Where are you?" — an open door, an invitation to step into the light and speak to Someone who already knew and loved them anyway.

What stepped out from behind the tree, however, was not a confession. It was the first recorded deflection. *"The woman whom You gave to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I ate"* (Genesis 3:12, NKJV). Notice Adam's arithmetic: his sin, plus God's gift, equaled God's fault. Eve followed suit — *"The serpent deceived me, and I ate"* (Genesis 3:13). *Ellen White* writes: *"The spirit of self-justification originated in the father of lies. . . . Instead of humbly confessing their sins, they try to shield themselves by casting the blame upon others, upon circumstances, or upon God."* Six thousand years have not improved the technique. Paul traced the same helpless loop in himself: *"For what I will to do, that I do not practice; but what I hate, that I do"* (Romans 7:15, NKJV).

And still God seeks. On the night heaven's greatest denial unfolded, *"the Lord turned and looked at Peter"* (Luke 22:61, NKJV) — no words at all, just a look that found His friend inside the courtyard of his failure. And in Jesus' best-known story, the father *"saw him and had compassion, and ran"* (Luke 15:20, NKJV) — God does not wait at the house for us to finish finding our way home. He runs down the road to meet us.

That is where every excuse finally ends: not in a better explanation but in *"deliverance through Jesus Christ our Lord"* (Romans 7:25, NKJV). The seeking God leads every hider straight to the Savior.

So — whose voice are you blaming for where you are today? Circumstances? Other people? God Himself? Drop the defense. Name the tree. Start walking. You will be met before you arrive.

*Ezra*
