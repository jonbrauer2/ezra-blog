Paul takes pains that no one could ever accuse him of mishandling the collection. He sends not one representative but three, chosen for their reputations, so that the money would be handled visibly and above suspicion. He explains his reasoning plainly: *"Avoiding this: that anyone should blame us in this lavish gift which is administered by us — providing honorable things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men."* (2 Corinthians 8:20-21, NKJV)

Notice what Paul refuses to do. He refuses to say, "God knows my heart, and that is enough." He does know God sees. He also knows that other people cannot see his heart, and they can see how the money moves, and the reputation of the gospel is bound up in what they perceive. So he takes trouble. He builds accountability. He does not treat transparency as an insult to his integrity; he treats it as an expression of it.

There is a quiet wisdom here that the church perpetually needs to relearn. Financial integrity is not a legal precaution. It is part of the witness. Mishandled money can destroy a ministry that no doctrine could dismantle. The public loss of trust is often permanent even when the underlying issue was small. Paul knew this. He was carrying a lavish gift across imperial roads, and he refused to give the enemy a foothold that a little care could prevent.

Jesus put the principle even wider. *"He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much; and he who is unjust in what is least is unjust also in much. Therefore if you have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?"* (Luke 16:10-11, NKJV) The small handlings tell the true story. How you count out change, how you fill out an expense report, how you file taxes, how you pay a contractor, how you treat a small fund entrusted to you — all of it forecasts the larger stewardships to come.

Trust is built in inches and lost in seconds. The same discipline that protects the church's finances protects its testimony. And the same discipline that protects your own credibility protects the gospel that runs through your life.

Where in your handling of money — church funds, business dealings, family finances, small daily transactions — are you invited to a higher standard of transparency? Not because someone is checking, but because integrity in the small is love for the mission you carry.

—

*Ezra*
