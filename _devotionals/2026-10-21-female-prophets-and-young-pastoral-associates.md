Four prophets ministered in the early church, and not one of their sermons survived. Luke tells us that Philip the evangelist "had four virgin daughters who prophesied" (Acts 21:9, NKJV) — and then he moves on. No book. No preserved message. Not a single recorded sentence. Yet Scripture calls them what they were: prophets, true bearers of the Spirit's gift.

That detail deserves a slow read. Philip's daughters were noncanonical prophets — genuine messengers of God whose words never became a page of the Bible. The canon measures Scripture; it was never meant to measure God's activity. The Spirit has never restricted His voice to the people whose names get remembered. Most of His work, in every generation, is done by the unrecorded.

Anna knew something about that. Before the one sentence history preserved — speaking of Him "to all those who looked for redemption in Jerusalem" (Luke 2:38) — she had spent long years in the temple, "serv[ing] God with fastings and prayers night and day" (Luke 2:37, NKJV). The recorded sentence was the fruit of a lifetime of unrecorded faithfulness.

And the same church trusted its young. Paul called Timothy "a true son in the faith" (1 Timothy 1:2, NKJV) and Titus "a true son in our common faith" (Titus 1:4, NKJV) — and then handed them weight. Titus was left to appoint leaders across the whole island of Crete. Timothy was charged with difficult work in Ephesus, and Paul pointed him back to the prophetic words spoken over him as equipment for the fight: *"This charge I commit to you, son Timothy, according to the prophecies previously made concerning you, that by them you may wage the good warfare"* (1 Timothy 1:18, NKJV). A word from God is not a keepsake; it is armor. *"Let no one despise your youth"* (1 Timothy 4:12, NKJV).

Young, old, male, female — the early church carried all of them, and God spoke through all of them. Ellen White saw the same army in our own day: *"With such an army of workers as our youth, rightly trained, might furnish, how soon the message of a crucified, risen, and soon-coming Saviour might be carried to the whole world!"*—Ellen G. White, *Education*, pp. 271, 272.

So here is the question worth carrying today: what is your role — and will you play it faithfully even if nobody ever writes it down? God is not looking for famous servants. He is looking for faithful ones, and He still calls whom He chooses.
