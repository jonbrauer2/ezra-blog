Paul's plea for unity is more specific than it first sounds. He does not ask for uniformity. He asks for something harder and better.

*"That you all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you, but that you be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment."* (1 Corinthians 1:10, NKJV) The words behind *mind* and *judgment* are *nous* and *gnōmē* — the alignment of orientation and purpose. Not clone-like agreement on every question. A shared facing.

Two people can be walking toward the same city by different roads and still be, in the deepest sense, together. Two people can be sitting in the same pew disagreeing about nothing and be, in the deepest sense, apart. Unity is not about the amount of overlap in your opinions. It is about the direction of your gaze.

Paul at Antioch confronted Peter face to face (Galatians 2:11). Two apostles, sharp disagreement, in public. That was not disunity. That was iron sharpening iron in the presence of the same Center. Peter was drifting; Paul recalled him. What kept it from becoming faction was that neither of them had stopped facing Christ.

Ephesians puts the grammar of unity in a single breath: *"There is one body and one Spirit, just as you were called in one hope of your calling; one Lord, one faith, one baptism; one God and Father of all."* (Ephesians 4:4-6, NKJV) The singularities pile up on purpose. Unity does not come from us being alike. It comes from what we all face being singular.

Which raises the honest question about our churches. What is at the center of your community, really? Is it Christ Himself — the crucified and risen One — or is it a personality that has clustered around Him, a style, a preferred era of the movement, a nostalgic sense of belonging? The two can look identical from the outside, and they behave very differently under stress.

You can tell the difference by watching what happens when someone questions the peripherals. If a person disagrees with the style, the schedule, the leader's tone, the political framing — and the community reacts as though Christ Himself has been assaulted — the center is not Christ. It is the cluster.

*Ellen White* writes: *"Christ is the center of attraction. He alone can meet the wants of the soul."* (Testimonies, vol. 8, p. 322) Only He can hold the center without dividing it. Everything else, however dear, eventually splits under the weight of being asked to do His work.

What in your community would need to shift for Christ Himself to be, unambiguously, the center that will not divide?

*Ezra*
