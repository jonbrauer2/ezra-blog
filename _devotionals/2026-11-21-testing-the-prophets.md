A jeweler with an unproven stone does one thing before he pays for it: he tests it. Heat it with a torch and drop it into cold water. A paste fake shatters; a true diamond shrugs it off. No single test settles the matter, but a stone that passes several can be bought with confidence.

Yesterday this devotional closed with a rule for reading Ellen White's writings — "test all things; hold fast what is good." Today the quarter widens the lens from her pages to every prophetic claim anywhere, and hands the week its charter: *"Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world"* (1 John 4:1, NKJV). October's devotional heard that verse as armor for dark days. This week it returns as a syllabus — six days of biblical tests for the most valuable claim a church can carry. The lesson calls the gift of prophecy more valuable than any diamond, and no one pays diamond prices for paste.

The first test in the week's readings is the strangest. Deuteronomy 13 imagines a prophet whose sign or wonder actually comes true — and then says: do not listen to him, for the LORD your God is testing you, to know whether you love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul. Let that register. The wonder can be real and the prophet still false, because the decisive question is not whether the miracle happens but where it pulls you. A fulfilled sign that leads away from God is a lure, not a credential.

Jeremiah 28 tells that story in person. Hananiah promised peace — the yoke of Babylon broken within two years — exactly what an exhausted nation ached to hear. Jeremiah answered with a yoke of iron and a sentence: "Hear now, Hananiah: the LORD has not sent you, but you make this people trust in a lie." Hananiah died that year, in the seventh month. The pleasant word was the paste.

And Daniel 10 keeps the tests honest in the other direction. When the vision came, even the true prophet collapsed — no strength in him, breath gone — until a hand touched him: "O man greatly beloved... peace be to you; do not fear." The tests guard a treasure, but the treasured tremble too. Being greatly beloved never exempted Daniel from the shaking; it carried him through it.

So bring your claims this week — the loud ones on your feed, the quiet ones in your own heart — to the same bench. The tests are not unbelief. They are how love inspects a diamond before it trusts it. The question the week will keep asking: is the stone you are holding real?
