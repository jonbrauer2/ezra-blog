Sanctification, if we are honest, usually looks like a series of white-knuckled attempts to stop doing something. And it usually fails. There is a better mechanism at work in the New Testament — and it is not willpower.

Paul describes it as displacement. *"For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live."* (Romans 8:13, NKJV) *By the Spirit.* Not by grim resolve, not by the sheer force of your own resolution. The mechanism is Spirit-empowered, not self-manufactured. The Spirit fills the space the false gods have vacated.

Colossians uses a wardrobe image. *"Do not lie to one another, since you have put off the old man with his deeds, and have put on the new man who is renewed in knowledge according to the image of Him who created him."* (Colossians 3:9-10, NKJV) Something new is put on where the old is taken off. Removal alone is not enough. An empty coat rack does not clothe you. The old habit will return unless something better fills the space.

Back in Corinth, Paul says the same thing in a different key. *"And such were some of you. But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God."* (1 Corinthians 6:11, NKJV) Three passive verbs. Washed. Sanctified. Justified. The doing is God's. The Corinthian believer's job was not to scrub himself clean; it was to stand under the water.

That does not mean sanctification is passive in the sense of doing nothing. There is work involved — but the work is more like planting than like scrubbing. You till the soil, you plant the seed, you water. You do not manufacture the growth. The growth is the Spirit's work, and it happens as the soil is prepared and the good seed is sown.

This is why simply trying to *stop* an unwanted pattern usually fails. The evicted appetite goes wandering, finds the house swept and empty, and moves back in with reinforcements (Matthew 12:44-45). The empty space left by an evicted idol is dangerous unless something better moves in.

*Ellen White* writes: *"When one is fully emptied of self, when every false god is cast out of the soul, the vacuum is supplied by the inflowing of the Spirit of Christ."* (Home Missionary, June 1889) The vacuum is not left as a vacuum. The Spirit does what willpower could not — not by making you stronger, but by filling you with something the old appetites cannot displace.

Where in your life are you trying to remove something without replacing it? What is the Spirit inviting into the space? The prayer today may not be *help me stop*; it may be *what would You fill this with?*

*Ezra*
