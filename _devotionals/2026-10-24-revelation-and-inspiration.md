The Roman Empire thought it had solved the John problem. The last living apostle, a voice too influential to execute, was banished to Patmos — a barren rock in the Aegean, a prison island about as far from a pulpit as a man could be sent. It was a communications strategy: isolate the messenger, and the message dies.

Instead, Patmos became the receiving station for the most sweeping revelation of Jesus Christ in all of Scripture. Heaven, it turns out, is not hindered by exile. The empire could silence a location; it could not silence a God who speaks.

And the first verse of this week's lesson opens the curtain on *how* He speaks: *"The Revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave Him to show His servants... And He sent and signified it by His angel to His servant John"* (Revelation 1:1, NKJV). Look at the chain: God the Father, to Jesus Christ, to an angel, to John, to the servants — to us. Every single link is a person. God could have inscribed the scroll across the sky, but He did not. He insists on messengers at every stage, because revelation is not data transfer; it is relationship, delivered hand to hand by beings who know both ends of the line.

This week traces the astonishing variety of that delivery. *"God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets"* (Hebrews 1:1, KJV). Divers manners indeed: the spoken word that "came unto" Jeremiah and Ezekiel; the written command — *"Write this for a memorial in a book"* (Exodus 17:14), *"What thou seest, write in a book"* (Revelation 1:11); even the dramatized message, when Ezekiel was told to act out Jerusalem's siege with a clay tile and a lie-down protest lasting more than a year (Ezekiel 4:1–8). God adapts the medium to the moment. He never alters the message.

And here is the mystery the lesson names "inspiration": the truth is entirely God's, yet the tongue is entirely human's. *"Holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost"* (2 Peter 1:21). Not divine dictation to wooden scribes, but God's thought clothed in Isaiah's poetry, Luke's careful research, John's eyewitness fire. As Ellen White put it: *"The Bible points to God as its author; yet it was written by human hands, in the style of men. The truths revealed are all 'given by inspiration of God'; yet they are expressed in the words of men."*—*The Great Controversy*, Introduction. That is not a flaw in the system. It is the whole point: a God who stoops to speak our language.

Notice, too, where every link of the chain aims. The revelation is *of Jesus Christ* — given *by* Him, about *Him*, delivered to show *His servants*. From Sinai's thunder to Patmos's trumpet voice, the message has never changed at the center.

So this morning, you are the final link in a chain of grace that began in the heart of God. Read Revelation 1:1–3 aloud and count the messengers heaven used to reach your ears today. A message carried that far deserves more than a glance.
