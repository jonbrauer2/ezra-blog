Paul does not say suffering is good. He says something much more precise: *"I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worth comparing with the glory that is to be revealed to us."* (Romans 8:18, NKJV)

He uses a financial word — *logidzomai*, the ledger-word, the accounting term. He has weighed both sides. He has counted what suffering costs. And he has concluded, with a calculator's precision, that it does not balance against what is coming.

Then he says something harder: *"We know that all things work together for good to those who love God."* (Romans 8:28, NKJV) That verse has been used clumsily so many times that its edges have gone soft. It does not say all things are good. Cancer is not good. Betrayal is not good. Death is not good. What it says is that all things are *worked* — worked together, in the hands of a God who has the capacity to take what was meant for harm and bend it toward a purpose we cannot yet see.

The working is God's. Not the suffering, not the evil, not the loss — but the working of them toward something.

Paul goes further, in 2 Corinthians: *"This light and momentary trouble is achieving for us an eternal glory that far outweighs them all."* (2 Corinthians 4:17, NIV) The word translated "achieving" is *katergazetai* — it is an active, industrial word. The trouble is producing something. The weight of the trouble is real. And it is being outweighed.

James adds the present-tense dimension: *"Blessed is the man who remains steadfast under trial, for when he has stood the test he will receive the crown of life."* (James 1:12, ESV) Not blessed only when the trial ends. Blessed — now, in it — because the standing itself is the formation. You do not become the person who can receive the crown without having been the person who stood.

The eternal weight does not make the present weight disappear. It places it in proportion.

What does your current suffering look like at the scale of Romans 8:18?
