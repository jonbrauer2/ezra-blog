Every spiritual gift is on a timer. Prophecies will end. Tongues will hush. Even knowledge — the thing we build seminaries around — will pass. Only one thing on the whole apostolic list survives into the age to come.

*"Love never fails. But whether there are prophecies, they will fail; whether there are tongues, they will cease; whether there is knowledge, it will vanish away."* (1 Corinthians 13:8, NKJV) The gifts are interim tools. They are given for the duration of a job that has an ending. When the job is done, the tools are set on the workbench, and the workmen go home. Only love goes with them, because love is not a tool. Love is the atmosphere of the house.

Paul explains the temporary nature of the gifts with two of the most beautiful images in his letters. First, the mirror: *"For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I shall know just as I also am known."* (1 Corinthians 13:12, NKJV) The Corinthian mirrors were polished bronze — you saw yourself, but only in outline, only in blur. That is what our current knowing is like. Genuine, but partial. Real, but muted. The gifts help us navigate the mirror-world. When the face-to-face comes, the gifts have done their work.

Second, the child: *"When I was a child, I spoke as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child; but when I became a man, I put away childish things."* (1 Corinthians 13:11, NKJV) Not a rebuke of childhood — an acknowledgment that childhood is preparation. The child's understanding is not deficient for a child. It becomes deficient only if the child refuses to grow. The spiritual gifts serve us the way childhood does — beautifully, appropriately, and for a limited time.

Then the sentence that lands like a bell: *"And now abide faith, hope, love, these three; but the greatest of these is love."* (1 Corinthians 13:13, NKJV) Faith will one day resolve into sight. Hope will one day resolve into possession. But love does not resolve into anything else, because love is already the thing itself. In eternity, faith and hope find their completion, and love remains what it always was.

John writes it flat: *"God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God, and God in him."* (1 John 4:16, NKJV) Love is not one of God's attributes among many. It is His name. Which means the practice of love in this life is not merely ethics; it is participation in the very being of God.

Here is the question that reorients a week: if every spiritual practice you invest in today would end tonight except for the love it produced — would you invest in it differently? Would you pray differently? Read differently? Serve differently? Whatever you would change, change it.

Love is the only thing you get to take with you.

—

*Ezra*
