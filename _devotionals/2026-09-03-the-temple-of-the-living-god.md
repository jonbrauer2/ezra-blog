The command in 2 Corinthians 6 sounds severe when you first hear it. Do not be unequally yoked. Come out from among them. Be separate. Modern ears bristle at the language. It sounds like withdrawal, like walls, like the church pulling up the drawbridge.

But the image Paul is using is agricultural, not tribal. *"Do not be unequally yoked together with unbelievers."* (2 Corinthians 6:14, NKJV) A yoke joined two draft animals to pull a plow. If the two were mismatched — an ox and a donkey, one stronger, one shorter — they could not pull straight. The furrow crooked. The work suffered. Both animals were miserable.

Paul is not saying: have no relationships with anyone outside the faith. Jesus did nothing but have relationships with people outside the faith. Paul himself lived and worked and dined and reasoned with unbelievers constantly. The yoke image is more specific. It is about binding entanglements — the partnerships, allegiances, arrangements that fuse your direction to someone whose direction is not toward God. You cannot pull straight together.

And notice how Paul grounds the call. Not in fear. Not in tribal purity. In presence. *"For you are the temple of the living God. As God has said: 'I will dwell in them and walk among them. I will be their God, and they shall be My people.'"* (2 Corinthians 6:16, NKJV) The reason for separation is not that God cannot handle contamination. It is that God has said He will *dwell* in His people. The temple imagery is drawn from Leviticus, where the promise was already given. Presence precedes purity. He comes, and His coming reshapes what belongs in the space.

*"Come out from among them and be separate, says the Lord. Do not touch what is unclean, and I will receive you."* (2 Corinthians 6:17, NKJV) The separation is not from people. It is from patterns. The channels of input that shape you into an image other than His. The habits that keep the veil on the heart. The alliances that pull the plow crooked.

Then the response, in the next verse: *"Therefore, having these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God."* (2 Corinthians 7:1, NKJV) Cleansing is the response, not the precondition. He has promised presence; therefore we clean the house. Not so He will come. Because He already has.

Ellen White writes: *"God dwells in every soul that receives Christ. And what an honor is this — to be so recognized by the Ruler of the universe."*

Where in your life is there a yoke pulling you off-line — a habit, a partnership, a channel of input that is making holy walking harder? Because He dwells in you, that yoke is not a small thing. What would coming out look like, this week?
