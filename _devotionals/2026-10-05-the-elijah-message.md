*"How long will you falter between two opinions? If the LORD is God, follow Him; but if Baal, follow him!"* (1 Kings 18:21, NKJV). One man. Four hundred fifty prophets of Baal, four hundred of Asherah, and a nation so steeped in compromise it could no longer tell which voice deserved its worship. Into that fog of half-belief, God sent a prophet — and a prophet's word does not comfort the drift; it ends it.

Elijah's message began with judgment: a drought announced to Ahab himself, heaven shut for years until a nation learned to hunger for God again. King and country blamed the messenger — prophets have rarely been thanked for their trouble. But the aim was never destruction. It was return. The affliction was a summons home.

Then Carmel. A broken altar repaired, a sacrifice drenched again and again, Baal's prophets dancing themselves hoarse before a god who did not exist — no voice, no answer, no fire. Then Elijah's brief prayer: *"LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, let it be known this day that You are God in Israel"* (1 Kings 18:36, NKJV). Fire fell. The sacrifice, the stones, the dust, the water — everything burned. And the people, watching, fell on their faces with the oldest and truest creed of all: *"The LORD, He is God! The LORD, He is God!"* (1 Kings 18:39, NKJV).

That is the prophetic office at work — not prediction for its own sake, but a Spirit-filled appeal that forces the heart to choose. And Malachi says the message is not confined to history: before *"the great and dreadful day of the LORD,"* an Elijah voice comes again, *"And he will turn the hearts of the fathers to the children"* (Malachi 4:5–6, NKJV). Just before Christ's second coming, God commands His church to proclaim the Elijah message once more — calling people away from their idols and into readiness to stand in the great day of the Lord. God still speaks — through His Word and through the gift of prophecy He has placed among His people, providing *"admonition, warning, counsel, and encouragement."*

But notice where the fire must fall first. *"A revival of true godliness among us is the greatest and most urgent of all our needs. To seek this should be our first work"* (Ellen G. White, *Selected Messages*, book 1, p. 121). Revival begins in the heart, then in the home — and from there spreads to the church, the community, and the nation.

So the Carmel question comes to us this morning: what idols — comfort, screens, success, our own opinions — hold a claim on our hearts and our households? Name them. Then let the Lord have the altar.

The LORD, He is God. Choose Him today.
