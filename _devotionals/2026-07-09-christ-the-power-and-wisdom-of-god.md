Paul does something in this passage that most preachers would not risk. He reads the demographics of the Corinthian church as theological evidence.

*"For you see your calling, brethren, that not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called."* (1 Corinthians 1:26, NKJV) Look around the room, Paul is saying. Look at each other. You are not a room full of senators and philosophers. You are a room full of people who, by the world's tally, should not be here — and that is exactly the point.

*"But God has chosen the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise, and God has chosen the weak things of the world to put to shame the things which are mighty."* (1 Corinthians 1:27, NKJV) This is not romanticism about the poor. It is an argument about how grace operates. If salvation depended on the qualifications people bring, we would end up with a church that could congratulate itself. So God, by design, chooses in a way that eliminates the congratulation. The mechanism is precise.

Paul makes the mechanism explicit two verses later: *"that no flesh should glory in His presence."* (1 Corinthians 1:29, NKJV) The entire economy of salvation is structured to eliminate boasting. Not just discourage it — eliminate it. If you could boast about the reason God chose you, God would have chosen wrongly.

And then Paul quotes Jeremiah: *"He who glories, let him glory in the LORD."* (1 Corinthians 1:31, NKJV; cf. Jeremiah 9:24) There is one permitted boast. Only one. And it is not about you.

*Ellen White* writes: *"The greatest praise that men can bring to God is to become consecrated channels through whom He can work."* (Christ's Object Lessons, p. 300) The consecration is not the qualification. It is the response to being chosen without qualification.

Look around your own life today. Not with resentment, not with pride — just honestly. What is here only by grace? The relationships, the health you still have, the moment of understanding that came just when you needed it, the sin that would have destroyed you if the Spirit had not moved. If God chose you because of what you brought to the table, the table would be small. Blessedly, the choosing came first.

Where does the glory belong today, and are you giving it there?
