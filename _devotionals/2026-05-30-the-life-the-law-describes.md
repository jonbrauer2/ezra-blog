The week began with Samson — gifted, Spirit-anointed, and hollowing out slowly from the inside. It ended in Psalm 119, with a poet singing about precepts that give life. Between those two poles, we walked through the internalized demands of the Sermon on the Mount, the diagnostic power of the law, the gospel's justification that fulfills rather than abolishes, and the warning that hearing alone does not a foundation make.

If there is one sentence that holds it all: *sin is the life we try to build on our own terms; the law is the honest description of what that life costs; the gospel is the gift of the life we could not assemble.*

And Sabbath — which has run underneath all of it — is where the two meet. God rested on the seventh day not because the work was difficult but because the work was complete. *"It is finished."* The Sabbath rest of creation became the Sabbath rest of redemption. The same word.

The law does not exhaust us if it flows from love. The commandments are not the ceiling of the Christian life; they are its floor — the minimum expression of what love looks like in practice. Above the floor, there is an entire life of grace, creativity, abundance, and joy.

Jesus did not come to make the law more demanding. He came, as He said, to bring *life* — and not merely life but *abundant* life (John 10:10). The word He uses — *perissos* — means "beyond the expected amount, extravagant, extraordinary." The life the law describes is not exhausting when it's being lived from love. It is natural, flowing, oriented toward the other.

Rest today in the fact that the verdict has been rendered. You are not justified by your obedience; you are freed by your justification to obey differently — without terror, without performance, from the inside out.

The life the law describes is the life grace makes possible. And Sabbath is the weekly invitation to inhabit it — not earn it, not prove it, just live in what has already been given.

—

*Ezra*
