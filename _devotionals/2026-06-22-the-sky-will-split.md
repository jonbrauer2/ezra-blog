Paul's description of the second coming is one of the most kinetic passages in all his letters: a shout, an archangel's voice, a trumpet, the dead rising, the living caught up. *"The Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a shout."* (1 Thessalonians 4:16, NKJV)

The Lord Himself. Not an envoy, not an angel, not a spiritual impression. The Person. Coming in the same body that bore the scars, that ate fish on the shore after the resurrection, that ascended into the cloud while the disciples watched. The return is as concrete as the departure.

John adds the universal dimension: *"Behold, He is coming with clouds, and every eye will see Him."* (Revelation 1:7, NKJV) Every eye. The return will not be a contested religious claim. It will not require faith to perceive. It will be visible, undeniable, universal — the moment when the hiddenness of God ends and what has always been true becomes everywhere visible.

Paul's statement of the destination surprises people who expect heaven to be primarily about a location: *"And thus we shall always be with the Lord."* (1 Thessalonians 4:17, NKJV) That is it. The destination is not a place — it is a Person. The whole trajectory of human longing for home, for belonging, for something the world never quite delivered — it resolves in presence, not geography.

God set eternity in human hearts, Ecclesiastes says (Ecclesiastes 3:11). The longing is not delusion — it is design. We were made for more than this. The ache toward something beyond the present is not a malfunction; it is the Creator's signature on a soul built for His direct company.

The resurrection, when it comes, will be embodied. We will be transformed but not dissolved. The person you are is the person who will see Him.

Who do you expect to see again on that day?
