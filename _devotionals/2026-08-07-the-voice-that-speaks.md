God has never been silent. Amos said it directly: *"Surely the Lord GOD does nothing, unless He reveals His secret to His servants the prophets."* (Amos 3:7, NKJV) From Noah to John, the pattern holds — the God who acts is the God who speaks first, and He speaks through people He chooses, often not the ones we would have picked.

Revelation says the final church will be marked by the same voice. *"And the dragon was enraged with the woman, and he went to make war with the rest of her offspring, who keep the commandments of God and have the testimony of Jesus Christ."* (Revelation 12:17, NKJV) Two identifying marks: obedience and prophetic testimony. The dragon's rage is not indiscriminate. It targets the people who take God's word seriously and who trust the voices He has appointed to speak on His behalf. If you have ever felt strange resistance in your soul when reading Scripture or listening to sound preaching, you have brushed up against the pattern Revelation describes.

But the gift comes with tests, and Scripture is generous with them. Deuteronomy 18: *"When a prophet speaks in the name of the LORD, if the thing does not happen or come to pass, that is the thing which the LORD has not spoken."* (Deuteronomy 18:22, NKJV) Fulfilled prediction is one test. Isaiah 8 gives another: the prophet's message must agree with prior Scripture — *"to the law and to the testimony! If they do not speak according to this word, it is because there is no light in them."* First John adds the christological test: *"By this you know the Spirit of God: Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God."* (1 John 4:2, NKJV) And Jesus adds the fruit test in Matthew 7: you know a tree by what it produces over time.

Four tests. Not one. Any voice that claims to speak for God should welcome them all. False prophets fail them; true prophets pass them; and the church learns discernment by applying them rather than by trusting or dismissing at first hearing.

Notice too what prophecy is for, and it is not what most people assume. It is not primarily prediction. It is edification, exhortation, and comfort — the same three functions we saw earlier in the chapter. Even the warning voice — Jeremiah crying in the streets, John crying in the wilderness — is a form of comfort in the long run, because it points the wanderer home before it is too late. The gift is not for elevating the prophet. It is for feeding the flock.

Ellen White wrote, "Position does not sanctify a man. It is the honoring of God and obeying His commands that makes true excellency of character." The prophet is not the point. The message is the point, and the message points past the messenger to Christ.

Here is the question that sits under the whole subject: how willing are you to be spoken to by God through a voice you did not choose? A pastor whose style grates on you. A friend whose observation stings. A prophet whose writings ask more of you than you were planning to give. The gift only lands where the ear is open. Open yours today.
