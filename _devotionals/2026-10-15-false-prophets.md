Four hundred to one is not a debate; it is an avalanche. When King Jehoshaphat asked to hear one voice beyond the wall of unanimous optimism, Ahab's messenger found Micaiah and gave him the honest briefing: "Now listen, the words of the prophets with one accord encourage the king. Please, let your word be like the word of one of them, and speak encouragement" (1 Kings 22:13, NKJV). Notice what the pressure actually was. Nobody asked Micaiah to doubt God. They asked him to be normal — to sound like everyone else. And Micaiah answered with the quiet defiance of a man who had actually heard from heaven: "As the LORD lives, whatever the LORD says to me, that I will speak" (1 Kings 22:14, NKJV).

Why did four hundred prophets miss God? Jeremiah pulls back the curtain on the whole species. In his day, with sword, famine, and pestilence already gathering at the borders, the official voices kept promising "peace" — and God's verdict was withering: "The prophets prophesy lies in My name. I have not sent them, commanded them, nor spoken to them; they prophesy to you a false vision, divination, a worthless thing, and the deceit of their heart" (Jeremiah 14:14, NKJV). Ezekiel says the same: they prophesied "out of their own heart" (Ezekiel 13:2, NKJV). That is the tell. False prophecy is not usually the work of demons in disguise; it is the work of hearts passing off their own wishes as a word from God. It says what the crowd already wants to hear, then signs heaven's name to it.

So how do we tell the difference? Not by volume, not by majority, not by how good the message feels. The true test has always been God's revealed Word — harmony with what He has already spoken, the character of the message, and time's confirmation of what was predicted. The hard word from God was true; the pleasant word from the four hundred was a lie. And the stakes only rise with time. Ellen White warns, *"So closely will the counterfeit resemble the true that it will be impossible to distinguish between them except by the Holy Scriptures"* —*The Great Controversy*, p. 593.

Which makes the old habit of the Bereans the most modern skill there is: they "searched the Scriptures daily to find out whether these things were so" (Acts 17:11, NKJV) — and they did that to Paul, an apostle with a proven message. If Berea checked Paul, we can check everyone.

So here is the challenge: name the voices you let speak into your life — the preachers, the podcasts, the feeds. Then test one of them this week against the Word. Four hundred voices agreed with each other. Only one agreed with God. The count has never been the test.
