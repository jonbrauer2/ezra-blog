In the old sanctuary calendar there was a small ceremony the world hardly noticed. A single sheaf of the first grain, waved before the Lord, as a promise that the whole harvest was on the way (Leviticus 23:9-14). The wave sheaf was not the harvest. It was the guarantee of it.

Paul picks up that image after the darkest paragraph of the chapter and pivots. *"But now Christ is risen from the dead, and has become the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep."* (1 Corinthians 15:20, NKJV)

*But now.* Two words that turn the whole letter. If the tomb is empty, then the resurrection is not a one-off miracle to be marveled at from a distance. It is the first sheaf. The harvest has begun. The rest of the field is still standing in the sun, but the pattern has been set and the season has opened.

Paul draws the two humanities out in the next line. *"For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ all shall be made alive."* (1 Corinthians 15:22, NKJV) Adam introduced death through disobedience; the last Adam introduces life through obedience. The order is fixed: *"Christ the firstfruits, afterward those who are Christ's at His coming."* (1 Corinthians 15:23, NKJV) His raising is the guarantee of yours.

Paul says it again in Romans in case we missed it. *"If the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, He who raised Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through His Spirit."* (Romans 8:11, NKJV) The same Spirit. The same raising. He is not a spectator to your resurrection; He is its down payment already at work in you.

This is not a small thing. It means your future is not speculation. It is not "if all goes well." It is not contingent on a strong enough faith or a well-managed spiritual life. Your resurrection has already been guaranteed by an event that already happened. The firstfruits are in.

Ellen White writes: *"To the believer, Christ is the resurrection and the life. In our Saviour the life that was lost through sin is restored; for He has life in Himself to quicken whom He will."*

If Christ is the wave sheaf and you belong to Him, the question is not *will I be raised*. The question is only *what do I do with the harvest that is already promised?* The certainty is meant to change something on the ground today.

Does the certainty of your resurrection change how you face today's problem?
