Last Friday we watched a terrified seventeen-year-old surrender to the call she had begged God to give someone else. But the surrender was only the beginning. The question this Friday's lesson quietly answers is: what held that ministry steady for the seventy years that followed? The answer is a compass with two points. Ellen White's whole work, the lesson says, had two primary functions: to lead people to Jesus, and to point them to His Word — the Bible. Everything else she did ran on those two rails.

Jesus first. Her five-book Conflict of the Ages series opens its first page with the words "God is love" — and closes its last page with the same words. From eternity past to sin's final end, that was the frame. And it was no abstract principle to her. In *Steps to Christ*, perhaps her most-read book, she wrote: *"Our growth in grace, our joy, our usefulness—all depend upon our union with Christ. It is by communion with Him, daily, hourly—by abiding in Him—that we are to grow in grace. He is not only the Author, but the Finisher of our faith."*—*Steps to Christ*, p. 69. Years later, deep into writing *The Desire of Ages*, she confessed in her diary that she would forget to breathe as she wrote, overwhelmed by what Christ had suffered (Manuscript 70, 1897). She did not merely write about Jesus. She knew Him, and it showed.

The second rail was Scripture. She described her own writings as a lesser light leading to the greater light of the Bible (*Selected Messages*, book 3, p. 29) — and she meant it in the most practical way. Early in her ministry she told readers: *"I recommend to you, dear reader, the Word of God as the rule of your faith and practice"* (*Selected Messages*, book 3, p. 30). Decades later the counsel had not softened: *"The Lord desires you to study your Bibles. He has not given any additional light to take the place of His Word. . . . If eaten and digested, [His Word] is as the life-blood of the soul"*—Letter 130, 1901. A prophet whose constant message was "go read your Bible, not me" — that is its own kind of credential.

She applied the same standard to everyone who claims the prophetic mantle. Such voices must teach *"what Christ had taught"* — *"Human teaching is shut out"* (*The Desire of Ages*, p. 826). Which hands this week a remarkably simple instrument panel. After six days of prophets and apostles, functions and counterfeits, the test of any voice compresses to two questions. Does it lead you to Jesus? Does it send you to the Word? Yesterday we saw how convincing the alternatives can be. This is the positive side of that discernment: not just what to refuse, but what to look for.

And the compass is not only for testing messengers. It is the pattern for readers. Jesus said of the Scriptures, *"these are they which testify of Me"* (John 5:39, NKJV) — the Word exists to bring you to Him, and He abides with you daily, hourly, through that Word. The week began with two cars sharing one engine; it ends with a life held in orbit by two centers that turn out to be one. Christ in the Book. The Book pointing to Christ.

So here is the closing invitation of the week, from someone who ran on this compass for seventy years: keep Jesus and His Word at the center of your day — not your doctrine only, but your Tuesday afternoon. Everything else in a busy life can change, and will. The compass doesn't.

—

*Ezra*
