The Corinthians were not lacking in spiritual gifts. Paul opens the letter by acknowledging that in every gift they were richly supplied — tongues, prophecy, knowledge, the visible signs of the Spirit's presence. And still Paul cannot address them as spiritual adults.

*"And I, brethren, could not speak to you as to spiritual people but as to carnal, as to babes in Christ. I fed you with milk and not with solid food; for until now you were not able to receive it, and even now you are still not able."* (1 Corinthians 3:1-2, NKJV)

That is worth sitting with. A church can be charismatically alive and spiritually shallow at the same time. Gifts of the Spirit and character formed by the Spirit are not the same thing. Gifts are distributed; character is grown. You can have a great deal of the first and very little of the second.

Solid food, in Paul's usage, is not doctrinal complexity. It is not the ability to argue about eschatology or trace the finer points of sanctuary theology. Solid food is the mature capacity to bear the Cross's implications — servanthood, hidden faithfulness, the death of self-promotion, the willingness to be misunderstood without needing to defend yourself. That is the diet grown-ups eat.

And what was Paul's evidence that they were still on milk? Their factionalism. *"For where there are envy, strife, and divisions among you, are you not carnal and behaving like mere men?"* (1 Corinthians 3:3, NKJV) Notice — Paul does not diagnose their immaturity by their theology. He diagnoses it by their relationships. The way they treated each other told him what the meals of their souls had been.

The writer of Hebrews watches the same pattern from a different angle: *"For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the first principles of the oracles of God."* (Hebrews 5:12, NKJV) There is a kind of ongoing infancy that is not natural. It is the result of never quite letting the truth do its adult work in you.

*Ellen White* writes: *"Many who profess the name of Christ, and claim to be looking for His speedy coming, know not what it is to suffer for Christ's sake."* (Testimonies, vol. 5, p. 214) That is the milk problem, exactly. Suffering — the small daily kinds, most of all — is where solid food is chewed.

Look honestly at your own habits of following. Which of them are milk — comfort, familiar rhythms, the parts of faith that require nothing costly? And which are the harder food you are being asked to grow into? Sometimes the invitation from the Spirit today is simply to swallow.

—

*Ezra*
