Jesus ends the Sermon on the Mount not with a blessing but with a building project. Two men, two houses, one storm. The houses look identical from the outside until the rain comes.

The foolish man did not build his house with no intention of it standing. He presumably expected it to last. He had heard the same wind warnings, seen the same landscape, used the same materials. The only difference was where he chose to anchor: sand — easier, more convenient, already level.

*"Everyone who hears these words of Mine and does not do them will be like a foolish man who built his house on the sand."* (Matthew 7:26, NKJV) The parable's sting is not aimed at people who reject Jesus. It is aimed at people who hear Him — who attend, who nod, who perhaps even admire — and walk away unchanged.

The prophet Hosea named the same failure in a different century: *"My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge."* (Hosea 4:6, NKJV) But the knowledge Hosea grieves is not informational knowledge — it is covenantal knowing, the kind that binds and shapes and transforms. His people had the scrolls. They had the priests. They had the festivals. What they lacked was the intimate knowledge that becomes practice.

Jesus is even more direct about the endpoint: *"This is eternal life, that they may know You, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent."* (John 17:3, NKJV) Eternal life is not knowing *about* God. It is knowing God — the kind of knowing that starts in this life and takes the shape of a changed one.

There is a peculiar comfort in accumulating knowledge about God. You can fill notebooks, win arguments, answer questions, and remain fundamentally untransformed. The notebook has not become your house's foundation; it has become its wallpaper.

The question the parable asks is simple and uncomfortable: What is the gap between what you know and what you do? Not to produce guilt — guilt without action changes nothing. But to identify the specific place where hearing has not yet become doing.

Name one today. Then build there.
