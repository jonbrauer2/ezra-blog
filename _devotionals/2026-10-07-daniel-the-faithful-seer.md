He was a teenager when they took him. Ripped from Jerusalem, marched hundreds of miles to Babylon, renamed with the names of Babylonian gods, enrolled in the empire's finest academy — every pressure pushed him toward assimilation. And yet Daniel's testimony begins not with a dramatic vision but with a lunch decision: *"Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself"* (Daniel 1:8, NKJV). He asked for vegetables and water. God answered with wisdom no philosopher in the palace could match.

That is where the prophetic gift begins: faithfulness in the small. The man who would map the future of empires first refused a compromise that seemed, to everyone around him, absurdly minor. Ten days of vegetables proved the point; the lions' den proved it again decades later. In the trivial and in the deadly, Daniel was the same man.

And pagans noticed. Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face and confessed, *"Truly your God is the God of gods, the Lord of kings"* (Daniel 2:47, NKJV). Darius wrote to every nation on earth that men should tremble before *"the living God... for He is the living God, and steadfast forever"* (Daniel 6:26, NKJV). A prophet in an enemy court taught kings to praise God — not by argument, but by a life so consistent and a God so real that the testimony could not be denied.

Then there is the statue. Half a millennium before Christ, Daniel traced the course of empire: Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome — and the divided kingdoms of modern Europe, *"partly strong and partly fragile... just as iron does not mix with clay"* (Daniel 2:42–43, NKJV). Rulers have tried to weld that continent together ever since; it will not fuse. Intelligence agencies never saw the fall of the Soviet Union coming, but Daniel saw four empires coming. When every kingdom in the statue rose and fell exactly as foretold, only one piece remains unfulfilled: *"The God of heaven will set up a kingdom which shall never be destroyed... it shall stand forever"* (Daniel 2:44, NKJV).

Even his name preached: *God is my judge.* *"In the annals of human history, the growth of nations, the rise and fall of empires, appear as if dependent on the will and prowess of man... But in the word of God the curtain is drawn aside, and we behold, above, behind, and through all the play and counterplay of human policy and power, the agencies of the All-merciful One, silently, patiently working out the counsels of His own will"* (Ellen G. White, *Prophets and Kings*, p. 501).

The seer's secret is still available this morning. Purpose in your heart about the small things — the meal, the media, the small compromise no one would see — and the God of Daniel, the God of gods and Lord of kings, stands behind your tomorrow too. What "little" compromise have you been entertaining, and what would it take to settle it today?
