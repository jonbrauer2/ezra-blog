Paul describes the offering being gathered for the Jerusalem saints and does something unexpected. He does not end with the recipients being fed. He ends with God being praised. *"The administration of this service not only supplies the needs of the saints, but also is abounding through many thanksgivings to God."* (2 Corinthians 9:12, NKJV)

The offering is meeting a real need — hungry believers in Jerusalem, empty pantries, actual bread. Paul takes that seriously. But he also sees something further. Every time bread arrives, so does gratitude. Every time gratitude arises, so does worship. The gift, sent to relieve poverty, ends up flowing back to heaven as praise. The circuit is completed at God.

And then Paul says something even more striking. The gift is not only material provision; it is public testimony. *"While, by the proof of this ministry, they glorify God for the obedience of your confession to the gospel of Christ, and for your liberal sharing with them and all men."* (v. 13, NKJV) The recipients look at the Corinthians' generosity and conclude something about the gospel. Visible sharing confirms invisible confession. The theology becomes credible because the wallet has already said what the mouth is saying.

The whole passage is bracketed by grace. It began with the reminder that Christ became poor to make us rich (2 Corinthians 8:9). It ends with the burst Paul cannot contain: *"Thanks be to God for His indescribable gift!"* (v. 15) Between the grace that received and the thanksgiving that follows, human giving finds its meaning. It is not the origin of anything. It is a middle station in a much longer flow that starts at God and returns to God.

Jesus said, *"It is more blessed to give than to receive"* (Acts 20:35, NKJV). The paradox holds because generosity is not primarily about the recipient. It is also about the giver being pulled deeper into the grace behind the gift. You cannot participate in the divine flow without being changed by the current.

*Ellen White* writes: *"It is by giving that we receive. God has provided that all should be workers together with Him... In blessing others we are ourselves blessed."*

When you last gave, what did it produce in you? Not in the recipient — in you. Where did the current carry your heart? The direction it moved is the direction God is inviting you further.
