You would expect the great resurrection chapter to end in the clouds. It ends in a workshop. After fifty-seven verses on empty tombs and glorified bodies and trumpets, Paul says one word: *therefore.*

*"Therefore, my beloved brethren, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not in vain in the Lord."* (1 Corinthians 15:58, NKJV)

The resurrection does not make Paul other-worldly. It makes him more grounded in this one. He does not treat the coming glory as a reason to disengage from the present but as the reason none of the present is wasted. The labor is not in vain. The obedience nobody sees is not in vain. The prayer for the ungrateful is not in vain. The small kindness done in a bad mood is not in vain. The years of raising a difficult child in the direction of Jesus are not in vain. None of it. Because He was raised, none of the work done in Him goes into a landfill.

That is a bigger comfort than we often let it be. Because so much of the Christian life feels like planting in poor soil. You show up. You give what you have. Nothing visible seems to grow. The temptation is to stop giving. And Paul says, standing over an empty tomb: *keep going. It is being kept.*

He puts the same idea another way in the next letter. *"Our light affliction, which is but for a moment, is working for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory."* (2 Corinthians 4:17, NKJV) Look at the ratio he draws. The affliction: light and momentary. The glory: exceeding, eternal, weighty. The scales tip in a direction the eye cannot yet measure.

And the resurrection is not just the reason the future is secure. It is the pattern of present life. *"We were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life."* (Romans 6:4, NKJV) The new walking begins now. The raising begins to shape the day. The old self, Paul says, was crucified with Him — which means the person doing the labor is not the person you used to be.

So the great chapter closes with a small directive. Be steadfast. Be immovable. Abound in the work. Not because you can see the fruit, but because the fruit is being kept by the One who does not lose what He is raising.

Where in your life have you been tempted to give up because the labor seemed wasted? Because Christ is raised, no faithful work is wasted. What will you not give up on today?

—

*Ezra*
