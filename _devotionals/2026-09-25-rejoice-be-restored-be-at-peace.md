The letter written from tears ends in benediction. Paul had confronted, corrected, defended, wept — and then, in the last paragraph, he lets his voice drop to a father's blessing. Five short imperatives, one holy kiss, and a promise. *"Finally, brethren, farewell. Become complete. Be of good comfort, be of one mind, live in peace; and the God of love and peace will be with you."* (2 Corinthians 13:11, NKJV)

Look at them. Rejoice. Be restored. Be encouraged. Be of one mind. Live in peace. Not requirements for salvation, though a careless reader could hear them that way. They are the pattern of life for a community that has already received salvation — the shape the redeemed life quietly takes once the grace has done its work. Paul does not command what he has not already made possible.

And then the promise, tucked at the end so gently it can be missed: *"and the God of love and peace will be with you."* Not "might be." Not "if you get the imperatives right, He will be." Simply — He will be. He is coming with you into every one of these instructions. He is the source and the destination of them all.

*"Greet one another with a holy kiss,"* Paul adds (v. 12). Even the farewell is embodied. Even the last line is fellowship. This is a man who knows that the community itself is the vessel of the grace he has been preaching, and he leaves them with an actual gesture — a hand extended, a face to face, a body of Christ visible in bodies of believers.

Paul writes elsewhere in exactly the same tone: *"Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice! Let your gentleness be known to all men. The Lord is at hand. Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God; and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."* (Philippians 4:4-7, NKJV) Same instincts. Same peace. Same God.

What Paul wanted for the Corinthians is what God wants for you. Wholeness. Encouragement. Unity. Peace. Restoration. Not one of these is out of reach today. Not one of them requires you to first fix yourself. All of them come with a Person walking alongside — the God of love and peace, already present, already active, already saying yes.

Which of the five imperatives — rejoice, be restored, be encouraged, be of one mind, live in peace — does the Spirit press on your heart today? Take one small step in that direction before Sabbath begins. The God of love and peace is already walking with you.
