The rediscovery could not have been more alarming. A book of the law, lost for generations, found in the temple and read aloud to King Josiah — and when he heard its words, he tore his clothes in terror of the judgments it named. So the king of Judah sent his five highest-ranking officials not to a council of state and not to a committee of scholars, but to a woman: "So Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan, and Asaiah went to Huldah the prophetess" (2 Kings 22:14, NKJV). The nation's greatest revival began with what she said next.

Huldah was not an exception to the rule. She was part of the pattern. Miriam was the first person in Scripture called a prophetess (Exodus 15:20), leading Israel in song on the far shore of the Red Sea — and in Micah 6:4 God Himself names her among those He "sent before" His people: "I sent before you Moses, Aaron, and Miriam." Deborah judged Israel, and when God's word came to her, Barak would not move without her (Judges 4:4–9, 14). Centuries later, Anna the prophetess recognized in a forty-day-old baby the salvation of the world (Luke 2:36). The prophetic gift was never a men's office; it was God's gift, given to whom God chose.

And notice who these women were. Miriam used her prophetic standing to lash out at Moses over his Ethiopian wife and was struck with leprosy for it (Numbers 12:1–10). Men prophets and women prophets alike were sinners in need of grace — their authority never rested on their perfection, but on the word God gave them. That is hope for anyone: God does not wait for flawless messengers. He waits for willing ones. As Ellen White wrote, *"The Lord has a work for women as well as for men"* —*Evangelism*, p. 464.

Joel saw further down the road: "I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy" (Joel 2:28, NKJV). Peter preached that promise fulfilled at Pentecost (Acts 2:17–18), and Revelation identifies the last-day church by "the testimony of Jesus," which is "the spirit of prophecy" (Revelation 12:17; 19:10, NKJV). The gift that spoke through Miriam, Deborah, and Huldah was always pointing to Christ — and it still is.

So the question lands on us: whose voice do we discount because they don't fit our categories? And whose grace do we need, as Miriam did, because we have used God's gift to wound instead of to bless? The Lord still calls whom He chooses. The only question is whether we will hear.
