Paul had a thorn. He never tells us what it was — commentators have speculated for centuries — and perhaps the ambiguity is the point. Whatever it was, it was enough to make him ask God to remove it. Three times.

*"Concerning this thing I pleaded with the Lord three times that it might depart from me."* (2 Corinthians 12:8, NKJV) Three times. The same repetition as Gethsemane. Three is enough times to know the answer is deliberate. And the answer he received was not removal:

*"My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness."* (2 Corinthians 12:9, NKJV)

Not: I will remove it. Not: you've passed the test, here's your relief. Not even: here is the explanation for why I allowed it. Just: *My grace is sufficient for this. And in the weakness that the thorn creates, something will happen that couldn't happen in your strength.*

It takes a certain kind of trust to receive that answer and not be destroyed by it. Paul was, by his own testimony, destroyed first: *"I will boast all the more gladly about my weaknesses."* He had to arrive at that. It did not come immediately.

But what it produced in him was something no success could have: the genuine knowledge that God's power was operating in him, not beside him. *"When I am weak, then I am strong."* (2 Corinthians 12:10, NKJV) Not strong in himself — strong because he had stopped relying on what he no longer had.

The writer of Hebrews adds a promise that is not contingent on circumstances: *"For He Himself has said, 'I will never leave you nor forsake you.'"* (Hebrews 13:5, NKJV) Not: I will never let bad things happen. I will never leave.

And Malachi: *"I am the LORD, I do not change."* (Malachi 3:6, NKJV) The rock beneath the setback. The same God in the storm and in the calm, in the healing and in the thorn, in Gethsemane and in Easter morning.

What thorn have you asked God to remove three times? What if the grace for it — not the removal of it — is the answer being offered?
