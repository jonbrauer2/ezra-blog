John does not say God has love. He says God is love. It is not one attribute among many. It is the name of what He is at the center — and it is the air that will fill the country we are being prepared to inhabit.

*"God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God, and God in him."* (1 John 4:16, NKJV) The verb is *menō* — to remain, to stay, to lodge. When you make your home in love, you are making your home in God. The two addresses turn out to be the same.

But John insists we get the direction right. *"We love Him because He first loved us."* (1 John 4:19, NKJV) The initiative is never ours. Every act of Christian love — patience with a difficult person, kindness toward someone who has cost us something, refusal of the ledger we could rightly keep — is a response to a love that reached us first. If we treat love as something we generate, we will burn out fast. If we treat it as something we receive and then pass along, we can keep at it for a lifetime.

Then the sentence that has quietly healed millions of anxious Christians: *"There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear, because fear involves torment. But he who fears has not been made perfect in love."* (1 John 4:18, NKJV) Love and fear cannot occupy the same room. Where love moves in, fear has to leave. Not gradually, not partially — cast out. The Greek verb is forceful, the same word used elsewhere for expelling demons. Fear is not a permanent tenant of the Christian heart. It is a squatter, and love has authority to remove it.

Which is worth pausing over, because fear pretends to be prudence in a lot of our lives. It masquerades as caution, as wisdom, as reasonable planning. And some of it is those things. But some of it is the old squatter refusing to leave the room love has already begun to inhabit. The way you can tell the difference is the torment. Wise caution does not torment. Fear does.

Paul completes the picture from a different angle in Colossians: *"But above all these things put on love, which is the bond of perfection."* (Colossians 3:14, NKJV) The image is a sash — the outer garment that holds every inner piece of clothing in place. Compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, patience — all beautiful, all provided. But without the sash, they slip. Love is what holds every other virtue together as a coherent life rather than a scattered collection of attempted goodnesses.

Ellen White captured it in a sentence worth carrying for a lifetime: "Only the love that flows from the heart of Christ can heal. Only he in whom that love flows, even as the sap in the tree or the blood in the body, can restore the wounded soul." The image is biological, not decorative. Love is not paint on the outside of the Christian life. It is sap. It is blood. It is the thing without which the branch withers and the body dies.

Where in your life today is fear currently pretending to be prudence? A relationship you have been avoiding. A conversation you keep postponing. A calling you have been quietly refusing because the cost feels unmanageable. Perfect love has authority to move in and evict what has been squatting in that room. Let Him in.
