By every measure the world uses, John the Baptist was unimpressive. No pulpit. No synagogue. No scroll to his name. His office was the open desert, his wardrobe camel's hair, his salary locusts and wild honey. Yet when Jesus ranked every person born of women, this wilderness voice came first (Matthew 11:11). Isaiah had named him seven centuries before he drew breath: *"The voice of one crying in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the LORD; make His paths straight'"* (Mark 1:3, NKJV).

His entire life was preparation. Consecrated before birth, growing up in the quiet hill country of Judah, John became *strong in spirit* long before he became famous. And when that voice finally rang through the Jordan valley, the effect was seismic — Ellen White notes that Satan himself trembled for his dominion when he heard John preaching in the wilderness, and every attempt to shake the prophet's simple loyalty to the coming Messiah failed (see *The Desire of Ages*, p. 224). Then all Judea came out to him, confessing sins and going down into the water. That is what preparing a people looks like: not polishing pavement, but straightening hearts — valleys lifted, mountains brought low, crooked places made honest.

But the story takes a turn no one expected. The prophet who thundered against Herod's sin found himself locked in Herod's dungeon, and the certainty that had thundered in the wilderness began to wobble in the dark. From the cell he sent disciples with one aching question: *"Are You the Coming One, or do we look for another?"* (Matthew 11:3, NKJV). Notice what Jesus did not do. He did not scold him. He pointed John to the evidence — the blind see, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the poor hear the gospel — and added a beatitude written, in that hour, for one discouraged man in a dungeon: *"Blessed is he who is not offended because of Me"* (Matthew 11:6, NKJV). Jesus reaffirmed His servant with evidence and honor, even before the cell door ever opened.

The lesson closes with a commission: like the prophets of old, we are called to proclaim repentance and faith and the totality of Christ's righteousness (Revelation 14:6–12) — a whole movement of witnesses preparing the way for the second advent, armed with heaven's promise of power (Acts 1:8). And when circumstances make no sense — when, like John, we cannot understand why the dungeon — we remember God's past leading, reach out in faith, and stay faithful anyway. Whatever people may do to us, He has promised to answer.

John prepared a people for the first coming; a prepared people now prepare the way for the second. Whose path is God asking you to straighten this week — and whose dungeon doubt could you answer with the simple evidence of what Jesus has done in your life?
