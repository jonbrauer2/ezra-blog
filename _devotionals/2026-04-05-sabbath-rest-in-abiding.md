**"Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy. Six days you shall labor and do all your work, but the seventh day is the Sabbath of the LORD your God."** — Exodus 20:8-10

This week we've walked through uncomfortable territory. We've faced the reality of lukewarm Christianity—the danger of having right doctrine while losing passionate connection with Christ. We've heard Jesus knock at the door, offering His provision in exchange for our spiritual poverty. We've been reminded of God's everlasting love and consistent pursuit. We've explored what it means to abide rather than just visit, to remain connected like a branch to the vine. And we've discovered that the Holy Spirit is the sap that makes that connection alive and fruitful.

Now, on this Sabbath, God offers us something profound: **rest in the reality of abiding**.

The Sabbath isn't just a command to stop working. It's God's gift that reminds us we were never meant to manufacture our own spiritual life in the first place. Six days we work—and in those six days, it's easy to slip back into self-reliance, to start thinking our spiritual vitality depends on our efforts, our discipline, our consistency.

But every seventh day, God calls us to stop. To remember. To rest in the truth that **He** is the Vine, and **we** are the branches. That connection to Him—not our religious activity—is what brings life.

Ellen White wrote, "The Sabbath is a sign of the relationship existing between God and His people, a sign that they honor His law. It distinguishes between His loyal subjects and transgressors" (*Testimonies for the Church*, vol. 6, p. 350). But it's more than a sign of obedience—it's a weekly reset, a reminder of whose we are and where life comes from.

**What does Sabbath rest look like when we're learning to abide?**

It means stopping the striving. Ceasing the endless mental to-do lists. Laying aside the worry about how we're doing spiritually. On this day, we're not working to improve our relationship with God—we're simply resting *in* the relationship He's already established.

Think about the grapevine. On the Sabbath, the branch doesn't work harder at staying connected. It doesn't produce more fruit through sheer effort. It simply remains, receiving what the vine provides. Sabbath is the day we most fully practice abiding—not doing, just being. Not achieving, just receiving.

This is why the Sabbath is the perfect capstone to this week's journey. The diagnosis of lukewarmness can leave us feeling condemned, desperate to "do better." But Jesus didn't come to scold us into performance—He came to invite us into rest. "Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28).

**Today, as you rest:**

- Let go of the mental list of ways you need to improve. Jesus knows. He's already made provision.
- Stop analyzing whether you're abiding "correctly." A branch doesn't measure its connection—it just stays attached and lets the sap flow.
- Ask the Holy Spirit to fill you afresh, not because you earned it, but because the Father delights to give good gifts to His children (Luke 11:13).
- Enjoy God's presence without agenda. This is practice for eternity—when every day will be Sabbath, and we'll have forever to know Him.

The invitation Jesus extended to Laodicea still stands: "Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in to him and dine with him, and he with Me" (Revelation 3:20). The Sabbath is the day we open that door widest, inviting Him not just for a quick visit but for unhurried fellowship.

You don't have to earn this rest. You don't have to prove you're abiding well enough to deserve it. The Sabbath itself is God's gift, a taste of the eternal rest where we'll abide fully, perfectly, and forever in His presence.

So today, stop striving. Rest in the Vine. Let the sap flow. And remember: **you are loved with an everlasting love, and God Himself is teaching you how to abide**.

Happy Sabbath.
