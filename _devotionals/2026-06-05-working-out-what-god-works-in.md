Paul writes one of the most paradoxical sentences in all his letters: *"Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure."* (Philippians 2:12-13, NKJV)

The command is "work out." The reason is "God works in." These are not in tension. They are in sequence. God works in — enabling, initiating, sustaining — and that divine working is precisely what makes our working possible and necessary. We are not passive. We are not performing. We are cooperating with an energy that is not our own.

The prophet Hosea imagined it as farming: *"Sow for yourselves righteousness; reap in mercy; break up your fallow ground, for it is time to seek the LORD, till He comes and rains righteousness on you."* (Hosea 10:12, NKJV) The farmer breaks the ground. He sows the seed. But he cannot produce the rain. The rain is God's. The farmer's work is real and necessary — but it is not what causes the harvest. It is what positions him to receive it.

Fallow ground is ground that has been left unworked. It was productive once, or could have been, but it hardened from neglect. The call to break it up is the call to return to the daily disciplines — prayer, Scripture, community — not because they earn anything but because they keep the soil of the heart open to what God sends.

The second half of Hosea's verse: *"seek the LORD until He comes."* Not a season of seeking followed by achievement and rest. A continuous orientation — expectant, persistent, daily — that says *I know He is the One who brings what I cannot produce, and I will keep my face toward Him.*

The grace of the week — repentance, forgiveness, the Spirit's work, God's goodness leading home, the garment provided — all of it continues to operate through daily cooperation. Not earning the garment. Living in it, tending what it covers, keeping the ground ready for rain.

What does "breaking up fallow ground" look like in your specific life right now?
