God seems to do some of His best calling in the wreckage of our worst expectations. Abraham heard the promise with no son in sight. Elijah heard it beside a drying brook. Isaiah heard it in the year the king died. And the most widely known prophetic call of modern times came just weeks after the biggest disappointment of them all.

Daniel had seen it coming centuries earlier: *"Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed"* (Daniel 8:14, NKJV). By 1844, thousands of earnest Bible students had built their hope on that prophecy. On October 22 they waited for Jesus to come. He did not come. The day that was supposed to end in glory ended in weeping, and history remembers it as the Great Disappointment.

And it was precisely there — in the weeks after the weeping started — that God called a prophet. Ellen Harmon was seventeen years old, in frail health, and frankly terrified of the assignment. Being God's messenger had never been easy or prestigious, and she knew it. Yet her first vision did not scold the disappointed. It showed them a path stretching from earth to the Holy City, with Advent believers still walking it — and one condition of safety: *"if they kept their eyes fixed on Jesus, who was just before them, leading them to the city, they were safe."*—Ellen G. White, *Spiritual Gifts*, vol. 2, p. 31.

That is the pattern of every true prophetic call: it never points at itself. Abraham's call pointed to a Seed. Elijah's pointed to the God who answers by fire. Isaiah's pointed to the suffering Servant he would describe seven centuries early. And the call that rose out of 1844 points, above everything, to Jesus — High Priest in the sanctuary of Daniel 8:14 — walking just ahead of His people on the narrow path home. The prophetic word reaches all the way to the judgment hour, and it reaches there to tell us that the Judge is the One who loves us.

Which means the call has never been withdrawn. This week's memory text still stands: *"Whom shall I send, and who will go for Us?"* ... *"Here am I! Send me"* (Isaiah 6:8, NKJV). God still speaks at specific times for special purposes — and He still recruits unlikely, unimpressive, disappointed people to carry His word.

Disappointment wants you to leave the path. The vision says: don't stare at the wreckage; look at Jesus, just ahead. Where is He asking you to say *"Here am I! Send me"* this week?
