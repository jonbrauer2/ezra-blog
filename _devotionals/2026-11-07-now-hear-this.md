Last week traced how the prophetic word reached paper. This week's lesson asks a harder question: what happens when it reaches ears?

Start with ten men holding grapes. They walked the same land as their two companions, tasted the same fruit, saw the same giants — and came home with two opposite reports. Ten said fortified cities and certain death. Two said, *"Let us go up at once, and possess it; for we are well able to overcome it"* (Numbers 13:30). The evidence was identical. The ears were not. Caleb was not reading a different map; he was believing a different Voice — the One who had already said He would give the land.

That collision between God's word and human hearing runs down the whole week like a fault line. A king sits by the winter fire and slices the prophetic roll to pieces, leaf by leaf, *"and they were not afraid"* (Jeremiah 36:24) — yet the roll came back rewritten, with more words than before. Messengers knocked at Judah's door for centuries, *"rising up early and sending,"* until the record could only say there was *"no remedy"* (2 Chronicles 36:15–16). And in Matthew 23 a generation swears it would never have shed the prophets' blood — while sharpening knives for the living Word Himself. Honoring dead prophets is easy; hearing a living one is not.

Paul draws the line under all of it and points it straight at us: *"Now these things happened to them as an example, and they were written for our instruction, upon whom the ends of the ages have come"* (1 Corinthians 10:11, NASB). Israel's story is not a museum. It is a mirror — and it was written down precisely because we would need the warning.

Ellen White told a General Conference session: *"We have nothing to fear for the future, except as we shall forget the way the Lord has led us, and His teaching in our past history."*—Life Sketches of Ellen G. White, p. 196. The counsel is Paul's counsel: history is holy ground because God walked through it speaking, and a people who forget how to hear Him are doomed to repeat it.

So hear this, this week: the question has never been whether God has spoken. He has — in a Book that outlived every penknife turned against it. The question is whose report you will believe: the majority's, or the word that says, *well able*.

—

*Ezra*
