**Scripture**: "In the morning, LORD, you hear my voice; in the morning I lay my requests before you and wait expectantly." — Psalm 5:3 (NIV)

Sunday mornings carry a unique invitation—the chance to set the tone for the entire week ahead. Not with frantic planning or anxious task lists, but with intentional connection to the One who created time itself.

David understood this rhythm. Before the demands of kingship pressed in, before the enemies gathered, before the decisions mounted—he brought himself to God. Morning by morning. Week by week. Not out of duty, but out of desperate need.

**The Pattern of Priority**

There's profound wisdom in how we begin. Jesus Himself, despite the crushing demands of ministry, "rose early in the morning, while it was still dark, and went out to a secluded place, and prayed there" (Mark 1:35). If the Son of God needed this morning connection with the Father, how much more do we?

But here's what we often miss: this isn't about earning God's favor through early rising. It's about positioning ourselves to receive what He's already eager to give. In the quiet morning hours, before the world makes its claims on us, we have the opportunity to remember whose we are.

**From Knowing About to Knowing**

As we transition into a new week, we're also moving into a deeper exploration of what it means to truly know God—not just know about Him. There's a vast difference between intellectual assent and relational intimacy. Satan's original strategy in Eden wasn't to make Eve doubt God's existence, but to distort her picture of His character. "Did God really say...?" was an attack on God's trustworthiness, His goodness, His heart toward His children.

We can attend church, quote Scripture, teach Sabbath School—and still carry a fundamentally distorted image of who God is. Perhaps we see Him as demanding rather than delighting. Harsh rather than tender. Distant rather than near. Many of our spiritual struggles aren't rooted in lack of information, but in a faulty picture of God's character.

**The Sunday Invitation**

This morning, as you begin a new week, God invites you into something more than religious routine. He invites you to know Him. Not just to abide in Him (though that's essential), but to discover who this God is that you're abiding in.

John 17:3 defines eternal life itself as knowing God and Jesus Christ. Not just believing correct doctrines about them. Not just following their commands. Knowing them. Personally. Intimately. Increasingly.

**Practical Beginning**

So how do you start? Simply. Honestly.

Instead of beginning your week with your to-do list, begin with a question: "God, what do You want me to know about who You are today?" Then open Scripture—perhaps this week exploring passages that reveal God's character—and read slowly, expectantly, waiting for Him to speak.

When you pray this morning, don't just present your requests. Pause and listen. Notice how God responds to your needs—not with irritation at your weakness, but with the tender patience of a Father who loves giving good gifts to His children.

As you move through this Sunday, pay attention to how the morning's connection shapes the day. Notice how knowing God is near changes how you face uncertainty. Notice how remembering His character of love affects how you interact with difficult people.

**The Week Ahead**

The weeks ahead will invite us deeper into understanding God's holiness, His love, His transcendence and immanence, His ultimate revelation in Jesus Christ. But it all begins here—with the choice to start your week, start your day, with Him.

Jesus is still knocking. The door is still yours to open. And when you do, you won't find a harsh taskmaster waiting to critique your failures. You'll find Immanuel—God with us—who has loved you with an everlasting love and drawn you with unfailing kindness.

Begin your week there. Begin every week there.

**Prayer**: Father, as this new week begins, I come to You not out of obligation but out of need. I want to know You—truly know You—not just know about You. Reveal Your character to me through Your Word. Help me see You as You truly are: holy, loving, near, faithful. Shape my picture of who You are so that I can trust You more fully and love You more deeply. Be with me through every moment of this week. In Jesus' name, amen.
