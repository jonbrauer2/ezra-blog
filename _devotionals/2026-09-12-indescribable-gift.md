Count the times the word "grace" appears in 2 Corinthians 8 and 9 and you will run out of fingers. Paul cannot describe generosity without using it. The Macedonians received grace (8:1). The offering itself is grace (8:6). Christ's giving is grace (8:9). Paul prays God will make all grace abound so that abundance may flow (9:8). And when the whole thing is over, the offering completed and delivered, Paul cannot help himself: *"Thanks be to God for His indescribable gift!"* (2 Corinthians 9:15, NKJV)

The gift is Christ. The exclamation belongs at the end of every offering.

Sabbath is the weekly reoccupation of the truth that you are the receiver first, always. Every impulse of generosity in your life this past week — every dollar, every hour, every act of kindness, every moment of self that was set aside for someone else — was a small echo of a much larger sentence God spoke when He sent His Son. You did not begin the giving. You joined it.

That is what the Macedonians grasped. They gave in poverty because they had received in extravagance. That is what Paul kept preaching. That is what makes the difference between the offering that drains and the offering that fills. If the giver believes he is starting the transaction, he calculates. If he believes he is participating in a grace that started long before him, he plays. Cheerful, hilarious, generous, at rest.

Rest is the mode of Sabbath. And rest is the mode of the giver who has grasped grace. The two are the same instinct, expressed differently. On Sabbath the body stops striving so the soul can remember the Provider. In generosity the wallet stops clutching for the same reason. Both are trust made visible.

*Ellen White* writes: *"God gives men and women talents, ability, means... Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights. Every talent that we have has been given us by the Lord, and He desires it to be used to His glory."* Everything you have is loaned. You cannot outgive the Owner.

And still He calls you into the flow. Not because He needs the money. Because you need to become the kind of person who does not need the money either.

Rest today in the fact that the indescribable gift has already been given to you. From that ground — settled, unhurried, unafraid — what small echo of that gift will you send outward this week?

—

*Ezra*
