The quarter began with a simple ambition: growing in a relationship with God. It ends in a garden — or something better than a garden. The New Jerusalem. God's dwelling among His people. All tears wiped. Death swallowed up. No more night.

The whole arc was always pointed here.

Knowing God — through His Word, through prayer, through the faith that receives what it cannot produce — was never meant to be merely an exercise in personal improvement. It was preparation for sight. *"We shall be like Him, for we shall see Him as He is."* (1 John 3:2, NKJV) The becoming is what happens when the seeing is complete. We are shaped by what we behold, and when we behold Him without the glass, without the partial, without the "now I know in part" — we become what we were always meant to be.

The relationship deepened through difficulty: through the mirror of the law that showed us what we are, through the repentance that came as a gift, through the setbacks that forged what could not be manufactured in ease, through the witness that overflowed from a cup that kept being refilled. Every stage was part of the same knowing.

And it continues. The relationship that began in this life does not end at death — it is interrupted, briefly, and then resumed at a volume the current world cannot hold.

The ancient prayer stands for the whole church in every era: *"Come, Lord Jesus."* (Revelation 22:20, NKJV) Not as urgency born of despair but as confidence born of hope. The world is not careening toward meaninglessness. It is moving — through all the pain and the loss and the long waiting — toward a day when every wrong is set right, every tear is wiped, every wandering one is home, and the God who began a good work in you stands at the finish of it.

Rest in that today. The last word is grace.

*"The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen."*
