We live in a loud world. Notifications ping, podcasts play, news cycles spin endlessly. And somewhere beneath the noise, God is speaking. The question isn't whether He's talking — it's whether we're listening.

"The Lord GOD has given Me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him who is weary. He awakens My ear to hear as the learned." (Isaiah 50:4, NKJV)

This verse is remarkable. Before the Servant speaks a word in season, He first listens. The awakened ear comes before the instructed tongue. God's pattern has always been: listen first, then speak. And yet how often do we reverse it — filling our prayer time with our own words, rushing through Scripture to check a box, and then wondering why God seems distant?

Samuel understood this. When God called him in the night, the young boy's response wasn't a monologue of requests — it was a simple, open-hearted posture: "Speak, LORD, for Your servant hears" (1 Samuel 3:9). That's the posture that opens heaven.

James puts it plainly: "Be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath" (James 1:19). The sequence matters. Quick to listen — to God, to His Word, to the prompting of His Spirit. Slow to fill the silence with our own thoughts. The Holy Spirit was promised to "guide you into all truth" (John 16:13), but a guide can only lead someone who is paying attention.

Ellen White wrote: "We must guard against the perils of a surface acquaintance with the Scriptures. We need to be listeners, not merely readers. When we open the Bible, we should say, 'Speak, Lord, for Thy servant heareth.'" (Adapted from *Messages to Young People*, p. 262). The discipline of listening transforms Bible study from an intellectual exercise into a genuine encounter.

Here's the challenge: the next time you open your Bible, try spending the first two minutes in silence. No requests, no agenda — just listening. Let the Holy Spirit bring to your mind what He wants to say. You might be surprised at how much God has been wanting to say to someone who finally decided to listen.
