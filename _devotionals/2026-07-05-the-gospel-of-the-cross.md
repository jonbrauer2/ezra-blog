Paul walks into Corinth after a bruising experiment in Athens. He had tried philosophical engagement on Mars Hill, quoted the poets, worked the room. Some had believed. Most had smiled and moved on. Now he arrives at a city famous for rhetoric, for showmanship, for the man who could hold a crowd — and he makes a decision.

*"I determined not to know anything among you except Jesus Christ and Him crucified."* (1 Corinthians 2:2, NKJV)

That is not intellectual poverty. That is strategic focus. Paul is not incapable of philosophy; he is refusing it as the pipeline for the gospel. He has seen what happens when the message travels in the wrong vehicle. Earlier in the letter he says it plainly: not with wisdom of words, *"lest the cross of Christ should be made of no effect."* (1 Corinthians 1:17, NKJV) The verb there is *kenoō* — to empty. It is the same word Paul uses for Christ's self-emptying in Philippians 2. Elegant delivery, if we are not careful, evacuates the very thing the Cross was filled with.

The Cross does not need to be dressed up. Dressed up, it stops being the Cross.

To the Galatians he goes further: *"God forbid that I should glory except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ."* (Galatians 6:14, NKJV) Notice — the Cross is not one item on Paul's boast list. It eliminates the list. It is not that Paul had ten trophies and finally added Christ crucified as the eleventh. The Cross came and quietly threw out the shelf.

Ellen White writes of Paul at Corinth: *"He determined to avoid elaborate arguments and discussions, and not to know anything among the Corinthians save Jesus Christ, and Him crucified."* (Acts of the Apostles, p. 244) Not because argument is wrong. Because argument is not the power. The power is a Person, still bearing the marks of a Roman execution, and no amount of packaging improves Him.

So the question turns inward. What have you added to the Cross to make it more presentable — to your friends, your workplace, your own reasonable mind? What decorative layer would fall away today if you determined, like Paul, not to know anything else?
