The Corinthians had a slogan. *"All things are lawful for me."* Paul quotes it back to them and then, in the same breath, inverts it. *"All things are lawful for me, but I will not be brought under the power of any."* (1 Corinthians 6:12, NKJV)

The word for *brought under power* is *exousiazō* — to be mastered, ruled, ruled over. Paul sees what the Corinthians have missed: using freedom to indulge an appetite is exactly how the appetite gains authority. The "free" person becomes the slave of his own compulsion. Freedom, misused, produces the opposite of freedom.

Then Paul introduces one of the most stunning images in his letters. *"Or do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God, and you are not your own?"* (1 Corinthians 6:19, NKJV) The word for *temple* is *naos* — not the outer courts, not the general precinct, but the inner sanctuary. The Holy of Holies. The place where the glory dwelt. Paul is telling ordinary believers in a port city famous for its pagan temples that the Shekinah has moved. The presence that once filled Solomon's inner room now fills their bodies.

Sit with that a moment. The Holy of Holies now moves where you move. When you walk into a room, you carry it in.

That reframes the body. The body is not incidental to salvation. It is the site of worship, the theater of glorification. The same Paul writes elsewhere that God will raise this body (1 Corinthians 6:14) — not discard it, not replace it with something ethereal. The body will be transfigured, but it will be the body.

And then the closing line: *"You are not your own. For you were bought at a price; therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God's."* (1 Corinthians 6:19-20, NKJV) The language is manumission language — the vocabulary of buying a slave into freedom. You have been purchased. The ownership has changed. The self-sovereignty the world assumes is not, for the Christian, ultimate. There is a prior claim.

That claim does not enslave. It liberates. It liberates the body from the compulsions that were slowly consuming it. It liberates the appetites from the tyranny of their own escalation. The one who owns you now paid for you at Calvary, and He does not run His household by cruelty.

*Ellen White* writes: *"The body is the only medium through which the mind and the soul are developed for the upbuilding of character."* (Ministry of Healing, p. 130) The enemy knows what the temple houses. He works on the walls because he cannot touch the presence inside.

What in your body's daily use would look different this week if you took seriously that the Holy of Holies now moves where you move?
