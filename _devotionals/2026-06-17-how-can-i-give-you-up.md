Hosea's marriage was a lived sermon. God told him to marry a woman who would be unfaithful, and Hosea obeyed — and through the years of betrayal and return and betrayal again, the prophet felt in his own body what God felt toward Israel. The methodology was painful and strange and utterly consistent with a God who enters human experience rather than simply observing it.

And out of that lived experience comes one of the most astonishing lines in the Old Testament:

*"How can I give you up, Ephraim? How can I hand you over, Israel?... My heart churns within Me; My sympathy is stirred."* (Hosea 11:8, NKJV)

God is reasoning with Himself about a people who have betrayed Him repeatedly. And He cannot find the resolve to abandon them. His heart churns. His sympathy — the same gut-word as Jesus' compassion — is stirred.

Jeremiah hears the same yearning expressed for the exiles: Rachel weeping for her children who are not, and then — *"Is Ephraim My dear son? Is he a pleasant child? For though I spoke against him, I earnestly remember him still; therefore My heart yearns for him."* (Jeremiah 31:20, NKJV) Yearns. The yearning of a parent who cannot stop loving a child who has walked away.

This is the heart that runs through genuine witness. Not a program executed because the calendar says evangelism month. Not recruitment into an institution. But God's own yearning — for the people He knows by name, for the wandering ones who are still His dear children even in their exile — expressed through us.

There is probably someone in your life right now for whom God is yearning through you. Someone you have perhaps quietly categorized as unreachable, too far gone, too settled in their distance. Hosea's God does not categorize them that way.

How do you see the wanderers in your own world through that kind of love?

—

*Ezra*
