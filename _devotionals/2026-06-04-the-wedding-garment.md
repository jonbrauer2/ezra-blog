There is a detail in Jesus' parable of the wedding feast that often gets lost in the drama of the uninvited guests streaming in from the streets: the host provided a garment.

When the king comes in to see the guests and finds a man without a wedding garment, the question is not "Why are you here?" The question is: *"Friend, how did you come in here without a wedding garment?"* (Matthew 22:12, NKJV) The man had no answer. He was speechless. Because the garment was available — the host had provided it — and he had declined it.

He had tried to attend the wedding on his own terms, in his own clothes.

It goes back further than that parable. In the garden, after the fall, Adam and Eve sewed fig leaves together. Their first instinct was to cover their own shame — to make something themselves, quickly, before anyone looked. God replaced the fig leaves with garments of skin (Genesis 3:21). The cost of the replacement was a life. God made what they could not, and it covered what they couldn't face.

Zechariah saw it in a vision: Joshua the high priest standing before the angel in filthy garments, Satan ready to accuse. And the Lord commanded: *"Take away the filthy garments from him."* Then to Joshua: *"I have removed your iniquity from you, and I will clothe you with rich robes."* (Zechariah 3:4, NKJV) The iniquity is removed by the Lord's command. The robe is placed on Joshua by someone else's hands. The priest stands in garments he did not weave.

Revelation 19:8 shows the same scene at the end of history: *"The fine linen is the righteous acts of the saints."* But those righteous acts themselves are the fruit of a righteousness that was first given. The garment is provided; the wearing of it produces the acts.

We are not saved by our righteousness. We are given righteousness that, worn long enough, begins to shape who we become.

Have you been trying to attend the feast in your own clothes? What would it look like to put on what the Host has provided?
