**"You say, 'I am rich; I have acquired wealth and do not need a thing.' But you do not realize that you are wretched, pitiful, poor, blind and naked."** — Revelation 3:17

Jesus has a problem with Laodicea. Not with their doctrine. Not with their lifestyle. His issue is their self-assessment.

They think they're doing fine. Jesus says they're in crisis.

This is the danger of lukewarm Christianity—not that we're cold and know it, but that we're comfortable and don't. We check the boxes: church attendance, Sabbath keeping, dietary laws. We know the doctrines. We can quote Ellen White. And we conclude: "I'm good. I don't need anything."

Meanwhile, Jesus stands outside, looking at the reality we refuse to see. He calls us "wretched, pitiful, poor, blind and naked"—not to shame us, but because He loves us too much to let us stay deceived.

The word "lukewarm" in Greek is *chliaros*—neither hot enough to be useful nor cold enough to be refreshing. It's the temperature of indifference. Going through motions without heart engagement. Maintaining the form while missing the relationship.

Here's the hard question: What does lukewarm look like in your life right now?

Maybe it's praying without expecting an answer. Reading Scripture as duty instead of delight. Worshiping while thinking about lunch. Witnessing from guilt instead of overflow. Serving to maintain reputation instead of expressing love.

We've become experts at spiritual self-deception. We measure ourselves by others and feel superior. We compare our public performance to our private struggles and think we're doing okay. We confuse knowledge about God with knowing God.

But Jesus is the "Faithful and True Witness" (v. 14). He sees through the performance. He knows the temperature of our hearts. And He refuses to flatter us into complacency.

The good news? This diagnosis isn't condemnation—it's an invitation. Jesus doesn't rebuke strangers; He corrects those He loves (v. 19). This reality check is actually mercy—because you can't fix what you won't face.

So today, let's get honest. Not in a shame-spiraling way, but in a hopeful, humble way. Ask Jesus to show you where your spiritual life has become routine instead of real. Where you've substituted familiarity for intimacy.

The mirror doesn't lie. But neither does the One holding it. And He's about to offer you everything you actually need.

**Reflection**: If Jesus were to write you a personal letter about your spiritual condition right now, what would He say? Where have you been fooling yourself?
