On the last night before He was crucified, Jesus went to a garden called Gethsemane and prayed until drops of sweat fell like blood onto the ground.

*"O My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as You will."* (Matthew 26:39, NKJV)

We sometimes read this and hear quiet resignation — the gracious acceptance of a foregone conclusion. But look more carefully. *"Let this cup pass from Me"* is a real request, an earnest plea from the Son of God who knew exactly what was coming and did not want it. He was not reciting a liturgy. He was praying the hardest prayer there is: *I would rather this not happen. But I trust You more than my own preference.*

And the second half of that sentence — *not as I will, but as Your will* — is not where the faith is. The first half is.

Faith that only says *"Your will be done"* without first admitting what it wishes weren't true is not faith — it is performance. Jesus' faith was real because it started with the honest request and ended with the honest release. He prayed the same prayer three times, returning each time to the same garden, kneeling in the same dark, and arriving at the same surrender. That repetition is not weakness. That is what faith under pressure actually looks like.

Revelation 14 describes the people of God in the last days as those who *"keep the commandments of God and have the faith of Jesus."* Not only faith *in* Jesus, though surely that too. The *faith of Jesus* — the kind He had. The Gethsemane faith. The trust that sweats, that asks honestly, that releases what it cannot hold.

Ellen White identified this as a truth the church had long understated: the commandments had been proclaimed clearly, but the faith of Jesus — what it looked like in His own life — had not been given equal weight. And what Gethsemane shows us is that His faith was not the absence of suffering or fear. It was the willingness to entrust the outcome to the Father even when the body was breaking.

Hebrews calls Jesus *"the author and finisher of our faith"* — or more literally, the founder and perfecter of faith. He is not only the one we trust in. He is the one who modeled what trust looks like when it costs everything.

Is there a prayer you have been afraid to pray honestly because it reveals what you would rather God not ask of you? The garden is the safest place to say it. Jesus already went there. He knows the ground.
