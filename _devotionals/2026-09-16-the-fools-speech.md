Paul begins the strangest passage in his letters by apologizing. He is about to defend himself with a list of credentials, and he wants everyone to know he hates doing it. He calls it foolishness. He does it anyway, because he loves the church too much not to. *"I have become a fool in boasting; you have compelled me."* (2 Corinthians 12:11, NKJV)

Then he lists what the false apostles cannot list. Not eloquence. Not spiritual pedigree. Not visions. Not miracles. Suffering.

*"In labors more abundant, in stripes above measure, in prisons more frequently, in deaths often. From the Jews five times I received forty stripes minus one. Three times I was beaten with rods; once I was stoned; three times I was shipwrecked; a night and a day I have been in the deep; in journeys often, in perils of waters, in perils of robbers, in perils of my own countrymen, in perils of the Gentiles, in perils in the city, in perils in the wilderness, in perils in the sea, in perils among false brethren; in weariness and toil, in sleeplessness often, in hunger and thirst, in fastings often, in cold and nakedness."* (2 Corinthians 11:23-27, NKJV)

Then, almost as an afterthought, the interior line that says everything: *"Besides the other things, what comes upon me daily: my deep concern for all the churches."* (v. 28, NKJV) The daily pastoral weight was heavier than the shipwrecks. The love that would not stop was more relentless than the rods.

This is not a résumé of superiority. Paul is not saying he suffered more than others and therefore is more important. He is saying that no imposter would endure this. No false apostle carries this list. The suffering authenticates him because it is the shape of a life actually following the suffering Servant. Jesus said the servant would not be greater than the Master. Paul had taken that literally. He had followed the Man of Sorrows into sorrow, and he wore the receipts.

*Ellen White* writes: *"The Lord does not lead any of His children otherwise than they would choose to be led, if they could see the end from the beginning, and discern the glory of the purpose which they are fulfilling as co-workers with Him."* The cost, seen through God's eyes, is never wasted.

When have you been asked to endure something that felt like too much? Paul does not glorify suffering. He names it. And he locates its meaning in following the Lord. What quiet cost are you carrying today that is silent witness that you are actually walking the Way?

*Ezra*
