The Greek word Paul uses for compels — *synechō* — is stronger than English can quite carry. It does not mean nudges. It does not mean motivates. It means presses in from every side, so that no other direction is available. Constrains. Leaves no room.

*"For the love of Christ compels us, because we judge thus: that if One died for all, then all died; and He died for all, that those who live should live no longer for themselves, but for Him who died for them and rose again."* (2 Corinthians 5:14-15, NKJV)

Read the sentence carefully. It is not our love for Christ that Paul says is doing the compelling. It is Christ's love for us. That is the pressure Paul feels. Not his own affection, which he knew came and went. The reality that One died for all, and all died in Him, and he could no longer live the small self-centered life he had lived on the way to Damascus.

Which changes what Christian obedience feels like from the inside. It is not primarily about generating enough love to serve. Most of us, when we honestly check, discover that our love is a leaky bucket. We refill it and it drains. Paul is not asking us to generate the fuel. He is telling us the fuel has already been poured in from outside, and it has closed every other direction we might have gone.

Jesus said what love looks like at its end. *"Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one's life for his friends."* (John 15:13, NKJV) That is the pattern. And it was not left as a description. It was completed on a hill outside Jerusalem. The love has done what love does. Now that love, having done its work, is what compels the people who receive it.

Paul says elsewhere that nothing can undo the pressure. *"Neither death nor life, nor angels nor principalities nor powers, nor things present nor things to come, nor height nor depth, nor any other created thing, shall be able to separate us from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."* (Romans 8:38-39, NKJV) The compelling love is not fragile. It is not seasonal. It is not conditional on your performance.

Which is why, when ministry costs — when the direction love is pressing you into is a direction you would rather not go — it is not proof that something has gone wrong. It is proof that the shape is right. The pattern of the Cross keeps showing up in the lives of the people the Cross has actually reached.

Ellen White writes: *"The love of Christ constrains us to labor for the salvation of souls. This love was manifested at infinite cost."*

Where in your life is Christ's love pressing you into a direction you would rather not go? What are you learning about His love — and yours — in that pressure?
