Jesus said something that should have been theologically explosive, but His audience's hearts were too hard to hear it: *"Do not think that I came to destroy the Law or the Prophets. I did not come to destroy but to fulfill."* (Matthew 5:17, NKJV)

Fulfill. Not repeal, not replace, not ignore. Every commandment that pointed forward — to justice, to holiness, to the kind of love that God Himself embodies — found its destination in the Person of Jesus. The law was always anticipating Him. He is what the law was always trying to describe.

Which is why Paul can say, seemingly in the same breath, two things that look contradictory: *"Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith apart from the works of the law"* (Romans 3:28) — and — *"Do we then make void the law through faith? Certainly not! On the contrary, we establish the law."* (Romans 3:31, NKJV)

These are not in tension. Justification is a verdict — "not guilty, acquitted, righteous before God" — and it is rendered on the basis of Christ's righteousness received through faith, not our performance achieved through effort. That is not the abolition of the law's standard. It is the acknowledgment that we could not meet it, and that Someone else met it for us.

Paul writes to the Galatians: *"Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us."* (Galatians 3:13, NKJV) He did not exempt us from the law's requirement; He absorbed the consequence of our failure to meet it. The law's demand was satisfied — just not by us.

And then — and this is what transforms the remaining commandments from burden to gift — Jesus says: *"If you love Me, keep My commandments."* (John 14:15, NKJV) Not: keep commandments so that you can love Me. Love first, obedience as expression. The order is everything. Obedience that precedes love is performance. Obedience that flows from love is worship.

The person who has genuinely received justification does not want to stop obeying. They want to start obeying from the right place — not from fear of the verdict, but from gratitude that the verdict has already been issued in their favor.

Are you obeying from fear, or from love? The law hasn't changed. But the motivation can.
