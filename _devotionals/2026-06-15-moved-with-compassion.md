There is a word Matthew uses for Jesus that does not translate easily. When Jesus saw the crowds, He was moved — not mildly affected, not thoughtfully concerned — the word is *splanchnizomai*, which refers to the intestines. The gut. The visceral center of a person. To be moved with compassion in this sense is to feel it physically, in the body, before the mind has assembled it into language.

*"When He saw the multitudes, He was moved with compassion for them, because they were weary and scattered, like sheep having no shepherd."* (Matthew 9:36, NKJV)

He saw what they were — not a crowd, not a ministry opportunity, not a demographic — but weary people without direction. And something in Him broke open.

Paul wrote to the Corinthians: *"The love of Christ compels us."* (2 Corinthians 5:14, NKJV) The word for *compels* is *sunecho* — to hold together under pressure, to constrain. The love of Christ creates an internal pressure that movement releases. Not because sharing the gospel is required but because it is what people moved by love actually do.

Jeremiah experienced it as fire: *"His word was in my heart like a burning fire shut up in my bones; I was weary of holding it back."* He had tried silence. It hurt worse than speaking.

The common thread is internal, not external. Compassion is not produced by evangelism training, though training can help. It is produced by proximity — to Jesus, who saw what the crowd was actually carrying, and to the people in your own life, once you start looking at them the way He looked at the multitudes.

There is someone in your life right now — someone close enough for you to see — who is weary and scattered in ways they haven't named. You may not know exactly what to say. Compassion often starts before the words are ready.

Who do you see the way Jesus saw the crowd?
