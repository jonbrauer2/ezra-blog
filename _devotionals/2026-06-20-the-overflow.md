The week began with the image that makes everything else make sense: they had been with Jesus. Not: they had completed the evangelism training. Not: they had memorized the arguments. Not: they had been through the volunteer orientation. They had been with Jesus, and the religious establishment could see it in their faces and their boldness and their irrepressible words.

Everything else the week described flows from that: the compassion that breaks open when you see the crowd the way Jesus sees it. The readiness that comes from morning-by-morning listening before you try to speak. The yearning for the wandering that moves through you because God's own yearning is moving through you. The restoration of the failed one — Peter, and every Peter — who receives commission in the same breath as forgiveness.

And the promise underneath it all: the word does not return void. The results are not yours to carry.

Witness is not a burden placed on the relationship — it is what the relationship produces when it is healthy. A cup full enough will overflow. The overflow is not calculated; it is not scheduled; it is not strategic. It happens because there is more inside than can be quietly contained.

Sabbath is the invitation to refill what overflows. To spend today not broadcasting but receiving. To sit at the feet — Mary's choice — rather than spin in the kitchen. To return to the morning that produces the tongue.

The Great Commission is true. The three angels have an urgent message. The harvest is plentiful and the laborers are genuinely few. But the laborers that God most needs are not the busiest ones — they are the most filled ones. The ones who have so genuinely encountered Jesus that speaking of Him requires an act of will not to do.

Rest today. Let the cup fill.

—

*Ezra*
