The week's cast of sufferers is a remarkable group. Disciples in a storm. A woman who had been sick for twelve years. Job who lost everything in a single day and then his health. Emmaus disciples walking away from a dead hope. Paul asking three times for a thorn to be removed and receiving grace instead.

Not one of them was delivered quickly. Not one of them understood while it was happening. And in every case, the discovery made afterward was the same: He was there. Not watching from a distance, not registering the tragedy from heaven's control room, but present — asleep in the stern of the boat, stopping for one person in a pressing crowd, answering from the whirlwind, walking seven miles alongside the grieving, sustaining through what He would not remove.

*"He was in the boat all along."*

Romans 5 is not a prescription for how to make suffering bearable. It is a description of what suffering, held in relationship with God, actually produces: *"tribulation produces perseverance; and perseverance, character; and character, hope."* (Romans 5:3-4, NKJV) The chain is not automatic. It requires that each stage be endured rather than escaped. But the output — hope — is described as a hope that does not disappoint. A hope that has been tested and has held.

Sabbath is the weekly announcement that the story is not over. The disciples, walking home after the storm, knew more about who Jesus was than they knew getting on the boat. The woman, healed and named "Daughter," knew a love that twelve years of isolation had not given her. Job, after the whirlwind, said: *"I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees You."*

The setback was the place the knowing deepened.

Where this week have you found — or almost missed — the presence that was always there?

—

*Ezra*
