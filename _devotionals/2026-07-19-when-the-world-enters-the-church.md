The situation at Corinth was so extreme that Roman pagans, who were not exactly famous for prudishness, would have named it criminal. A man in the church was sleeping with his father's wife. And the congregation was not scandalized — they were proud.

*"And you are puffed up, and have not rather mourned, that he who has done this deed might be taken away from among you."* (1 Corinthians 5:2, NKJV)

The word Paul uses is *pephysiōmenoi* — inflated, swollen. The Corinthians had turned tolerance into a spiritual credential. Look how open-minded we are. Look how gracious. We are the church that does not judge, the community where nothing is off-limits, the fellowship of radical acceptance. Paul is not impressed. He is horrified.

There is a lineage here worth tracing. When the Cross gets emptied of its content, factions form. When factions form, moral confusion is not far behind. Sin in the church is downstream of theology in the church. You do not usually get to unrepentant sexual scandal without first losing sight of the Cross.

And Paul's remedy is not a lecture. It is a return to identity. *"Purge out the old leaven, that you may be a new lump, since you truly are unleavened. For indeed Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us."* (1 Corinthians 5:7, NKJV) Notice the grammar. Be what you already are. You are unleavened; therefore purge the leaven. Grace precedes purity — but grace does not dispense with purity.

The most piercing sentence comes a chapter later. Paul lists sins that will not inherit the kingdom, and then he says: *"And such were some of you. But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God."* (1 Corinthians 6:11, NKJV) *Such were some of you.* Past tense. The identity has shifted. The ethics come downstream of the identity, not the other way around.

That is the difference between moralism and gospel. Moralism says: behave, and then belong. Gospel says: you belong — now, behave like it.

*Ellen White* writes: *"There is a large class who will regard as uncharitable any close discrimination between the precious and the vile."* (Great Controversy, p. 570) Uncharitable, indeed. But love that refuses to name sin is not love — it is the shrug that ends in ruin.

What have you started to be proud of tolerating in yourself — in the name of grace, of freedom, of maturity — that Scripture would still name plainly as sin? The question is not designed to shame you. It is designed to reintroduce you to the person you already are in Christ: washed, sanctified, justified. Live from there today.

—

*Ezra*
