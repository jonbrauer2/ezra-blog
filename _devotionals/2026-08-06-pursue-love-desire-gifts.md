Paul does not say choose between love and gifts. He gives two commands in the same sentence, and the order matters. *"Pursue love, and desire spiritual gifts, but especially that you may prophesy."* (1 Corinthians 14:1, NKJV) Both imperatives — but only one of them is the strong verb. *Diōkete* is the word for hunting, chasing, pursuing something you will not lose sight of. *Zēloute* is the softer verb for eager desire. You pursue love. Within the pursuit, you desire gifts. Get the order wrong and the whole framework distorts.

Then Paul explains what the gift of prophecy is actually for, and the definition is smaller and kinder than most modern imaginations of it: *"But he who prophesies speaks edification and exhortation and comfort to men."* (1 Corinthians 14:3, NKJV) Three effects. Building up, encouragement, comfort. Notice what is not on the list: prediction, sensation, spectacle, self-display. Prophecy in Paul's usage is Spirit-illumined speech that leaves the hearer more grounded, more courageous, more consoled. If your words have those three effects on people, you are already carrying more of this gift than you probably realize.

The chapter is thick with a single criterion. It shows up again and again like a metronome: *"Let all things be done for edification."* (1 Corinthians 14:26, NKJV) Six words. That is the whole test. In every public act of worship — every song, every prayer, every word from the front, every explanation, every silence — the question is whether the body is being built. When that criterion governs, the gifts serve their purpose. When it doesn't, the gifts start functioning like weapons.

Paul picks up the same idea in Ephesians and expands the horizon: *"And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers, for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ, till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God."* (Ephesians 4:11-13, NKJV) The gifts are given for the duration of a job that is not done yet. Until we all come to unity. Until we all come to the knowledge of the Son. Until the body reaches its measure. Nobody gets to lay down the tools while the building is still going up.

Ellen White wrote that "the special gifts of the Spirit are not the only talents represented in the parable. It includes all gifts and endowments." Every capacity you have — including the ones that feel ordinary — is a working tool for constructing what Christ died to build.

Here is a small assignment for the day. Somewhere in your circle is a person who needs one word of edification, exhortation, or comfort. You already know who. The Spirit has been quietly prompting it — a text you haven't sent, a phone call you keep postponing, an in-person conversation you have been rehearsing without initiating. Send the word. The gift is not for hoarding. It is for building.
