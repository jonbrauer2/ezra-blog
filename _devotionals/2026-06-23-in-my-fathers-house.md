Jesus spoke the words of John 14 on the worst night of His disciples' lives. He had just told them that one of them would betray Him, that Peter would deny Him three times before morning, that He was going somewhere they could not come. The atmosphere in the room was grief barely held together.

*"Let not your heart be troubled; you believe in God, believe also in Me. In My Father's house are many mansions; if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you."* (John 14:1-2, NKJV)

The context is important. These are not words spoken from a position of easy certainty. They are words spoken to terrified people, on the eve of a catastrophe, by Someone who knew exactly what was coming and offered stability anyway. *Let not your heart be troubled.* Not: there is nothing to be afraid of. But: there is One who is with you, and He is faithful, and what He prepares holds.

The ten virgins parable asks a sharper question than it first appears. All ten had lamps. All ten were waiting. All ten fell asleep when the bridegroom was delayed. The difference was not wakefulness — it was oil. Inner readiness that cannot be transferred. *"Give us some of your oil, for our lamps are going out."* And the wise said, in the kindest possible reading: there is not enough. You need your own.

The oil is not doctrine or church attendance or Sabbath observance — though none of those are irrelevant. The oil is the daily, private relationship with God that either is or is not there when the cry comes at midnight.

And the New Jerusalem, when it comes, comes *down* (Revelation 21:2) — not as an escape from earth but as God's movement toward it. The permanent address of eternity is not "up there." It is the renewed creation, with God Himself present as both Father and Home.

What oil are you tending today?
