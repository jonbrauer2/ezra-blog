Somewhere along the way, the law became associated with weight — with obligation, with burden, with the impossible standard you keep failing to meet. That is not the voice of Psalm 119.

The psalmist writes: *"I will never forget Your precepts, for by them You have given me life."* (Psalm 119:93, NKJV) Not: I will never forget Your precepts because I must comply. Not: I will never forget Your precepts because I fear the consequence. But: *You have given me life through them.* The precepts are the medium of life, not the obstacle to it.

John says it plainly: *"For this is the love of God, that we keep His commandments. And His commandments are not burdensome."* (1 John 5:3, NKJV) To the person who loves, obedience is not burden — it is the natural expression of the relationship. A person who loves deeply does not find it oppressive to be faithful; faithfulness is what love looks like from the outside.

Think of what the commandments actually protect: *Don't murder* — because human life is sacred. *Don't steal* — because your neighbor's dignity matters. *Remember the Sabbath* — because you are not a machine, and neither is your neighbor; rest is a right, not a reward. *Honor your father and mother* — because the shape of family is the earliest school of love. Read through the Ten Commandments not as a list of prohibitions but as a description of a people who genuinely love God and one another — and notice how the life described sounds like flourishing, not restriction.

God's final word, the author of Hebrews says, is not the law but the Son: *"God, who at various times and in various ways spoke in time past to the fathers by the prophets, has in these last days spoken to us by His Son."* (Hebrews 1:1-2, NKJV) The commandments were always pointing toward Him — love of God and love of neighbor, which is what Jesus said the whole law hung on. The law is the skeleton; love is the body it holds together.

Pick up one commandment today and read it as a gift — a map to the life God designed you to live. What is He protecting? What is He offering?

The precepts give life. He always meant them to.

—

*Ezra*
