Monday's devotional watched a king feed a prophetic roll to the fire — and watched the word come back rewritten, with more words added. Today the lesson calls the story "Part 2," and the sequel is quieter, and in its quietness more alarming. The last king of Judah, Zedekiah, never touched a penknife. He simply never opened the door.

He was twenty-one when the throne found him (2 Chronicles 36:11), already in the shadow of Babylon, with Jeremiah himself — the very prophet whose roll had been burned — still living and still preaching in Jerusalem. The record of his rebellion is one sentence long: *"He did evil in the sight of the Lord his God, and did not humble himself before Jeremiah the prophet, who spoke from the mouth of the Lord"* (2 Chronicles 36:12, NKJV). No bonfire. No mobs. Just a young man on a throne who would not bow his heart.

Notice how the sentence balances. Jeremiah, the lesson points out, was "only a man, a sinner like all the rest of us" — and yet his words are credited to "the mouth of the Lord." God has always spoken through breakable vessels. Moses stammered. Peter denied. The messenger's humanity is not a loophole; it is the whole test. Zedekiah could have found Jeremiah's flaws on any given day, if he needed an excuse. Humility would have heard the voice despite the vessel — and the text counts that humility as the hinge of a kingdom.

Behind Zedekiah stood centuries of the same knocking — the Lord *rising up early and sending* messengers, because He had compassion (2 Chronicles 36:15). And the record says they *mocked the messengers of God, despised His words, and scoffed at His prophets* — until there was *no remedy* (2 Chronicles 36:16). That phrase should terrify and comfort at once. Terrify, because refusal can become fixed. Comfort, because until that last sentence, every dawn meant another messenger was sent.

And this is the God we still meet: *"not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance"* (2 Peter 3:9, NKJV). Israel's story was written down for us (1 Corinthians 10:11) not so we could marvel at ancient hardness, but so we would not rehearse it.

So the lesson's question lands on us: they mocked the messengers of God — *surely, we today would never do anything like that... or would we?* The eye-roll at an imperfect messenger. The word from God filed under "not for me." Where is He knocking right now, through a voice you find inconvenient? Open the door while it is called today.
