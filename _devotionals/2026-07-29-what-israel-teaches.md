They had every advantage. A cloud that shaded them by day and glowed by night. A sea that opened in front of them. Bread from the sky, water from a rock. Paul says the wilderness generation was baptized in the cloud and the sea, and drank from a spiritual Rock that was Christ. If sacramental privilege could save, they were saved.

And yet: *"But with most of them God was not well pleased, for their bodies were scattered in the wilderness."* (1 Corinthians 10:5, NKJV)

Paul is not writing this to shame Israel. He is writing it to warn Corinth — and by extension, to warn us. *"Now all these things happened to them as examples, and they were written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the ages have come."* (1 Corinthians 10:11, NKJV) The wilderness is the church's mirror. What befell them can befall anyone who assumes that participation in the ordinances is proof against spiritual danger.

The sentence that lands in the middle of this passage is one of the sharpest in the epistle: *"Therefore let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall."* (1 Corinthians 10:12, NKJV) It is the confident who go down. Not because confidence is inherently wrong, but because confidence in the wrong thing — in one's baptism, one's church membership, one's theological pedigree, one's years of service — quietly replaces the daily dependence that actually keeps a soul upright.

Ellen White wrote that "those who feel called out to join the movement against God usually have a well-defined story to tell of their trust in the Lord and their faith in Him." The last words a fallen believer speaks are often religious ones. The sacraments are not talismans. The privilege is real; the immunity is not.

But the paragraph does not end in warning. It ends in a door: *"No temptation has overtaken you except such as is common to man; but God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make the way of escape, that you may be able to bear it."* (1 Corinthians 10:13, NKJV)

There is always a door. God builds one into every temptation He permits. But the door has to be looked for. The escape does not force itself on a soul distracted by rationalization. If you find yourself in a place where confidence has begun to feel like immunity, that is precisely where the door is. Turn your head. Look.

Where is your standing strongest, and what door has God already opened next to it that you have not yet noticed?

*Ezra*
