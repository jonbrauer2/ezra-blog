The last invitation in Scripture is one word, spoken three times in the same verse: *Come.* The Spirit says it. The bride says it. And then: whoever hears it is authorized to say it.

*"The Spirit and the bride say, 'Come!' And let him who hears say, 'Come!' And let him who thirsts come. Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely."* (Revelation 22:17, NKJV)

Every person who hears the invitation becomes a carrier of it. The transmission is built into the message: you cannot genuinely receive *Come* without becoming someone who says *Come* to others.

The invitation is inclusive beyond what human institutions tend to be. *Whoever desires.* Not: whoever has the right doctrine. Not: whoever has maintained the right practices for a sufficient period. Not: whoever has never failed. *Whoever desires.* The desire itself — the thirst — is sufficient qualification.

Isaiah heard the same invitation centuries before John: *"Ho! Everyone who thirsts, come to the waters; and you who have no money, come, buy and eat."* (Isaiah 55:1, NKJV) The economy of grace is astonishing: the thing being offered costs nothing, which means the only people who cannot receive it are the people who insist on paying for it themselves. The banquet is free, but you have to show up empty-handed.

Jesus took the same word and turned it toward the heavy and the burdened in Matthew 11: *"Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."* Same voice. Same invitation. Beginning of His ministry, end of Scripture — the word does not change.

And Revelation closes it: *"Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely."* You do not have to earn the thirst. You have it. That is enough.

Who in your life needs to hear one word from you today?
