Paul uses a phrase in the closing of 2 Corinthians that appears nowhere else in all his letters. Just once, at the very end: *"the God of love and peace."* (2 Corinthians 13:11, NKJV) Not the God who does love. The God who *is.*

The distinction is not academic. God gives us many things — peace, comfort, wisdom, hope, provision — but love occupies a different category. Love is not something God has in stock, ready to distribute. Love is the substance of who God is. When you draw close to God, you do not receive a portion of His love that He then passes out from a warehouse; you are drawn into an eternal current that never began and will never end.

John, at the end of a life spent leaning on the Lord's chest, says it plainly: *"God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God, and God in him."* (1 John 4:16, NKJV) Abide, not visit. Live inside. This is not a temporary shelter you access when things are hard. It is a permanent residence for the child of God, whether the child feels it in any given hour or not.

Consider what this means about the eternal life of the Trinity. Love, by its very nature, requires a subject and an object — someone loving and someone being loved. God has not just recently become love, as if creation gave Him something to love and thereby made Him loving. Before the world was made, love was already flowing — between Father, Son, and Spirit — endless, unhurried, uninterrupted. Creation did not begin the love. Creation was invited into it.

That is what the Cross does. It opens the door of the eternal love and pulls creatures in. You are not standing outside asking to be let in. You are inside — brought in by the blood of Christ and the seal of the Spirit — and asked simply to abide.

David wrote, before any of this had been fully unfolded: *"O God, You are my God; early will I seek You; my soul thirsts for You; my flesh longs for You in a dry and thirsty land where there is no water. So I have looked for You in the sanctuary, to see Your power and Your glory. Because Your lovingkindness is better than life, my lips shall praise You."* (Psalm 63:1-3, NKJV) Better than life. He was reaching for a category that had no room for anything but God Himself.

How would today feel different if you started it settled into that truth — not standing outside the eternal love asking to be let in, but standing inside it, already home, and only needing to remember?
