Samson's story never has the moment you'd expect — the dramatic scene where he turns his back on God and walks away. There is no crisis of faith, no visible apostasy, no night of decision. What there is: a series of ordinary compromises, each one so small it barely registers, until the day Delilah cuts the hair and the Spirit departs and Samson doesn't even notice.

*"He did not know that the LORD had departed from him."* (Judges 16:20, NKJV)

That may be the most chilling sentence in Judges. Not that he sinned. Not that he fell. But that he couldn't tell.

His gifts remained intact long after his relationship with God had hollowed out. He still had the strength. He still won the skirmishes. The Spirit still came upon him, for a while. He could feel the power without sensing the absence. And that combination — continued gifting with eroded character — is perhaps the most dangerous spiritual condition there is.

Jesus identified it too. *"Many will say to Me in that day, 'Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in Your name, cast out demons in Your name?' And then I will declare to them, 'I never knew you.'"* (Matthew 7:22-23, NKJV) The gifts were real. The fruit was visible. The knowing was absent.

The antidote — if you can call it that — is what Jesus modeled rather than theorized. *"Very early in the morning, while it was still dark, He departed to a solitary place, and there He prayed."* (Mark 1:35, NKJV) Not because He needed to prove something. Not because the schedule required it. But because the relationship was the point, and it needed tending — even for the Son of God.

Samson's drift didn't begin with Delilah. It began years earlier with small choices to seek what he wanted rather than who God was. By the time she was asking, the question was already answered. He had been telling his heart to lesser things for so long that he finally told her everything.

The question worth sitting with today is not "Am I sinning dramatically?" It is something quieter: *What is my heart currently telling itself?* And is the voice of God still audible above the noise?
