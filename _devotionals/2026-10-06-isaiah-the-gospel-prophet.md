The year King Uzziah died, everything in Judah felt unsteady. The throne that had held the nation together for fifty-two years stood empty — and Isaiah walked into the temple carrying that grief. What he saw there rearranged everything: *\"the Lord sitting on a throne, high and lifted up,\"* seraphim crying *\"Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of His glory\"* (Isaiah 6:1–3, NKJV). The earthly king was gone. The King of kings was not.

One honest look at that holiness undid the prophet. He did not compare himself to the corrupt court around him; he measured himself against God and cried, *\"Woe is me, for I am undone! Because I am a man of unclean lips\"* (Isaiah 6:5, NKJV). And God did not leave him there. A seraph flew with a live coal from the altar: *\"Your iniquity is taken away, and your sin purged\"* (Isaiah 6:7, NKJV). Only then came the question — *\"Whom shall I send, and who will go for Us?\"* — and only then the answer: *\"Here am I! Send me\"* (Isaiah 6:8, NKJV). Cleansing preceded commissioning. It always does.

Isaiah became the gospel prophet. Seven centuries before the cross, he described it: *\"He is despised and rejected by men... surely He has borne our griefs... He was led as a lamb to the slaughter\"* (Isaiah 53:3–7, NKJV). *\"All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD has laid on Him the iniquity of us all\"* (Isaiah 53:6, NKJV). No passage in Scripture lays out the substitutionary, saving heart of the gospel more plainly. The same prophet who saw the Lord high and lifted up saw the Servant lifted up for us all.

The calling was not easy — his warnings sounded preposterous to a people who called evil good and good evil (Isaiah 5:20). But Isaiah had learned the secret: the message carries weight only when the messenger has first been to the altar. *\"The strongest argument in favor of the gospel is a loving and lovable Christian\"* (Ellen G. White, *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 470).

The question still sounds through the temple courts this morning: *Whom shall I send?* Not, *who is talented?* or *who is qualified?* — *who has been cleansed, and is willing?* Come to the altar first. Then answer with Isaiah: Here am I. Send me.
