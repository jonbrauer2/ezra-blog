*"Get out of your country, from your family and from your father's house, to a land that I will show you"* (Genesis 12:1, NKJV). The call came without a map — no itinerary, no destination on record, only a promise and the God who gave it. And Abram went.

The world he left was drifting — scattered from Babel, drowning in idolatry and moral ambivalence. Into it God called one man to become an example of covenant faith, not just to his family but for all future generations. And at the heart of his story stands one astonishing sentence: *"And he believed in the LORD, and He accounted it to him for righteousness"* (Genesis 15:6, NKJV). Centuries before Sinai, the gospel in a single line — righteousness received by faith, never earned by works. Paul builds Galatians 3 on it: all who believe are blessed with believing Abraham.

The covenant ceremony that follows shows how salvation would actually be accomplished. Abraham falls into a deep sleep, and God — symbolized by the smoking oven and burning torch — alone passes between the pieces. The covenant does not rest on Abraham's performance but on God's promise, pointing forward to the sacrifice of Christ. And when the supreme test came, the father of the faithful was asked to surrender the very son of promise. He obeyed, *"concluding that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead"* (Hebrews 11:19, NKJV) — until the Lord stayed his hand and provided a ram. Every detail whispered ahead to another Father who would not spare His Son, but give Him up for us all.

This is what the prophets were for. Not merely to warn of doom, not only to confront apostasy and oppression, but above all to reveal the promise of salvation found in the Messiah. Every warning was wrapped around a gospel.

The parallels between Abraham's day and ours are hard to miss. Idolatry surrounded Abram; competing ideologies today press to eliminate or marginalize faith. Yet the remnant still *"keep the commandments of God and have the testimony of Jesus"* (Revelation 12:17, NKJV), carrying the three angels' messages to the world (Revelation 14:6–12). To be a defender of the covenant now looks less like a sword and more like a life — reflecting God's image in every ordinary interaction. Ellen White described the people such an hour requires: *"The greatest want of the world is the want of men — men who will not be bought or sold, men who in their inmost souls are true and honest, men who do not fear to call sin by its right name, men whose conscience is as true to duty as the needle to the pole, men who will stand for the right though the heavens fall"* (Education, p. 57).

That was Abraham. And that is the person God is forming in you.

So here is the question the starlit sky still asks: where is God calling you to trust Him without a map — and will you go on the bare word, *"to a land that I will show you"*?
