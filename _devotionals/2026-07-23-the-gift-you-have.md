Paul writes his most personal chapter on marriage and singleness in response to questions the Corinthians sent him. And the striking thing is how balanced he is. He honors both callings. He treats neither as second-tier.

*"For I wish that all men were even as I myself. But each one has his own gift from God, one in this manner and another in that."* (1 Corinthians 7:7, NKJV) Marriage is a gift. Singleness is a gift. Both are grace. Neither is a consolation prize for people whose real life did not turn out.

That is a countercultural sentence in almost every direction. Church cultures often quietly treat marriage as the default and singleness as a stage to be endured. The wider culture, in its opposite way, often treats commitment as a burden and independence as the real freedom. Paul refuses both errors. He looks at the person actually in front of him and says: the calling you are in — however you got there — is the calling in which God is fitting you for glory.

He generalizes the point a few verses later. *"Let each one remain with God in that state in which he was called."* (1 Corinthians 7:24, NKJV) That is not resignation. It is receiving. Contentment is the difference between hating your life and inhabiting it. The single life you did not choose. The marriage you sometimes wish were easier. The season in between that you were sure would be over by now. That is the life. That is where the Spirit is at work.

Paul does not pretend both callings are identical. He points to their different textures. Marriage, when it is lived as it is meant to be lived, becomes an icon. *"Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her."* (Ephesians 5:25, NKJV) A living picture of the gospel — Christ pouring Himself out for a bride He is sanctifying. Singleness, freed from the divided attentions Paul honestly names in 1 Corinthians 7:32-35, becomes a kind of concentrated availability to the Lord.

Neither is easier. Neither is inherently holier. Both belong to the same Lord, and both are graced by the same Spirit.

*Ellen White* writes about marriage: *"If those contemplating marriage would not have miserable, unhappy reflections after marriage, they must make it a subject of serious, earnest reflection now."* (Adventist Home, p. 45) The counsel could just as easily be given to those contemplating singleness in a marriage-oriented culture, or contemplating marriage in a culture that increasingly treats it as optional. Serious reflection. Because both callings are gifts, and both, unreflected upon, can be squandered.

Whatever life you are actually in right now — married, single, widowed, waiting — is the exact life in which God is at work. What are you resisting about it? What might you, today, begin to receive as gift?

—

*Ezra*
