**"Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside, redeeming the time. Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, that you may know how you ought to answer each one."** — Colossians 4:5-6

The watching world doesn't need more sermons. They need more witnesses.

Paul's counsel to the Colossians isn't about having all the right answers ready—it's about having the right *life* ready. Walking in wisdom means understanding that before anyone hears your words, they've already read your actions. Your patience in the checkout line. Your kindness when someone cuts you off in traffic. The way you treat the person who can do nothing for you.

"Those who are outside" aren't just watching for doctrinal correctness—they're watching for authenticity. Does this Christianity actually work? Does it make you more patient, more generous, more honest? The gospel you live will always preach louder than the gospel you speak.

But notice Paul doesn't stop at actions. Our speech matters too—"gracious, seasoned with salt." Grace means we speak with kindness, not condescension. Salt means we speak with flavor and preservation—words that are fitting, appealing, helpful. Not preachy platitudes, but genuine responses that reflect the Spirit's leading.

Here's the beautiful tension: we can't manufacture this on our own. Paul himself asked for prayer "that I may make it manifest, as I ought to speak" (v. 4). Even the apostle needed the Holy Spirit to give him the right words at the right time. How much more do we?

The world is watching your home, your workplace, your interactions. They're listening to how you speak about others when they're not in the room. They notice whether your faith makes you bitter or better, anxious or anchored.

**Your walk either honors God's name or causes it to be blasphemed** (Titus 2:5). That's sobering. But it's also hopeful—because every small act of Christlike love, every gracious word, every moment of Spirit-led wisdom becomes a doorway for the gospel.

You don't need to be perfect. You need to be real. And when you stumble (because you will), your humble acknowledgment of failure witnesses to the grace you preach.

Walk wisely today. Speak graciously. And trust the Spirit to make both count for eternity.

**Reflection:** Think about the person who will interact with you today who doesn't know Jesus. What will your walk and your words tell them about Him?

— Ezra
