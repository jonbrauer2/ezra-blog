It is one of the strangest admissions in the New Testament. The apostle who would eventually write half the letters we have — the missionary who took the gospel to three continents — describes his arrival in Corinth this way.

*"I was with you in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling."* (1 Corinthians 2:3, NKJV)

That is not a humble-brag. Paul is not fishing for reassurance. He is telling the Corinthians the actual internal state of the man they first met — and then telling them why it mattered. *"And my speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power, that your faith should not be in the wisdom of men but in the power of God."* (1 Corinthians 2:4-5, NKJV)

The form of his ministry matched its content. Christ had emptied Himself; Paul emptied his rhetoric. The Cross empties every boast; Paul walked into Corinth without one. His weakness was not an obstacle to the Spirit. It was the room the Spirit had to work in.

We tend to assume the opposite. We assume that impressive performance is required for God to move — that the well-designed slide deck, the polished delivery, the confident bearing are what make the gospel land. And so we arrive, on our small scales, in strength: rehearsed, protected, ready to be admired. And we wonder why the Spirit seems quiet.

Paul at Pisidian Antioch preached the Cross by walking through Israel's whole history (Acts 13:16-47). He did not perform. He located Christ as the climax of the story God had been telling for centuries. The power was not in Paul's presentation. It was in the Christ he presented.

Later he would write, more famously: *"My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness."* (2 Corinthians 12:9, NKJV) The sentence is not merely comfort for the discouraged. It is a description of the way divine power actually operates in the world. Strength perfected in weakness. The Cross-shape of every real ministry.

Where in your life or work have you assumed the door opens with a stronger performance? What if the door opens with a truer weakness — with arriving as who you actually are, and leaving room for the Spirit to fill?
