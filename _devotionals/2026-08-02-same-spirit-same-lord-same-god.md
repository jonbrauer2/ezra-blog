The Corinthians were ranking each other by gift. Paul answers by pointing at the Giver. There are diversities, yes — but the same Spirit. Different ministries — but the same Lord. Different workings — but the same God.

*"There are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit. There are differences of ministries, but the same Lord. And there are diversities of activities, but it is the same God who works all in all."* (1 Corinthians 12:4-6, NKJV)

Paul stacks three Greek words to describe the same reality from three angles. *Charismata* — gifts of grace, what you receive. *Diakoniai* — ministries, what you do with what you received. *Energēmata* — workings, what God does through the doing. Every gift is a Trinitarian event. The Spirit deposits, the Lord deploys, the Father accomplishes. From beginning to end, none of it belongs to the person holding it.

Which changes what a spiritual gift is. It is not personal property. It is not a merit badge. It is not a metric for comparison. Paul says, plainly: *"The manifestation of the Spirit is given to each one for the profit of all."* (1 Corinthians 12:7, NKJV) Every gift is directed outward. If it is turning inward — feeding your self-image, decorating your resume, distinguishing you from the person next to you — something has gone wrong at the source, because the source never intended that.

And notice the sovereignty at the end of the paragraph: *"But one and the same Spirit works all these things, distributing to each one individually as He wills."* (1 Corinthians 12:11, NKJV) The Spirit chooses. You did not audition for your gift. You did not accumulate enough spiritual capital to unlock it. You did not deserve it. It was given, freely, according to the Spirit's own discernment of what the body needed and where. Which means there is no room for pride if you have a visible gift, and no room for shame if you have a quiet one. Both are the Spirit's placement.

Ellen White wrote that "the Spirit was not given — nor can it ever be bestowed — to supersede the Bible; for the Scriptures explicitly state that the word of God is the standard by which all teaching and experience must be tested." The Spirit that gifts the church is the same Spirit that inspired the Word — never at war with Himself, always building the same body.

Paul also drops a small sentence near the beginning of the chapter that is worth pausing over: *"No one can say that Jesus is Lord except by the Holy Spirit."* (1 Corinthians 12:3, NKJV) The very confession that saves you is itself a gift. Before you ever notice what else the Spirit has placed in your hands, He has already placed the confession in your mouth.

What gift has He given you that you have been carrying as "mine" rather than as trust? Whatever it is — teaching, hospitality, prayer, administration, encouragement, the ordinary quiet fidelities that hold a church together — hand it back to the Giver today, then pick it up again as steward, not owner.

The body only works when the gifts remember whose they are.
