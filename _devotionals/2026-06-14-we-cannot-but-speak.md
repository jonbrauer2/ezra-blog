The religious council had done everything they could to silence Peter and John. They had put them in prison. They had threatened them. They had demanded they stop speaking in the name of Jesus. And the council noted something that surprised them — noted it, Luke records, as though it needed explanation:

*"They recognized that they had been with Jesus."* (Acts 4:13, NKJV)

That was the whole explanation for the boldness. Not training. Not technique. Not rhetorical skill. They had been with Jesus, and it showed.

Peter and John didn't agree to the council's demand. They said, simply: *"We cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard."* (Acts 4:20, NKJV) The Greek word is *dunamai* — we do not have the capacity. It is not that we have decided to disobey you; it is that we are incapable of the silence you're requesting. There is a fire in us that does not permit it.

Jeremiah knew the feeling: *"His word was in my heart like a burning fire shut up in my bones; I was weary of holding it back, and I could not."* (Jeremiah 20:9, NKJV) Jeremiah had tried silence. He had sat in it, decided it was safer, nursed his resentments in private. And discovered he couldn't sustain it. The word would not stay in.

This is witness as overflow, not as obligation. The council's command couldn't stop Peter and John not because they were unusually brave but because what was inside them had reached a pressure the silence couldn't contain.

John writes elsewhere: *"We love Him because He first loved us."* (1 John 4:19, NKJV) The love of God, received — genuinely received, not just acknowledged — produces exactly this kind of overflow. You don't speak because the evangelism schedule says to. You speak because something real happened to you, and the people around you are carrying weight that it might help.

What would fill your conversation naturally if the relationship were deeper?
