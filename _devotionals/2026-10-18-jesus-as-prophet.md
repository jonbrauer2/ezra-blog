For fifteen hundred years, Israel carried a promise the way a family carries an expected name. Moses said it plainly just before he died: *“The Lord your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from your midst, from your brethren. Him you shall hear”* (Deuteronomy 18:15, NKJV). A Prophet like Moses — a deliverer, an intercessor, a man who would speak with God face to face. Generation after generation scanned the horizon for Him.

Then Peter, standing in the temple not long after Pentecost, said the name out loud: the Prophet Moses spoke of was Jesus (Acts 3:22). And ordinary people had already begun to guess it. After the loaves and fishes by the lake, the crowd said, *“This is of a truth that prophet that should come into the world”* (John 6:14, KJV). They were more right than they knew.

But here is where the story bends. Jesus was not simply the next in the line of prophets — He was the One the whole line had been pointing to. Walking to Emmaus with two heartbroken disciples, *“beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself”* (Luke 24:27, KJV). The Prophet like Moses was the subject of Moses.

John goes further. The Word who was *“in the beginning with God”* is the One through whom everything was made (John 1:1–3). That means every *“and God said”* of Genesis 1 is the voice of Jesus. He who said, *“Let there be light”* is Himself *“the light of men”* (John 1:3–4). A prophet carries God’s message; in Jesus the Messenger and the message are one. Ellen White wrote, *“Christ in His self-denial, Christ in His humiliation, Christ in His purity and holiness, Christ in His matchless love — this is the subject that is to be presented to the world in every discourse”* —*Testimonies for the Church*, vol. 6, p. 54. Every true prophetic voice has never had another theme.

So Peter, who once heard the Father’s voice on a mountain, points us to something surer than even that experience: *“We have the prophetic word confirmed, which you do well to heed as a light that shines in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts”* (2 Peter 1:19, NKJV). The Prophet still speaks — through the written Word — and He is still light for people standing in the dark.

The One who spoke galaxies into existence is also the One who died for your sins. Today, when you open Scripture, don’t read it looking for information. Read it looking for Him. Moses’ old assignment has never been rescinded: *Him you shall hear.*
