Paul is worried, and he says so plainly. The Corinthians are receiving teachers who preach *"another Jesus whom we have not preached, or if you receive a different spirit which you have not received, or a different gospel which you have not accepted"* (2 Corinthians 11:4, NKJV). Not obviously another. Not one they would recognize as pagan. A close cousin. A convincing counterfeit.

He writes as a father anxious for his daughter's wedding day. *"For I have betrothed you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ. But I fear, lest somehow, as the serpent deceived Eve by his craftiness, so your minds may be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ."* (v. 2-3, NKJV) The comparison is deliberate. Paul reaches all the way back to Eden. The pattern is ancient. It is not shouted lies but whispered adjustments. Not open enmity but a helpful voice offering a slightly better version of what God said. A tiny displacement, and paradise was lost.

Then Paul says the thing that should sober every listener in every era: *"For such are false apostles, deceitful workers, transforming themselves into apostles of Christ. And no wonder! For Satan himself transforms himself into an angel of light. Therefore it is no great thing if his ministers also transform themselves into ministers of righteousness."* (v. 13-15, NKJV)

The most dangerous error does not arrive dressed as evil. It arrives dressed as light. It quotes Scripture. It uses the vocabulary. It appears in gifted people with pleasant demeanors and effective ministries. And it moves the center of gravity — just a little — off of Christ crucified, risen, and returning.

Jesus warned about this too: *"Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravenous wolves. You will know them by their fruits."* (Matthew 7:15-16, NKJV) The fruit is the tell. Not the presentation, not the platform, not the polished doctrine — the actual life produced downstream over time.

Discernment, in this letter, is not paranoid. It is pastoral. Paul is not urging the Corinthians to become suspicious of everyone. He is urging them to notice what has already been happening: they were putting up with subtle displacements from the Jesus of the gospel, and it was quietly corroding them.

What version of Jesus have you been quietly receiving — from books, podcasts, cultural currents, well-meaning voices — that might not be the Jesus of Scripture? What one specific belief needs to be re-examined against the actual gospel?

—

*Ezra*
