Jesus had a way of taking the law and moving it inward. *You've heard it said — don't murder. But I tell you, anyone who is angry with his brother is guilty. You've heard it said — don't commit adultery. But I tell you, anyone who looks with lust has already committed adultery in his heart.*

The first time you read the Sermon on the Mount this way, it can feel like Jesus is raising the bar beyond any possible clearing. And in a sense, He is. But that's not the whole point.

The Pharisees thought righteousness was a set of external behaviors they could perform without transformation. Keep the commandments. Wash the hands. Tithe the mint and dill. And they had done it. Carefully, demonstrably. Yet Jesus looked at them and saw whitewashed tombs.

What He is exposing in the Sermon on the Mount is not a higher standard for us to achieve — it's a more honest diagnosis of what we actually are without grace. The commandments, internalized, reveal not just what we do but what we are. And what we are, naturally, is people who harbor anger, nurse lust, perform generosity for an audience, and love those who love us back. The inward demands of the law don't crush us with impossibility; they show us what the gospel actually had to accomplish.

Paul puts it plainly: *"Through the law comes knowledge of sin."* (Romans 3:20, NKJV) The law is the mirror, not the cure.

But here is what Jesus says immediately after raising all these uncomfortable interior demands: *"Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God."* He is not withholding the sight of God from the impure and leaving them to figure it out. He is announcing what the Spirit's work produces — purity of heart — and promising what it leads to.

There is a kind of self-awareness the Sermon produces that has to precede grace, not follow it. You can't receive a remedy until you acknowledge you're sick. The inward law — the thought before the act — is the diagnosis that makes the gospel actually good news.

Paul again: *"Let anyone who thinks he stands watch himself so that he does not fall."* (1 Corinthians 10:12) Not as threat but as wisdom. The most dangerous spiritual position is confidence in our own interior cleanliness.

Bring one thought to the light today. Just one.
