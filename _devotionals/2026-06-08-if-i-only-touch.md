Twelve years. That is how long the woman had been hemorrhaging. Twelve years of waking up to the same condition, trying the same remedies, spending everything she had on physicians who left her worse than they found her. Twelve years of the ritual uncleanness that would have kept her at the edge of every gathering, one step back from every community she might have called home.

Mark tells us she had "suffered many things from many physicians" and had "spent all that she had and was no better, but rather grew worse." (Mark 5:26, NKJV) The resources exhausted. The hope appropriately narrowed. And then she heard about Jesus.

*"If only I may touch His garment, I shall be made well."* (Mark 5:28, NKJV)

That is not the faith of the undamaged. That is the faith of someone who has run out of other options and directed what remains toward the one Person who might be different. She pushed through a crowd that would not have welcomed her touch. She reached. She connected. And immediately the bleeding stopped.

And then — in a pressing crowd, with someone important waiting on the other end of the journey — Jesus stopped. *"Who touched Me?"* The disciples thought it was a strange question. Everybody was touching Him. But He felt the power go out.

He stopped for one anonymous person's need.

*"Daughter, be of good cheer; your faith has made you well."* (Matthew 9:22, NKJV) The first word is *Daughter*. She had been a condition for twelve years — a medical problem, a ritual category, an inconvenience. He made her a person. Then He made her whole.

Romans 5 says tribulation produces perseverance, and perseverance proven character, and proven character hope. (Romans 5:3-5) The chain is forged in suffering, not despite it. The woman's faith was not born in twelve easy years. It was the faith that survives twelve hard ones — diminished, battered, but still reaching.

Are you still reaching? He stops for one.
