A father and his son are driving along when the boy starts pointing out every car that looks like his dad's. At home, the father digs out two owner's manuals — the one from his first car and the one from the car they were just driving — and walks his son through the differences. Body styles, features, technology: almost everything has changed. But when he opens the hood and compares the two engines, they are nearly identical. Much had changed. Not the engine.

It is this week's Sabbath School illustration, and it marks the turn the quarter takes today. Last week we watched the prophetic gift pulsing through the Old Testament — Elijah's fire, Huldah's courage, Jeremiah's tears. Now the pages turn, and we find the same Engine running under the New Testament's hood. Hebrews says it in one breath: *"God, who at various times and in various ways spoke in time past to the fathers by the prophets, has in these last days spoken to us by His Son"* (Hebrews 1:1–2, NKJV). Different era, different messengers, same Spirit, same Christ at the center.

And the prophetic gift was alive and well in the early church. Anna, an eighty-four-year-old widow, prophesied over the infant Jesus in the temple courts. Philip had four daughters who prophesied. Judas and Silas, *"being prophets also themselves, exhorted the brethren with many words, and confirmed them"* (Acts 15:32, KJV). Paul could point a young minister back to "the prophecies which went before on thee" as the anchor for a lifetime of warfare and service. Prophecy was not a museum piece from Israel's past; it was load-bearing structure in the church, built *"upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone"* (Ephesians 2:20, KJV).

Because the message never moved. From Moses' promise of a Prophet like himself to Peter's confession at Caesarea Philippi — *"Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God"* (Matthew 16:16, KJV) — every true prophetic voice has pointed the same direction. Ellen White wrote, *"The central theme of the Bible, the theme about which every other in the whole book clusters, is the redemption plan, the restoration in the human soul of the image of God."*—*Education*, p. 125. One engine. Two Testaments. One message.

This week we will watch that engine at work: who the New Testament prophets were, what they actually did, how their calling related to the apostles', and how the church faced the old danger of false voices. But the week begins with a simpler claim worth sitting with: the God who spoke through the prophets of old has never stopped speaking — and every word He gives still runs on the same Spirit and centers on the same Son.

Pop the hood on your own life this week. Is the Engine the same one the prophets ran on?
