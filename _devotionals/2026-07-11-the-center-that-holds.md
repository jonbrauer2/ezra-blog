If you read Paul's letter to the Corinthians straight through, a strange thing happens. The problems keep changing — factions, lawsuits, immorality, meat offered to idols, disorder in worship, confusion about the resurrection — but the diagnosis stays the same.

The Cross had been decentered. Everything else was symptom.

Paul does not treat the Corinthians as separate problems requiring separate rules. He treats them as one problem wearing many faces. When the Cross is central, everything else finds its right place. When the Cross drifts, everything unravels — not in a dramatic collapse, but in a hundred small ways at once. The quarrels start. The tolerances begin to slip. The gifts get misused. The identity gets confused.

Which is why Paul, near the start of the letter, plants a flag: *"For I determined not to know anything among you except Jesus Christ and Him crucified."* (1 Corinthians 2:2, NKJV) That is not a slogan. That is the whole strategy. The entire letter is, in one sense, Paul saying to the Corinthians in twelve different registers: *look again at the Cross.*

The Cross is not one doctrine among many. It is the ordering reality. It is what tells you what to boast in, how to lead, how to love, how to eat, how to worship, how to bury and be raised. When it is the sun, the planets stay in orbit. When something else takes its place, the planets begin to drift into each other.

That is what Sabbath is for. Sabbath is not the day you catch up on rest before Monday. It is the weekly return to the center. Six days of doing things — most of them good, some of them urgent, all of them capable of quietly displacing the Cross from the middle of your life — and then a day when the noise is stilled and the center reasserts itself.

*Ellen White* writes: *"It would be well for us to spend a thoughtful hour each day in contemplation of the life of Christ. We should take it point by point, and let the imagination grasp each scene, especially the closing ones."* (Desire of Ages, p. 83) The closing ones. The Cross. The place where beholding does what all our striving cannot.

Rest in that today. And if the Cross were reintroduced as the actual center of your week — not a doctrine held, but the organizing reality of everything you do — what would look different by Monday morning?

—

*Ezra*
