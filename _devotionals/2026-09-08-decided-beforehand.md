Paul's instruction about giving is one word longer than it looks. He does not just say give; he says decide first, then give. *"Let each one give as he purposes in his heart, not grudgingly or of necessity; for God loves a cheerful giver."* (2 Corinthians 9:7, NKJV)

The Greek behind "purposes" is *proaireō* — to decide beforehand, to choose in advance. It is deliberate, prayerful, unpressured. Paul is not opposed to spontaneous generosity, but he is not idealizing it either. In fact, he seems to think planned giving is often more spiritual than reactive giving, because it reflects a heart that has already worked out what it believes about God, money, and mission before an appeal ever arrives.

Then comes the joyful surprise of the verse: *"God loves a cheerful giver."* The Greek word is *hilaros*, from which we get "hilarious." Paul envisions a giver who is having fun. Not gritting teeth. Not doing math with resentment. Actually enjoying the act. Because the giver has grasped what the money is for, and where it comes from, and who owns it in the first place, and the offering has become participation rather than deprivation.

Paul reaches for the field-yield analogy: *"He who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and he who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully."* (v. 6, NKJV) Generosity is not a leak; it is a seed. Money withheld does not accumulate into blessing; money released into God's purposes does. Solomon knew this centuries earlier: *"Honor the LORD with your possessions, and with the firstfruits of all your increase; so your barns will be filled with plenty"* (Proverbs 3:9-10, NKJV).

And Paul closes the thought with a promise that undercuts every fear that generosity might leave us short: *"God is able to make all grace abound toward you, that you, always having all sufficiency in all things, may have an abundance for every good work."* (2 Corinthians 9:8, NKJV) God funds His own project. He does not ask you to strain past capacity; He asks you to open a channel He is committed to keep filling.

How much of your giving is decided beforehand versus reactive? What would it look like to sit down this week, with prayer and a calendar, and settle next month's giving now — cheerfully, deliberately, before the next appeal arrives?
