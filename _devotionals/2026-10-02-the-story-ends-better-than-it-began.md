The best stories don't end where they began. They end where they were always headed. And the Bible — the longest story ever told — does exactly that. It opens with a voice in the dark: *"Then God said, 'Let there be light'; and there was light"* (Genesis 1:3, NKJV). It closes with a voice on a throne: *"Behold, I make all things new"* (Revelation 21:5, NKJV). Between those two sentences stretches every human sorrow. And yet the ending is brighter than the beginning.

That is Peter's astonishing claim. Writing to scattered, suffering believers, he insists: *"Nevertheless we, according to His promise, look for new heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells"* (2 Peter 3:13, NKJV). Righteousness does not merely visit that world; it *dwells* there — it is finally at home. And the God who spoke the first heavens into existence has promised, by the same voice, to speak the second. *"Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words will by no means pass away"* (Matthew 24:35, NKJV). His word made a world. His word will make it new.

This week we have been tracing that voice. It spoke creation out of nothing, called hidden sinners out of the bushes, ran down the road to meet a prodigal, and finally took on flesh — *"God, who at various times and in various ways spoke in time past to the fathers by the prophets, has in these last days spoken to us by His Son"* (Hebrews 1:1–2, NKJV). And the voice has never fallen silent. That is the foundation beneath this new quarter on the gift of prophecy: God's communication was not limited to Bible times. He still speaks — preeminently in Scripture, personally through the Spirit, faithfully through the prophetic gift — until the day the voice needs no medium at all.

Ellen White knew the difference between hearing God wrongly and hearing Him rightly. Terrified of a "stern tyrant," she described the moment grace broke through: *"My views of the Father were changed. I now looked upon Him as a kind and tender parent, rather than a stern tyrant compelling men to a blind obedience. . . . My heart went out toward Him in a deep and fervent love."* (Life Sketches of Ellen G. White, p. 39). That is what the Creator's voice always does to an open heart: it turns dread into love. And the ending outshines Eden itself, for in the New Jerusalem *"the tabernacle of God is with men"* (Revelation 21:3, NKJV) — and to every listening ear He says, *"Surely I am coming quickly. Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus!"* (Revelation 22:20, NKJV).

So here is Friday's question: what would change today if you lived as someone who has heard the Creator's voice — and knows how the story ends? Fear loses its grip. Obedience becomes love. And "come, Lord Jesus" stops being poetry and becomes a plan. He has been speaking to you all week. Say something back.
