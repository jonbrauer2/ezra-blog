There are seasons when faith feels like a light left on in the next room — present, warm, but not quite where you are. And then there are seasons when that light seems to have simply gone off. You reach for the familiar assurance and find nothing there.

What do you do with that?

Paul writes to the Ephesians: *"For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God."* (Ephesians 2:8, NKJV) That is not, on its face, a particularly emotional sentence. What it is saying, quietly and precisely, is this: the faith that saves you did not originate with you. It was given.

Elsewhere Paul writes that God has *dealt* to every person *a measure of faith* (Romans 12:3). The word is the language of distribution — like a hand of cards dealt from a deck. No one at the table received nothing. Everyone was given something to work with. Which changes the question: not *How do I generate more faith?* but *What am I doing with what I have already been given?*

Jesus said that faith the size of a mustard seed — the smallest thing you could hold between your fingers — could move a mountain. He is not describing a technique for the spiritually exceptional. He is describing the nature of faith's power: it is not in its quantity but in its object. A small key can open a great lock. The key doesn't need to be large; it needs to fit the right door.

Ellen White captures this: *"The unbelief which is cherished in the soul has a bewitching power... The soul must have the precious plants of faith and love put in the soil of the heart."* What she describes is not a performance but a tending — and the faith-plant itself was placed there by God. Our work is to tend what He gave, not to manufacture what He hasn't.

So when faith feels absent, it is worth asking: Is it absent, or is it quiet? A feeling fades in and out. A gift, though, is still there when you cannot feel it.

Hebrews 12 points the direction: fix your eyes on Jesus, the author and perfecter of faith. Not on the faith itself. Not on your experience of faith, which will rise and fall with your circumstances. On the One who gives it and sustains it.

The invitation is not to manufacture trust through force of will. It is simpler and harder than that: to direct your attention, again and again, toward the One who is faithful — even when the feeling has gone ahead without you.

What would it look like today to act on what you believe, even before you feel it?
