Paul had three legitimate rights and he set them down. Financial support from the churches he planted. The companionship of a wife on mission. Freedom from working with his hands to feed himself. All three were his by any fair reckoning — he lays out the case for each with lawyerly clarity in 1 Corinthians 9. And then he explains why he refused to press any of them.

*"For though I am free from all men, I have made myself a servant to all, that I might win the more."* (1 Corinthians 9:19, NKJV) Not compromise. Not fatigue. Not martyr complex. Strategic self-denial in the service of someone else's clearer view of Christ.

This is not the language of a man asked to give up sin. It is the language of a man giving up goods — legitimate, permissible, God-given goods — because the gospel's advance required it. The three things Paul relinquished were not the wrongs he had to leave behind. They were the rights that had to become subordinate to something larger. That distinction matters, because most of us are still trying to decide whether the sin should go. Paul is asking a question further downstream: what am I willing to surrender that I could have justly kept?

His conclusion is the sentence that has haunted every honest missionary since: *"I have become all things to all men, that I might by all means save some."* (1 Corinthians 9:22, NKJV) The content is fixed — the cross does not shift. But the form is fluid. Paul is willing to speak Greek to Greeks and eat kosher with Jews and work leather when the money is thin, all so nothing in his lifestyle becomes the reason someone missed Jesus.

Philippians pushes the pattern back to its source. Christ, *"being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but made Himself of no reputation."* (Philippians 2:6-7, NKJV) He had every right of divinity and He set them down. The kenōsis of the Son is the shape of every faithful ministry. If the Head laid down His rights to reach us, the body cannot cling to its rights to protect itself.

Somewhere in your life is a right you are entitled to hold. A grievance you have earned. A privilege you have accumulated. A comfort you have arranged. What would it look like to lay it down — not because it is wrong, but because someone else's soul might come clearer if it were out of the way?

The gospel travels light. So do the people who carry it.

—

*Ezra*
