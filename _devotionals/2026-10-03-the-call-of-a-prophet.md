Every prophet's story begins the same way: not with a shout, but with a summons. Abram in Ur. Elijah on Carmel. Daniel in the palace school of Babylon. John in the wilderness. None of them volunteered; every one of them was called. And the call tended to come exactly when God's people were drifting — at the monumental moments when someone needed to speak a word strong enough to turn them around.

That is the journey this new week opens. Lesson two of our quarter on the gift of prophecy surveys five of those moments and the voices God raised in them: the believer who left home on a bare promise, the prophet who stood alone against four hundred fifty, the courtier who prayed with his window open, the wilderness preacher who pointed past himself to the Lamb.

But the window into all of them is Isaiah's call. In the year King Uzziah died, he saw the Lord high and lifted up, heard the seraphim cry *"Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts,"* and knew at once what he was: *"Woe is me! For I am undone! Because I am a man of unclean lips"* (Isaiah 6:5, NKJV). Then came a coal from the altar, the touch of cleansing — and the question heaven is still asking: *"Whom shall I send, and who will go for Us?"* Isaiah's answer has echoed for twenty-seven centuries: *"Here am I! Send me"* (Isaiah 6:8, NKJV).

Scripture is honest about what such a calling costs. God's prophets were ignored, ridiculed, beaten, jailed; some were stoned, hunted, killed. And behind every one of them stands the Servant Isaiah saw — *"despised and rejected by men, a Man of sorrows and acquainted with grief"* (Isaiah 53:3, NKJV). The greatest Prophet was Himself the Message, and the world's answer to His preaching was a cross.

The lesson's author leaves us with a double call: to heed the voice of God's prophets *and* to be messengers of the hope given through them. Heed — and herald. In a generation flooded with violence, greed, and confusion, God has not gone silent. Ellen White wrote, *"Little heed is given to the Bible, and the Lord has given a lesser light to lead men and women to the greater light"* (Selected Messages, book 1, p. 78). The prophetic gift still leads to Scripture, and Scripture still leads to Christ.

So here is the Sabbath question: if the Lord asked tonight, *"Whom shall I send?"* — what would your first instinct be? Excuses come easily. But the call has never required perfection. It required of Abraham only faith, and of Isaiah only a cleansed heart willing to go. It asks of us the oldest answer of faith: *"Here am I."*

*Ezra*
