The week began with a sigh.

Jesus, looking at the Pharisees who had demanded a sign after all they had seen, breathed out that anguished groan from deep in His spirit. It was grief, not irritation — the grief of love that has offered everything and is still being asked for something different.

From that sigh, the week unfolded: a Canaanite woman who would not give up, finding grace in the crumbs that fell from the table. A centurion who reasoned his way to trust with a soldier's unpretentious logic. A father who prayed the truest prayer — *Lord, I believe; help my unbelief* — and received both the help and the healing. A hall of saints who died without seeing the promise, and walked by faith anyway through things the world was not worthy of. A garden in Gethsemane where the Son of God sweated and surrendered and prayed the same prayer three times, arriving each time at the same release. And then, beneath it all, a love that has been drawing since before you knew you were being drawn.

If there is one thread running through all of it, it is this: faith's power is not in its intensity. It is in its Object.

The mustard seed does not move the mountain because it is strong. It moves the mountain because it connects to the One who is. The Canaanite woman was not impressive in the conventional sense — she was persistent, honest, and directionally right. The centurion did not have great theological credentials — he had a great understanding of where authority actually lived. The father did not have pristine faith — he had the kind that tells the truth about itself and keeps asking.

Sabbath is not the day to audit how much you managed to believe this week, or to generate enough faith for the week ahead. It is the invitation to stop generating altogether — and to sit, still and unhurried, in the presence of the One who is faithful when you are not.

*"Here is the perseverance of the saints, those who keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus."* (Revelation 14:12)

The faith of Jesus is your inheritance. It was modeled in a garden, purchased at a cross, and made available to every person who brings what little they have to the only One who can do something with it. Even the smallest, most cracked, most honestly-confessed faith — brought to the right place — is enough.

You were drawn here by love. Rest in that today.

—

*Ezra*
