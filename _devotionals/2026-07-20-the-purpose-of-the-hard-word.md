Paul's language about the Corinthian offender is startlingly severe. *"Deliver such a one to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, that his spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord Jesus."* (1 Corinthians 5:5, NKJV) Then, in a later letter, we hear the rest of the story — and it turns out the severity was in service of something tender.

*"You ought rather to forgive and comfort him, lest perhaps such a one be swallowed up with too much sorrow."* (2 Corinthians 2:7, NKJV) The discipline had worked. The man had repented. And Paul, who had once called for excommunication, now urges the church to complete the arc by receiving him back.

That arc is the whole shape of church discipline as Paul understood it: discipline, then repentance, then restoration. It is not designed to end at the discipline. It is designed to end at the restoration.

Churches fail this arc in three predictable ways. Some skip the discipline entirely — call everything grace, refuse to speak the hard word, and watch the community rot from within. Some discipline but never restore — they know how to remove a person and never learn how to welcome one back. Some drift into a permanent chill, unable to complete the restoration once repentance has come, because they never really believed the person could change.

Paul refuses all three failures. His language in Galatians makes the goal plain: *"Brethren, if a man is overtaken in any trespass, you who are spiritual restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness, considering yourself lest you also be tempted."* (Galatians 6:1, NKJV) The word *restore* is again *katartizō* — the fisherman's word for mending. Restoration is the mission. Harshness is not a virtue. And notice the humility clause at the end: *considering yourself lest you also be tempted.* No confrontation without the awareness that you could be the one on the ground next time.

Jesus told a similar story. The father in Luke 15 did not lower the standard; he ran to meet the son. The son had to come to his senses. But once he did, the robe and the ring were not withheld pending a probationary period.

*Ellen White* writes: *"Christ's method alone will give true success in reaching the people. The Saviour mingled with men as one who desired their good. He showed His sympathy for them, ministered to their needs, and won their confidence. Then He bade them, 'Follow Me.'"* (Ministry of Healing, p. 143) The sympathy is not softness. It is the ground on which the harder word can be received.

When you have needed to speak hard truth to someone — or receive it — where did love and restoration show up in the process? Or where did they get lost? A conversation that ended in shame instead of repair is not finished yet. The arc can still be completed.
