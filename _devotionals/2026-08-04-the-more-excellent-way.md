Between two long chapters about spiritual gifts, Paul drops a sentence that changes everything: *"and yet I show you a more excellent way."* (1 Corinthians 12:31, NKJV) Then he writes the chapter that has been read at more weddings and more funerals than any other in his letters. Chapter 13 is not a lyrical detour. It is the load-bearing hinge of the whole book.

Paul begins with the spiritual credentials the Corinthians most admired and voids each one in turn. *"Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become sounding brass or a clanging cymbal."* (1 Corinthians 13:1, NKJV) Angelic tongues without love: pagan racket. Prophecy plus mysteries plus mountain-moving faith without love: nothing. Even giving away everything and burning at the stake — without love, no profit. Three "nothings" in three verses. Without love, the spiritual life is not merely diminished. It is voided.

Then the chapter shifts from what love is not to what love does. Paul uses fifteen verbs. Love is a verb, or rather, a whole verb-list of small deliberate actions — patience, kindness, refusal of envy, refusal of self-display, refusal of a running ledger of wrongs. Love does not congratulate itself on wrongdoing. Love rejoices with the truth. Love bears, believes, hopes, endures. *"Love bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things."* (1 Corinthians 13:7, NKJV)

Read those verbs slowly and something becomes obvious: love is not a feeling you summon. It is a set of choices you make regardless of how you feel. You can be patient when your feelings are not. You can be kind when your temperament is not. You can refuse to keep a ledger when your memory is very willing to keep one. Every verb on the list is available to a will that has been submitted to the Spirit, and none of them require the emotional accompaniment before they can be practiced.

Ellen White wrote that "love is the golden chain which binds human hearts to one another, and which binds the human to the divine. Love is the atmosphere of heaven." Not one of the atmospheres among many. The atmosphere. The stuff the redeemed will breathe forever, and the stuff a Spirit-filled life begins breathing now.

The devastating exercise is to read verses 4-7 slowly, replacing "love" with your own name. Ezra is patient, Ezra is kind, Ezra does not envy, Ezra does not parade himself. Somewhere the sentence will tell the truth. Somewhere it will need to become truer. The point is not shame — it is diagnosis. The Spirit does not read the list to condemn; He reads it to conform.

Pick one verb. Not fifteen — one. Practice it this week with one specific person. Not as aspiration, as action. That is how the more excellent way begins to run through a life.

—

*Ezra*
