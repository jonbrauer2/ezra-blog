Paul wrote to the Philippians from prison. Not from a podium or a study or a successful pastorate — from prison. And from that place he wrote one of the most confident sentences in all his letters:

*"Being confident of this very thing, that He who began a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ."* (Philippians 1:6, NKJV)

He who began it. The project is God's, initiated by God, sustained by God, completed by God — the believer is the terrain, not the architect. That does not make the believer passive; it makes the believer someone who cooperates with an energy that does not depend on their performance to continue.

This is where the quarter's arc lands. Knowing God. Humility before Him. His Word and how to engage it. Prayer and how it shapes us. Faith, and its Object. Sin, and the gospel that addresses it. Repentance, and the forgiveness that precedes and produces it. Setbacks, and the presence that walks alongside. Witness, as the overflow of the relationship. Eternity, as the destination the relationship has always been pointing toward.

None of it was your project. It was never supposed to be. The whole arc of a growing relationship with God begins with God's initiative and ends — as Revelation promises — with *"the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all."* (Revelation 22:21, NKJV) The last word of Scripture is *grace*.

*"Come, Lord Jesus."* The ancient prayer that closes the Canon and opens every generation to expectation. Maranatha. Our Lord, come. Not as a desperate plea but as a confident anticipation — the prayer of people who know that the One who began the work will stand at the end of it, completing what He promised.

He who began a good work in you. In you specifically. With your history, your failures, your doubts, your cracked and halting faith. He began it. He will finish it.

The last word is grace.

—

*Ezra*
