A mirror doesn't heal. It shows. And the most unsettling mirror in the world is one that shows you not just what you've done but what you are.

Paul writes: *"Through the law comes knowledge of sin."* (Romans 3:20, NKJV) Not correction. Not cure. Knowledge. The law diagnoses what no amount of self-examination can reliably surface, because the very instrument we use to examine ourselves is itself compromised. Jeremiah knew this: *"The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked; who can know it?"* (Jeremiah 17:9, NKJV) We are not objective observers of our own condition.

That's why the mirror of the law is a gift, not a punishment.

David understood it after the worst year of his life. After the affair, the cover-up, the arranged death of a loyal soldier, the long silence that preceded Nathan's visit — David sat down and wrote: *"Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin my mother conceived me."* (Psalm 51:5, NKJV) He is not blaming his mother. He is naming something deeper than his recent sins: a condition beneath the behaviors. Sinfulness, not just sins.

John picks up the same thread: *"Sin is lawlessness."* (1 John 3:4, NKJV) Not merely the breaking of rules, but the posture of self-governance — the declaration, however subtle, that I am the final authority over my own life. Every sin is, at its root, a tiny sovereignty claim.

And this is why the mirror, despite its severity, is something to be grateful for. It shows us what we could not have diagnosed on our own. And it shows us the right place to go: not to more effort, not to better performance, but to the Physician.

The law's ultimate direction is not condemnation but love. Jesus said the whole law and the prophets hang on two commands: love God and love your neighbor (Matthew 22:40). The commandments are love's skeleton — the structure that gives shape to what love actually looks like in practice. They don't produce love; they describe it. And they show us how far short we fall, so that when grace meets us, we know what we are receiving.

Look honestly in the mirror today — not to despair, but to be ready for the Physician.
