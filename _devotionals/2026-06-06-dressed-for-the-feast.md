The week began with Mary sitting still at Jesus' feet while Martha spun in productive anxiety. It ends here — at Sabbath — with an image of a feast.

A feast that is not earned. A garment that is not woven. An invitation that goes out into the roads and lanes, to people who had no standing to expect it.

This is the shape of repentance and forgiveness when they are understood correctly. Repentance is not the tunnel we dig to get to God — it is the door we stop blocking so He can come through. Forgiveness is not the reward for adequate remorse — it is the prior declaration that makes repentance possible at all. *"The goodness of God leads you."* Not the judgment of God. Not the tally of God. The goodness.

The Spirit has been at work all week — convicting, not condemning; leading, not compelling; showing goodness, not threatening punishment. And the garment — Joshua's rich robe, the fine linen of Revelation 19, the skin-garment God made in the garden — has been offered to whoever would stop weaving their own.

Sabbath is the feast day for people who arrived wearing Someone else's righteousness.

Not people who have it all together. Not people who have successfully completed their spiritual to-do list. But people who said *"God, be merciful to me, a sinner"* — one sentence, no extras — and went home justified.

*"If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."* (1 John 1:9, NKJV) The promise is conditional on confession and unconditional in its scope. All unrighteousness. Every fiber of the fig-leaf garment you sewed in the panic of shame — taken away, replaced.

Rest today as a person who has been dressed by the Host. You are seated at the table not because you earned your chair but because the invitation went out and you came.

—

*Ezra*
