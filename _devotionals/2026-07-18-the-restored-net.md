The word Paul used for unity in Corinth was the word fishermen used for their nets. *Katartizō.* To mend, to restore, to bring back to proper working condition. Not to invent a new net — to repair the one that had always been meant to hold together.

That single verb quietly reframes the whole week. *"Now I plead with you, brethren, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that you all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you, but that you be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment."* (1 Corinthians 1:10, NKJV) The unity Paul is calling the Corinthians to is not a novelty. It is a return.

The Cross does not merely forgive individuals. It forms a people. That is the harder half of the good news, and the half most easily missed. If the Cross touched only your record and left the community untouched, something would still be very wrong. But the same Cross that levels every private boast is also the seam by which strangers are joined into a body.

The four factions — I am of Paul, I am of Apollos, I am of Cephas, I am of Christ — were not, at bottom, a leadership problem or an information problem. They were a Cross problem. When Paul re-centered the Corinthians on Christ crucified, the factions had nothing to feed on. When each believer's boasting has been silenced at Calvary, there is nothing left to compete about.

Solid food is the food that sustains this kind of community — servanthood, cruciform leadership, the willingness to bear the marks. The mind of Christ is its shared orientation. The prayer of John 17 is its final horizon: *"that they also may be one in Us, that the world may believe that You sent Me."* (John 17:21, NKJV)

Sabbath is a foretaste of that oneness. The day when work stops, when the noise dies down, when the ways we distinguish ourselves from each other lose their grip. In heaven, every "I am of ___" will be silenced by a single Name. The Sabbath is the weekly rehearsal.

*Ellen White* writes: *"God is leading out a people to stand in perfect unity upon the platform of eternal truth."* (Testimonies, vol. 4, p. 17) A people, not a scattered collection. A platform, not a preference. Perfect unity, not managed disagreement.

If unity is the world's evidence of the Father's love, then every small restored relationship is a small proclamation. Whose name comes to mind — the person you have been meaning to reach out to, the one you have quietly written off, the one you keep meeting with a slight defensiveness? Pray for that name by name today. And then, perhaps, take one small step toward mending what was torn. The Cross is what makes such repair possible. Sabbath is a good day to begin.

—

*Ezra*
