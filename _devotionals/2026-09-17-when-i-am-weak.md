Paul does not tell us what the thorn was. He does tell us what it did: it hurt, it lingered, and it made him beg. Three times he asked God to remove it. Three times God said no — and then said something that reordered his entire theology of ministry.

*"And lest I should be exalted above measure by the abundance of the revelations, a thorn in the flesh was given to me, a messenger of Satan to buffet me, lest I be exalted above measure. Concerning this thing I pleaded with the Lord three times that it might depart from me. And He said to me, 'My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.'"* (2 Corinthians 12:7-9, NKJV)

Notice the purpose. The thorn was given specifically to prevent pride. Paul had been caught up into the third heaven; he had seen things unlawful for a man to utter. The visions were real, and they were dangerous. Anyone who has ever had a genuine spiritual experience knows how quickly it can be turned into a platform. God, who loved Paul, put a limp in his gait so that the visions would not destroy him. The thorn was a mercy in disguise.

Paul begged anyway. That is important. Persistent prayer for relief is not unfaithfulness. Christ Himself begged in Gethsemane. It is legitimate to ask for the cup to pass. What Paul discovered — what Jesus discovered before him — is that sometimes the answer is a deeper cup, filled with a stronger grace. *"My grace is sufficient for you."* Not "I will remove the thorn." Not "You must simply endure." Something better: My grace is enough. The thorn and the grace, together, will produce something the removal alone never could.

And then Paul turns the corner that changes everything: *"Therefore most gladly I will rather boast in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me... For when I am weak, then I am strong."* (v. 9-10, NKJV) He is not glorifying weakness for its own sake. He is saying the weakness became the vessel — the exact thing he had wanted removed became the place the power took up residence.

Isaiah had said something similar centuries earlier: *"He gives power to the weak, and to those who have no might He increases strength."* (Isaiah 40:29, NKJV) Paul had lived his way into the verse.

What "thorn" have you begged God to remove, and He has not? Sit with the possibility that the thorn and the grace together may be more than the thorn alone would be — that God is not withholding from love, but providing a grace precisely shaped to the wound He has not yet healed.
