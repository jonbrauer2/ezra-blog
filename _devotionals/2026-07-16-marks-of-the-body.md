When Paul finally listed his apostolic credentials for a church that wanted a more impressive résumé, he did not list what they expected.

*"In labors more abundant, in stripes above measure, in prisons more frequently, in deaths often. From the Jews five times I received forty stripes minus one. Three times I was beaten with rods; once I was stoned; three times I was shipwrecked; a night and a day I have been in the deep."* (2 Corinthians 11:23-25, NKJV)

He goes on. Perils of robbers, perils of rivers, perils of his own countrymen, perils in the city, perils in the wilderness, perils in the sea, perils among false brethren. Sleeplessness, hunger, thirst, cold. That is Paul's résumé. That is what he brings when the Corinthians ask him to prove his credentials against the more polished, more confident, more well-fed apostles who had come after him.

This is not self-pity. It is not a plea for sympathy. It is a description of what Cross-shaped ministry actually looks like on a body.

And then he goes further in another letter. *"I now rejoice in my sufferings for you, and fill up in my flesh what is lacking in the afflictions of Christ, for the sake of His body, which is the church."* (Colossians 1:24, NKJV) Fill up what is lacking. Not because Christ's atoning work was insufficient — Paul is not talking about salvation. He is talking about the ongoing mission of a suffering Messiah in a world that still resists Him. That mission continues in His people. It is participatory, not substitutionary. What the body of the Lord bore, the body of the follower carries forward.

To the Galatians Paul writes, more compactly still: *"From now on let no one trouble me, for I bear in my body the marks of the Lord Jesus."* (Galatians 6:17, NKJV) *Stigmata.* Actual scars, from actual beatings, in actual towns. Those are his letters of authority. They are the ones the false teachers cannot forge.

Cruciform character is what makes leadership worthy of being followed — often more than theological correctness alone. A shepherd who has never bled for the flock will not be trusted by the sheep when the wolf comes.

*Ellen White* writes: *"The path of self-denial and sacrifice which He trod, we must tread. There is no other way of following in His footsteps."* (Signs of the Times, October 3, 1892)

What in your walk with Christ has actually cost you something? Not spectacularly — the small marks count. The friendship you did not pursue because it would have compromised. The advancement you did not chase because it would have absorbed you. The truth you spoke and paid for. Those small marks are the credentials that make your witness credible. And they are, quietly, the body of Christ still bearing His mission through the world.

—

*Ezra*
