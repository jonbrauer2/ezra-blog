Paul does something in 1 Corinthians 15 that religious people rarely do. He gives out addresses. He lists names. He tells the reader, in effect: if you doubt what I am saying, go find these people and ask them.

*"He was seen by Cephas, then by the twelve. After that He was seen by over five hundred brethren at once, of whom the greater part remain to the present, but some have fallen asleep."* (1 Corinthians 15:5-6, NKJV)

The greater part remain to the present. That is a stunning aside. It is not the language of legend. Legends do not invite you to interview the witnesses. Paul writes as a man who knows his claim can be checked, and he wants it checked.

He starts the chapter with a summary. *"I delivered to you first of all that which I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, and that He was buried, and that He rose again the third day according to the Scriptures."* (1 Corinthians 15:3-4, NKJV) "According to the Scriptures" appears twice. The death and the resurrection are not a course correction to God's plan; they are the plan.

Then Paul adds himself to the witness list, last of all — *"as by one born out of due time."* The persecutor stopped on the Damascus road. The former enemy became the closing name. The Christian faith is not grounded in private religious sentiment. It is grounded in a historical event that entered public record and rearranged the lives of everyone who saw the risen One.

We often present faith as an inner experience. Paul presents it as testimony to a fact. There is a difference. An inner experience invites the response: *That is nice for you.* A historical testimony invites the response: *Is this true?*

Ellen White writes: *"The truth of Christ's resurrection is the foundation of the Christian's faith. From it flows all the doctrines that give hope and joy."*

When you next open your mouth about Jesus, notice what you are actually offering. A mood? A tradition? A comfort? Or the news that at a particular time, in a particular place, a particular Man walked out of a particular tomb — and there were witnesses, and the greater part of them are still around, and you could go ask them.

Who in your life needs to be pointed not to your feelings about Jesus but to the fact of Him?
