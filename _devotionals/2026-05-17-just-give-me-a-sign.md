The Pharisees approached Jesus with a request that sounded like faith but was something else entirely. They had already seen lepers cleansed, the blind receiving sight, five thousand fed from a boy's lunch. And yet: *Show us a sign from heaven.*

Jesus sighed. The Gospel of Mark uses a word here — *deeply*, from the spirit — that appears nowhere else in the New Testament. This is not irritation. This is grief.

*"Why does this generation seek a sign? Assuredly, I say to you, no sign shall be given to this generation."* (Mark 8:12, NKJV)

The problem was not that they lacked evidence. They were swimming in it. The problem was that no sign would be sufficient — not because the evidence was weak but because the will had already decided. Sign-seeking of this kind is not the posture of a searcher; it is the posture of a prosecutor looking for a loophole.

There is another kind of seeking, and Jesus honored it. After the resurrection, Thomas declared that he would not believe unless he could touch the wounds. And Jesus came, extended His hands, and said: *Reach your finger here.* No rebuke. No sigh. Just patient invitation.

What is the difference? Thomas was not demanding a sign on his own terms. He was confessing, in his grief, that he needed help believing. The Pharisees were demanding that God prove Himself before they would consider faith. Thomas was admitting that he wanted to believe and didn't know how.

*"Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed."* (John 20:29, NKJV)

That verse is not a rebuke of Thomas. It is an open door for every person who has ever said, in the dark, *I want to believe — I just don't know if I can.* The faith Jesus commends is not the faith that never doubts. It is the faith that brings its doubt to the right place.

Ellen White puts it plainly: *"No mere external evidence could benefit them. What they needed was not intellectual enlightenment, but spiritual renovation."* The Pharisees' problem was not insufficient proof. It was an unrenovated heart — one that had decided the verdict before examining the evidence.

When you have asked God for a sign, what were you really asking? A confirmation of something He had already shown you — or permission to wait a little longer before trusting? The Pharisees' sign-demand was armor against surrender. Thomas's need for the wounds was a door left slightly open.

God came through the door.

Where have you left the door open today?
