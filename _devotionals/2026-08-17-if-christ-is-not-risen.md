Paul does something bracing in the middle of 1 Corinthians 15. He entertains, for the sake of argument, the possibility that the resurrection did not happen. Then he follows the logic all the way to its bottom.

If Christ is not risen, our preaching is empty. Your faith is empty. We are false witnesses. Your faith is futile. You are still in your sins. Those who have fallen asleep in Christ have perished. And — the closing line most people quietly step around — *"If in this life only we have hope in Christ, we are of all men the most pitiable."* (1 Corinthians 15:19, NKJV)

Paul refuses the middle option. Christianity is not a nice moral framework that can be quietly retained if the miracle turns out to be metaphor. If the tomb was not empty, we are the most pitiable people on the planet — deceived in our worship, wrong about our dead, wrong about our sins.

The line that catches me every time is this one: *"If Christ is not risen, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins!"* (1 Corinthians 15:17, NKJV) Notice what he is saying. The resurrection is not simply about what happens after we die. It is about the status of our sins right now. If the Father did not raise the Son, the Father did not accept the payment. If the payment was not accepted, we are still carrying the debt.

Paul makes the point cleanly elsewhere. *"He was delivered up because of our offenses, and was raised because of our justification."* (Romans 4:25, NKJV) The resurrection is inside the transaction, not tacked onto its end. The empty tomb is the Father's public receipt. It says: *the account is settled.*

Which means the news of Easter is not primarily about the far country of the age to come. It is about your standing today. Because He was raised, your sins are not still on your account. Because He was raised, the guilt you keep re-picking up was set down by the One qualified to remove it. Because He was raised, the fear that whispers *nothing has really changed* is a lie.

The stakes Paul lays out are terrifying — but only if the tomb is still occupied. It is not. Which means the news is not terrifying but staggering.

What in your life would look different this week if you actually rested in this: because He was raised, you are not still in your sins?
