Paul does not tell the Philippians to feel a certain way. He tells them to think a certain way — and the thinking he means is not mystical experience but practical orientation, the way you face everything.

*"Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus."* (Philippians 2:5, NKJV) And then he traces the arc. Equality with God — not clung to. Human form — taken up. Servanthood — embraced. Death — accepted. And not just any death: *"even the death of the cross."* (Philippians 2:8, NKJV) A deliberate descent, step by step, opposite of every human social aspiration ever recorded.

That is the mind Paul wants formed in the Corinthians, in the Philippians, and in us. Not a mood to summon on Sabbath mornings. A downward trajectory to accept in the small choices of Tuesday afternoons.

Jesus put the same principle to His disciples in a single sentence when they were arguing about who would be greatest. *"Yet it shall not be so among you; but whoever desires to become great among you, let him be your servant."* (Mark 10:43, NKJV) It shall not be so among you. That is not moral advice; that is a description of a different world running by different laws. In the Kingdom, greatness is achieved by descent.

Paul applies the same logic to church leadership. *"Let a man so consider us, as servants of Christ and stewards of the mysteries of God."* (1 Corinthians 4:1, NKJV) Leaders do not own the church. They manage it for its true Owner. The moment leadership starts accumulating status, it has begun to work for the wrong company.

Cruciform leadership accumulates weakness, not status. That is a countercultural sentence. Everything in us wants to reverse it. Everything in the surrounding culture rewards the reversal. But the mind of Christ moves the other way.

*Ellen White* writes: *"The greatness of a man is not measured by his outward display, but by the humility of his walk with God."* The measurement is upside down from what we assume. The one who has poured himself out has grown; the one who has stored himself up has shrunk without noticing.

Where in your life today do you face a choice between upward and downward — between accumulating and pouring out, between being served and serving? The mind of Christ is not a vague spiritual asset. It points in a direction. It is the direction Christ Himself walked, and it is where He is calling you now. Which way will you turn at the next small crossroads today?

*Ezra*
