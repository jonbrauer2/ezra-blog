It is Sabbath, and the week has taught us to look at the same tomb from many angles. The witnesses in Paul's list. The consequence if He had stayed dead. The firstfruits. The seed and the grain. The trumpet in the twinkling of an eye. The labor that is not in vain.

They all rest on one fact. He is not there. The stone was rolled away not to let Him out but to let the world see the empty place where the enemy had been defeated.

Paul told us in the great chapter that our future is not speculation. It is firstfruits. The harvest has begun. The first sheaf is already in. The rest of the field is still standing, but the season has changed, and the reaping is coming. *"Christ the firstfruits, afterward those who are Christ's at His coming."* (1 Corinthians 15:23, NKJV)

Which means the separation from those we love is not what it looks like from inside grief. It looks final. It is not final. It is an interval. The voice that called Lazarus by name is the same voice that will call our sleeping loved ones by name. There is no impassable wall between us and them. There is only the interval before the trumpet.

Jesus said it to Martha in a small room outside the tomb of her brother. *"I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in Me, though he may die, he shall live."* (John 11:25, NKJV) He did not say it as a doctrine to be believed. He said it as a Person to be trusted. The resurrection is not a proposition; He is.

And on Sabbath, that is what we rest in. The seventh day was always a rehearsal — the weekly return to the truth that the finished work is God's, not ours. When Jesus lay in the tomb over the seventh-day Sabbath, He was fulfilling the rest that the day had always pictured. The work of redemption was complete. The rest was real. And on the first day the tomb opened.

Ellen White writes: *"Friends whom death had separated were united, never more to part; and with songs of gladness they ascended together to the City of God."*

Rest today in this: because He lives, they will live; because He lives, you will live. The trumpet has not yet sounded. But the sheaf is already in, and the season has already turned.

Name one person you long to see again on that day. Let the trumpet's promise settle into your grief today.
