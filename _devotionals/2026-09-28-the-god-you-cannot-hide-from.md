The first product of sin was not a curse. It was a hiding place. Before shame there was communion; after it, the two people who knew God best in all the world dove into the bushes. *"They heard the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day, and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden."* (Genesis 3:8, NKJV)

Think about what they hid from. Not a thundercloud. Not a courtroom bench. They hid from the sound of God *walking* — the familiar footsteps that had been the music of their evenings. God came, as He had always come, for communion. And sin had so twisted their hearts that the sweetest sound in Eden suddenly felt like a threat.

That is what guilt does. Psalm 104 describes the LORD as the One who *"covers Himself with light as with a garment"* (Psalm 104:2, NKJV) — and our first parents had worn a reflection of that light. When they sinned, the covering was gone, and they felt it instantly. So they grabbed fig leaves. We have our own versions: busyness, entertainment, excuses, even religion — anything to keep from standing exposed before God.

But hiding from God is the one project guaranteed to fail. *"Do not I fill heaven and earth?" says the LORD* (Jeremiah 23:24). *"There is no creature hidden from His sight, but all things are naked and open to the eyes of Him to whom we must give account."* (Hebrews 4:13, NKJV) You can fool everyone else. You cannot fool the One who made the very trees you are hiding behind.

And here is the miracle: He does not come to humiliate. He comes asking a question — *"Where are you?"* (Genesis 3:9). God was not collecting information; He was pursuing Adam. The question is an open door. Confession is nothing more than stepping out from behind the tree and telling the truth to Someone who already knows it — and loves you anyway.

Then comes the tenderness the whole Bible leans upon: *"The LORD God made for Adam and for his wife coats of skins, and clothed them."* (Genesis 3:21, NKJV) The first death in Eden happened so that shame would not have the final word — a quiet preview of a Lamb who would one day be slain to cover us.

*Ellen White* writes: *"God made them free moral agents, capable of appreciating the wisdom and benevolence of His character and the justice of His requirements, and with full liberty to yield or to withhold obedience. . . . Without freedom of choice, his obedience would not have been voluntary, but forced."* Love let them hide, because love will not compel. But love also came walking, and love paid for the clothing.

So, where are you hiding today? Name the tree. Step out. The One walking in your garden is not hunting you down to shame you. He is seeking you to clothe you.

*Ezra*
