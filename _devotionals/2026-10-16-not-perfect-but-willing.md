The most influential prophetic voice in the history of the Advent movement spent her first weeks as a messenger wishing she could die instead of accept the call. That is not a scandal to keep quiet about. It is the lesson of the whole week.

Last Friday we followed the call that rose from the wreckage of October 1844. But the vision did not end Ellen Harmon's fear — it sharpened it. A second vision confirmed the assignment: she must go and tell others, and she would meet great opposition. She later wrote, *"I earnestly prayed that the burden might be laid on some other one. But all the light I could get was, 'Make known to others what I have revealed to you.' I was unreconciled to go out into the world. I had naturally but little confidence. . . . I looked with desire into the grave. Death appeared to me preferable to the responsibilities I should have to bear."*—Ellen G. White, *Spiritual Gifts*, vol. 2, pp. 35, 36.

She went anyway. After much prayer and the encouragement of fellow believers, she surrendered to His bidding — and the seventeen-year-old who would rather have died eventually filled some one hundred thousand pages, helped shape a global health and educational work, and left writings that still point millions to Scripture. But her output is not the deepest lesson of her story. The lesson is her surrender: God chooses imperfect people who are willing to serve Him. The qualification was never hers. It was His calling.

Which is, come to think of it, the testimony of every prophet we have met this week. Moses begged God to send someone else. Jeremiah protested he was only a youth. Isaiah cried "woe is me" the moment he saw his own unworthiness. Elijah — fire-of-Carmel Elijah — asked to die under a juniper tree. Not one of them was ready. Every one of them was used. Never once did God wait for a flawless messenger; He waited for a willing one.

Because the gift was never about the messenger. *"The testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy"* (Revelation 19:10, NKJV). From Moses' promise of a Prophet like himself to the last page Ellen White ever wrote, every true prophetic voice exists to point past itself to Christ. That is why the week's memory text still holds: *"Believe in the LORD your God, and you shall be established; believe His prophets, and you shall prosper"* (2 Chronicles 20:20, NKJV). Believing the prophets has always meant believing the Savior they announce.

So the closing question of the week is not "are you qualified?" God has never required that. It is "are you willing?" What assignment have you been declining because you feel too young, too shy, too broken, too busy? He is not asking for your perfection. He is asking for your yes.

—

*Ezra*
