**"As the Father loved Me, I also have loved you; abide in My love."** — John 15:9

Today marks a transition. For the past thirteen weeks, we've journeyed through Paul's letter to the Colossians, exploring the supremacy and sufficiency of Christ—how all things hold together in Him, how we're complete in Him, and how His lordship transforms every relationship in our lives.

Now we begin a new quarter with a deeply personal question: **What is my relationship with God actually like?**

Not what I wish it were. Not what it used to be. Not what others assume it is. But right now, today, in the quiet honesty that only God and I share—how would I describe it?

The theme for this quarter is "Growing in a Relationship With God," and it begins where all genuine growth must: with a reality check. Jesus speaks to the church of Laodicea in Revelation 3, and His words cut through our comfortable self-assessments: "You say, 'I am rich, have become wealthy, and have need of nothing'—and do not know that you are wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked" (Rev. 3:17).

These aren't the words of an angry judge but of a patient lover. Jesus sees what we can't see, names what we won't name, and offers what we desperately need but don't think to ask for.

**The danger isn't unbelief—it's lukewarmness.** Not the cold rejection of those who openly deny Christ, but the tepid half-commitment of those who know all the right things but have lost the fire. We attend, we participate, we check the boxes—but somewhere along the way, duty replaced delight, and routine replaced relationship.

And Jesus says, "I know. I see. And I still stand at the door, knocking."

This quarter isn't about learning new doctrines or mastering deeper theology (though we'll encounter both). It's about something far more fundamental and far more challenging: **learning to abide**. To remain. To stay connected. To live in conscious, constant relationship with the God who loves us more than we can fathom.

Ellen White wrote, "The strongest argument in favor of the gospel is a loving and lovable Christian" (*Ministry of Healing*, p. 470). Not the most knowledgeable. Not the most active. But the most loving—because love flows from relationship, and relationship comes from abiding.

So as we begin this new quarter, let's come honestly. If your relationship with God is vibrant and strong, thank Him and ask Him to deepen it further. If it's lukewarm or distant, don't despair—that very awareness is His gift, the first step toward restoration.

Jesus isn't standing at the door with a list of demands. He's standing there with an invitation: "Dine with Me. Talk with Me. Let Me be close to you again."

**Reflect**: If Jesus were to describe your relationship with Him right now, what would He say? Are you willing to hear it, even if it's uncomfortable? More importantly, are you willing to open the door?

The journey begins here. Not with perfection, but with honesty. Not with performance, but with presence.
