Hebrews 11 is a list of names. Eighteen times in the chapter, the phrase appears: *By faith.* By faith Abel. By faith Noah. By faith Abraham. It builds like a drumbeat, a testimony piled on testimony, a cloud of witnesses gathering around a single claim: that trusting the God you cannot see changes what is possible.

But here is the detail most sermons skip.

Halfway through the chapter, the writer lists a different kind of faith:

*"Others were tortured, not accepting deliverance... still others had trial of mockings and scourgings, yes, and of chains and imprisonment. They were stoned, they were sawn in two, they were tempted, they were slain with the sword."* (Hebrews 11:35-37, NKJV)

These are also in the hall of faith. Not the ones who were delivered — the ones who weren't. The ones who died without receiving the thing they were promised. *"These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off."* (Hebrews 11:13, NKJV)

This separates biblical faith from the version that promises smooth outcomes: faith here is not a technique for getting God to act on your schedule. It is a posture of trust that persists across both deliverance and suffering, across answered and unanswered prayer, across mountains moved and mountains that stayed exactly where they were. *"We walk by faith, not by sight."* (2 Corinthians 5:7, NKJV) That includes walking through the valley where sight would show nothing hopeful at all.

The grammar of the chapter is also worth noticing. It does not say these saints did great things *because* they had great faith. It says they did what they did *by* faith — faith as the means, not the achievement. Abel didn't offer a better sacrifice because he was more spiritual than Cain; he offered it by faith. Noah didn't build the ark because he was naturally courageous; he built it by faith. The "by" matters. It keeps the credit where it belongs.

Faith comes by hearing the word of God, Paul writes in Romans 10:17 — not by circumstances going well, not by feeling spiritually alive. The word. Which means that when everything else suggests you should give up, there is still a source available: Scripture, read slowly, with honest expectation, held in memory and spoken aloud.

The saints of Hebrews 11 were not extraordinary people. Abel was murdered by his brother. Noah endured decades of being the ridiculous one. Abraham spent his life wandering without a permanent address. And they all died in faith. Not in triumph. In faith.

Which figure in that long list looks most like where you are right now — and what did they do with what they were given?

—

*Ezra*
