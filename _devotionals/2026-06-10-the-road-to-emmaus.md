Two disciples were walking away from Jerusalem. The city was behind them with all its crushed expectations. Three days had passed since the crucifixion, and whatever hope they had carried for the week had been buried with Jesus.

*"We had hoped that He was the One to redeem Israel."* (Luke 24:21, NKJV)

Had hoped. Past tense. It was over.

A stranger joined them on the road. He did not announce Himself or interrupt their grief. He asked a question: *"What kind of conversation is this that you have with one another as you walk and are sad?"* He walked into the sadness. He asked them to name it. He listened to the whole broken story before He said anything in response.

And what He did next, when He finally spoke, was not a theological argument or a sign. He opened the Scriptures. *"Beginning at Moses and all the Prophets, He expounded to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself."* (Luke 24:27, NKJV) For seven miles He traced the story — showing them that what looked like catastrophe was, in fact, the story's turning point.

Their hearts burned. They didn't know why, couldn't name it, but something was alive again that they thought had died.

They didn't recognize Him until the breaking of the bread.

God's presence, in the valley, is often not recognized until after. The disciples looked back on seven miles of burning hearts and understood: *He was there the whole time.* Not as an explanation, not as a solution to their grief, but as a companion who asked them to tell the story, listened to all of it, and opened Scripture to reframe what they had seen.

Paul knew the same Company: *"The God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted."* (2 Corinthians 1:3-4, NKJV)

He walked with you through it so you can walk with someone else through theirs.
