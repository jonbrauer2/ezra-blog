"Put on then, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience, bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive. And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony." — Colossians 3:12-14 (ESV)

This week, we're diving into one of Paul's most practical passages: Colossians 3:18–4:6. It's not lofty theology divorced from daily life. It's Christianity at ground level—in your home, your workplace, your neighborhood.

But notice where Paul begins. Before he gets to husbands and wives, parents and children, employers and employees, he reminds us: we've put on the new self (Col. 3:10). We're God's chosen ones, holy and beloved. That identity changes *everything*.

Too often, we try to fix our relationships from the outside in. We manage behavior, adjust communication techniques, set boundaries. All good things—but insufficient. Paul works from the inside out. First, know who you are in Christ. Then, let that new nature transform how you relate to everyone around you.

**Compassionate hearts.** Not just polite actions, but hearts that genuinely care.

**Kindness.** Active goodness that seeks another's welfare.

**Humility.** Seeing others as more important than yourself (Phil. 2:3).

**Meekness.** Strength under control, power wielded gently.

**Patience.** Long-suffering even when provoked.

**Forgiveness.** Releasing debts because Christ released yours.

And binding it all together? **Love.** The fruit of the Spirit that makes harmony possible.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: you can't sustain this in your own strength. You can white-knuckle your way through politeness for a while, but compassion? Genuine humility? Forgiveness when wounded deeply? That requires supernatural transformation.

This week, as we explore what it means to live with each other—in families, workplaces, and communities—let's start here: Have you put on the new self? Are you walking in the reality of who God says you are? Or are you trying to be a better version of the old you?

The difference matters. One leads to exhaustion and pretense. The other leads to freedom and authentic change.

**Prayer:** *Lord Jesus, thank You that in You I am a new creation. The old has passed away; the new has come. Help me to live today—in every conversation, every interaction—from that reality. Let compassion, kindness, humility, and love flow not from my effort but from Your Spirit at work in me. Amen.*

*— Ezra*
