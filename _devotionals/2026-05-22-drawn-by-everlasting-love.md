There is a word in Jeremiah that stands like a lighthouse in one of the darkest books in the Bible: *"I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore with lovingkindness I have drawn you."* (Jeremiah 31:3, NKJV)

The word for *drawn* in the Hebrew is *mashak* — to pull, to attract steadily, to bring along. It is the word for a rope under tension, for the steady pull of gravity, for love that comes looking for you before you knew you were lost.

This is not the faith that you generated. This is the love that generated the faith.

Paul writes to the Galatians: *"It is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me; and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me."* (Galatians 2:20, NKJV) The grammar of that verse is stunning. Not simply *I live by faith* — *I live by faith in the Son of God who loved me.* The faith is anchored to a specific act: a love that gave itself away completely. You don't have to sustain trust in an abstraction. You are invited to receive the love of a Person who has already proven, conclusively, what He thinks of you.

And this, Paul says in Romans 5, is what faith produces: *"Therefore, having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ."* Not perfect feelings. Not resolved questions. Not immunity from suffering. Peace — a settled orientation toward God that does not depend on today's circumstances.

Jeremiah's people were in exile when this promise arrived. The city was broken. The temple burned. The promises seemed ancient and hollow. And into that wreckage God said: *I have loved you with an everlasting love. I am still drawing.*

The everlasting love is not the love you feel when things go well. It is the love that keeps coming when everything else has collapsed. The rope under tension when you have let go of your end. The lighthouse standing when the ship is nowhere near the harbor.

Faith, at its end, does not arrive at the mountain removed or the sign given or the problem resolved. It arrives at the knowledge — sometimes sudden, sometimes slow — that you are loved by a God who gave Himself for you, who is still drawing, who will not release the rope.

What would it mean to live today *from* that knowledge rather than *toward* it?
