The image Paul chooses is deliberately unimpressive. Not a golden vessel. Not an ornate reliquary. A clay pot. The cheap kind. The kind you would find on any peasant's shelf, easy to break, easy to replace.

*"We have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellence of the power may be of God and not of us."* (2 Corinthians 4:7, NKJV)

The clay is not the flaw. That is the sentence to sit with. Most of us assume our weakness is the obstacle to God using us. Paul says the weakness is the point. The plain container is what makes it obvious that the treasure came from somewhere else. If the pot were gold, people would admire the pot. Because the pot is clay, they cannot help asking where the light is coming from.

Then Paul does something honest. He gives us his diary, not a motivational poster. *"We are hard pressed on every side, yet not crushed; we are perplexed, but not in despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed."* (2 Corinthians 4:8-9, NKJV)

Four paradoxes. Each of them starts with the real thing. Hard pressed. Perplexed. Persecuted. Struck down. Paul does not skip the first half. He does not pretend the pressure is not real. He does not fake a resilience he has not earned. He names what it is.

And then the *yet.* Not crushed. Not in despair. Not forsaken. Not destroyed. The something-holding remains. Paul does not claim to know how. He knows only that the pot has not shattered, and that the treasure is still visible.

He gives the theological account in the next line. *"Always carrying about in the body the dying of the Lord Jesus, that the life of Jesus also may be manifested in our body."* (2 Corinthians 4:10, NKJV) The pattern of Christ's death makes room in you for the pattern of Christ's life. The crack in the jar is where the light comes out.

Which changes what to do with your weakness. You have probably spent years trying to hide the crack in your clay. The place you feel most inadequate. The failure you keep quiet. The chronic thing that will not fix. Paul says: that is exactly where the treasure will be seen most clearly. Not despite the crack. Through it.

He puts the ratio into words in the same chapter. *"Our light affliction, which is but for a moment, is working for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory."* (2 Corinthians 4:17, NKJV) Light and momentary against exceeding and eternal. The scale tips in a direction the eye cannot yet see.

Ellen White writes: *"God can and will use those who have suffered adversity. Those to whom He assigns important work are always sent to school in the furnace of affliction."*

What crack in your clay jar are you most tempted to hide? Paul says it is exactly the place the treasure shines through. What would it look like to stop hiding?
