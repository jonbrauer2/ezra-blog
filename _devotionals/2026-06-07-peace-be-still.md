Jesus was asleep in the stern of the boat. That detail alone carries more theology than most sermons. They were in a storm. The waves were breaking over the sides. The disciples were bailing. Experienced fishermen who had spent their lives on this lake were terrified. And Jesus was asleep.

Not absent. Asleep. Resting in the Father's keeping so completely that the storm could not disturb Him.

*"Teacher, do You not care that we are perishing?"* (Mark 4:38, NKJV) The accusation in the question is honest. It is also the question every person in a storm eventually asks. *Do You not care?* He was in the boat with them. He had told them to cross to the other side. He had not warned them. And now He was sleeping.

He woke up and spoke to the wind and the sea: *"Peace, be still."* And there was a great calm.

What He said next was a question, not a rebuke: *"Why are you so fearful? How is it that you have no faith?"* The disciples had rowers' arms and fishermen's eyes and lifetimes of experience on this water. They had also spent months watching Jesus do impossible things. All of that knowledge, and still — when the storm came, they panicked.

Because the crisis revealed something about what they actually believed, versus what they believed they believed.

The storm did not create their fear. It exposed it. The fear was there already, waiting for the wind. And when it came, the question was not "Can Jesus stop the storm?" — they had seen enough to answer that — but "Will He? Does He know? Does He care?"

The storm left them with a different question altogether: *"Who is this?"* The crisis reoriented them. They knew more about Jesus walking off the water than they did walking onto the boat.

What storm has taught you something about Jesus that calm weather couldn't?
