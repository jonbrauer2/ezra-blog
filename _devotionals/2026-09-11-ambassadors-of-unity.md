The offering Paul is organizing is not just charity. It is a demonstration. Gentile believers in Corinth are giving to impoverished Jewish believers in Jerusalem — the dividing wall coming down in the form of denarii. For centuries these two groups had been separated by covenant, culture, cuisine, and centuries of suspicion. And now money is moving across the line in one direction, and prayer is moving back in the other, and the effect is a picture of the new humanity Paul kept preaching.

*"But by an equality, that now at this time your abundance may supply their lack, that their abundance also may supply your lack — that there may be equality."* (2 Corinthians 8:14, NKJV) Twice in one verse Paul says "equality." He is not talking about a flat leveling of income. He is talking about reciprocal vulnerability across a boundary that used to be a wall. The Corinthians have money the Jerusalem believers lack. The Jerusalem believers have spiritual roots and apostolic witness the Corinthians lack. Both flow. Both give. Both receive.

Paul writes elsewhere what the offering is enacting: *"There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus."* (Galatians 3:28, NKJV) That verse is easy to quote and hard to live. Statements of unity are cheap. The offering is unity made concrete. Paul is not asking the Corinthians to feel more unified with the Jerusalem church; he is asking them to *be* more unified — through the actual movement of actual resources across the actual boundary.

The Romans letter adds the same instinct: *"For it pleased those from Macedonia and Achaia to make a certain contribution for the poor among the saints who are in Jerusalem. It pleased them indeed, and they are their debtors. For if the Gentiles have been partakers of their spiritual things, their duty is also to minister to them in material things."* (Romans 15:26-27, NKJV) Spiritual things one way, material things the other. Neither group is superior. Neither is diminished.

Your money always goes somewhere. Where it goes is a statement about what you actually believe unites the body. A budget is a theology in numbers.

Is there a body of believers different from your own — geographically, culturally, economically — that God is inviting you to see as truly your family? What would one small act of concrete unity look like this week?
