Paul had watched the athletes train for the Isthmian Games. Months of restricted diet, measured sleep, controlled exertion, all for a wreath of pine leaves that would be brown by the next week. And he looked at the church — the people who had a genuine, imperishable prize in view — and asked why the athletes trained harder for a fading crown than the disciples trained for an eternal one.

*"Everyone who competes for the prize is temperate in all things. Now they do it to obtain a perishable crown, but we for an imperishable crown."* (1 Corinthians 9:25, NKJV)

Then he says something that ought to arrest anyone who has been in ministry long: *"But I discipline my body and bring it into subjection, lest, when I have preached to others, I myself should become disqualified."* (1 Corinthians 9:27, NKJV) The word for "disqualified" is *adokimos* — rejected after testing, failing the assay. The greatest apostle names his own vulnerability. If he can be disqualified, no one is exempt from the training.

Discipline in the Christian life is not the earning of grace. Grace comes free, and it comes first, and it keeps coming when the discipline lapses. But grace received without any subsequent discipline is grace squandered — the prize is imperishable, but the runner still has to run. The athletic metaphor is not incidental. Paul is not talking about a stroll.

Hebrews picks up the same image: *"Let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which so easily ensnares us, and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us."* (Hebrews 12:1, NKJV) Notice the distinction. Sin gets laid aside — of course. But also every weight. Not everything the runner drops is evil. Some of it is just heavy. The legitimate luggage of a well-arranged life can slow the race as effectively as any obvious sin.

Ellen White wrote that "the enemy of souls has sought to introduce the supposition that a great work can be done without much personal effort... but we cannot succeed in this life-race without exerting personal effort." Grace does not remove the effort; it grounds it.

What are you carrying today that is not sin but is slowing you down? A habit that used to be innocent and is now costly. A commitment that made sense two years ago and now clogs the schedule. An arrangement of your day that never leaves room for training. Some of it may need to be laid aside — not repented of, just set down.

The finish line is real. Train like it.

—

*Ezra*
