It is Sabbath, and we have spent a week watching what love looks like when the warm feelings have run out. The letter written in tears. The changed itinerary. The comfort that traveled through the wound. The triumph felt from the middle of anxiety. The love that compels because Christ's love got there first.

Underneath every one of them is the same principle. Ministry that lasts is not sustained by our capacity. It is sustained by Christ's capacity flowing through us to people who often cannot see it.

Which is a strangely restful thing to notice on the seventh day. Because most of us, when we take honest stock of the week, know we have run dry. The reserves are gone. The compassion we started Monday with is not what we have on Friday. And the temptation is to spiritualize the emptiness — to pretend the tank is still full when the light on the dashboard has been on for days.

Sabbath is where we stop pretending. It is where we let the tank be visibly empty. It is where we come back to the God of all comfort and let Him do what only He can do — refill, from the outside, what could not be manufactured from within.

The fragrance a Christian life carries is not manufactured. It is received. That is worth saying twice. The aroma of Christ that other people notice on you is not something you produced by trying harder. It is something that has soaked into you from proximity to Him. You cannot fake it, and you do not need to. Sit close to the fire long enough and you smell like smoke without effort.

Which is what Sabbath is for. Not primarily rest from work, though it is that. It is proximity to the Person. The soul returning to the fire it was made for. The lungs filling with the air of the age to come, so that some of it can be exhaled into the week that is coming.

Ellen White writes: *"A consecrated Christian life is ever shedding light and comfort and peace... [and] leaves a track of light wherever its possessor may go."* The track is not a project. It is a residue. It comes from where the person has been.

Paul is not asking us to generate ministry from scratch each week. He is asking us to keep returning to the source. To let Christ's love keep pressing in. To let the comfort we received become the comfort we pass on. To walk in the procession we are being led in, and to trust that the fragrance is going up, even from days that felt like small clay pots and nothing more.

Rest today in the love that compels you before you compel yourself. Whose life will you carry the fragrance of Christ into this week — not because you have plenty to give, but because He has plenty to give through you?

—

*Ezra*
