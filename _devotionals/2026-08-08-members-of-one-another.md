The week began with the Spirit distributing gifts as He wills. It ends with the recognition that we belong to each other before we belong to anything else. The body is not a metaphor Paul reaches for when he runs out of arguments. It is the actual shape of what Christ built.

*"Now you are the body of Christ, and members individually."* (1 Corinthians 12:27, NKJV) The order matters. First the body, then the members. Not a collection of individuals who happen to share a building. A single organism, with parts, each of which is essential precisely because none of them is the whole.

The Corinthian mistake was to treat gifts as a leaderboard. Paul spent a chapter dismantling the leaderboard, another chapter placing love at the center, and a third chapter subordinating every gift to the criterion of edification. The whole argument moves in one direction: the church is not a competition, it is a body, and the body only functions when the members remember it.

Love is the atmosphere in which every gift breathes. When love thins, the gifts start looking like weapons — knowledge cuts, prophecy embarrasses, tongues confuse, giving accumulates recognition, service becomes performance. But when love thickens, even the smallest gift becomes a channel of grace. The elderly woman praying at home, the teenager stacking chairs after potluck, the deacon who checks the sound system before the service — the body cannot lose any of them, because in a body of love no gift is small.

Ellen White wrote that "love is the golden chain which binds human hearts to one another, and which binds the human to the divine. Love is the atmosphere of heaven." Sabbath is the weekly reoccupation of that atmosphere. The rest of the week we work — sometimes alongside others, sometimes alone, often distracted by our own outputs. Sabbath refuses the distraction. It sets us in a room with the God who gave the gifts and with the people who received their own, and it insists that we notice each other.

There is a small act of Sabbath obedience that is worth practicing today. Name one person in your local church whose gift complements yours. Not the person whose gift is the same, but the person who does what you cannot. The gentle one who covers your intensity. The organized one who covers your improvisation. The visionary one who covers your fidelity to routine. The steady one who covers your enthusiasm. Whoever that is — thank God for them by name. Then, if it is possible, tell them.

*"And if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; or if one member is honored, all the members rejoice with it."* (1 Corinthians 12:26, NKJV) That is the test. A church whose joys and pains flow through the whole body is a church actually becoming what it is called to be. A collection of Christians who cannot feel each other is not yet the body — it is only the potential of one.

Rest today in this: you are a member. Not a candidate for membership. Not a probationer. A member. The Spirit placed you here. The body needs you here. And the day is coming when the whole body — every member, every gift, every hidden fidelity — will stand together before the Head who gave Himself for it.

*Ezra*
