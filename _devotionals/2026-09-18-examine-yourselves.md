Paul has spent chapters warning the Corinthians about false teachers. Then, at the end, he turns the lens around. The first line of defense against error, it turns out, is not scrutinizing them. It is scrutinizing you. *"Examine yourselves as to whether you are in the faith. Test yourselves. Do you not know yourselves, that Jesus Christ is in you? — unless indeed you are disqualified."* (2 Corinthians 13:5, NKJV)

Before pointing outward, Paul turns them inward. This is a discipline the church has always found difficult, because self-examination feels less thrilling than heresy-hunting and less flattering than doctrinal correction. But Paul knows that the person who is drifting is rarely aware of the drift until it has become substantial. Small compromises accumulate silently. And so, on a schedule, the honest believer stops and looks — not to induce anxiety, but to catch small drifts while they are still small.

David modeled the prayer of a person willing to be examined: *"Search me, O God, and know my heart; try me, and know my anxieties; and see if there is any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting."* (Psalm 139:23-24, NKJV) Notice the sequence. God is the searcher. David does not do the surgery on himself. He invites the Physician to hold the light. And notice the destination — "the way everlasting." Examination is not a dead end. It clears the way for grace to do fresh work.

Jeremiah put it in the collective voice: *"Let us search out and examine our ways, and turn back to the LORD."* (Lamentations 3:40, NKJV) The examined life turns. The unexamined life drifts.

Paul's specific question is straightforward: is the Jesus you are trusting the Jesus of the apostolic gospel? The one who died for sins, rose bodily, poured out His Spirit, is coming again? Because if you have quietly received a different Jesus — a Jesus of pure moralism, or of pure sentiment, or of pure prosperity, or of pure private mysticism — no amount of confidence in your faith will help you, because your faith is trusting in something that is not the actual Christ.

*Ellen White* writes: *"The Lord designs that our opinions shall be put to the test, that we may see the necessity of closely examining the living oracles."* The test is not an accusation. It is a gift. It gives you the chance to correct while there is still time.

Set aside twenty quiet minutes this weekend. Ask God to search you. Ask honestly: is the Jesus I am trusting the Jesus the apostles preached? What holds up? What needs re-anchoring? Grace waits at the end of every honest look.

*Ezra*
