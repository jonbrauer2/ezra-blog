There is a quiet promise tucked into the end of one of Isaiah's most expansive chapters, the kind of verse that only matters when you've shared something true about God with someone and watched them walk away unchanged.

*"So shall My word be that goes forth from My mouth; it shall not return to Me void, but it shall accomplish what I please, and it shall prosper in the thing for which I sent it."* (Isaiah 55:11, NKJV)

The burden of outcome belongs to God. The burden of faithfulness belongs to us.

Those two lines taken together release something that the witness who cares deeply needs to hear: you are not responsible for the result. You are responsible for the word. The result — the landing of it, the germination of it, the timing of when it begins to matter to the person who heard it — belongs entirely to the One who sent it.

Paul asks the right question: *"How shall they believe in Him of whom they have not heard? And how shall they hear without a preacher?"* (Romans 10:14, NKJV) The chain of salvation runs through ordinary words spoken by ordinary people to specific people who need to hear them. The chain would not function without the speaking. But neither does it function by the speaking alone — there is a God who draws (John 6:44), who softens (Acts 16:14), who brings to completion what He begins (Philippians 1:6).

The witness plants, or waters, or scatters seed along the road, or invites to a feast. The harvest comes in God's season.

And Jesus ends the Great Commission not with a performance standard but with a promise: *"I am with you always, even to the end of the age."* (Matthew 28:20, NKJV) He does not say: I will be with you when the results are good. He says: always. The presence outlasts the visible fruit.

Is there someone you've shared with and seen no change? Leave the result with the One whose word does not return void. Your faithfulness is the only part that is yours to carry.
