It is Sabbath, and the week has taken us through what an authentic Christian life actually looks like from the inside. Written on hearts, not on stone. Beholding with an unveiled face. Cracked clay through which the treasure shines. Ambassadors carrying a plea that did not begin with them. A temple where God has chosen to dwell. Godly sorrow that moves instead of circling.

Underneath every one of those images is a single fact. God has reconciled you to Himself. Not: God is willing to reconcile you if you first fix yourself. Not: God will reconcile you when your godly sorrow becomes deep enough. But: *"God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself, not imputing their trespasses to them, and has committed to us the word of reconciliation."* (2 Corinthians 5:19, NKJV) Done. Announced. Real.

That is what Sabbath rests in. Not the ambition to become authentic by the end of the day. Not the resolve to try harder next week. The finished work of a God who made the exchange at the cross and confirmed it at the empty tomb. Your account is settled. Your face has been turned. Your reconciliation is not a project.

Which is important, because the rest of the week's images can drift into performance if we are not careful. Being a living letter can become anxious self-monitoring. Beholding can become another spiritual technique to master. Being an ambassador can become a role we perform. Sabbath is the day the performances end and we return to the ground under them. He has reconciled us. From that ground, all the rest becomes possible.

Notice too that the ministry of reconciliation was given to the church, not just to pastors. Every believer carries a pleading voice into their small territory of the world. The neighbor who watches you across the fence. The colleague you sit beside for years. The relative who has grown distant. Someone in your ordinary life needs to hear the announcement that no one is imputing their trespasses to them — that the door is open, that the ambassador is speaking on behalf of a God who is not tired of them.

But you cannot carry that message convincingly if you have not first rested in it yourself. Which is what today is for. To sit under the reconciliation. To let the unveiled light do its slow forming work. To watch the treasure catch through the crack in your own clay, and know it is not a defect in the vessel but the point of it.

Ellen White writes: *"As a church, we have received great light. This light has been given us that we might diffuse it to others. To us has been given the ministry of reconciliation."*

Rest today in the fact that God has reconciled you to Himself — not because you finally got it together, but because Christ became sin for you. From that ground, whose face does the Father want you to lift up next week?

—

*Ezra*
