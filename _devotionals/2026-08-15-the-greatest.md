The center of Paul's longest letter is not a doctrine. It is a portrait — the face of Christ shining through fifteen verbs. And by the end of the week, if we have been listening at all, we should know that we cannot manufacture what the portrait describes. We can only receive it.

The week began with the noisy gong — the terrifying possibility of a religious life without love, spiritual credentials that turn out to be empty racket. It ran through the fifteen verbs, the closed ledger, the face of Jesus in every line of the description, the survival of love through the eschaton, the atmosphere of heaven moving into a fearful heart. And it lands here, on Sabbath, on one small sentence: *"But the greatest of these is love."* (1 Corinthians 13:13, NKJV)

The greatest, because it is what God is. The greatest, because it is what will remain when everything else has done its work. The greatest, because every other virtue borrows its life from this one. Faith, hope, humility, patience, courage — each of them draws its warmth from love, and each of them cools when love thins.

Which changes what today, this seventh day, is actually for. Sabbath is not primarily rest from labor. It is the weekly reoccupation of the atmosphere of heaven. It is the day when we stop trying to produce anything — including our own spiritual growth — and simply sit inside what we have been given. God loved you into existence. God loves you into eternity. There is nothing you did to earn either bookend, and there is nothing you can do to lose them. That is the sap in the branch and the blood in the body, and Sabbath is the day we let it flow without interference from our anxious effort to help.

Ellen White wrote that "the burning, consuming love of Christ for perishing souls is the life of the whole system of Christianity." Not a component. Not a slogan. The life. Without it, the system is machinery. With it, the system becomes a body — the same body Paul described in the previous chapter, alive because the Head has loved every member with an unearned, indefatigable love.

This is the ground you are invited to rest on today. You were not loved because you achieved something worth loving. You were loved before you had achieved anything, and you were loved on the days you achieved things you should not be proud of, and you will be loved on the day you can no longer achieve anything at all. The love of God is not a reward for the person you are becoming. It is the reason the person is becoming.

And from that ground, love flows outward — not as duty, not as strain, not as spiritual performance, but as the natural overflow of a life that has finally stopped trying to earn what it already possesses. *"We love Him because He first loved us."* (1 John 4:19, NKJV) And we love each other because that same love, having reached us, will not stop moving until it reaches through us.

So today, rest. And from the rest, name one relationship where you can be a small carrier of this love tomorrow. Not a perfect carrier. A small one. The Spirit takes small carriers and does uncommon work through them.

The greatest thing in the universe has already reached you. Let it move.

—

*Ezra*
