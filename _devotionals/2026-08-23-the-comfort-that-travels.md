You would expect Paul to open his most personal letter with a grievance. The church at Corinth had wounded him. He had written a hard letter. He had waited for news, and the waiting had nearly undone him. When you and I write to the people who have hurt us most, we usually begin at the wound.

Paul begins in worship. *"Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble, with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God."* (2 Corinthians 1:3-4, NKJV)

Read that carefully. There is a cycle in it. God comforts you in your trouble; you then comfort someone else with the comfort you received; the comfort travels through you to the next person. Like sap in a vine. Like water through a channel. What you were given is not meant to stay with you.

Which means the hardest thing you have walked through is likely also the most useful. The specific comfort God gave you — the verse that steadied you at 2 a.m., the friend who did not flinch, the peace that came out of nowhere — is not a private souvenir. It is currency for the next person in that same country.

Paul does not spiritualize what he has been through. He names it plainly. *"We do not want you to be ignorant, brethren, of our trouble which came to us in Asia: that we were burdened beyond measure, above strength, so that we despaired even of life."* (2 Corinthians 1:8, NKJV) Above strength. Despaired of life. Not the language of a man performing spiritual resilience. The language of a man who was undone, and knew he was undone, and would not pretend otherwise.

Then the reason. *"That we should not trust in ourselves but in God who raises the dead."* (2 Corinthians 1:9, NKJV) The extremity was doing something. It was stripping the resources. It was closing every door but one. And behind that one door was the God who raises the dead — the same God who had proven at the tomb what He would do again.

Then Paul does something remarkable. He credits the difficult Corinthians as part of his rescue. *"You also helping together in prayer for us."* (2 Corinthians 1:11, NKJV) The people who wounded him were also praying for him. He refuses to see them as only one thing. Love takes the harder, truer view.

What comfort has God given you that you have not yet passed on? Someone in your circle is waiting for exactly the comfort you have received.
