Isaiah spoke this promise to a people who had experienced exile, siege, the destruction of their temple, and the death of their children. He spoke it as poetry, not argument:

*"He will swallow up death forever, and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from all faces."* (Isaiah 25:8, NKJV)

The active verb is God's. He wipes. Not: tears will eventually dry. Not: time will heal. *He* wipes — personally, directly, with all the intimacy of a Father with a child's face in His hands. The promise is not that suffering will be explained but that it will be over. The comfort is not cognitive; it is contact.

John sees it in his vision and writes it twice, as if once is not enough: *"God Himself will be with them and be their God. And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying. There shall be no more pain, for the former things have passed away."* (Revelation 21:3-4, NKJV)

The most prominent feature of the New Jerusalem is not its gates or its foundations or its streets of gold — remarkable as all of those are. It is the Resident: *God Himself will be with them.* The whole elaborate architecture is a setting for a presence. And in that presence, nothing that has made this world heavy will survive.

Revelation 21 also tells us there is no temple in the New Jerusalem (Revelation 21:22). The temple's entire purpose was to provide a way of drawing near to God — the system of sacrifice and veil and approach. In eternity, the drawing is complete. There is no more mediation required; there is only the direct, unmediated sight of the One the whole history of redemption has been working toward.

*"Worthy is the Lamb who was slain."* (Revelation 5:12, NKJV) That song, already begun in heaven, becomes the eternal fullness of what began at the cross.

Name the tear you are carrying today. Bring it into the certainty that He will personally wipe it.
