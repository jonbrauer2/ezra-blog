The line runs like a quiet current under all of Paul's ethical instruction in the letter. *You are not your own. You were bought at a price.* Everything he says about the body, the appetites, the community, and the sanctification of ordinary life comes downstream of that one purchased fact.

*"For you were bought at a price; therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God's."* (1 Corinthians 6:20, NKJV)

The body was included in the purchase. That is worth pausing over. Not just the soul, not just the record before God, not just the eternal destination — the body. The hands, the mouth, the eyes, the appetites, the choices you make with them today. All of it was included in what Calvary bought.

Which means every ethical command in the letter comes downstream of a completed redemption. The church is not asked to earn its identity. It is asked to inhabit it. When Paul confronts the scandal in Corinth, when he tells them to purge the old leaven, when he calls them to flee immorality, when he warns them about the way freedom can quietly enslave — he is not adding conditions to their salvation. He is asking them to catch up to what has already been true of them since the Cross.

*"And such were some of you. But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God."* (1 Corinthians 6:11, NKJV) Past tense. Finished action. Now, live in it.

Every place the world has entered the church, the answer is not first a rule. The answer is a return to who we already are — bought, washed, sanctified, indwelt. The rule follows. The rule is right. But the rule without the identity produces moralism, and moralism produces either despair or hypocrisy. Identity first. Ethics as the natural expression of a redeemed life. That order matters.

Sabbath is the weekly reoccupation of that identity. Six days of a world that keeps trying to tell you who you are — by your job, your productivity, your appetites, your affiliations — and then a day when the noise stops and you remember. You were bought at a price. The Owner is good. The purchase was real. And it included every part of you.

*Ellen White* writes: *"The Sabbath is a golden clasp that unites God and His people."* (Testimonies to Ministers, p. 137) A clasp. Something that holds two ends together. On this day the identity is refastened.

Rest today in this: you are not your own; you were bought at a price. Which of your struggles this past week would look different tomorrow morning if you started there — not from the rule you have been trying to keep, but from the person you already are in Christ? Begin from the purchase. The obedience will follow.

—

*Ezra*
