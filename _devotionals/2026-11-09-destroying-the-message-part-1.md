Yesterday's congregation boiled over — shouting, weeping, stones for Joshua and Caleb. Today's scene is colder. A winter house in Jerusalem, a quiet court, a roll being read aloud column by column — and every few columns, the penknife. *And he cut it with the penknife, and cast it into the fire* (Jeremiah 36:23). No one in the room so much as tore his robe. Sabbath's devotional already caught that the king was unafraid; look closer at what the fearlessness actually is. Rage at God's word can still be warm — sometimes rage is conviction looking for a target. Contempt is something else. The lesson calls Jehoiakim's response what it was: disdain — for God, for His message, and for His messenger. Cold. Comfortable. Unbothered.

And here the story turns almost absurd in its futility — like a man trying to dam a river by slashing the water. The word of the Lord came to Jeremiah again: take another roll, and write all the former words that were in the first roll. And the record adds, *there were added besides to them many like words* (Jeremiah 36:28, 32). The knife did not erase a single syllable. It added chapters — and the verdict the burned leaf had announced settled, in the end, precisely on the burner (Jeremiah 36:30–31). Rejecting the word does not cancel it. It confirms it.

But notice what else God sent that season, because Monday's lesson does. While Babylon's siege loomed and Jeremiah himself sat chained, God told His prophet to buy a field — witnesses assembled, deed signed and sealed, money weighed out, all for land about to fall to an invading army. Madness — until you hear the whole sentence: *"Houses and fields and vineyards shall be possessed again in this land"* (Jeremiah 32:15, NKJV). The same word that warned of fire promised restoration after the fire. The purchase was obedience acting as visible hope — Jeremiah investing, in God's own currency, in God's declared tomorrow.

That is the character the lesson asks us to see. What kind of God answers a burned message with a bigger message? Not patience on a timer, but love that keeps knocking — rising early, sending again, refusing to let defiance have the last word.

We still keep penknives. The verse we skip. The conviction dismissed as not for me. The prophetic warning filed under someday. The knife feels final for an evening; the word simply returns with more words. So find the page you have been cutting — and instead of slicing it, buy the field. Do something today that makes no sense unless God keeps His word.
