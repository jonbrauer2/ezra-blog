The sun was disappearing behind the hills of Israel.

Three children crouched behind a large rock, trying very hard not to make a sound.

Beside them was Uncle Jonathan.

And Uncle Jonathan had a problem.

A very large problem.

He slowly raised his head above the rock.

He looked into the valley.

Then he ducked back down.

“Ohhhh boy.”

“What?” whispered the six-year-old twin.

“How many are there?” whispered his brother.

Their eight-year-old sister carefully peeked over the rock.

Her eyes grew enormous.

The entire valley below them seemed to be covered with tents.

Campfires flickered everywhere.

There were soldiers.

Camels.

Weapons.

More soldiers.

More camels.

And even MORE soldiers.

The Bible says the Midianites and their allies were so numerous that they looked like swarms of locusts. Even their camels couldn’t be counted.

And Israel was terrified of them.

For seven years, the Midianites had swept into Israel whenever the Israelites planted their crops. They destroyed the food and took their animals.

Some Israelites had even hidden in caves.

The children looked at Uncle Jonathan.

Uncle Jonathan looked at the children.

Then all four of them looked at the enormous army below.

One twin whispered:

“I think we’re going to need a REALLY big army.”

“That,” said Uncle Jonathan, “is exactly what I was thinking.”

Just then they heard footsteps.

A man was approaching.

His name was Gideon.

And Gideon had some surprising news.

“We have an army.”

“How big?” asked the older sister.

“Thirty-two thousand men.”

Uncle Jonathan grinned.

“NOW we’re talking!”

Thirty-two thousand soldiers!

Surely God could use an army like that.

But then Gideon said something very strange.

“God says our army is…”

He paused.

“…too big.”

Everyone stared at him.

“TOO BIG?!”

God had told Gideon that if Israel defeated Midian with such a large army, the Israelites might boast:

“We saved ourselves!”

God wanted Israel to understand something they would never forget.

Their rescue wasn’t going to happen because Israel was powerful.

It was going to happen because God was powerful.

So Gideon stood before his army.

“Anyone who is afraid may go home!”

For a moment, nobody moved.

Then one soldier quietly stepped away.

Then another.

Then ten.

Then a hundred.

Then—

WHOOSH!

Soldiers began leaving everywhere!

The children started counting.

“Wait! Come back!”

Twenty-two thousand men left.

Uncle Jonathan’s mouth dropped open.

They had started with 32,000 soldiers.

Now they had only…

10,000.

One twin gulped.

“Well… ten thousand is still a lot.”

Gideon nodded.

Then God spoke again.

“There are still too many.”

The children couldn’t believe it.

“TOO MANY?!”

God told Gideon to take the soldiers down to the water.

The men were thirsty.

They rushed toward the water and began drinking.

Some knelt down and put their faces toward the water.

Others scooped the water into their hands and brought it to their mouths.

Gideon watched carefully.

The children watched too.

And when everyone finished…

God chose only 300 men.

Three hundred.

Against an army that seemed impossible to count.

The eight-year-old sister did some very quick thinking.

“So what’s the battle plan?”

Gideon smiled.

“I’m glad you asked.”

The children leaned closer.

“Do we have swords?” asked one twin.

“No.”

“Spears?”

“No.”

“Bows and arrows?”

“No.”

Uncle Jonathan scratched his head.

“Gideon… what exactly ARE we taking into battle?”

Gideon began handing things out.

First—

A trumpet.

Then—

A clay jar.

And finally—

A torch.

There was silence.

Uncle Jonathan stared at his equipment.

He held up the trumpet.

Then the jar.

Then the torch.

“Gideon?”

“Yes?”

“I’m not an expert at fighting enormous armies…”

Gideon nodded.

“…but I think somebody packed the wrong stuff.”

The children giggled.

But Gideon wasn’t worried.

Because God had already promised:

“I will save you.”

And that changed everything.

That night, Gideon divided the 300 men into three groups.

The children and Uncle Jonathan joined one of them.

They crept quietly through the darkness.

Step.

Step.

Step.

“SHHHHHH!”

Someone had stepped on a stick.

Everyone froze.

Down below, the Midianite camp was sleeping.

Thousands upon thousands of enemy soldiers surrounded them.

The little group reached its position.

Everyone hid their torches inside their clay jars, so the enemy couldn’t see the light.

Uncle Jonathan whispered to the children:

“When Gideon gives the signal, you know what to do.”

The twins nodded.

Their sister nodded.

They waited.

And waited.

And waited.

Then suddenly—

BWAAAAAAAAAAAH!

Gideon’s trumpet BLASTED through the darkness!

“NOW!”

CRASH!

Three hundred clay jars smashed onto the ground!

Suddenly 300 flaming torches appeared all around the Midianite camp.

BWAAAAAAAAAH!

The Israelites blasted their trumpets.

And everyone shouted:

“A SWORD FOR THE LORD AND FOR GIDEON!”

The sleeping Midianite soldiers woke up.

“What is happening?!”

“WE’RE SURROUNDED!”

“RUN!”

There were trumpets blasting everywhere.

Torches surrounded the camp.

People were shouting.

And in the darkness, the Midianites became completely confused.

The Bible says God caused the enemy soldiers to turn their swords against one another.

Then the Midianite army began to RUN.

Thousands of soldiers fled into the darkness.

The children stood on the hillside holding their torches.

They could hardly believe what they were seeing.

Three hundred men.

No enormous army.

No brilliant weapons.

No human plan that should have worked.

And yet the enormous Midianite army was fleeing.

Uncle Jonathan knelt beside the children.

“Do you know why God made Gideon’s army smaller?”

One twin thought about it.

“So they would know God did it?”

“Exactly.”

Their sister looked down into the valley.

God could have used 32,000 men.

But He chose 300.

Because sometimes God allows His people to face something that feels much too big for them so they can discover something important:

It isn’t too big for Him.

Maybe someday the children would face something scary.

Maybe something would seem impossible.

Maybe they wouldn’t feel strong enough.

Smart enough.

Old enough.

Or brave enough.

And when that happened, they could remember a dark night in Israel when 300 men stood against an enormous army carrying…

Trumpets.

Torches.

And clay jars.

Because courage doesn’t mean thinking:

“I’m strong enough to do anything.”

Sometimes courage means saying:

“I don’t know how this is going to work—but I know I can trust God.”

The children looked at each other.

Then one twin smiled.

“So… did we win?”

Uncle Jonathan grinned.

“Oh, we won.”

The other twin raised his imaginary trumpet.

Their sister lifted her imaginary torch.

And all three shouted:

“FOR THE LORD AND FOR GIDEON!”

Uncle Jonathan jumped up.

“CHAAAAARGE!”

And the four of them raced down the hillside together.

THE END

Based on Judges 6–7.

A fun way to tell this is to give the kids parts: one can make the trumpet sound, another can yell “CRASH!” whenever the jars break, and their sister can lead everyone in “A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!” It turns the climax into controlled chaos—which is probably perfect for three kids and Uncle Jonathan.
