Grayson tipped the water jug upside down.

One drop fell out.

Plink.

He shook it.

Nothing.

He shook it harder.

Still nothing.

Colton grabbed it.

“You’re doing it wrong.”

“How do you do an empty water jug wrong?”

“Like this.”

Colton turned it upside down and shook it violently.

Plink.

One more drop fell.

Colton grinned.

“See?”

Hadley stared at him.

“You got one drop.”

“One more than Grayson.”

“That’s because he loosened it.”

“Nope. Technique.”

Uncle Jonathan took the jug from him.

“I have unfortunate news for both of you.”

“What?”

“We’re still thirsty.”

Very thirsty.

And they weren’t the only ones.

Something terrible had happened to the land.

It hadn’t rained.

Not last week.

Not last month.

Not for a very, very long time.

The ground beneath their feet was cracked and hard. Grass that once covered the hills had turned brown. Streams had disappeared. Wells were drying up.

Farmers walked through fields where crops had withered.

Animals searched for water.

Hadley had seen a skinny goat trying to nibble leaves from a nearly bare bush that morning.

She had given it part of what little food she had.

The goat had followed her ever since.

She had named him Pickles.

Uncle Jonathan had objected.

“Why Pickles?”

“Because he looks like a Pickles.”

That had apparently settled the matter.

Now Pickles was walking behind them.

“Maaaaa.”

“I know,” Hadley said, rubbing his head. “We’re thirsty too.”

They passed an empty field.

Grayson stopped.

He crouched and picked up a handful of dirt.

It ran through his fingers like dust.

“What happened?”

A man nearby heard him.

“You really don’t know?”

Grayson shook his head.

The man lowered his voice.

“It was Elijah.”

Hadley looked up.

“Who’s Elijah?”

The man glanced around as though even saying the name might cause trouble.

“A prophet of the LORD.”

Colton immediately became interested.

“What did he do?”

“He walked right into King Ahab’s palace.”

Uncle Jonathan raised an eyebrow.

“That’s usually a good way to get into trouble.”

“He told the king there would be neither dew nor rain except at his word.”

Hadley’s eyes widened.

“And then?”

“He disappeared.”

“Where?”

“Nobody knows.”

The man shook his head.

“King Ahab has searched everywhere for him.”

Then he walked away.

The children stared at one another.

Colton broke the silence.

“So there’s a mysterious prophet hiding somewhere from an angry king?”

“Yes,” said Hadley.

“And nobody can find him?”

“Apparently.”

Colton grinned.

“This sounds like an adventure.”

Uncle Jonathan sighed.

“It always does to you.”

⸻

Over the next few days, they heard Elijah’s name everywhere.

Some people blamed him for the drought.

Others blamed King Ahab.

And again and again they heard another name:

Baal.

King Ahab had married Queen Jezebel, and together they had promoted the worship of Baal throughout Israel.

Baal’s worshipers claimed he could bring rain and make the land fruitful.

Hadley found that confusing.

“If Baal is supposed to control rain…”

She looked at the cloudless sky.

“…where is it?”

That was exactly the problem.

Israel had turned away from the LORD and followed false gods.

And now the land itself seemed to be asking a question:

Who really is God?

⸻

One afternoon, the four of them were walking along a dusty road when Grayson suddenly stopped.

“Someone’s coming.”

A lone man was walking toward them.

His clothing was rough.

His stride was strong.

And there was something about him that made all four of them go quiet.

Pickles did not go quiet.

“Maaaaaaa.”

The stranger stopped.

He looked at the goat.

Then at Hadley.

“Friend of yours?”

“His name is Pickles.”

Uncle Jonathan muttered, “I had no involvement in naming him.”

The stranger almost smiled.

Hadley studied him.

“Are you…”

She hesitated.

“Are you Elijah?”

Uncle Jonathan’s eyes widened.

“Hadley!”

“What? I wanted to know.”

The man looked at her.

“Yes.”

Colton’s jaw dropped.

Grayson immediately had about fifty questions.

“Where have you been? Is the king really looking for you? Did you really stop the rain? Have you been hiding? How did you get food? Are you going back to the king?”

Elijah waited.

Then answered the last question.

“Yes.”

That silenced everyone.

“You’re going to Ahab?” Uncle Jonathan asked.

Elijah nodded.

“The LORD has told me to present myself to him.”

Hadley looked toward the distant hills.

“Is it going to rain?”

Elijah didn’t answer immediately.

But there was something in his eyes.

Something was about to happen.

⸻

News traveled quickly.

Elijah had appeared before Ahab.

And the meeting had not been friendly.

When Ahab saw Elijah, he said:

“Is that you, you troubler of Israel?”

But Elijah wasn’t intimidated.

“I have not made trouble for Israel. But you and your father’s family have.”

Then Elijah issued an extraordinary challenge.

Gather Israel.

Gather 450 prophets of Baal.

Gather 400 prophets of Asherah who ate at Jezebel’s table.

And bring them to one place.

Mount Carmel.

When Hadley heard the news, she stared at Uncle Jonathan.

“Mount Caramel?”

“Carmel.”

“Caramel?” Grayson asked.

“Carmel.”

Colton’s eyes widened.

“There’s a caramel mountain?”

“No.”

“Can we eat it?”

“No.”

“Does caramel grow there?”

“No.”

“Then why would you name a mountain Caramel?”

“CARMEL.”

Hadley started laughing.

Even Uncle Jonathan gave up.

“Fine. We’re going to Mount Caramel.”

⸻

People came from everywhere.

Thousands climbed the mountain.

Hadley, Grayson, Colton and Uncle Jonathan found a place where they could see.

Pickles came too.

Nobody had successfully convinced him otherwise.

At the center stood Elijah.

Alone.

Across from him stood hundreds of prophets of Baal.

Colton counted for a while.

“That’s a lot.”

It was.

Then Elijah stepped forward.

The crowd became quiet.

His voice carried across the mountain.

“How long will you waver between two opinions?”

People listened.

“If the LORD is God, follow Him; but if Baal is God, follow him.”

Nobody answered.

That was the problem.

Israel wanted both.

They wanted the LORD when it suited them.

Baal when it suited them.

Elijah wasn’t allowing that anymore.

Choose.

Then he proposed a test.

Two bulls.

Two altars.

Wood underneath.

But nobody would light the fire.

The prophets of Baal would call upon their god.

Elijah would call upon the LORD.

And—

“The god who answers by fire—He is God.”

Suddenly the crowd erupted.

“YES!”

Hadley looked at the cloudless sky.

No matches.

No torches.

No hidden flame.

Just two altars.

And a question.

Who would answer?

⸻

The prophets of Baal went first.

They prepared their sacrifice.

Then they began calling.

“BAAL, ANSWER US!”

Nothing happened.

“BAAL!”

Nothing.

“ANSWER US!”

Nothing.

Minutes passed.

Then hours.

The prophets began dancing around the altar.

They shouted louder.

Colton watched.

“Maybe Baal can’t hear them.”

Elijah apparently had the same thought.

Except Elijah said it considerably more sarcastically.

“Shout louder!”

Hadley looked at him.

Did Elijah just—

Yes.

Yes, he did.

“Surely he is a god!” Elijah continued.

Then he began suggesting possible explanations.

Maybe Baal was deep in thought.

Maybe he was busy.

Maybe he was traveling.

Or perhaps—

“Maybe he’s sleeping and must be awakened!”

Grayson burst out laughing.

Hadley tried not to.

Uncle Jonathan covered his mouth.

Colton cupped his hands around his mouth.

“WAKE UUUUUP!”

Hadley grabbed him.

“COLTON!”

“What? Elijah said—”

“He was talking to THEM!”

The prophets shouted louder.

They continued their frantic rituals.

Morning became afternoon.

Afternoon crept toward evening.

Still—

no voice.

No answer.

No fire.

Nothing.

Hadley stopped laughing.

Because suddenly it wasn’t funny anymore.

Hundreds of people had spent hours desperately crying out to something that couldn’t hear them.

Baal wasn’t sleeping.

Baal wasn’t traveling.

Baal wasn’t busy.

Baal wasn’t real.

⸻

Then Elijah said:

“Come here to me.”

The people moved closer.

Elijah turned toward something that had been neglected for a long time:

the altar of the LORD.

It was broken.

Hadley watched Elijah begin repairing it.

He took twelve stones.

One for each tribe descended from the sons of Jacob.

Grayson immediately joined the people carrying things toward the site.

He loved building.

This was an altar.

Finally something in this adventure was directly in his area of expertise.

“Big stone there.”

Another stone.

“Turn that one.”

Another.

Soon the altar stood again.

Elijah dug a trench around it.

He arranged wood.

Prepared the sacrifice.

Then he said something that made absolutely no sense.

“Fill four large jars with water.”

Hadley stared at him.

Water?

Here?

During a drought?

Water was precious.

Elijah said:

“Pour it on the offering and on the wood.”

They poured.

Water splashed over everything.

The sacrifice.

The wood.

The stones.

It ran into the trench.

Elijah said:

“Do it again.”

Grayson blinked.

Again?

Four more jars.

SPLASH!

The wood was soaked.

Then Elijah said:

“Do it a third time.”

Colton looked at Uncle Jonathan.

“I don’t know a lot about making fires…”

“Neither do I.”

“…but isn’t water the thing you put on a fire when you want it to STOP?”

“Generally.”

More water poured over the altar.

The sacrifice was drenched.

The wood was drenched.

The stones were drenched.

The trench around the altar filled with water.

Hadley understood.

Nobody was going to be able to say later:

“Maybe Elijah hid a spark.”

Or:

“Maybe the wood accidentally caught fire.”

Elijah had made the impossible…

more impossible.

⸻

Evening came.

The sun hung low.

The shouting had stopped.

No dancing.

No screaming.

No frantic performance.

One man simply stepped toward the altar.

Elijah prayed.

“LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Israel, let it be known today that You are God in Israel and that I am Your servant and have done all these things at Your command.”

Hadley listened.

Elijah wasn’t asking God to make Elijah look impressive.

He wanted the people to know God.

Then Elijah prayed:

“Answer me, LORD, answer me, so these people will know that You, LORD, are God, and that You are turning their hearts back again.”

For one heartbeat—

nothing.

Then—

WHOOOOOOOOM!

Fire fell.

Not from a torch.

Not from Elijah.

From heaven.

Hadley jumped backward.

Grayson shouted.

Colton’s mouth fell open.

The fire consumed the sacrifice.

Then the wood.

Then something impossible happened.

The fire consumed the stones.

The soil.

And then—

SSSSSSSSSS!

The water in the trench vanished.

Everything.

Gone.

For one stunned moment, thousands of people stood completely silent.

Then the mountain erupted.

People fell facedown.

Voices thundered across Carmel:

“THE LORD—HE IS GOD!”

Again:

“THE LORD—HE IS GOD!”

Hadley felt chills run down her arms.

For years the people had wavered.

Maybe the LORD.

Maybe Baal.

Maybe both.

But Baal’s prophets had shouted for hours.

Nothing.

Elijah had prayed a simple prayer.

And the God who actually existed had answered.

“THE LORD—HE IS GOD!”

⸻

But the story wasn’t finished.

Elijah turned to King Ahab.

“Go, eat and drink, for there is the sound of a heavy rain.”

Colton looked up.

The sky was blue.

“Rain?”

Not a cloud.

Not one.

Ahab went away.

Elijah climbed toward the top of Carmel.

Then he did something Hadley hadn’t expected.

The man who had just stood boldly before a king and hundreds of false prophets…

knelt down.

And prayed.

He sent his servant to look toward the sea.

The servant returned.

“Nothing.”

Elijah prayed again.

“Go back.”

Nothing.

Again.

Nothing.

Again.

Nothing.

Hadley began counting.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Still nothing.

Colton looked disappointed.

“Maybe the rain isn’t coming.”

Hadley looked at Elijah.

He hadn’t stopped praying.

The seventh time, the servant returned.

This time his expression was different.

“I saw something.”

Everyone leaned forward.

“What?”

“A cloud.”

Grayson looked toward the horizon.

“How big?”

The servant held up his hand.

“As small as a man’s hand.”

Colton stared.

“That’s it?”

After three years without rain?

One tiny cloud?

But Elijah jumped to his feet.

“Tell Ahab to get his chariot ready and go down before the rain stops him!”

The wind began to move.

Hadley’s hair lifted.

She looked toward the sea.

The little cloud grew.

And grew.

And grew.

Dark clouds rolled across the sky.

For the first time in years, people felt something they had almost forgotten.

A cool wind.

Grayson held out his hand.

Plip.

He looked at it.

Water.

Another drop hit Colton’s nose.

Plip.

Then Hadley.

Plip.

Colton looked upward.

His grin grew.

Plip. Plip. Plip.

Then—

WHOOSH!

Rain poured from heaven.

People shouted.

Children danced.

Animals lifted their heads.

Water ran across stones.

Dust became mud.

Pickles the goat began bouncing around like he had completely lost his mind.

And naturally…

Colton joined him.

“COLTON!”

“What?!”

He leaped directly into a puddle.

SPLASH!

Grayson jumped into another.

Hadley tried to remain sensible for approximately three seconds.

Then—

SPLASH!

Uncle Jonathan was soaked from head to toe.

He stared at them.

“Oh, you’re all in trouble now.”

He charged.

They scattered, laughing.

For the first time in years, rain fell on Israel.

⸻

Later, Hadley found Elijah standing in the rain.

She walked over.

“You knew God was going to send the fire.”

Elijah looked at her.

“The LORD told me what to do.”

“But afterward you still prayed for rain.”

“Yes.”

“And you kept praying even when your servant saw nothing.”

“Yes.”

“Six times.”

“Yes.”

Hadley thought about that.

Grayson and Colton wandered over.

So did Uncle Jonathan.

Pickles followed, because Pickles apparently believed he was now a permanent member of the family.

Grayson looked back toward the ruined altar.

“So what’s the most important part?”

“The fire?” Colton asked.

“The rain?” said Grayson.

Hadley thought it might be Elijah’s courage.

But Elijah looked toward the people celebrating below.

“The LORD wanted His people back.”

Hadley understood.

The greatest miracle wasn’t really the fire.

And it wasn’t even the rain.

For years, Israel’s hearts had wandered away from God.

Elijah’s prayer had been:

Turn their hearts back again.

The fire showed them who God was.

The rain showed His mercy.

God wasn’t performing a trick to impress everyone.

He was calling His children home.

And there was another lesson Hadley didn’t want to forget.

Elijah had stood almost alone against hundreds of people who disagreed with him.

Being surrounded by people who believed something didn’t automatically make that thing true.

And being the one person standing for what was true didn’t automatically make you wrong.

Truth wasn’t decided by counting how many people were on each side.

They needed to know God for themselves.

To listen to Him.

To follow Him.

And when they prayed, they weren’t shouting into an empty sky hoping somebody might hear.

They were talking to the living God.

The God who hears.

The God who answers according to His wisdom.

The God who can turn hearts back to Himself.

The God who can send fire from heaven—

and rain onto thirsty ground.

Colton suddenly looked thoughtful.

“I have one more question.”

Uncle Jonathan should have known better.

“What?”

Colton pointed toward the mountain.

“So there really isn’t any caramel?”

Uncle Jonathan closed his eyes.

“No, Colton.”

“Not even a little?”

“No.”

Grayson joined in.

“Seems like false advertising.”

Hadley started laughing.

Elijah looked confused.

“What’s caramel?”

All four of them stopped.

They looked at Elijah.

Then at one another.

Colton gasped.

“You’ve never had caramel?”

“No.”

Colton put a hand on Elijah’s shoulder.

“Elijah…”

His voice became very serious.

“You have seen fire fall from heaven.”

Elijah nodded.

“You’ve been fed by ravens.”

“Yes.”

“You’ve stood against 450 prophets of Baal.”

“Yes.”

“You’ve prayed and rain came back.”

“Yes.”

Colton shook his head sadly.

“But you’ve never tasted caramel.”

Elijah stared at him.

Colton sighed.

“Nobody’s life is perfect.”

Hadley collapsed laughing.

Grayson fell into the mud.

Even Uncle Jonathan couldn’t hold it together.

And beneath a sky finally pouring rain onto the thirsty land, they laughed until their sides hurt.

But long after the laughter ended, they remembered the sound of thousands of voices on Mount Carmel declaring something worth remembering for the rest of their lives:

“The LORD—He is God!

The LORD—He is God!”

Based on 1 Kings 17–18.
