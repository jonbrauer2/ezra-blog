“Are we there yet?”

Uncle Jonathan looked at Colton.

“No.”

Colton walked approximately twelve more steps.

“Are we there now?”

“No.”

Another twelve steps.

“How about—”

“Colton.”

Hadley laughed.

Grayson was too busy examining a cart rolling past them to complain.

“I could build one of those.”

“You could?” Hadley asked.

“Yep.”

The cart hit a hole.

CLUNK.

One wheel wobbled dangerously.

Grayson frowned.

“I’d build mine better.”

They had been traveling for what felt like forever.

The road wound through the hills toward a little town called Bethlehem, and apparently everyone else in the world was going there too.

Families walked together. Donkeys carried bundles. Merchants led animals along the road. Children complained that their feet hurt. Adults complained about the Romans.

Uncle Jonathan had learned something important:

Traveling with three children in Bible times was exactly like traveling with three children anywhere else.

Except there were no cars.

And considerably more donkey poop.

Colton jumped over another pile.

“PARKOUR!”

“That’s not parkour,” Hadley said.

“It is if you jump far enough.”

“Please,” said Uncle Jonathan, “just don’t land in it.”

They continued walking.

Then Hadley noticed someone ahead.

A woman was moving slowly along the road.

Very slowly.

A man walked beside her, watching her carefully.

Hadley stopped.

“Uncle Jonathan.”

“What?”

She nodded toward the couple.

“That lady looks like she’s going to have a baby.”

Uncle Jonathan looked.

She certainly did.

And she looked exhausted.

The man beside her carried what he could, but he looked tired too.

The children caught up with them.

“Hello,” Hadley said.

The man smiled.

“Hello.”

“Are you going to Bethlehem?”

“We are.”

“Us too!”

Colton appeared beside Hadley.

“I’m Colton.”

Then Grayson.

“I’m Grayson.”

“Hadley.”

The man smiled.

“My name is Joseph.”

The woman looked at them.

“And I’m Mary.”

Uncle Jonathan stopped walking.

Mary.

Joseph.

Bethlehem.

Pregnant.

He looked at Hadley.

Hadley looked at him.

Her eyes widened.

Grayson caught the expression.

“What?”

Hadley whispered:

“Mary and Joseph.”

Grayson stared.

Then he understood.

His mouth opened.

Colton didn’t understand yet.

“What?”

Hadley leaned toward him.

“MARY and JOSEPH.”

Colton looked at Mary.

Then Joseph.

Then Mary’s belly.

Then Bethlehem in the distance.

“Ohhhhhhhhhhh.”

Suddenly nobody complained about walking anymore.

⸻

They stayed with Mary and Joseph.

Not because anyone asked them to.

It simply seemed wrong not to.

Mary was tired.

The journey had been long, and every mile seemed harder now.

Hadley walked beside her.

“Does your back hurt?”

Mary smiled.

“A little.”

Hadley gave her a look that said she didn’t believe the word little.

Joseph noticed.

“So,” Grayson asked him, “what do you do?”

“I’m a craftsman.”

That was the correct answer.

Grayson immediately moved closer.

“What do you build?”

Joseph laughed.

“All sorts of things.”

“Tables?”

“Yes.”

“Doors?”

“Yes.”

“Houses?”

“I’ve worked on them.”

“Carts?”

“Yes.”

Grayson nodded approvingly.

“I build things too.”

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

They spent the next mile talking about wood.

Joseph explained how tools worked.

Grayson examined everything.

At one point Joseph showed him a wooden joint.

Grayson studied it.

“Ohhh.”

Uncle Jonathan recognized that expression.

Grayson had just discovered approximately seventeen new things he intended to build.

Meanwhile, Colton had discovered rocks.

Specifically, rocks that could be jumped from.

Jump.

Hop.

Leap.

Balance.

Jump.

“Colton,” Uncle Jonathan called, “could you possibly travel in a straight line?”

“I AM traveling in a straight line!”

“You’re twenty feet off the road!”

“But I’m going the same direction!”

Technically, he had a point.

⸻

As evening approached, Bethlehem appeared.

The children expected Joseph to be relieved.

Instead, he looked worried.

The town was packed.

People were everywhere.

The Roman emperor had ordered a census, and people had traveled to their ancestral towns to be registered.

Joseph belonged to the family line of David.

That was why he had come here.

But finding somewhere to stay was another matter.

They tried one place.

No room.

Another.

Nothing.

Joseph knocked again.

Hadley watched Mary’s face.

Mary tried to smile.

But Hadley could tell.

She was exhausted.

Another place.

Full.

Another.

No room.

Colton wasn’t bouncing anymore.

Grayson wasn’t talking about building.

They were beginning to understand.

Mary was about to have a baby.

And they had nowhere to go.

“Can’t somebody help them?” Hadley whispered.

Uncle Jonathan wished he had an answer.

Finally, they found shelter.

It wasn’t a beautiful guest room.

It was a place where animals were kept.

Grayson looked around.

There were rough walls.

Wooden beams.

Hay.

A feeding trough.

Hadley looked at Mary.

“This is where you’re staying?”

Mary nodded.

Hadley didn’t like that.

Not one bit.

She had imagined the birth of Jesus before.

Christmas pictures made everything look peaceful and perfect.

But now she was standing there.

She could smell the animals.

She could feel the cold night air.

She could see how tired Mary was.

This wasn’t a palace.

It wasn’t even a proper room.

And suddenly Hadley thought:

If this baby really is who I think He is…

Why here?

⸻

Then they heard:

Mew.

All three children froze.

Grayson turned.

“Mew?”

There it was again.

Mew.

“KITTEN!” Colton shouted.

“Shhhhh!” Hadley said.

They followed the sound.

Behind some straw they found a tiny kitten.

It was curled against a larger cat.

“Mama kitty!” Hadley whispered.

The mother cat watched them suspiciously.

Beside her were two tiny kittens.

One was sleeping.

The other was attempting to climb directly over its mother’s head.

Colton pointed.

“That one’s me.”

Hadley laughed.

The little kitten tumbled off.

It immediately tried again.

“Definitely you.”

Grayson crouched.

“Can we pet them?”

“Let Mama decide,” Hadley said.

They held out their hands.

The mother cat sniffed Hadley’s fingers.

Then Grayson’s.

Then Colton’s.

Apparently they passed inspection.

Hadley gently stroked the kitten.

It began to purr.

For a little while, the children helped make the place more comfortable.

Grayson became very interested in the manger.

“This is for animal food.”

“Yes,” Joseph said.

Grayson examined its construction.

“I could build a better one.”

Joseph smiled.

“I believe you.”

They found clean straw.

Hadley helped where she could.

Uncle Jonathan tried to keep Colton from testing whether the stable beams were suitable for climbing.

And then things became quiet.

Because the time had come.

⸻

The children waited nearby.

They heard hushed voices.

Joseph moving around.

Mary.

Then—

a sound.

A tiny sound.

The cry of a newborn baby.

Hadley looked up.

Grayson stopped moving.

Even Colton froze.

Nobody needed to tell them.

Jesus had been born.

After a while, Mary let them come closer.

And there He was.

Wrapped snugly in cloths.

Lying in the manger.

Hadley slowly approached.

She had heard about Jesus all her life.

She knew what He would someday do.

He would grow.

He would teach people what God was like.

He would touch lepers when everyone else backed away.

He would welcome children.

He would forgive sins.

He would calm storms.

He would die on a cross.

And He would rise again.

But right now…

He was tiny.

Hadley knelt beside the manger.

Jesus moved one little hand.

Hadley smiled.

She didn’t say anything.

She didn’t need to.

The little kitten wandered over.

Its mother followed closely behind.

The kitten sniffed the manger.

“Mew.”

Colton whispered:

“I think the kitten likes Jesus.”

Hadley smiled.

“Everybody should.”

Grayson was staring at the manger.

Joseph noticed.

“What are you thinking?”

Grayson pointed.

“I still think I could build a better one.”

Joseph laughed quietly.

“I think you’re probably right.”

“But…”

Grayson looked at the baby.

“…this one’s pretty good right now.”

⸻

Then Colton heard something outside.

He ran to the doorway.

People were coming.

Fast.

“GUYS!”

Uncle Jonathan stood.

Men appeared out of the darkness.

Shepherds.

They looked breathless.

Excited.

Terrified.

Happy.

All at once.

Joseph stepped forward cautiously.

One shepherd held up his hands.

“Please—we’re looking for a baby.”

Everyone stared.

“A baby,” another said, “wrapped in cloths.”

He looked past Joseph.

“And lying in a manger.”

Hadley’s eyes widened.

How could they possibly know that?

The shepherds came closer.

Then one began telling the story.

They had been outside Bethlehem watching their sheep.

It had been an ordinary night.

Then suddenly—

an angel appeared.

The glory of the Lord shone around them.

The shepherds were terrified.

“But the angel told us not to be afraid!”

The shepherd could barely contain himself.

“He said, ‘I bring you good news that will cause great joy for all the people.’”

Hadley listened.

“Today,” the shepherd continued, “in the town of David, a Savior has been born.”

He looked toward the manger.

“Christ the Lord.”

Then another shepherd interrupted.

“And then the whole SKY was full of angels!”

“How many?” Colton asked.

The shepherd laughed.

“I couldn’t count them!”

“What did they say?” Grayson asked.

The shepherd’s expression softened.

“Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace to those on whom His favor rests.”

The stable became very quiet.

Hadley looked at Jesus.

Outside, Bethlehem continued sleeping.

People talked.

Doors opened and closed.

Animals moved through the streets.

Most of the town had no idea what had happened.

There hadn’t been a royal parade.

No trumpets had sounded in the streets.

No king had announced it from a palace.

Instead, God had told…

shepherds.

Ordinary people watching sheep at night.

And the King they came to see was lying in an animal’s feeding trough.

⸻

Then one of the sheep wandered into the stable.

Another followed.

Then another.

“Uh-oh,” said Uncle Jonathan.

Apparently the shepherds had brought some of their flock.

The stable suddenly became considerably more crowded.

A goat squeezed through.

A dog trotted behind it.

The mother cat immediately moved her kittens farther from the dog.

The dog sniffed them.

HISSSSS!

The dog reconsidered his life choices.

Grayson laughed.

Then a lamb began chewing Colton’s clothing.

“Hey!”

He pulled away.

The lamb followed.

“STOP EATING ME!”

The lamb tugged harder.

Colton ran.

The lamb chased him.

Naturally, Colton interpreted this as a game.

He jumped over a pile of hay.

The lamb went around.

Colton jumped onto a low ledge.

“HA!”

“Colton,” Hadley whispered urgently, “we’re next to BABY JESUS!”

“Oh.”

Colton carefully climbed down.

Then the kitten darted past him.

Colton hopped over it.

The lamb ran underneath him.

For one magnificent second Colton was completely airborne.

Joseph stared.

One shepherd stared.

Mary looked up.

Colton landed.

Everyone was silent.

“Sorry.”

Mary started laughing.

That made Joseph laugh.

Then the shepherds.

Soon everyone was trying very hard to laugh quietly so they didn’t disturb the baby.

⸻

After a while, the shepherds left.

But they didn’t leave quietly.

They told everyone they met what had happened.

They praised God.

They told people what the angel had said about the child.

Hadley watched them disappear into Bethlehem.

Then she returned to the manger.

Mary was holding Jesus now.

The kitten and its mother had curled together in the straw nearby.

Grayson sat beside Joseph.

Colton had finally stopped jumping.

For perhaps forty-five consecutive seconds.

Uncle Jonathan sat with the children.

Hadley looked at him.

“Why did God do it this way?”

“What way?”

“Jesus.”

She looked around.

“Why wasn’t He born in a palace?”

That was the question.

If God was sending His Son into the world, surely He could have arranged anything.

A palace.

Servants.

Important people.

The finest bed.

Instead—

Bethlehem.

A tired young mother.

Joseph.

A manger.

Animals.

Shepherds.

Uncle Jonathan thought about it.

“Maybe God wanted us to understand what kind of King Jesus would be.”

Hadley looked at the baby.

Jesus hadn’t come only for kings.

He hadn’t come only for rich people.

Or important people.

Or people who had everything figured out.

He had come close to ordinary people.

People like shepherds.

People who were tired.

People who didn’t have much.

Children.

Families.

Everyone.

Grayson looked at the manger.

“The angel said it was good news for all the people.”

Hadley nodded.

Colton looked at Jesus.

“So He came for us too?”

“Yes,” Uncle Jonathan said.

“For us too.”

That thought seemed almost too big for the little stable.

The God who made the stars had come into His own world as a baby.

Not far away.

Not unreachable.

Close.

Hadley looked through the doorway.

One star seemed extraordinarily bright against the dark sky.

She wondered how many people outside knew what had happened.

Then she looked back at Jesus.

The greatest gift God had ever given the world was lying in a manger.

And almost nobody knew yet.

⸻

Mary eventually placed Jesus down again.

Hadley knelt beside Him.

The little kitten woke and toddled toward her.

Hadley scooped it up.

The mother cat came too and curled beside her.

Grayson leaned against the manger, still studying how it had been built.

Colton climbed onto a hay bale.

Uncle Jonathan pointed at him.

“No jumping.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

Hadley looked at him.

Grayson looked at him.

Uncle Jonathan looked at him.

Even Joseph looked at him.

Colton sighed.

“Okay. I was thinking about it.”

He climbed down.

Then he walked over and stood beside Hadley.

All three children looked into the manger.

No one made a joke.

No one jumped.

No one asked a question.

For once, they simply looked.

Hadley reached out and let Jesus’ tiny fingers close around one of hers.

And she thought about what the angel had said:

Good news.

Great joy.

For all people.

A Savior had been born.

The kitten purred in Hadley’s arms.

Its mother purred beside her.

The animals settled into the straw.

Joseph sat beside Mary.

And somewhere outside, excited shepherds were running through Bethlehem telling anyone who would listen:

The Savior is here.

Colton finally whispered:

“Hadley?”

“What?”

“Do you think Jesus likes kittens?”

Hadley looked down.

The tiny kitten had fallen asleep beside the manger.

She smiled.

“I think Jesus made kittens.”

Colton considered this.

“Good point.”

Grayson whispered:

“And trees.”

“Yes.”

“And wood.”

“Yes.”

“And people who build things.”

Hadley smiled.

“Yes, Grayson.”

Colton grinned.

“And people who jump?”

Uncle Jonathan leaned over.

“Yes, Colton. Even people who jump.”

Colton looked delighted.

Then Uncle Jonathan added:

“But that doesn’t mean you should jump over the manger.”

“I wasn’t going to!”

Everyone looked at him.

“…probably.”

And beneath the stars over Bethlehem, surrounded by shepherds and sheep, goats and cows, dogs and cats, a mother cat and her tiny kitten, three children and their uncle stayed close to the manger a little longer.

Because they knew something Bethlehem was only beginning to discover:

This wasn’t simply the birth of another baby.

God had kept His promise.

Jesus had come.

And the world would never be the same.

Based on Luke 2:1–20.
