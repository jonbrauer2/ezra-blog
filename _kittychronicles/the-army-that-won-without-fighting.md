Colton was marching.

Left.

Right.

Left.

Right.

He carried a stick over one shoulder.

An orange kitten marched beside him.

At least, Colton believed the kitten was marching.

The kitten believed it was following a beetle.

“Halt!” Colton commanded.

The kitten did not halt.

“Soldier, I said halt!”

The kitten pounced on the beetle.

Hadley watched from the steps of a house in Jerusalem.

“Your army is not listening.”

“He is on a secret mission.”

Grayson looked up from the wooden cart wheel he was examining. “What is the mission?”

Colton lowered his voice.

“Beetle.”

Before anyone could ask what that meant, a man ran through the street shouting.

“To the king! Make way!”

People stepped aside.

The man’s face was pale.

He hurried toward the palace.

Then another messenger came.

Then another.

Uncle Jonathan joined the children.

“Something is wrong.”

The city began to change.

Shopkeepers stopped talking.

Soldiers moved toward the walls.

Families gathered in doorways.

Whispers spread through Jerusalem.

An enormous army was coming.

Not one army.

Three nations had joined against Judah.

And they were already close.

Very close.

---

King Jehoshaphat received the report.

“A vast army is coming against you from Edom, from the other side of the Dead Sea.”

The king was alarmed.

That is the Bible’s honest description.

He was afraid.

The enemy was large.

Judah was small.

There was not enough time.

Jehoshaphat did not pretend the danger was imaginary.

But he did something important with his fear.

He turned toward the LORD.

He proclaimed a fast throughout Judah.

People came from every town to seek help from God.

Hadley, Grayson, Colton and Uncle Jonathan joined the crowd in the temple courtyard.

Families stood together.

Old men leaned on staffs.

Mothers held babies.

Children gripped their parents’ hands.

The orange kitten had followed Colton and now sat on a low wall.

Colton named him Trumpet.

“He has never made a trumpet sound,” Hadley whispered.

“Not yet.”

The king stood before the people.

He prayed.

“LORD, God of our ancestors, are You not the God who is in heaven? You rule over all the kingdoms of the nations. Power and might are in Your hand, and no one can withstand You.”

Grayson listened carefully.

Jehoshaphat began with who God was.

Then he remembered what God had done.

Then he described the danger.

At last the king said words Hadley would never forget.

“We do not know what to do, but our eyes are on You.”

The courtyard was silent.

That was exactly how everyone felt.

They did not know what to do.

But they knew where to look.

---

Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon a Levite named Jahaziel.

He stood in the assembly.

“Listen, King Jehoshaphat and all who live in Judah and Jerusalem!”

Every face turned toward him.

“This is what the LORD says to you: Do not be afraid or discouraged because of this vast army.”

Jahaziel paused.

“For the battle is not yours, but God’s.”

A murmur moved through the crowd.

The battle was not theirs?

Jahaziel told them where the enemy would be.

They were still to march out.

They were still to take their positions.

But they would not need to fight.

“Stand firm and see the deliverance the LORD will give you.”

King Jehoshaphat bowed with his face to the ground.

All Judah bowed too.

Then the Levites stood and began praising God with very loud voices.

Very loud.

Trumpet the kitten arched his back.

“MREEOW!”

Colton’s eyes widened.

“See? Trumpet.”

Hadley could not argue with that.

---

Early the next morning, the people gathered outside Jerusalem.

The sky was still pink.

Soldiers checked their shields.

Spears pointed upward.

Families watched from the road.

Jehoshaphat stood before the army.

“Have faith in the LORD your God and you will be upheld.”

Then the king did something very unusual.

He appointed singers.

Grayson looked at the musicians.

Then at the soldiers.

Then at the road leading toward three enemy armies.

“Where are the singers going?”

The answer was: in front.

Colton stared.

“Shouldn’t the people with shields go first?”

That was the normal arrangement.

But this would not be a normal victory.

The singers took their places ahead of the army.

Some carried lyres.

Some carried trumpets.

Some lifted their voices.

Hadley joined near the back of the group.

She loved to sing.

Grayson walked beside her.

Colton carried Trumpet, who did not appear to understand military formations.

Uncle Jonathan stayed close.

The procession began moving through the hills.

At first, the only sounds were sandals on stone and the creak of leather.

Then the singers began.

“Give thanks to the LORD, for His love endures forever.”

Again.

“Give thanks to the LORD, for His love endures forever.”

The sound traveled down the line.

More voices joined.

Hadley sang.

Grayson sang.

Colton sang very loudly and not entirely on the same note.

Trumpet added one confused meow.

Behind them marched an army.

Ahead of them waited an enemy.

And in front of everyone went praise.

---

As they began to sing and praise, the LORD acted.

The allied armies fell into confusion.

The men of Ammon and Moab turned against the forces from Mount Seir.

Then they turned against one another.

Judah did not cause it.

Jehoshaphat did not plan it.

The singers never raised a weapon.

By the time Judah reached the lookout over the wilderness, the battle was over.

Grayson climbed onto a rock.

He expected to see an army charging toward them.

He saw no one coming.

The enemy had been defeated.

The soldiers of Judah stood in astonishment.

They had marched out as God commanded.

They had taken their positions.

They had trusted.

And God had fought the battle.

Hadley looked at the singers.

Their hands still held instruments.

Not swords.

Instruments.

Colton held up Trumpet.

“We won!”

The kitten sneezed.

“He says, ‘Obviously.’”

---

There was so much equipment and valuable material in the abandoned camp that it took Judah three days to gather it.

On the fourth day, everyone assembled in a valley.

They praised God there.

The place became known as the Valley of Berakah—the Valley of Blessing.

Then the people returned to Jerusalem.

How?

With music.

Lyres rang.

Harps played.

Trumpets sounded.

The road that had carried a frightened army away from the city now carried a joyful procession home.

When they reached the temple, the music grew louder.

People poured into the streets.

Children danced.

Soldiers laughed.

Jehoshaphat lifted his hands toward heaven.

And Trumpet the kitten finally made a sound nearly worthy of his name.

“MRRRAAAOW!”

Colton beamed.

“He has a solo.”

---

That evening, the four travelers sat on the city wall.

The hills were peaceful.

No enemy torches moved in the distance.

Grayson thought about Jehoshaphat’s prayer.

“Was the king brave?”

“Yes,” Uncle Jonathan said.

“But he was afraid.”

“Being brave does not mean you never feel afraid.”

Hadley looked toward the temple.

“He told God he didn’t know what to do.”

“That was part of his faith,” Uncle Jonathan said. “He was honest about his weakness and confident about God’s strength.”

Colton scratched Trumpet under the chin.

“And then he sang before the problem was fixed.”

That was the surprising part.

The singers did not wait until they saw the victory.

They praised while the enemy was still ahead.

Praise was not pretending there was no danger.

It was remembering that the danger was not greater than God.

Grayson looked serious.

“Does God always stop every battle if we sing?”

“No,” Uncle Jonathan said. “This story is not a formula. God may lead His people through hard things in many different ways. But the truth Jehoshaphat trusted never changes.”

God still rules.

God still hears.

God still meets people who do not know what to do.

And no battle is too large for Him.

Hadley quietly repeated the king’s words.

“We do not know what to do, but our eyes are on You.”

That was a prayer she could remember.

For frightening news.

For impossible decisions.

For problems too large to solve.

Colton stood and lifted his stick.

“Forward, army!”

Trumpet dashed after another beetle.

“Your army is leaving,” Hadley said.

“Secret mission.”

“Beetle?”

“Beetle.”

They laughed and followed the kitten down the steps.

Behind them, Jerusalem rested in peace.

The army had gone out with singers at the front.

They had returned without fighting.

And everyone knew who had won the battle.

Based on 2 Chronicles 20:1–30.
