![For Such a Time as This](/ezra-blog/assets/images/family/kittychronicles/esther.jpg)

Long ago, in the enormous Persian Empire, there stood a palace unlike anything Hadley, Grayson, and Colton had ever seen.

Its pillars stretched high above their heads. Colorful curtains hung between columns of marble. Soldiers guarded enormous doors. Servants hurried through the halls carrying trays of food.

And somewhere inside lived a king.

Hadley stared up at the palace.

“Whoa.”

Grayson was already studying the pillars.

“I could build this.”

Hadley looked at him.

“Alwl of it?”

“Yep.”

Meanwhile, Colton had spotted a low stone wall.

Up he went.

Hop.

Jump.

Spin.

BOING!

“Colton!” Hadley whispered. “You can’t parkour through the king’s palace!”

Colton landed perfectly.

“Why not?”

“Because it’s the KING’S PALACE!”

Just then Uncle Jonathan came hurrying around the corner.

“There you are! I’ve been looking everywhere for—COLTON, GET DOWN FROM THERE!”

But before anyone could move, they heard something.

Mew.

Everyone froze.

“Mew?”

Grayson’s eyes widened.

“KITTY!”

All three children raced toward the sound.

Behind a large flowerpot was a tiny Persian kitten.

Hadley scooped it up.

“Ohhhhhhh!”

Immediately, all thoughts of kings and palaces disappeared.

They were petting the kitten when a young woman approached.

She had kind eyes and a gentle voice.

“I see you’ve met one of the palace kittens.”

Hadley looked up.

“What’s her name?”

The woman smiled.

“What do you think I should call her?”

Hadley thought carefully.

Before she could answer, Uncle Jonathan came around the corner.

He stopped.

His eyes widened.

He bowed.

“Children…”

He whispered urgently.

“That’s Queen Esther.”

Grayson nearly dropped the kitten.

“THE QUEEN?!”

Esther laughed.

And that was how the children met one of the bravest women in the Bible.

⸻

Esther hadn’t always been queen.

She was a young Jewish woman who had been raised by her older cousin Mordecai after losing her parents.

Mordecai loved Esther like a daughter.

And when Esther unexpectedly became queen, Mordecai gave her some important advice.

“Don’t tell anyone yet that you are Jewish.”

So Esther kept it secret.

Hadley understood that Esther’s life in the palace wasn’t simply fancy clothes and beautiful rooms.

She had to learn.

She had to listen.

She had to be wise.

And Esther was very good at listening.

Hadley liked that.

Being brave didn’t always mean being the loudest person in the room.

Sometimes it meant paying attention.

Sometimes it meant learning.

Sometimes it meant knowing when to speak.

⸻

But there was another man in the kingdom.

His name was Haman.

And Haman was extremely important.

At least…

Haman certainly thought so.

When Haman walked through the streets, people bowed to him.

Bow.

Bow.

Bow.

Haman loved it.

Then one day Haman passed Mordecai.

Everyone bowed.

Except Mordecai.

Haman stopped.

He turned around.

He stared at Mordecai.

“You didn’t bow.”

Mordecai wouldn’t bow down to honor Haman in the way Haman demanded.

And Haman became furious.

But Haman didn’t just decide to punish Mordecai.

When he discovered that Mordecai was Jewish, Haman made a terrible plan.

He persuaded King Ahasuerus to issue a law that would result in the destruction of the Jewish people throughout the empire.

When Mordecai heard the news, he was heartbroken.

Soon a message secretly reached Queen Esther.

Hadley, Grayson, Colton, and Uncle Jonathan gathered around as Esther read it.

Her face changed.

“What is it?” Hadley asked.

Esther looked up.

“My people are in danger.”

The room became quiet.

Even Colton stopped bouncing.

“Then tell the king!” Grayson said.

Esther didn’t answer.

Hadley noticed immediately.

“There’s a problem, isn’t there?”

Esther nodded.

There was a law in Persia.

You couldn’t simply walk into the king’s inner court whenever you wanted.

Not even the queen.

If you entered without being invited, you could be put to death—

unless the king held out his golden scepter.

Esther hadn’t been invited to see the king for thirty days.

Grayson’s eyes grew huge.

“So if you go…”

“I could die,” Esther said.

Nobody spoke.

Then Colton looked toward the palace doors.

“That’s scary.”

“Yes,” Esther said softly.

It was.

And that’s important.

Esther wasn’t courageous because nothing frightened her.

Courage is choosing to do what is right even when you are afraid.

Then another message came from Mordecai.

Esther read it slowly.

Mordecai warned her not to think she would necessarily escape the danger just because she lived in the palace.

And then came words Esther would never forget:

“Who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?”

Hadley repeated them quietly.

“For such a time as this…”

Maybe becoming queen hadn’t happened simply so Esther could live in a beautiful palace.

Maybe she was in this exact place, at this exact moment, because there was something important she could do.

Esther stood.

Her decision was made.

“Tell Mordecai to gather the Jewish people in Susa. Fast for me for three days. My attendants and I will fast too.”

Then Esther said:

“I will go to the king, even though it is against the law. And if I perish, I perish.”

The children stared at her.

This was no pretend adventure anymore.

Esther was putting her life on the line to save other people.

⸻

For three days, Esther prepared.

No feasting.

No celebrating.

This was serious.

Hadley wondered what Esther was thinking.

Grayson, who normally had approximately fourteen things happening at once, became unusually quiet.

Even Colton wasn’t jumping off the furniture.

Okay.

He jumped off one thing.

But quietly.

On the third day, Esther put on her royal robes.

Hadley helped straighten them.

Grayson inspected the enormous palace doors.

Colton looked down the long hallway toward the throne room.

Uncle Jonathan swallowed.

“Ready?”

Esther took a breath.

Then she walked.

Step.

Step.

Step.

The children followed at a distance.

They reached the inner court.

And Esther stepped inside.

The king looked up.

Everything stopped.

Guards watched.

Servants watched.

Hadley held her breath.

Grayson held his breath.

Colton held his breath.

Uncle Jonathan was pretty sure he stopped breathing entirely.

The king stared at Esther.

Then slowly…

he reached for his golden scepter.

And held it out toward her.

Hadley exhaled.

Grayson almost cheered.

Colton did a tiny victory jump.

Esther was safe.

The king asked what she wanted.

He even promised to grant her request.

But Esther didn’t immediately blurt everything out.

She had a plan.

“Would the king and Haman come to a banquet I’ve prepared?”

Hadley smiled.

Esther was brave.

But she was also smart.

⸻

At the banquet, the king asked Esther again what she wanted.

But Esther waited.

She invited the king and Haman to another banquet the next day.

Haman left feeling VERY pleased with himself.

“A banquet with the king AND queen!”

He strutted home.

But then he saw Mordecai.

And suddenly Haman was angry all over again.

He became so consumed by hatred that he prepared a terrible punishment for Mordecai.

Haman thought everything was going exactly according to his plan.

But that night…

something unexpected happened.

The king couldn’t sleep.

So he had the royal records read to him.

Hadley blinked.

“That’s what the king does when he can’t sleep?”

Uncle Jonathan shrugged.

“I guess they didn’t have audiobooks.”

As the records were read, the king discovered something.

Years earlier, Mordecai had uncovered a plot against the king and saved his life.

“What reward did Mordecai receive?” the king asked.

“Nothing.”

The king was surprised.

Then Haman arrived.

Perfect timing.

The king asked him:

“What should be done for the man the king wants to honor?”

Haman immediately thought:

Obviously he means ME.

So Haman suggested an enormous public celebration.

Royal robes!

The king’s horse!

A parade through the city!

Someone announcing:

“This is what is done for the man the king delights to honor!”

The king smiled.

“Excellent.”

Haman puffed out his chest.

Then the king said:

“Go do all of that for Mordecai.”

Haman’s face fell.

Hadley covered her mouth.

Grayson burst out laughing.

Colton fell backward onto a cushion.

Uncle Jonathan whispered:

“That did NOT go the way Haman expected.”

⸻

Then came Esther’s second banquet.

The king was there.

Haman was there.

Esther was there.

And this time Esther knew she needed to speak.

The king asked:

“Queen Esther, what is your request?”

Esther looked at him.

This was the moment.

She could stay quiet and perhaps protect herself.

Or she could tell the truth and try to protect her people.

Hadley remembered Mordecai’s words:

For such a time as this.

Esther spoke.

She told the king that she was Jewish.

Then she told him that her people had been condemned to destruction.

The king was shocked.

“Who would dare do such a thing?”

Esther looked across the table.

Haman suddenly didn’t look quite so important anymore.

Esther answered:

“The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman.”

Grayson whispered:

“Uh-oh.”

Colton whispered:

“Busted.”

The king was furious.

Haman’s evil plan had been exposed.

Haman was punished, and Mordecai was eventually given Haman’s important position.

But there was still a problem.

The king’s earlier command couldn’t simply disappear under Persian law.

So the king allowed Esther and Mordecai to issue another decree giving the Jewish people the right to gather together and defend themselves.

Messengers raced throughout the empire.

The Jewish people were no longer helpless.

And when the dangerous day finally came, they successfully defended themselves against their enemies.

The day that was supposed to bring terrible sadness became a day of victory and celebration.

There was feasting.

There was happiness.

There were gifts.

And the Jewish people remembered what had happened.

That celebration became known as Purim.

⸻

Later, when everything had grown quiet, Hadley sat with Esther.

Grayson was nearby constructing an extremely complicated palace out of whatever objects he could find.

Colton was attempting to travel across the room without touching the floor.

Uncle Jonathan was trying unsuccessfully to convince him that royal furniture was not a parkour course.

And naturally, the little kitten had returned.

Hadley held it in her lap and gently stroked its fur.

“I think you’re really brave,” she told Esther.

Esther smiled.

“Do you think brave people ever feel afraid?”

Hadley thought about it.

“Yes.”

Esther nodded.

“So do I.”

Grayson looked up from his construction.

“Then what makes somebody brave?”

Esther looked at all three children.

“Choosing what is right when doing what is right is hard.”

The room became quiet.

“You don’t have to be a queen to do that,” Esther continued.

“You can tell the truth when lying would be easier.

“You can include someone who feels left out.

“You can stand up for someone who is being treated badly.

“You can obey God when other people aren’t.

“You can use whatever abilities God has given you to help someone else.”

Hadley looked thoughtful.

She loved learning.

She loved music.

Maybe those weren’t just things she enjoyed.

Maybe God could use them to bless people.

Grayson looked at the structure he’d built.

His mind was always moving, imagining and creating.

Maybe God could use that too.

Colton did a spectacular leap from one cushion to another.

Everyone stared at him.

“What?”

Esther laughed.

Even all that energy could be used for something good.

None of them were queens.

None of them lived in a Persian palace.

And hopefully none of them would ever have to walk into a throne room wondering whether a king would hold out his golden scepter.

But they would have their own moments.

Moments when telling the truth was difficult.

Moments when someone needed a friend.

Moments when they were nervous.

Moments when they had to decide between doing what was easy and doing what was right.

And perhaps, in those moments, they could remember Esther.

God had placed Esther somewhere she could make a difference.

And God could use Hadley exactly where she was.

He could use Grayson exactly where he was.

He could use Colton exactly where he was.

They didn’t have to wait until they were grown-ups to be kind, courageous, helpful, truthful, or faithful.

Hadley smiled.

Then she began humming a little song.

Grayson continued building.

Colton prepared for another jump.

The kitten curled up comfortably.

And Uncle Jonathan looked suspiciously at Colton.

“Don’t.”

Colton grinned.

“Don’t what?”

“Whatever you’re thinking.”

“I wasn’t thinking anything.”

“That worries me even more.”

Colton launched himself through the air.

“COOOOLTON!”

The children exploded with laughter.

And somewhere in that enormous Persian palace, three children carried away a lesson they would remember long after the adventure was over:

Being brave doesn’t mean you are never scared.

Being brave means trusting God enough to do what is right—even when it is hard.

And maybe God has placed each of us exactly where we are…

for such a time as this.

Based on the Book of Esther, especially Esther 2–9.
