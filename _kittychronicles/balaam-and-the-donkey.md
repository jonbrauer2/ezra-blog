“Uncle Jonathan! Wait!”

Hadley’s voice came from somewhere behind him.

Uncle Jonathan turned around just in time to see Colton and Grayson racing down the dusty road, with Hadley hurrying after them.

“What happened?”

“We found a cat!” Grayson announced.

That explained almost nothing.

“And she has **kittens**,” Colton added.

That explained considerably more.

Hadley arrived, slightly out of breath. “Five kittens.”

“Five?” Uncle Jonathan asked.

“Five,” she confirmed.

They led him to an old stone wall beside the road. Tucked underneath a bush was a beautiful mother cat, curled protectively around five tiny kittens.

One was orange.

One was gray.

One was black with little white paws.

One was striped.

And the smallest was mostly white, with a tiny dark patch over one ear.

“Meow,” said the mother cat.

“Meow!” said one kitten.

“Mew!” squeaked another.

“Mrrrrp,” said the tiny white one.

Grayson’s eyes widened.

“I think that one said something.”

“No,” said Uncle Jonathan. “Cats can make lots of sounds, but cats don’t talk.”

Hadley scratched the mother cat gently behind her ears.

**Purrrrrrrrrrrrr.**

Colton grinned. “She can purr.”

“She can definitely purr,” said Uncle Jonathan.

Just then—

**HEE-HAWWWW!**

The mother cat jumped.

All five kittens disappeared underneath her.

Colton jumped too.

Grayson jumped because Colton jumped.

Hadley did not jump, although she would later insist that she had *merely moved quickly*.

Around the bend came a man riding a donkey.

Two servants followed behind.

The man looked serious.

Very serious.

His name was **Balaam**.

The donkey looked considerably less serious.

She plodded along—

**clop… clop… clop…**

—ears bouncing with every step.

Grayson stared.

“Can donkeys talk?”

“No,” Uncle Jonathan said.

“Never?”

“Not ordinarily.”

The donkey passed them.

**HEE-HAW!**

Grayson giggled.

“That sounds almost like talking.”

“But it isn’t,” Hadley said.

The children had no idea that before the day was over, they were going to have to reconsider this entire conversation.

---

Balaam was going to see a king named Balak.

Balak was frightened of the Israelites, and he wanted Balaam to curse them.

But God had already told Balaam something very important:

**The Israelites were blessed.**

Balaam was not to curse the people God had blessed.

Yet Balaam wanted to go.

And now he was riding down the road.

The children watched him disappear around the bend.

Then Uncle Jonathan heard:

“Meow?”

The mother cat had stood up.

She sniffed the ground.

Then she began walking in exactly the same direction Balaam had gone.

Her five kittens toddled after her.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four—

Uncle Jonathan frowned.

“Where’s number five?”

Everyone froze.

They counted again.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

The tiny white kitten was gone.

“THE KITTEN!” cried Grayson.

The mother cat called anxiously.

“MEOW! MEOW!”

From somewhere down the road came a tiny—

“Mew!”

“There!” shouted Hadley.

And suddenly they were running.

---

The road twisted between hills.

They spotted the little kitten ahead, chasing a fluttering yellow butterfly.

“Mew!”

“Come back!” Colton called.

Of course, the kitten did not understand the request.

It bounced after the butterfly.

Around a rock.

Under a bush.

Across the road.

Then it disappeared around the next bend.

Uncle Jonathan and the children hurried after it.

But when they rounded the bend, they stopped.

Balaam’s donkey was there.

And she was acting strangely.

The road ran between two fields, bordered by low stone walls.

The donkey had stopped.

Completely.

Balaam pulled on the reins.

“Go!”

The donkey’s ears stood straight up.

She stared down the road.

“What is she looking at?” Hadley whispered.

Uncle Jonathan looked.

Nothing.

Colton looked.

Nothing.

Grayson squinted so hard his whole face wrinkled.

“I don’t see anything.”

Neither did Balaam.

But the donkey did.

Standing directly in the road was **the angel of the Lord**, holding a drawn sword.

The donkey’s eyes grew enormous.

Then—

**WHOOSH!**

She bolted off the road and straight into the field.

“WHOA!”

Balaam grabbed the reins.

The children jumped backward.

The mother cat shot up the stone wall.

Four kittens scrambled after her.

Balaam angrily forced the donkey back onto the road.

“That donkey really didn’t want to go that way,” Hadley said.

“Maybe she saw something,” Grayson suggested.

Balaam certainly didn't think so.

He was angry with his donkey.

But Uncle Jonathan looked thoughtful.

“Sometimes,” he said, “we should be careful about assuming we understand everything that's happening.”

They continued after the missing kitten.

---

The road became narrower.

Now there were walls on both sides.

Ahead of them, the donkey stopped again.

The angel was there again.

The donkey tried to squeeze past.

She pressed herself tightly against the wall—

and Balaam’s foot got squashed.

“OW!”

Balaam became even angrier.

The donkey trembled.

“She’s scared,” Hadley whispered.

Colton noticed it too.

“She really is.”

Balaam still couldn’t see why.

But the children were beginning to realize something.

The donkey wasn’t being naughty.

She knew something Balaam didn’t.

Then they heard—

“Mew!”

“The kitten!”

The tiny white kitten appeared farther ahead.

It had apparently forgotten all about the butterfly and was now investigating a beetle.

“Don’t go any farther!” Hadley called.

She started forward.

Uncle Jonathan caught her hand.

“Stay close.”

The path ahead became even narrower.

Too narrow to turn around.

Too narrow to escape.

Balaam’s donkey walked into it—

then suddenly stopped.

The angel stood directly ahead.

There was nowhere for the donkey to go.

So she did the only thing she could.

She sat down.

**PLOP.**

Balaam nearly slid over her head.

Grayson burst out laughing.

Even Hadley giggled.

But Balaam was furious.

He struck the donkey.

“Why won't you move?!”

The donkey turned her head.

And then something happened that made Hadley, Colton, Grayson, and Uncle Jonathan absolutely motionless.

God opened the donkey’s mouth.

And the donkey said:

**“What have I done to you that you have struck me these three times?”**

Silence.

Absolute silence.

Colton’s mouth dropped open.

Hadley’s eyes became enormous.

Grayson grabbed Uncle Jonathan’s arm.

“UNCLE JONATHAN.”

“I heard it.”

“THE DONKEY TALKED.”

“I heard that too.”

“DONKEYS CAN TALK!”

“Well…” Uncle Jonathan said, “apparently they can when **God makes them**.”

The mother cat stared at the donkey.

“Meow?”

Everyone looked at her.

She licked one paw.

Just an ordinary cat.

Grayson seemed relieved.

“The cat still doesn't talk.”

“No,” said Uncle Jonathan.

The donkey, however, was not finished.

Balaam actually began arguing with her!

The children stared in amazement.

The donkey reminded Balaam that she had carried him faithfully for years.

“Have I ever behaved like this before?”

Balaam stopped.

“No.”

And then—

God opened Balaam’s eyes.

Suddenly Balaam saw what the donkey had seen all along.

The angel.

The sword.

Balaam dropped to the ground.

The children stopped smiling.

Now they understood.

The donkey hadn't been trying to make Balaam's journey difficult.

**She had been saving his life.**

The angel told Balaam that his way was wrong.

“If your donkey had not turned away from me those three times,” the angel said, Balaam would have died.

Balaam looked at his donkey.

The animal he had been angry with had protected him.

“I have sinned,” Balaam said.

For the first time that day, Balaam seemed to realize that the problem hadn't been his donkey.

The problem had been **Balaam**.

---

“Mew!”

Everyone turned.

The missing kitten!

It was sitting on a rock—

very close to the narrow road.

The mother cat spotted it.

“MEOW!”

“Mew!”

She bounded forward.

The little kitten came running.

The mother immediately began licking its face as though she intended to clean it for the next three days.

The other four kittens piled around them.

“One, two, three, four, five,” Hadley counted.

“Five!” said Colton.

Grayson crouched beside them.

The tiny white kitten climbed onto his shoe.

Then his leg.

Then somehow managed to get halfway up his shirt.

“Uh… Uncle Jonathan?”

Uncle Jonathan carefully rescued him.

Soon all five kittens were safely curled around their mother.

**Purrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrr.**

Balaam looked at his donkey differently now too.

He rubbed her neck.

The donkey flicked one ear.

Colton approached cautiously.

“Do you think she'll talk again?”

The donkey looked directly at him.

Everyone waited.

She opened her mouth.

**HEEEEE-HAWWWW!**

The children burst out laughing.

“Nope,” said Hadley.

“Back to normal,” said Uncle Jonathan.

---

But Balaam still had to meet King Balak.

And the children wanted to know what would happen.

So the adventure wasn't over.

They traveled onward until they could see the camp of Israel spread across the valley.

There were thousands of tents below them.

King Balak was waiting.

He wanted Balaam to curse Israel.

But Balaam had learned something on the road.

He could not simply say whatever he wanted.

He must say what **God** told him to say.

Altars were built.

Sacrifices were offered.

Balak waited eagerly.

Balaam looked over God's people.

Then he opened his mouth.

Balak leaned forward.

Surely now the curse would come.

But instead—

**Balaam blessed them.**

Balak's face changed.

“That is NOT what I brought you here to do!”

The children tried very hard not to laugh.

So Balak took Balaam somewhere else.

“Try from here.”

Balaam looked at Israel again.

And blessed them again.

Balak was horrified.

They moved again.

Perhaps *this* location would work.

Again Balaam spoke.

Again God would not allow him to curse His people.

And Balaam gave another blessing.

Grayson whispered, “I don't think the king likes this.”

“He definitely does not,” Hadley whispered back.

The children looked across the enormous camp below.

Something finally made sense.

The little Israelite families down there didn't even know what was happening on the mountain.

They didn't know a king wanted them cursed.

They didn't know Balaam was standing above them.

They didn't know about the donkey.

They didn't know about the angel.

But **God knew.**

And God was watching over them.

Uncle Jonathan sat down beside the children.

The mother cat settled beside him.

One kitten climbed into Hadley's lap.

Another curled beside Colton.

The tiny white one chose Grayson.

Naturally.

“You know what I think is interesting?” Uncle Jonathan asked.

“What?” said Hadley.

“Balaam thought his donkey was the problem.”

“But she was helping him,” said Colton.

“Exactly.”

Grayson stroked the kitten in his lap.

“And the donkey could see the angel when Balaam couldn't.”

“Exactly.”

Hadley looked thoughtfully across the valley.

“So Balaam didn't know everything he thought he knew.”

Uncle Jonathan smiled.

“Neither do we.”

Sometimes something happens that we don't understand.

Sometimes we don't get what we want.

Sometimes someone tells us **no**.

Sometimes the road we want to take seems to close right in front of us.

We might become frustrated.

But we only see the little piece right in front of us.

**God sees the whole road.**

And sometimes what feels like something getting in our way might actually be God protecting us, teaching us, or guiding us somewhere better.

Colton looked at Balaam's donkey.

“So we should listen to God even when we don't understand?”

“Especially then.”

The donkey wandered over and sniffed Colton's hair.

Colton froze.

“Hello.”

The donkey stared at him.

Colton stared back.

“Do you have anything else you would like to tell us?”

The donkey opened her mouth.

Everyone leaned closer.

**HEEEEEEE-HAWWWWWW!**

The mother cat jumped straight into Uncle Jonathan's lap.

Five kittens scattered in five different directions.

“THE KITTENS!” cried Hadley.

“One!”

“Two!”

“THREE!”

“Where's four?!”

“I've got four!”

“WHERE'S FIVE?”

And Uncle Jonathan suddenly realized that getting Balaam safely past an angel with a sword might have been easier than getting **five kittens safely home**.

The adventure, apparently, was not quite finished.
