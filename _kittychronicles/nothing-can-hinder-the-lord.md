The first thing Hadley noticed was the smoke.

Not campfire smoke.

Something bigger.

She opened her eyes.

Above her, the sky was turning orange with the first light of morning.

For several seconds, she didn’t move.

Something was wrong.

Very wrong.

She wasn’t in a bed.

She was lying on a rough wool blanket underneath a tree.

And somewhere nearby—

CLANG!

Hadley sat straight up.

Men were moving through the darkness.

Lots of them.

Some carried wooden shields. Others were tightening leather straps around their arms. A nervous-looking man hurried past carrying a bundle of arrows.

Hadley looked down at herself.

She was wearing a long tunic.

“Uhhhh…”

A head suddenly popped up beside her.

It was Grayson.

His hair was sticking up in approximately fourteen directions.

“Where are we?”

“I have absolutely no idea.”

Then they heard a noise behind them.

THUMP.

Colton had apparently awakened on a rock.

Not beside a rock.

On it.

He looked down.

“Why am I sleeping on a boulder?”

“You probably climbed it in your sleep,” said Hadley.

“Fair.”

Then another voice came from beneath a nearby blanket.

“Could everybody please stop talking? It’s approximately five o’clock in the—”

Uncle Jonathan sat up.

He stopped.

A man carrying a spear walked past.

Uncle Jonathan blinked.

The man kept walking.

Uncle Jonathan slowly lay back down.

“Nope.”

Hadley pulled his blanket away.

“Get up.”

“I would like to try waking up again.”

“Uncle Jonathan.”

He opened one eye.

“This time I would like Colorado.”

⸻

The four of them began looking around.

They were in some kind of military camp.

But it wasn’t like any army they had ever seen.

No trucks.

No radios.

No electricity.

No guns.

No engines.

Just tents, shields, bows, slings and nervous men.

Beyond the camp, enormous rocky cliffs rose on either side of a narrow pass.

And on the opposite ridge…

Hadley saw movement.

Soldiers.

Hundreds of them.

Maybe thousands beyond them.

Their weapons glinted in the morning light.

Grayson squinted.

“Those guys have much better equipment than we do.”

He was right.

Something else was strange.

The men around them barely seemed armed.

One carried a farming tool.

Another had a wooden staff.

Another had a sling.

“Where are all our swords?” Colton asked.

A nearby soldier overheard him.

“Swords?”

He gave a bitter laugh.

“You’ll have to ask the Philistines.”

Hadley froze.

“The… who?”

“The Philistines.”

All four stared at one another.

Grayson whispered:

“Wait.”

Hadley’s eyes widened.

Colton slowly grinned.

Uncle Jonathan said:

“Ohhhhh no.”

The soldier pointed toward the enemy.

“The Philistines won’t allow blacksmiths among us. They don’t want Hebrews making swords or spears.”

He shook his head.

“On the day of battle, there are only two swords among us.”

“Two?!” said Grayson.

“In the entire army?”

The soldier nodded.

“King Saul has one.”

Hadley already knew what he was going to say next.

“And the other?”

“His son.”

The soldier walked away.

Nobody spoke.

Finally Colton said:

“His son is named Jonathan.”

Hadley nodded.

Uncle Jonathan looked toward the cliffs.

“So…”

Grayson finished the thought.

“We’re in First Samuel.”

⸻

That changed things.

Somewhere in this camp was King Saul.

Somewhere nearby was Jonathan.

That Jonathan.

The son of Saul.

The warrior.

Hadley immediately began searching faces.

Then she spotted him.

A young man stood near the edge of camp with another soldier beside him.

Jonathan’s armor-bearer.

They weren’t talking loudly.

In fact, they seemed to be making sure nobody was listening.

Jonathan glanced toward his father’s camp.

Then toward the Philistine outpost.

Then he and his armor-bearer quietly walked away.

Hadley stared.

“Guys.”

No answer.

“GUYS.”

Grayson turned.

She pointed.

Prince Jonathan was disappearing toward the cliffs.

Colton’s face lit up.

“Oh, we’re following him.”

“Why,” asked Hadley, “is that ALWAYS your answer?”

But Grayson was already gathering his things.

Uncle Jonathan sighed.

“I suppose we’re following him.”

⸻

They kept their distance.

Jonathan and his armor-bearer slipped farther and farther from the Israelite camp.

The terrain became rough.

Huge rocks jutted from the earth.

Thorny plants grabbed at their clothing.

Hadley noticed something strange.

Jonathan wasn’t bringing an army.

There were hundreds of Israelites back at camp.

He had brought…

one man.

His armor-bearer.

“That can’t be right,” Grayson whispered.

“Maybe he’s scouting,” Hadley said.

They climbed higher.

Then they saw where Jonathan was headed.

Everyone stopped.

Two enormous rocky crags guarded the pass.

One was called Bozez.

The other Seneh.

And above them stood the Philistine outpost.

Armed soldiers.

Watching the valley.

Colton stared upward.

His eyes grew brighter.

Hadley saw the expression.

“No.”

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You’re looking at that cliff like it’s a playground.”

“It has excellent handholds.”

“Colton.”

“I am merely making a geological observation.”

⸻

Ahead of them, Jonathan stopped.

The four crouched behind some rocks close enough to hear him speaking to his armor-bearer.

Jonathan looked toward the Philistine soldiers.

Then he said something remarkable.

“Come, let’s go over to the outpost of those uncircumcised men.”

Grayson whispered:

“With two people?”

Jonathan continued.

“Perhaps the LORD will act in our behalf.”

Then came the words that silenced everyone:

“Nothing can hinder the LORD from saving, whether by many or by few.”

Hadley stopped breathing for a moment.

There it was.

Jonathan didn’t say:

I’m an incredible warrior.

He didn’t say:

We can definitely beat them.

He didn’t even say that he knew exactly what God was going to do.

He said:

Perhaps the LORD will act.

Jonathan had courage without pretending to know God’s plan.

And he knew something else with absolute confidence:

God wasn’t limited by numbers.

Six hundred men.

Sixty men.

Six men.

Two men.

If God chose to act, the size of the army wasn’t the thing that would stop Him.

Jonathan’s armor-bearer looked at him.

“Do all that you have in mind. Go ahead; I am with you heart and soul.”

Uncle Jonathan quietly smiled.

“That’s a pretty good friend.”

⸻

Then Prince Jonathan explained his plan.

And it was…

unusual.

“We’ll show ourselves to the Philistines.”

Grayson blinked.

Hadley whispered:

“Show themselves?”

This was apparently not a sneaking mission anymore.

Jonathan continued.

“If they tell us, ‘Wait there until we come to you,’ we’ll stay where we are.”

Reasonable.

“But if they say, ‘Come up to us,’ we’ll climb up, because that will be our sign that the LORD has given them into our hands.”

Colton’s jaw dropped.

He looked at the cliff.

Then at Jonathan.

Then back at the cliff.

“I REALLY like this guy.”

⸻

Jonathan and his armor-bearer stepped into view.

Almost immediately, someone above shouted.

“LOOK!”

Philistine soldiers appeared along the ridge.

“The Hebrews are crawling out of the holes they were hiding in!”

More Philistines laughed.

Then came the shout:

“COME UP TO US AND WE’LL TEACH YOU A LESSON!”

Silence.

Jonathan turned toward his armor-bearer.

His face changed.

“Climb up after me.”

Then he said:

“The LORD has given them into the hand of Israel.”

And Jonathan started climbing.

On his hands.

On his feet.

Straight up the rocks.

His armor-bearer followed.

Colton was practically vibrating.

“Can I go now?”

Hadley looked at Uncle Jonathan.

Uncle Jonathan looked at Grayson.

Grayson looked at Colton.

Colton was already halfway up.

“COLTON!”

⸻

Up they went.

Grayson found places where the rock could support their weight.

Hadley stayed close, calling out safer footholds she could see from below.

“Left!”

Grayson moved left.

“Higher!”

Uncle Jonathan reached up.

“Colton, slow DOWN!”

“I AM slowing down!”

“You just passed everybody!”

“I’M SLOWING DOWN FAST!”

Above them, Prince Jonathan reached the top.

A Philistine charged.

Jonathan struck him down.

Another came.

Then another.

His armor-bearer followed directly behind him.

The tiny Israelite attack crashed into the outpost.

Within a small area of ground, around twenty Philistine soldiers fell before Jonathan and his armor-bearer.

Then something happened that none of them caused.

The ground moved.

At first, Hadley thought Colton had knocked something over.

Then—

RRRRRRRUMBLE.

Rocks trembled.

The hillside shook beneath their feet.

Grayson grabbed the ground.

“EARTHQUAKE!”

Down in the Philistine camp, soldiers began shouting.

Men ran from their tents.

Confusion spread.

And then panic.

The Bible says terror struck the Philistine army—the troops in the camp, those in the field, and even the raiding parties.

The earth shook.

And the panic became enormous.

⸻

Far away, Saul’s watchmen saw something strange.

The Philistine army was melting away.

Soldiers were scattering in every direction.

Saul ordered his men counted.

Someone was missing.

Jonathan.

And his armor-bearer.

Soon the Israelites rushed into battle.

Hebrews who had previously joined or been among the Philistines switched back to Israel.

Others who had been hiding in the hill country heard that the Philistines were fleeing and joined the pursuit.

The army that had been frightened and stuck…

was suddenly moving.

Hadley stood on the ridge watching it unfold.

One act of courageous faith had changed the entire battlefield.

⸻

Later, after the fighting had moved away, the four sat exhausted among the rocks.

Colton had a scrape on his arm.

He was extremely proud of it.

Grayson was studying the cliff they had climbed.

Hadley sat quietly.

Something bothered her.

“I think I understand the story differently now.”

Uncle Jonathan looked at her.

“How?”

Hadley thought for a moment.

“I used to think the lesson was just, ‘Be brave and God will make you win.’”

Prince Jonathan had overheard her.

He walked over.

Hadley continued.

“But that’s not actually what you said.”

Jonathan sat down.

“No.”

“You said perhaps God would act.”

Jonathan nodded.

Hadley understood.

Jonathan hadn’t received a promise that morning saying:

Go attack that outpost and I guarantee nothing bad will happen.

He didn’t know everything that would happen next.

He simply knew who God was.

Grayson looked up.

“So faith isn’t believing that whatever I want to happen is definitely going to happen?”

“No,” Jonathan said.

“Then what is it?”

Jonathan looked toward the battlefield.

“Knowing that the LORD is able—and choosing to be faithful to Him.”

Uncle Jonathan smiled.

That was the difference.

Faith wasn’t pretending there was no cliff.

The cliff was real.

Faith wasn’t pretending there weren’t Philistines.

There were plenty of them.

Faith wasn’t pretending Jonathan had a huge army.

He had one armor-bearer.

Faith looked directly at everything that seemed impossible and remembered something bigger:

God is not limited by the things that limit us.

Not numbers.

Not weapons.

Not strength.

Not age.

Not what everyone else is afraid of.

“Nothing can hinder the LORD from saving, whether by many or by few.”

For a while, nobody said anything.

Then Colton stood.

“So…”

Hadley immediately knew.

“No.”

“What?”

“We are NOT climbing back down that cliff your way.”

“I haven’t even explained my way.”

“Your face explained your way.”

Grayson looked over the edge.

“I’d actually like to hear his proposal.”

“Thank you, Grayson.”

Uncle Jonathan stood.

“Absolutely not.”

Colton grinned.

Then he jumped onto a rock.

“Last one down is a Philistine!”

“COLTON!”

And he was gone.

Grayson took off after him.

Uncle Jonathan groaned and followed.

Hadley shook her head.

Then she looked once more at Prince Jonathan.

Two men had climbed that cliff.

They hadn’t known exactly what God would do.

They had simply believed that God was bigger than the obstacle in front of them—and had been willing to act faithfully instead of sitting safely beneath a pomegranate tree.

Hadley smiled.

Then she ran after the others.

And she carried one sentence with her all the way down:

Nothing can hinder the LORD from saving, whether by many or by few.

Based on 1 Samuel 13:19–23 and 1 Samuel 14:1–23.
