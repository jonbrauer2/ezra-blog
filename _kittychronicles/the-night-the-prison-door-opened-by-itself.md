![Peter arrives at the house after the prison doors open](/ezra-blog/assets/images/family/kittychronicles/volume-2/peter-at-the-door.png)

Colton knocked on the wooden door.

**KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.**

Nothing happened.

He knocked again.

**KNOCK-KNOCK-KNOCK-KNOCK-KNOCK!**

Hadley caught his wrist.

“You don’t have to attack it.”

“Maybe they didn’t hear me.”

“Everyone in Jerusalem heard you.”

Grayson examined the heavy iron ring hanging from the door. “This is a very good door.”

“It is a closed door,” Uncle Jonathan said, “which is the part currently causing us difficulty.”

From the other side came a tiny sound.

**Mew.**

All three children froze.

Colton leaned close to the crack. “Kitty?”

A small orange paw appeared underneath the door.

That was all it took.

Hadley knelt. Grayson lay flat on his stomach. Colton tried to reach one finger under the door.

The paw tapped him.

“It likes me!”

“It thinks your finger is a mouse,” Hadley said.

“It still likes me.”

The door opened so suddenly that all three children tumbled forward in a heap.

A young woman holding an oil lamp stared down at them.

The orange kitten sat beside her feet.

“Who are you?” she asked.

Uncle Jonathan helped the children stand. “Travelers. We were told Mary’s house was a safe place to stay.”

The woman’s expression softened. “I’m Rhoda. Come in quickly.”

Then she shut the door and slid the wooden bar into place.

**THUNK.**

Grayson looked at the bar.

“Why are you locking it?”

Rhoda glanced toward the dark street.

“Because tonight, doors matter.”

---

Inside the house, people were praying.

Not quiet, sleepy prayers.

Urgent prayers.

Men and women knelt on woven mats. Some whispered. Some lifted their hands. Some had tears on their faces.

The orange kitten wandered between them as if checking each prayer personally.

Hadley sat beside Rhoda.

“Who are they praying for?”

Rhoda lowered her voice.

“Peter.”

The children knew that name.

Peter had followed Jesus. Peter had walked on water—briefly. Peter had preached about the risen Christ.

And now Peter was in prison.

King Herod had arrested some of the believers. James, the brother of John, had been killed. When Herod saw that this pleased certain leaders, he arrested Peter too.

“When will they let him go?” Grayson asked.

Rhoda’s eyes filled with worry.

“They plan to bring him out after Passover.”

Nobody needed to ask what that meant.

Herod did not intend to set Peter free.

Colton looked at the strong door behind them.

“Can we go get him?”

Uncle Jonathan put an arm around him. “Peter is guarded by soldiers.”

“How many?”

“Sixteen are assigned in shifts,” Rhoda said. “And he is chained between two of them.”

Colton frowned.

Two soldiers beside Peter.

More at the door.

More outside.

Chains.

Stone walls.

An iron gate.

This was not a rescue Colton could solve by climbing something.

Hadley watched the believers pray.

They had no army.

They had no key.

They could not even get close to Peter.

But they could pray to the One who could.

So Hadley bowed her head.

Grayson did too.

Colton pulled the kitten into his lap.

And together they prayed.

---

Across the city, Peter slept.

That surprised Grayson when he heard about it later.

“He slept?”

Yes.

On the night before Herod planned to bring him out, Peter was chained between two soldiers—and sleeping.

One chain fastened his right wrist.

Another fastened his left.

Guards stood at the prison entrance.

The doors were locked.

The iron gate was shut.

Then light filled the cell.

Not torchlight.

Heavenly light.

An angel of the Lord stood beside Peter.

Peter kept sleeping.

So the angel struck him on the side.

“Quick! Get up!”

Peter opened his eyes.

And the chains fell from his wrists.

They did not need a key.

They did not need to be filed through.

They simply fell.

The angel told Peter to put on his clothes and sandals.

Peter obeyed.

“Wrap your cloak around you and follow me.”

Peter followed the angel out of the cell.

He passed the first guard.

Then the second.

Neither stopped him.

Peter thought he might be seeing a vision.

They reached the iron gate leading into the city.

It was the final barrier.

Heavy.

Locked.

Impossible for a chained prisoner to open.

The angel did not touch it.

Peter did not push it.

The gate opened by itself.

**CREEEEEEAK.**

They walked through.

Down one street.

Then suddenly the angel was gone.

Peter stood alone beneath the night sky.

He looked back.

The prison was behind him.

The city was ahead.

And at last he understood.

“Now I know without a doubt that the Lord has sent His angel and rescued me.”

---

Back at Mary’s house, everyone was still praying.

Colton had fallen asleep sitting up.

The kitten had fallen asleep on Colton.

Grayson was trying to stay awake by counting floor stones.

Hadley was listening to a woman pray for Peter’s courage when—

**KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.**

Every head lifted.

Rhoda stood.

“I’ll go.”

She hurried through the courtyard.

Hadley followed far enough to peek around a corner.

Rhoda stopped at the outer door.

“Who is it?”

A man answered.

Rhoda gasped.

She knew that voice.

“Peter?”

“Yes. Rhoda, please open the door.”

Rhoda’s face exploded into joy.

Then she did something no one expected.

She ran away.

She left Peter standing outside the locked door.

Hadley stared after her.

“Rhoda?”

But Rhoda was already racing into the room.

“Peter is at the door!”

The praying stopped.

People looked at her.

“You’re out of your mind.”

“I am not! It’s Peter!”

“It must be his angel.”

Hadley looked back toward the courtyard.

**KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.**

“Shouldn’t someone open it?”

Nobody moved.

**KNOCK-KNOCK-KNOCK!**

Colton woke with a start.

The kitten slid into his lap.

“What’s happening?”

“Peter may be outside,” Grayson said.

“Then open the door!”

It was an excellent suggestion.

Everyone rushed into the courtyard.

Uncle Jonathan lifted the bar.

Rhoda pulled the door open.

And there stood Peter.

Alive.

Free.

Still knocking.

For one second, nobody said anything.

Then everybody started talking at once.

“PETER!”

“HOW?”

“YOU’RE FREE!”

“WHAT HAPPENED?”

Peter raised both hands.

“Quiet!”

The room slowly settled.

Peter told them everything.

The light.

The angel.

The chains falling off.

The sleeping guards.

The iron gate opening by itself.

Hadley watched the faces around her.

These people had been asking God to help Peter.

But when Peter arrived, they almost did not believe it.

God had answered while they were still praying.

And His answer was standing at the door.

---

Later, after Peter had gone somewhere safer, the children sat in the courtyard.

The orange kitten chased a moth through the lamplight.

Grayson leaned against the door.

“It’s funny.”

“What is?” Hadley asked.

“There were two locked doors tonight.”

Colton looked around. “The prison door and this door.”

“Right. God opened the impossible one. But Peter had to keep knocking at the ordinary one because nobody believed Rhoda.”

Uncle Jonathan smiled. “That is one of the wonderfully honest details in the story.”

Hadley thought about the people praying inside.

“Does that mean they didn’t have faith?”

“It means their faith wasn’t perfect,” Uncle Jonathan said. “But they prayed anyway.”

That mattered.

They were afraid.

They were grieving.

They did not know how God would answer.

They even struggled to believe the answer when it arrived.

But they kept praying.

And God was not limited by the weakness of their prayers.

Colton picked up the kitten.

“What should we call him?”

Hadley considered the warm glow from the oil lamp.

“Lantern.”

The kitten purred.

“Lantern it is,” said Grayson.

Colton looked toward the door.

“If Peter comes back, I’m opening it the first time.”

“You’ll probably be asleep,” Hadley said.

“Then Lantern will wake me.”

The kitten yawned.

That did not look promising.

Uncle Jonathan stood and rested his hand on the heavy wooden door.

“There will be times when a problem looks locked,” he said. “Too many guards. Too many chains. Too many gates. We may not know what God will do. But we can always bring it to Him.”

Hadley looked up at the stars above Jerusalem.

God had not promised that His people would never face prison or loss. James had died, and the church still grieved him.

But Herod was not in control of everything.

The guards were not in control of everything.

The chains were not in control of everything.

God was.

And sometimes, while His people were still praying, an iron gate simply opened.

Based on Acts 12:1–19.
