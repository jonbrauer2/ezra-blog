## A Kitten With Loud Feet

“Can you be quieter than a kitten?” Hadley whispered.

Colton took one careful step. He took another. Then Tumbles pounced on a dry leaf.

*CRUNCH.*

“Yes,” Colton said. “Yes, I can.”

The three children were imagining a visit to Shiloh long ago. In their adventure, they could watch the Bible story, but no one there could see them or hear them. That was lucky for Tumbles. He was now wrestling the leaf with all four paws.

An oil lamp was still burning in the Lord’s house. Grayson watched the little flame hold steady. “I could make a lamp like that,” he whispered. “But I think I’d ask for help with the fire part.”

Not far from the lamp, a boy named Samuel had lain down to sleep. He helped Eli the priest, whose eyes had grown weak. Eli was lying down too.

Then someone called:

“Samuel!”

Samuel answered, “Here I am!” He got up and ran to Eli.

“I didn’t call you,” Eli said. “Go back and lie down.”

Samuel went back. So did Tumbles, who had decided this was an excellent game.

## Who Was Calling?

“Samuel!”

The boy got up again. Once more he went straight to Eli. And once more Eli said he hadn’t called.

“Samuel must be really good at getting up,” said Grayson. “I can barely find my shoes the first time somebody calls me.”

Then it happened a third time. “Here I am,” Samuel told Eli. “You called me.”

This time Eli understood. It was the Lord calling the boy.

“Go and lie down,” Eli told him. “If you hear the voice again, say, ‘Speak, Lord, for your servant is listening.’”

Hadley had been trying to guess who the voice belonged to. Now she stopped guessing. Even Tumbles had gone still, one white paw resting on his thoroughly defeated leaf.

Samuel lay down again. The Lord came and called as before, but this time He said Samuel’s name twice.

“Samuel, Samuel!”

Samuel answered, “Speak, for your servant is listening.”

Colton didn’t even wiggle.

## The Hard Part of Listening

The Lord told Samuel something serious. Eli’s sons had done wrong, and Eli knew about it but hadn’t stopped them. God said He would hold Eli’s family accountable.

Samuel stayed where he was until morning. When the time came, he opened the doors of the Lord’s house. But he was afraid to tell Eli what God had said.

“I’d be afraid too,” whispered Colton. “Especially if I liked the person.”

Eli called Samuel and asked him not to leave anything out. So Samuel told him everything. Eli knew the message had come from the Lord, and he accepted that God would do what was right.

“That was the brave part,” Grayson said.

“Getting up in the dark?” asked Colton.

“Telling the truth in the morning.”

Hadley watched daylight spread across the doorway. She thought about how Samuel had said “Here I am” when he thought he was needed, and how he had stayed ready to listen when he learned who was really calling. She wondered which would be harder for her: getting quiet enough to listen, or doing what she knew was right once she had listened.

As Samuel grew, the Lord was with him. The people of Israel came to know that Samuel was a prophet of the Lord. And it had started while he was still a boy, on an ordinary night when an oil lamp hadn’t quite gone out.

Tumbles finally lifted his paw. The leaf didn’t move. He looked almost disappointed.

“Come on,” Hadley said. “I think it’s heard enough.”

## What Can We Take Home?

Samuel didn’t recognize God’s voice right away, and Eli helped him learn how to respond. We can make room to listen to God by slowing down, reading the Bible, and asking a trusted grown-up to help us understand. Listening also means being willing to do what’s right, even when it’s hard.

**Talk about it:** What helped Samuel listen? When is it hardest for you to slow down and listen to someone?

**Try it:** Read [1 Samuel 3:1–10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%203%3A1-10&version=KJV) together. Then sit quietly for a moment and pray: “Lord, help me listen and do what is right today.”
