![The Footprints That Were Too Big](/ezra-blog/assets/images/family/kittychronicles/rehab-and-the-spies.jpg)

Hadley was the first one to notice the footprint.

She stopped so suddenly that Grayson walked straight into her.

“Ow.”

“Shhh.”

“Why?”

Hadley pointed at the ground.

There, pressed deep into the dusty earth, was a footprint.

A huge footprint.

Colton crouched beside it.

Then he put his own foot inside.

His entire shoe fit comfortably in the heel.

Colton looked up.

“Uncle Jonathan?”

“Yes?”

“I think somebody here has really big feet.”

Uncle Jonathan stared at the footprint.

“That is an excellent observation.”

Grayson knelt beside it.

“Maybe it’s from a really tall person.”

Another footprint lay several feet away.

Then another.

Hadley followed them with her eyes.

They disappeared toward a stone wall in the distance.

Colton grinned.

“Let’s follow them.”

“No,” said Uncle Jonathan.

“Yes,” said Colton.

“No.”

Colton was already moving.

“COLTON!”

And that was how Uncle Jonathan, Hadley, Grayson, and Colton found themselves following the largest footprints they had ever seen toward a city they had never seen before.

They still weren’t entirely sure where they were.

But several things were becoming obvious.

There were no cars.

No electricity.

No paved roads.

Everyone they had seen was dressed in ancient-looking clothes.

And now apparently there were people with feet the size of dinner plates.

Which was usually a sign that they had somehow ended up in another Bible story.

Again.

⸻

They hadn’t gone far when Hadley heard something.

Mrowwww!

She stopped.

“Kitty?”

The sound came again.

MROWWW!

It sounded frightened.

They followed it toward a pile of rocks.

A small brown-and-white cat was crouched underneath them.

Her fur was puffed up.

Her eyes were enormous.

“She’s scared,” Hadley whispered.

Then something moved underneath the cat.

Mew.

Hadley’s eyes widened.

“KITTENS!”

There were four.

Tiny.

Fluffy.

One gray.

One orange.

One black-and-white.

And one little striped kitten that was attempting to climb directly over its mother’s head.

Colton pointed.

“That one’s me.”

Hadley laughed.

“Why?”

The kitten reached the top of its mother, lost its balance—

plop.

—and rolled into the dirt.

“That’s why.”

Hadley slowly held out her hand.

“It’s okay, Mama. We’re not going to hurt them.”

The mother cat sniffed her fingers.

Then—

THUMP.

The cat flattened herself over her kittens.

Everyone froze.

THUMP.

Dust trembled on the ground.

THUMP.

Grayson whispered:

“What is that?”

A shadow passed over them.

Hadley slowly looked up.

And up.

And up.

A man was walking past.

Except he was enormous.

He had shoulders like a doorway.

His arms looked bigger than Uncle Jonathan’s legs.

And when he stepped—

THUMP.

Colton’s mouth fell open.

“That’s…”

Nobody answered.

Another huge man appeared behind him.

Then another.

The mother cat hissed softly and pushed her kittens farther beneath the rocks.

Apparently she was afraid of the giants too.

Grayson whispered:

“Are those actually giants?”

Uncle Jonathan suddenly had a very uncomfortable idea.

Ancient land.

Fortified city.

Giants.

He looked toward the enormous footprints.

Then toward the hills.

“Ohhhhhh.”

Hadley knew that tone.

“What?”

“I think I know where we are.”

“Where?”

Uncle Jonathan whispered:

“Canaan.”

Hadley’s eyes widened.

“The Promised Land?”

Grayson looked at the giants.

Then back at Uncle Jonathan.

“Wait.”

Hadley understood.

“The twelve spies!”

⸻

A movement in the trees caught their attention.

Men.

Twelve of them.

They were hiding.

Watching the city.

One whispered:

“Descendants of Anak.”

Another shook his head.

“Look at them.”

“They’re enormous.”

The children crouched behind some bushes.

Colton whispered:

“So we’re spies now?”

“No,” Uncle Jonathan whispered. “They’re spies.”

“We’re hiding with the spies.”

“Yes.”

“So we’re spy helpers.”

“I don’t think that’s a thing.”

Colton nodded seriously.

“It is now.”

The twelve men had been sent by Moses.

One leader from each tribe of Israel.

Their mission was to explore Canaan—the land God had promised His people.

For forty days they traveled through it.

And the children went with them.

They saw rich farmland.

Beautiful hills.

Cities surrounded by enormous walls.

Figs.

Pomegranates.

And grapes so huge that when the spies cut down one cluster, two men carried it between them on a pole.

Grayson stared.

“That’s a lot of grapes.”

Colton looked thoughtful.

“I could eat those.”

“All of them?” Hadley asked.

“Eventually.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“I’d train.”

“For eating grapes?”

“Yes.”

Uncle Jonathan shook his head.

“Please don’t begin grape training.”

⸻

But the farther they traveled, the more nervous some of the spies became.

Every fortified city seemed bigger than the last.

And then there were the giants.

The children saw another one near a town.

Colton had been climbing along a low stone wall.

He jumped onto a boulder.

Then another.

Then—

THUMP.

He froze.

A giant walked around the corner.

Colton’s eyes went enormous.

The giant hadn’t seen him.

Very slowly, Colton lowered himself behind the boulder.

Hadley whispered:

“Colton.”

“What?”

“You’re actually hiding.”

“I am being tactically invisible.”

“You weren’t tactically invisible five minutes ago.”

“That was before the giant.”

Uncle Jonathan couldn’t argue with that.

⸻

Then they encountered something that frightened them almost as much.

SNORT.

Everyone stopped.

A huge bull stood beside the road.

The mother cat, who had continued following them with her kittens, stopped too.

The bull looked at them.

They looked at the bull.

The bull pawed the ground.

SCRAAAAAPE.

Hadley grabbed Uncle Jonathan’s arm.

“I do not like that.”

“Neither do I.”

The mother cat puffed herself up.

“MROWWWW!”

Grayson whispered:

“Is she trying to scare the bull?”

Apparently she was.

The bull took one step toward them.

The cat reconsidered her entire strategy.

She turned around and ran.

Four kittens scrambled after her.

Colton nodded.

“Smart cat.”

The bull snorted.

MOOOOOO!

Colton jumped so high that Hadley nearly screamed.

Everyone hurried behind a stone wall.

Even some of the spies moved.

For several seconds nobody spoke.

Then Hadley looked at Colton.

“You jumped about three feet.”

“That was tactical.”

“Everything is tactical now?”

“Yes.”

Grayson started laughing.

Then Hadley.

Soon Uncle Jonathan was trying desperately not to laugh loudly enough for anyone in the town to hear them.

⸻

That evening, they camped with the spies.

The mother cat curled beside Hadley.

Her kittens piled together against her belly.

The little striped one kept escaping.

Every time its mother pulled it back, it immediately tried again.

Colton watched approvingly.

“Never give up.”

Hadley laughed.

“That is not what that Bible verse means.”

Someone passed around food.

Figs.

Bread.

Grapes.

Pomegranates.

Then one of the spies held something toward Hadley.

“Would you like one?”

Hadley looked.

Her expression changed instantly.

“No.”

The man blinked.

“No?”

“No.”

Uncle Jonathan looked over.

Then started laughing.

It was a pickle.

“You don’t want it?” the man asked.

Hadley scooted backward.

“No.”

“They’re good.”

“I disagree.”

“You should try—”

“I HAVE.”

Colton took it.

Grayson took another.

Hadley stared at them as though they had betrayed her.

Uncle Jonathan grinned.

“So let’s review. Giants?”

“Scary.”

“Bull?”

“Very scary.”

“Pickles?”

Hadley pointed at the pickle.

“Keep that thing away from me.”

Even Caleb laughed.

⸻

Later that night, however, the mood changed.

The spies were talking.

“We can’t do this.”

Hadley listened.

“The cities are too strong.”

“The walls are too high.”

“And those people…”

One man shook his head.

“The descendants of Anak.”

The giants.

“We’ll never defeat them.”

Caleb sat nearby.

Joshua was with him.

They had seen everything the others had seen.

The walls.

The armies.

The giants.

But Caleb wasn’t pretending those things didn’t exist.

He wasn’t saying the giants were small.

They weren’t.

He wasn’t saying the cities were weak.

They weren’t.

He wasn’t saying there was nothing to be afraid of.

There was.

Hadley noticed that.

Being brave didn’t mean pretending something wasn’t scary.

Caleb knew exactly how frightening the situation looked.

He simply remembered something the others were beginning to forget.

God had brought them here.

The same God who had brought Israel out of Egypt.

The same God who had opened the Red Sea.

The same God who had fed them in the wilderness.

The giants were real.

But so was God.

And Caleb refused to remember the giants while forgetting God.

⸻

After forty days, they returned to the Israelite camp.

Thousands of people gathered.

Moses stood before them.

Aaron was there.

Everyone wanted to hear about the land.

The spies showed them the fruit.

“It really is an amazing land,” one reported.

A huge cluster of grapes was lifted for everyone to see.

People murmured excitedly.

Then came the word that changed everything.

“But…”

The people became quiet.

“The people who live there are powerful.”

Someone else added:

“The cities are fortified and very large.”

Then:

“We saw the descendants of Anak there.”

A murmur moved through the crowd.

“Giants?”

“Giants.”

“Did he say giants?”

Hadley watched fear spread from one person to another.

Then Caleb stepped forward.

He quieted the people.

His voice was confident.

“We should go up and take possession of the land, for we can certainly do it.”

But the other spies interrupted.

“We can’t attack those people; they are stronger than we are.”

Then their fear began growing.

And growing.

Until they weren’t simply describing the danger anymore.

They were making it sound hopeless.

“We seemed like grasshoppers in our own eyes.”

Grayson looked at Uncle Jonathan.

“Grasshoppers?”

Uncle Jonathan nodded.

That was how small they felt.

The people listened.

And instead of remembering what God had done—

they panicked.

⸻

That night, the camp filled with crying.

Thousands of people were afraid.

Hadley sat quietly with the mother cat beside her.

One kitten slept in her lap.

She wasn’t laughing anymore.

People complained against Moses and Aaron.

Some wanted to choose another leader.

Some actually talked about going back to Egypt.

Grayson couldn’t believe it.

“Back to Egypt?”

“Yes.”

“But they were slaves there!”

“I know.”

“Why would they want to go back?”

Uncle Jonathan looked around at the frightened people.

“Sometimes fear makes us forget.”

Hadley looked at him.

“Forget what?”

“What God has already done.”

That made her quiet.

These people had seen the plagues in Egypt.

They had walked through the Red Sea on dry ground.

They had eaten food God provided in the wilderness.

They had followed the cloud and the fire.

And now—

giants.

Suddenly the giants were all they could see.

Hadley looked toward Caleb and Joshua.

They looked heartbroken.

Not because the people were scared.

They had felt fear too.

They were heartbroken because the people had decided their fear was more trustworthy than God.

Joshua and Caleb tore their clothes in grief.

Then they spoke to the people.

“The land we passed through and explored is exceedingly good.”

The crowd listened.

“If the LORD is pleased with us, He will lead us into that land.”

Then came the words Hadley would remember:

“Do not be afraid of the people of the land… The LORD is with us.”

The LORD is with us.

Not:

There are no giants.

There were giants.

Not:

Nothing frightening will ever happen.

Frightening things already had.

Not even:

We are stronger than they are.

They probably weren’t.

Their courage came from somewhere else.

The LORD is with us.

⸻

Hadley looked down.

The kitten in her lap had awakened.

Something startled it.

It scrambled underneath her arm.

Hadley gently stroked its head.

“It’s okay.”

The kitten was still afraid.

But it stayed close to Hadley.

Hadley suddenly smiled.

“What?” Grayson asked.

“I think I understand.”

“What?”

She looked at the kitten.

“She’s still scared.”

“Yes.”

“But she knows she’s safe with us.”

Uncle Jonathan nodded.

Hadley looked toward Joshua and Caleb.

“Maybe courage isn’t when you stop being scared.”

“No,” Uncle Jonathan said. “Sometimes courage is deciding who you’re going to trust while you’re scared.”

Colton thought about that.

“So when I saw the bull…”

“You were scared.”

“I was tactically concerned.”

Hadley rolled her eyes.

“You were terrified.”

“Okay. A little.”

“The point,” Uncle Jonathan said, “isn’t that brave people never feel fear. Caleb and Joshua saw exactly the same giants everyone else saw.”

Grayson nodded.

“They just remembered God.”

“Exactly.”

⸻

The children wished the story had ended differently.

They wished the people had listened.

They wished everyone had stood up and said:

“God brought us this far. Let’s trust Him.”

But they didn’t.

And that was the saddest part.

Because unbelief had consequences.

That generation would not enter the Promised Land.

They would wander in the wilderness for forty years—one year for every day the spies had explored Canaan.

But Joshua and Caleb were different.

God promised that they would enter the land.

Not because they were never frightened.

Not because they were stronger than everyone else.

But because they trusted God and followed Him wholeheartedly.

⸻

The next morning, the children sat together overlooking the camp.

Grayson was building a miniature fortified city out of rocks.

“It needs a gate.”

Colton climbed onto a boulder.

“I’ll be the giant.”

“You’re not tall enough.”

Colton stood on his toes.

“How about now?”

“No.”

He climbed onto a higher rock.

“Now?”

“No.”

Another rock.

“COLTON,” Uncle Jonathan said.

Hadley sat nearby with the mother cat and her kittens.

Someone walked past eating a pickle.

Hadley immediately moved farther away.

Uncle Jonathan laughed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“I can be brave and still dislike pickles.”

“Absolutely.”

“I trust God.”

“Yes.”

“I do not trust pickles.”

Grayson laughed so hard his rock wall fell over.

The kittens scattered.

Colton jumped down from the boulder.

And for a while, everyone laughed.

Then Hadley looked across the camp.

She thought about all those frightened people.

She thought about the giants.

And the bull.

And the tiny kitten hiding against its mother.

And Joshua.

And Caleb.

Maybe someday she would face something that felt enormous too.

Not an actual giant.

Probably.

But something that made her feel very small.

Something that made her want to run.

And when that happened, she wanted to remember what Israel had forgotten.

Fear can tell you how big the problem is.

Faith reminds you how big God is.

Courage wasn’t pretending the giant wasn’t there.

Courage wasn’t never feeling afraid.

Courage was looking honestly at what frightened you—

and refusing to forget God.

The giants were real.

The fear was real.

But neither of those was the biggest truth in the story.

The faithfulness of God was.

Based on Numbers 13–14.
