Colton loved lightning.

From inside a warm house, anyway.

Whenever a Colorado thunderstorm rolled in, he would hurry to a window and wait.

FLASH!

“WHOA!”

Then—

BOOOOOM!

“Did you SEE that?!”

Grayson liked it too, especially when the thunder was loud enough to make the windows shake.

Hadley preferred watching the whole storm develop. She liked seeing the clouds change and listening to the rhythm of the rain.

But this storm was different.

Because there was no window.

There was no house.

And there was definitely no roof.

There was only—

WATER.

Colton opened his eyes.

Something cold splashed directly into his face.

“BLEHH!”

He sat up.

Another splash hit him.

He wiped his eyes and looked around.

He was sitting in a wooden boat.

A very old wooden boat.

Hadley was beside him.

Grayson was crouched near the side, staring into the water.

Uncle Jonathan was sitting behind them.

For several seconds nobody said anything.

Then Grayson asked the obvious question.

“Why are we in a boat?”

Hadley looked around.

“I don’t know.”

Uncle Jonathan looked over the edge.

Water stretched in every direction.

“I have a slightly more important question.”

“What?”

“Does anybody here know how to sail a boat?”

Silence.

“Anybody?”

More silence.

Grayson raised his hand.

“I’ve been on a boat.”

“Good!”

“But I’ve never gone fishing.”

“That is considerably less helpful.”

Colton stood.

The boat rocked.

Hadley grabbed his shirt.

“COLTON!”

“What?”

“Sit down!”

“I was just looking!”

“You can look while sitting!”

Colton sat.

Then he noticed something moving beside the boat.

“Guys!”

Everyone turned.

Something flashed beneath the surface.

“AN ANIMAL!”

Grayson hurried over.

“A fish!”

Another appeared.

Then another.

The twins leaned over the side.

Hadley joined them.

For a moment, the mystery of why they were sitting in an ancient boat was completely forgotten.

Then someone behind them laughed.

“Never seen fish before?”

The children turned.

There were men all around them.

Men with rough hands and weathered faces.

Several were handling ropes and sails with the ease of people who had spent their lives on the water.

One man was coiling a rope.

Another was adjusting something near the mast.

And another—

Hadley stared.

She recognized him.

Not because she had ever met him.

But because she knew the stories.

“Uncle Jonathan…”

He followed her gaze.

Hadley’s voice dropped to a whisper.

“I think that’s Peter.”

Uncle Jonathan looked.

Then he looked at the other men.

Then at the boat.

Then at the lake.

“Oh.”

Grayson caught on.

His eyes widened.

“Are these the disciples?”

Hadley nodded slowly.

Colton’s face lit up.

“Are we in a Bible story?!”

“Shhhhh!”

“But WHICH ONE?”

Nobody answered.

Then a voice came from nearby.

A voice that was calm and kind.

“Let’s go across to the other side.”

They all turned.

Jesus was there.

Suddenly Hadley didn’t care about the boat anymore.

Neither did Grayson.

Neither did Colton.

They had heard stories about Him their whole lives.

They knew about Bethlehem.

They knew about His miracles.

They knew that He healed people.

They knew He loved children.

But knowing stories about Jesus and actually standing a few feet away from Him were two very different things.

Colton whispered:

“That’s Jesus.”

Hadley nodded.

Jesus looked tired.

Not pretend tired.

Really tired.

He had been teaching crowds of people, and now evening had come.

As the boat moved away from shore, Jesus went toward the back of the boat.

There was a cushion there.

He lay down.

And before long—

Jesus was asleep.

Grayson stared.

“He can sleep in a boat?”

Apparently He could.

⸻

At first, the trip was wonderful.

The evening air was cool.

The water gently bumped against the wooden hull.

Grayson discovered approximately twenty-seven things about boats that he wanted to understand.

“What does that rope do?”

“What is that knot?”

“Why is the sail shaped like that?”

“How do you steer?”

Peter answered some of his questions.

Hadley sat near the middle of the boat, listening to the disciples talk.

Occasionally she could hear someone singing from one of the other boats crossing the lake with them.

She quietly hummed along.

Colton had found the best possible spot from which to watch everything.

He had already been told three times not to climb the mast.

“But I could.”

“That isn’t the point,” Hadley told him.

Uncle Jonathan was enjoying himself enormously.

“This,” he announced, “is a pretty good adventure.”

Then Hadley noticed something.

The wind had changed.

She stopped humming.

The sail snapped.

WHAP!

Peter looked up.

So did several other disciples.

Far across the water, the sky was becoming dark.

Not evening dark.

Storm dark.

Colton saw it.

“Ooooooh.”

A flash appeared inside the clouds.

His face lit up.

“LIGHTNING!”

FLASH!

A few seconds later—

RUMMMMMBLE.

Colton grinned.

“Did you see that?!”

Grayson looked toward the clouds.

“That’s coming this way.”

Another disciple was already pulling on a rope.

The boat changed direction slightly.

The wind became stronger.

Hadley looked back.

Jesus was still sleeping.

⸻

FLASH!

This one was closer.

BOOOOM!

Colton laughed.

“That was AWESOME!”

Then the first big wave hit.

WHOOOOSH!

Water exploded over the side.

It soaked Colton from head to toe.

His smile disappeared.

“Oh.”

Another wave struck.

The boat tilted.

Colton grabbed the side.

Hadley grabbed Colton.

Grayson grabbed Hadley.

Uncle Jonathan grabbed Grayson.

“EVERYBODY SIT DOWN!”

The wind screamed across the lake.

The disciples were suddenly moving fast.

“GET THAT SAIL DOWN!”

“PULL!”

“WATCH THE ROPE!”

The gentle little waves were gone.

Now the boat climbed one dark wall of water—

up…

up…

UP…

then plunged down the other side.

Colton’s stomach seemed to stay at the top.

“AAAAAH!”

CRASH!

Another wave broke over them.

Water poured into the boat.

Grayson looked down.

“WE’RE FILLING UP!”

Peter shoved a container toward him.

“BAIL!”

Grayson didn’t need to be told twice.

Scoop.

Throw.

Scoop.

Throw.

Hadley grabbed another container.

Uncle Jonathan joined them.

Even Colton started scooping water.

But every time they threw some out—

WHOOOOSH!

another wave threw more in.

Grayson was working as fast as he could.

“We can’t get it out fast enough!”

FLASH!

For one brilliant instant the whole lake lit up.

Hadley saw enormous waves.

The other boats were being tossed around too.

Then darkness swallowed everything again.

KRAK-KOOOOOM!

Colton ducked.

A few minutes ago he had loved the lightning.

Now he was soaking wet, freezing, being thrown around inside a wooden boat in the middle of it.

He moved closer to Uncle Jonathan.

“I don’t like this lightning.”

Uncle Jonathan put an arm around him.

“I don’t particularly like it either.”

The boat lurched violently.

Hadley fell against Grayson.

Peter and the other fishermen were shouting.

And that’s when Hadley realized something that frightened her more than the thunder.

These men were fishermen.

They had spent years on this lake.

And they were scared.

Very scared.

If Peter thought they were in trouble…

they were in trouble.

⸻

Another wave crashed into the boat.

“WE’RE GOING DOWN!” someone shouted.

Colton looked toward the back.

Then he froze.

“Guys?”

Nobody heard him.

“GUYS!”

“What?” Grayson yelled.

Colton pointed.

There, while the wind howled…

while lightning flashed…

while waves crashed into the boat…

Jesus was still asleep.

Grayson stared.

“How?!”

Hadley couldn’t understand it either.

The disciples rushed toward Him.

They woke Jesus.

“Teacher!”

Another wave struck.

“Don’t You care if we drown?”

Jesus opened His eyes.

Hadley watched Him stand.

The boat pitched beneath His feet.

Wind whipped His clothing.

Rain struck His face.

Lightning split the sky.

Around Him were grown men who thought they might die.

But Jesus wasn’t panicking.

He looked at the storm.

Then He spoke.

“Peace! Be still!”

And—

the wind stopped.

Not slowly.

It stopped.

The sail fell limp.

The screaming wind vanished.

The waves settled.

The thunder disappeared into the distance.

And suddenly…

everything was quiet.

Completely quiet.

Water dripped from Colton’s hair.

Drip.

Grayson was still holding his container.

Hadley slowly lowered hers.

Nobody moved.

Nobody spoke.

A moment earlier they had been shouting just to hear one another.

Now they could hear water gently touching the side of the boat.

Lap.

Lap.

Lap.

Colton stared at the sky.

Then at Jesus.

Then back at the sky.

“What…”

He couldn’t even finish.

Jesus turned toward them.

“Why are you so afraid? Do you still have no faith?”

The disciples stared at Him.

Hadley expected them to cheer.

They didn’t.

Something much bigger was happening.

Peter looked at the calm water.

Then at Jesus.

One disciple whispered to another:

“Who is this?”

Another said:

“Even the wind and the waves obey Him!”

And suddenly Hadley understood why they weren’t celebrating.

They had known Jesus could teach.

They had watched Him heal people.

But they had just seen something astonishing.

Jesus had spoken to a storm—

and creation had obeyed Him.

⸻

For a long time, Colton sat quietly.

That was unusual enough that Uncle Jonathan noticed.

“You okay?”

Colton nodded.

Then he looked toward Jesus.

“I was scared.”

“I know.”

“I like storms.”

Uncle Jonathan smiled.

“From inside the house.”

“Yeah.”

Colton thought about it.

“When I was actually IN one…”

He looked at the water.

“…it was different.”

Hadley had been thinking too.

“But Jesus was in the same storm.”

Grayson looked toward the back of the boat.

“And He wasn’t scared.”

Hadley nodded.

“But here’s the strange part.”

“What?”

“The disciples were scared even though Jesus was already in the boat with them.”

Nobody said anything.

That thought felt important.

The storm had made it seem as though everything was out of control.

The disciples had even asked Jesus whether He cared about them.

But Jesus hadn’t stopped caring.

He hadn’t abandoned them.

He hadn’t accidentally lost control of the lake.

And He had been right there the whole time.

Grayson frowned thoughtfully.

“But Jesus doesn’t always make bad things stop immediately.”

Uncle Jonathan looked at him.

“No. He doesn’t.”

That mattered too.

The lesson couldn’t simply be:

If you’re scared, ask Jesus and He’ll make every scary thing disappear.

Sometimes storms continued.

Sometimes people prayed and still had to go through something difficult.

But that night on the lake showed them who was in the boat with them.

Jesus was greater than the thing they feared.

And they could trust Him even when they couldn’t yet see what He was going to do.

Hadley looked toward Jesus.

Maybe faith wasn’t saying:

“Nothing scary will ever happen to me.”

Maybe faith was being able to say:

“Even when I am scared, I can trust Jesus.”

⸻

The rest of the journey was very different.

The moon appeared between the clouds.

Grayson went back to examining the boat.

Hadley began humming again.

Colton watched distant flashes of lightning far behind them.

He still thought they were beautiful.

Especially now that they were far away.

Then something bumped the side of the boat.

Splash!

Colton looked down.

“FISH!”

Immediately all three children hurried over.

Several fish moved through the moonlit water.

Grayson watched them.

“Do you think those fish knew Jesus just stopped the storm?”

Hadley laughed.

“I don’t know.”

Colton leaned closer.

“Maybe the fish were scared too.”

“Colton,” Hadley said, “fish LIVE in water.”

“So?”

“So I don’t think they’re afraid of waves.”

Colton considered this.

“Maybe that fish was.”

He pointed.

“That one looks nervous.”

“You cannot tell if a fish looks nervous.”

“I can.”

“No, you can’t.”

“I speak fish.”

Grayson joined in.

“What did it say?”

Colton made enormous fish eyes.

“BLUB BLUB JESUS STOPPED THE STORM BLUB BLUB.”

Hadley burst out laughing.

Uncle Jonathan tried not to.

He failed.

Soon all four of them were laughing so hard that Peter turned around to see what was happening.

And from the back of the boat…

Jesus smiled.

The little boat continued across the dark water.

Hadley.

Grayson.

Colton.

Uncle Jonathan.

The disciples.

And Jesus.

The children didn’t know what adventures waited on the other shore.

They didn’t know when the next frightening thing would happen.

But they knew something now that they hadn’t understood before.

When the sky grew dark…

when the wind became loud…

when they were frightened…

and when it seemed like everything was out of control…

they could remember the night they sat soaking wet in a little wooden boat and heard three words:

“Peace! Be still!”

And they could remember something even more important.

Before Jesus calmed the storm—

He had been with them in the storm.

Based on Mark 4:35–41.
