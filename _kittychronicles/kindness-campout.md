The first sign that this camping trip might not go exactly according to plan came when Uncle Jonathan opened the back of the vehicle and said:

“Okay! Let’s get the tents set up.”

Grandpa pulled out his tent.

Jeff and Jodi pulled out theirs.

Grandma had everything she needed.

Then Uncle Jonathan looked into the vehicle.

He moved a cooler.

He moved a backpack.

He moved a bag of food.

He looked underneath a blanket.

Angie watched him.

“Jonathan?”

“Yes?”

“Where’s our tent?”

Uncle Jonathan continued searching.

“I am currently investigating that.”

Angie folded her arms.

“Did you bring our tent?”

“I brought several extremely important things.”

“That wasn’t my question.”

“I brought snacks.”

“Jonathan.”

“A flashlight.”

“Jonathan.”

“Extra socks.”

“THE TENT.”

Uncle Jonathan stood up.

“I have excellent news.”

Everyone waited.

“We definitely own a tent.”

Angie stared at him.

“Where is it?”

“At home.”

There was silence.

Then Grandpa started laughing.

Grandma shook her head.

Jodi covered her mouth.

Jeff said, “Well done, Jonathan.”

Hadley giggled.

And Grayson’s face suddenly lit up.

“You forgot the tent?”

“Yes.”

“So we need to build a shelter?”

“Well—”

“A FORT?!”

Grayson was already looking at the forest.

“This is the best thing that’s happened all day.”

⸻

They were high in the Colorado mountains, surrounded by enormous pine trees.

The air smelled like pine needles and earth.

Mountains rose beyond the forest.

A little stream rushed over rocks near the campsite.

It was beautiful.

And within approximately thirty seconds, Colton had climbed something.

“COLTON!” Jodi called.

“What?”

He was standing on top of a boulder.

“How did you even get up there?”

Colton looked confused by the question.

“Climbing.”

Then he jumped.

WHOOP!

He landed on another rock.

“Colton!” Jeff called.

“I’M OKAY!”

He jumped again.

BOING!

Uncle Jonathan looked at Jeff.

“Does he ever travel on the actual ground?”

“No.”

Meanwhile, Hadley had discovered something considerably more important.

“KITTYYYYY!”

Everyone turned.

A cat was walking into the campsite.

Not a wildcat.

An ordinary house cat.

It was gray and white, with a fluffy tail held straight up.

It walked directly toward Hadley as though it had been invited.

Hadley crouched.

“Hi, sweetie!”

The cat rubbed against her legs.

Grayson came over.

Colton abandoned his rocks.

Within seconds all three children were surrounding it.

“Where did you come from?” Hadley asked.

The cat responded:

“Mrow.”

“Excellent explanation,” said Angie.

Then they heard another sound.

Yip!

A puppy came bounding out from behind a nearby campsite.

Then another.

The first puppy ran directly toward Colton.

Colton ran.

The puppy chased him.

Colton jumped over a log.

The puppy went under it.

Colton jumped onto a rock.

The puppy tried to climb it.

“PARKOUR PUPPY!”

Soon the campsite contained three children, one mysterious cat, two puppies, and several adults attempting to determine which animals actually belonged to whom.

“This,” Grandma announced, “is already my favorite camping trip.”

⸻

That afternoon, everyone went hiking.

Grandpa led the way.

Grandma walked with Hadley.

Jeff and Jodi followed the children.

Angie and Uncle Jonathan carried backpacks.

And apparently—

the cat was coming too.

At first they assumed it would stay behind.

It didn’t.

They walked fifty feet.

Mrow.

Hadley turned around.

The cat was following them.

They walked another hundred feet.

Still there.

Across a little bridge.

Cat.

Up a hill.

Cat.

Around a fallen tree.

Cat.

“This cat is seriously hiking with us,” Grayson said.

The cat trotted past him.

Apparently Grayson was too slow.

Then the puppies appeared again.

“PUPPIES!”

Their owners were hiking nearby, so soon the groups were walking together.

They reached the stream.

Colton immediately began hopping from rock to rock.

“Colton,” Jodi called, “those rocks are wet!”

“I know!”

“That means they’re slippery!”

“I know!”

“That means—”

SLIP!

SPLASH!

Colton disappeared into approximately eight inches of water.

There was a moment of silence.

Then his head popped up.

He was soaked.

Hadley burst out laughing.

Grayson fell against a tree.

Even Colton started laughing.

Jodi shook her head.

“And what have we learned?”

Colton stood up.

“Bring extra socks?”

Uncle Jonathan pointed triumphantly.

“WHICH I DID!”

Angie stared at him.

“Yes, Jonathan. Socks. But no tent.”

“Oh, we’re still discussing that?”

“We will be discussing that for years.”

⸻

Farther up the trail, they found trouble.

One of the puppies had wandered toward a pile of fallen branches.

Now it was stuck.

Yip! Yip!

Its leash had tangled around the branches.

The puppy pulled.

The leash tightened.

“Wait!” Hadley said.

Everyone stopped.

Hadley carefully approached.

“Don’t pull, puppy. We’ll help you.”

Grayson studied the branches.

“We need to lift that one.”

Jeff helped.

Grayson crawled underneath.

Hadley kept the frightened puppy calm.

Colton, for once, stood completely still so he wouldn’t scare it.

Soon—

POP!

The leash came free.

The puppy immediately covered Hadley’s face in kisses.

“BLEHHH!”

Everyone laughed.

Grandma smiled.

“You know what that reminds me of?”

“What?” Hadley asked.

“A Bible verse.”

Grandma quoted it:

“Be kind and compassionate to one another.”

“Ephesians 4:32,” Grandpa added.

Hadley scratched the puppy behind its ears.

Grandma continued.

“Kindness is noticing that somebody—or sometimes some little creature—needs help, and choosing to do something about it.”

Colton nodded seriously.

Then the puppy licked him directly in the mouth.

“AAAAHHHH!”

He ran.

The puppy chased him.

Grandpa laughed.

“Apparently the puppy is showing kindness back.”

“TOO MUCH KINDNESS!”

⸻

When they returned to camp, Grayson had important work to do.

The tent situation had not magically fixed itself.

“So,” Grayson announced, “we build.”

He selected a safe spot near the campsite, with the adults helping him make sure they weren’t damaging living trees.

He gathered fallen branches.

Jeff helped with larger pieces.

Grandpa showed him a way to brace them.

Uncle Jonathan carried wood.

Grayson directed operations.

“No, that one goes there.”

Uncle Jonathan stopped.

“You know I’m an adult, right?”

“Yes.”

“And you’re six.”

“Yes.”

“And yet somehow you’re the construction supervisor?”

“Yes.”

Uncle Jonathan considered this.

“Okay.”

They leaned long branches into a sturdy frame and covered parts of it with a tarp, blankets, and other camping gear.

Hadley collected soft pine needles and bedding for the floor.

Colton carried sticks.

Sometimes.

Other times he climbed the rocks beside the construction site and announced:

“FORT INSPECTION!”

Then jumped down.

Angie looked at the finished shelter.

“This is actually pretty impressive.”

Grayson beamed.

Uncle Jonathan crawled inside.

“I think we can sleep here.”

The mysterious cat walked inside.

Turned around three times.

Lay down.

Grayson pointed.

“Approved.”

Apparently the building inspector had spoken.

⸻

That evening, everyone gathered around the campfire.

They ate.

They talked.

Hadley sang a song.

Grandma joined her.

Soon Angie did too.

The puppies had returned to their own campsite.

But the cat remained.

Of course.

It sat beside Hadley like it had been part of the family for years.

As darkness settled over the mountains, Grandpa opened his Bible.

“Here’s another verse about kindness.”

He read from Micah 6:8:

“What does the LORD require of you? To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God.”

“What does mercy mean?” Grayson asked.

Grandpa thought.

“Seeing someone who needs compassion and giving it.”

“Like helping the puppy?” Hadley asked.

“Exactly.”

“Or helping Uncle Jonathan because he forgot his tent?” Angie asked.

Everyone laughed.

Uncle Jonathan sighed.

“I feel like I’m being persecuted.”

“No,” Angie said. “You’re experiencing consequences.”

Grandma laughed so hard she almost spilled her drink.

Then Grandpa became thoughtful.

“Jesus taught something even harder.”

Everyone listened.

“He told us to love our neighbors. And He showed that our neighbor isn’t just our favorite person or somebody who’s nice to us.”

Grandma nodded.

“Kindness is easy when someone is kind to you first.”

Grandpa said, “Jesus calls us to something bigger.”

To notice.

To care.

To help.

Even when there was nothing in it for us.

Hadley stroked the cat.

Grayson looked at his fort.

Colton poked the fire with a stick.

For approximately three seconds, everything was peaceful.

Then—

SNAP.

Everyone froze.

Something had moved in the forest.

The cat’s head shot up.

SNAP.

Closer.

Jeff stood.

Grandpa reached for a flashlight.

Jodi quietly called the children toward the adults.

“Come here.”

Colton came immediately.

That told Uncle Jonathan everything he needed to know.

Grandpa shone the light toward the trees.

Nothing.

Then—

two eyes reflected back.

Hadley grabbed Grandma’s hand.

Something moved between the trees.

Large.

Silent.

Then it stepped into view.

A long body.

Tawny fur.

A long tail.

Uncle Jonathan’s heart began pounding.

Grayson whispered:

“Is that…”

Grandpa answered quietly.

“Mountain lion.”

Nobody moved toward it.

Nobody ran.

The adults drew the children together and gave the animal plenty of space.

The mountain lion stood at the edge of the trees.

It watched them.

Colton whispered:

“That’s a REALLY big kitty.”

Hadley whispered:

“Do not pet that kitty.”

“I wasn’t going to!”

“This is important enough that I wanted to make sure.”

Even the mysterious hiking cat had decided this was not an appropriate moment to make a new friend.

It disappeared behind Grandma.

Uncle Jonathan looked at it.

“Oh, NOW you need us?”

Mrow.

The mountain lion remained at the edge of the forest.

Grandpa made sure everyone stayed together.

They gave it a clear way to leave.

Nobody approached it.

Nobody tried to feed it.

Nobody ran.

After several tense moments, the mountain lion turned.

It stepped between the trees.

Its long tail disappeared into the darkness.

And it was gone.

Nobody spoke.

Then Colton whispered:

“That.”

He paused.

“Was.”

Another pause.

“AWESOME.”

Jodi stared at him.

“That is not the word I was thinking.”

Grandma laughed nervously.

Hadley picked up the little cat.

“I like this size better.”

Mrow.

⸻

That night, everyone headed toward their sleeping places.

Those who had remembered their tents climbed inside them.

Those who had not…

looked toward Grayson’s forest shelter.

Grayson stood proudly at the entrance.

“Welcome.”

Uncle Jonathan bowed.

“Thank you for rescuing us from my poor planning.”

“Five dollars.”

“What?”

Grayson grinned.

“Hotel fee.”

“GRayson!”

Inside, they arranged their sleeping bags.

The forest was dark beyond them.

Hadley lay near the entrance.

The cat curled up beside her.

Colton was still talking about the mountain lion.

“It was like a gigantic cat.”

“Yes.”

“With huge paws.”

“Yes.”

“And it could probably jump REALLY far.”

“Yes.”

“Farther than me?”

“Much farther.”

Colton considered this.

“I need to practice.”

“No,” several voices said simultaneously.

⸻

Sometime during the night, Grayson woke up.

Something was wrong.

Drip.

He opened his eyes.

Drip.

A drop landed on Uncle Jonathan.

Drip.

Another.

Grayson whispered:

“Uncle Jonathan.”

Nothing.

“Uncle Jonathan.”

“Hmm?”

“The fort is leaking.”

Uncle Jonathan opened one eye.

Drip.

A drop hit his forehead.

“I noticed.”

Rain began tapping against the branches.

Grayson looked disappointed.

“My fort isn’t working.”

Uncle Jonathan sat up.

“Hey.”

Grayson looked at him.

“You built a shelter in the forest when we didn’t have a tent. You worked hard. You asked for help. You helped solve a problem for everybody.”

Another drop landed on Uncle Jonathan.

Drip.

He wiped it away.

“It’s an excellent fort.”

“But it leaks.”

“Excellent things sometimes need improvement.”

Grayson thought about that.

Then Hadley woke.

Then Colton.

Then Angie.

Soon everyone was adjusting the tarp together.

Grandpa came out with another one.

Jeff brought rope.

Jodi brought extra blankets.

Grandma brought something even more useful.

Snacks.

Ten minutes later, the fort was dry again.

Grayson looked around.

“We fixed it.”

Uncle Jonathan smiled.

“No.”

Grayson looked confused.

“We fixed it.”

And somehow that felt even better.

⸻

The next morning, sunshine streamed through the trees.

The forest smelled fresh from the rain.

Everyone gathered for breakfast.

The cat appeared again.

The puppies visited.

No mountain lion visited.

Everyone agreed this was acceptable.

Grandpa looked around the group.

“Do you know something interesting about kindness?”

Colton was feeding a puppy a permitted treat.

“What?”

“It usually spreads.”

He pointed toward Hadley.

“Hadley helped the puppy.”

Then toward Grayson.

“Grayson built a shelter because some people needed one.”

He glanced at Uncle Jonathan.

Uncle Jonathan raised his hand.

“I was one of the needy.”

Angie nodded.

“Very needy.”

Grandpa continued.

“Then everyone helped Grayson when the shelter leaked.”

Grandma smiled.

“That’s how God designed us to live. Not always asking, ‘What do I get?’ but asking, ‘Who can I help?’”

Hadley remembered another verse.

“Didn’t Jesus say something about doing things for other people being like doing them for Him?”

Grandpa smiled.

“He did.”

He quoted Jesus’ words from Matthew 25:

“Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for me.”

That made everyone quiet.

Even little acts mattered.

Helping a frightened puppy.

Sharing food.

Building a shelter.

Comforting someone.

Carrying something heavy.

Including someone who felt left out.

Being patient.

Forgiving.

Noticing when someone needed help.

Kindness didn’t require a giant adventure.

But sometimes—

it turned into one.

⸻

After breakfast, they went for one last hike.

Grandma and Grandpa walked together.

Jeff and Jodi followed.

Angie and Uncle Jonathan came behind the kids.

Hadley walked with the cat.

Or, more accurately, the cat walked wherever it wanted and Hadley followed.

Grayson collected ideas for his next construction project.

And Colton—

Colton spotted three enormous rocks.

He climbed the first.

Jumped to the second.

Scrambled onto the third.

“COLTON!” Jeff called.

Colton stood at the top.

Below him were his family.

Grandma.

Grandpa.

Mom and Dad.

Uncle Jonathan.

Aunt Angie.

Hadley.

Grayson.

Two puppies.

And one extremely determined hiking cat.

Colton spread his arms.

“BEST CAMPING TRIP EVER!”

Then the cat jumped onto the rock beside him.

Colton stared.

The cat stared back.

“Mrow.”

Colton grinned.

“You want to race?”

“Do NOT race the cat!” Jodi called.

Too late.

Colton jumped down.

The cat took another route.

They raced toward the trail.

“COLTON!”

“I’m being kind! I’m letting him win!”

The entire family burst out laughing.

And as they walked together beneath the tall mountain pines, Hadley thought about Grandpa’s words.

Maybe kindness wasn’t usually one enormous heroic thing.

Maybe it was a hundred little choices.

Notice.

Care.

Help.

And perhaps that’s why the Bible didn’t merely tell them to occasionally do something kind.

It said:

“Be kind and compassionate to one another.”

Because kindness wasn’t simply something God wanted them to do.

With His help, it was something they could become.

Behind them, Grayson’s fort stood among the trees.

Ahead of them, Colton leaped from another rock.

The puppies raced along the trail.

The little cat trotted after everybody with its tail held high.

And somewhere—hopefully very far away—a mountain lion probably wondered why the loudest family in Colorado had invaded its forest.

“Be kind and compassionate to one another.” — Ephesians 4:32

The End.
