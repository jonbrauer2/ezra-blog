*A practical method for observing, modeling, experimenting, and updating under uncertainty.*

---

Most people are taught to think in terms of true or false, right or wrong, proven or disproven.

Reality is rarely that simple.

The Curious Self-Detective Framework provides a practical method for learning from observations without becoming attached to conclusions.

---

## Step 1: Observe

What actually happened?

Observations are facts, not explanations.

Examples:

- I woke up at 2 a.m. with itching.
- My tinnitus increased.
- I took a new supplement.
- My energy improved.

Avoid explanations at this stage.

**Not:** *The supplement caused the improvement.*

**Just:** *The improvement occurred.*

---

## Step 2: Find Patterns

What observations tend to occur together?

Examples:

- Tinnitus often appears with itching.
- Certain foods often precede fatigue.
- Certain interventions often precede improvement.

Patterns are more informative than isolated observations.

---

## Step 3: Build Models

What explanations could account for the patterns?

Examples:

- Histamine activation.
- Microbial ecology changes.
- Food sensitivity.
- Sleep disruption.
- Stress response.

Multiple models may be simultaneously valid.

Do not force yourself to choose only one.

---

## Step 4: Assign Confidence

Instead of asking:

> "Which model is correct?"

Ask:

> "How much confidence do I currently have in each model?"

Example:

- Histamine model: 70%
- Microbial ecology model: 60%
- Food trigger model: 40%
- Stress model: 30%
- Unknown: 80%

Keep "Unknown" on the board.

Unknown is not failure. Unknown is honesty.

---

*Observations become patterns; patterns become models; models become predictions to test.*

---

## Step 5: Make Predictions

Every useful model predicts something.

If the histamine model is important:

- Certain foods may worsen symptoms.
- Antihistamine strategies may help.

If the microbial ecology model is important:

- Antimicrobial interventions may matter.
- Microbiome disruptions may change symptoms.

Predictions create opportunities to learn.

---

## Step 6: Run Low-Risk Experiments

The goal is not to prove a model.

The goal is to gather information.

Examples:

- Remove a food temporarily.
- Add a supplement.
- Change sleep habits.
- Track symptoms.

Experiments should be proportionate to the risk involved.

---

## Step 7: Update

After new information arrives, update confidence levels.

Do not ask:

> "Was I right?"

Ask:

> "What should I believe now?"

The goal is not certainty.

The goal is a progressively better model of reality.

---

## Advanced Step: Evaluate Your Markers

Sometimes the problem is not the model.

Sometimes the problem is the marker.

Ask:

- Is this symptom a reliable indicator?
- Does this marker consistently predict outcomes?
- Am I measuring the right thing?

Good detectives evaluate both their theories and their measuring tools.

---

## The Core Principle

The goal is not to find the right model.

The goal is to continuously improve the model.

Being wrong is not failure.

Failing to update is failure.
